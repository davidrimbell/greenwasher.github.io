Plástico vs. papel: ¿qué envolturas son más ecológicas? – DW – 24/04/2024

The article reveals that the preference for cardboard packaging over plastic is often a green illusion based on misconceptions. Although paper enjoys a better reputation and is easier to recycle in many places, its production generates significant emissions, and its greater weight increases pollution during transportation. Moreover, many seemingly sustainable solutions, such as paper cups, require plastic coatings that hinder their actual recyclability, demonstrating that sustainability is not determined simply by the primary material of the packaging. The chemical complexity of plastics and the limited global recycling infrastructure underscore that the solution is not a unilateral shift to other materials. Experts point out that bioplastics, promoted as eco-friendly alternatives, often conceal negative impacts such as deforestation and chemical pollution—a clear example of greenwashing that distracts from environmental realities. Ignoring the drawbacks of these new options prevents addressing the true nature of the problem, which goes beyond choosing between two types of packaging. The relevance of this text to greenwashing lies in exposing how both industry and consumers are misled by simplified narratives that present one alternative as inherently superior. The debate over paper versus plastic serves as a smokescreen to hide the root of the problem: our culture of excessive disposability. The final message is that focusing on material substitution, rather than promoting reuse and reducing consumption, perpetuates the environmental crisis and allows green marketing to mask a lack of systemic responsibility.
