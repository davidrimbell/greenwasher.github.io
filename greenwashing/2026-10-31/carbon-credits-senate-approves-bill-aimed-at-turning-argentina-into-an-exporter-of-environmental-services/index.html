Carbon credits: Senate approves bill aimed at turning Argentina into an exporter of environmental services

The Argentine Senate approved a regulatory framework for carbon credits, allowing the commercialization of certificates generated by emission reduction projects. This legislation aims to formalize a market that grants these assets a property status, incentivizing investments in conservation and clean energy through long-term fiscal stability and tax exemptions. The law is relevant to the debate on corporate greenwashing, as it facilitates emission offsetting, creating the risk that polluting industries may use these credits to soften their ecological footprint without genuinely reducing their negative impacts. Critics argue that oversight mechanisms could be insufficient, raising the possibility that conservation financing becomes more of an environmental marketing tool than a genuine climate measure. Although the initiative promotes the idea that Argentina could become an environmental creditor, the speed of the legislative process and weaknesses in verification mechanisms fuel criticism. This context illustrates how legal frameworks can facilitate the commercialization of superficial environmental benefits, allowing companies to "purchase" their social license to operate without undergoing deep structural transformations, thereby perpetuating misleading ecological narratives.
