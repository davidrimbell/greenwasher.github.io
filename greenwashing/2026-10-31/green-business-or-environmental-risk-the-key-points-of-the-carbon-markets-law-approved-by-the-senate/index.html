Green business or environmental risk? The key points of the carbon markets law approved by the Senate

The article examines carbon markets in Argentina, highlighting that they allow companies to offset emissions through credits generated by conservation or renewable energy projects. However, their implementation faces complex regulatory challenges, stemming from lengthy international negotiations aimed at establishing clear standards and preventing fraud. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the technical difficulty of ensuring the genuine traceability of greenhouse gas reductions. The risk of double counting, combined with a lack of strict oversight, makes it easier to claim environmental mitigation that either does not exist or has already been accounted for, creating a misleading appearance of sustainability. In conclusion, although these mechanisms aim to incentivize decarbonization, the opacity in verifying these credits can lead to greenwashing practices. This context is crucial for understanding how, without rigorous international guarantees, voluntary markets can serve to mask the actual failure to meet climate targets under the guise of ecological responsibility.
