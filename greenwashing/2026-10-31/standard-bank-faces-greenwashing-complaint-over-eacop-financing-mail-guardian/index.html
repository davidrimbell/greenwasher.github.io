Standard Bank faces greenwashing complaint over EACOP financing | Mail & Guardian

Standard Bank faces a formal complaint for allegedly engaging in greenwashing by promoting itself as a leader in sustainable finance and the just energy transition while providing critical funding for the environmentally destructive East African Crude Oil Pipeline. The complainants argue that the bank’s advertising creates a misleading impression of its environmental commitment through the use of renewable energy imagery and claims of net-zero targets, which directly contradict its support for major fossil fuel infrastructure. The criticism highlights the factual inconsistency between the bank’s public sustainability narratives and its financial backing of a project expected to generate significant carbon emissions and damage sensitive ecosystems in Uganda and Tanzania. By financing this pipeline, the bank is accused of locking regions into long-term oil extraction, thereby undermining its stated goals of reducing exposure to fossil fuels and promoting genuine climate action. This case is relevant to greenwashing because it demonstrates how corporations can use vague, positive environmental claims and selective imagery to mask contradictory business practices. It underscores the danger of unsubstantiated corporate sustainability marketing, showing how financial institutions may appear eco-friendly to consumers while simultaneously enabling high-carbon projects that violate the very principles they publicly champion.
