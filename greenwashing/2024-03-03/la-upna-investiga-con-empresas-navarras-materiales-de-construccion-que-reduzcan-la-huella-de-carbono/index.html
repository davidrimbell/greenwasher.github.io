La UPNA investiga con empresas navarras materiales de construcción que reduzcan la huella de carbono

The article describes the ENERCON project, which aims to revolutionize construction by utilizing eco-active, photocatalytic materials that actively remove carbon from the atmosphere when exposed to sunlight. Backed by a consortium of Navarre-based construction firms and the Public University of Navarre, the initiative seeks to create zero-emission urban spaces where architecture plays an active role in environmental cleanup. By harnessing solar energy and integrating digital solutions, the project strives to establish a new paradigm of circular, green building communities that significantly reduce the carbon footprint of the built environment. A crucial component of this initiative is the implementation of a transparent digital platform using advanced hashgraph technology to validate and certify the ecological impact of these construction actions. This system connects potential buyers of green carbon tokens with generators of verified environmental benefits, ensuring maximum trust and transparency in the certification process. By monitoring and supervising the decontamination processes through a dedicated sensor network, the project ensures that the claimed environmental benefits are legitimate and measurable, thereby promoting genuine sustainable construction practices. This article is highly relevant to greenwashing because it directly addresses the core problem of unverifiable environmental claims by establishing a rigorous, technology-driven certification mechanism. In an era where many companies use vague sustainability rhetoric to mask poor environmental performance, ENERCON offers a concrete model for accountability. By linking material innovation with immutable digital verification, it demonstrates how to move beyond superficial "green" branding toward actual, auditable carbon reduction, setting a high standard for integrity in corporate sustainability reporting.
