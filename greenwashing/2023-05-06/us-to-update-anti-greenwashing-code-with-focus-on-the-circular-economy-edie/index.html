US to update anti-greenwashing code with focus on the circular economy - edie

The FTC is reviewing its Green Guides to combat greenwashing, addressing the critical disconnect between corporate environmental claims and consumer understanding. This update aims to prevent vague marketing by potentially requiring companies to specify the exact proportion of recycled content and provide clear instructions for recycling or biodegradation. By tightening standards for material claims, regulators hope to ensure that environmental benefits are substantiated rather than merely implied. Simultaneously, the review addresses the growing prevalence of carbon offsetting and net-zero claims. Environmental coalitions are urging stricter guidance because many offsetting schemes fail to deliver genuine climate benefits due to issues like double-counting. Critics argue that companies often use these vague labels to market products without committing to direct emission reductions, thereby misleading consumers about their true environmental impact. This regulatory evolution is highly relevant to greenwashing as it signals a global shift toward stricter, verifiable sustainability standards. With parallel developments like the European Green Claims Directive, companies can no longer rely on ambiguous terminology. These updates force transparency, ensuring that “green” marketing reflects actual practices rather than superficial impressions, ultimately holding businesses accountable for accurate environmental communication.
