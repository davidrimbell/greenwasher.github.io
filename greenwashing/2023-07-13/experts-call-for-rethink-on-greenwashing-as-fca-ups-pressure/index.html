Experts call for rethink on greenwashing as FCA ups pressure

Experts call for rethink on greenwashing as FCA ups pressure

UK regulators are urging a fundamental overhaul of sustainability-linked lending to combat greenwashing, citing weak incentives and conflicts of interest within the banking sector. The Financial Conduct Authority warns that lenders often fail to set ambitious targets or impose meaningful financial penalties for missed goals, allowing institutions to meet internal ESG quotas with superficial efforts. This lack of rigor creates a market where banks may prioritize their own remuneration targets over genuine environmental impact, resulting in products that lack substantive integrity and transparency. Experts argue that ambiguous definitions and high implementation costs discourage borrowers and encourage lenders to offer unambitious terms, effectively repackaging standard loans as "green." Without clear reporting standards and stricter documentation, the market risks defaulting to business-as-usual practices where sustainability claims do not withstand scrutiny. The absence of robust mechanisms to penalize non-compliance means that financial institutions can benefit from green labels without making the deep structural changes required for meaningful transition finance. This regulatory push is critical because nearly a fifth of global greenwashing allegations now target the financial sector, highlighting systemic vulnerabilities in how banks validate sustainability claims. As loan volumes decline, there is a heightened risk that complex green requirements could disadvantage smaller suppliers while allowing larger entities to exploit regulatory gaps. Strengthening definitions and enforcement is essential to rebuild trust and ensure that trade finance instruments genuinely support sustainable development rather than serving as mere marketing tools.
