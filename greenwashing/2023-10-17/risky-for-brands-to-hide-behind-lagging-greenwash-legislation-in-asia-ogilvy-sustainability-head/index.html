Risky for brands to ‘hide’ behind lagging greenwash legislation in Asia: Ogilvy sustainability head

The article argues that brands must proactively adopt the strictest global sustainability standards, such as those in Europe, rather than waiting for local legislation in regions like Asia Pacific. This urgency is driven by new regulations banning misleading general claims and increasing legal risks for multinational corporations. By exceeding current regulatory requirements, companies can mitigate the danger of being perceived as deceptive while preparing for a landscape where transparency is increasingly mandated by law. Communication strategies must shift from complex corporate jargon to honest, accessible messaging that respects consumer intelligence. The piece highlights a significant trust gap, with majorities of both consumers and executives acknowledging widespread greenwashing practices. Consequently, businesses are urged to communicate with the honesty and clarity used when speaking to children, avoiding obfuscation. Failure to do so not only risks reputational damage but also leads to tangible consequences, including customer attrition and social media backlash. Relevance to greenwashing lies in the article’s exposure of the industry’s defensive posturing and the growing scrutiny facing agencies and clients alike. It illustrates how the "Burning the Planet" campaign serves as a prime example of greenwashing, where extensive marketing budgets overshadowed actual environmental efforts. Ultimately, the text underscores that as stakeholders become more demanding and educated, the era of vague eco-claims is ending, forcing a fundamental recalibration of how sustainability is portrayed and practiced.
