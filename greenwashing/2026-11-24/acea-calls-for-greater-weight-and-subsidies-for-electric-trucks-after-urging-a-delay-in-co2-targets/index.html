ACEA calls for greater weight and subsidies for electric trucks after urging a delay in CO₂ targets

ACEA’s initiative to accelerate the electrification of heavy-duty transport through operational advantages—such as increased authorized weights and funds derived from the carbon market—poses a crucial strategic dilemma. While these measures aim to address real technical disadvantages, such as reduced payload capacity due to battery weight, the simultaneous request to delay climate targets creates a fundamental contradiction. This hybrid approach demands clarity regarding manufacturers’ commitments to ensure that institutional support does not merely serve as an extension to maintain dependence on diesel. Market realities show that the mere availability of models is insufficient to guarantee the transition, as the share of electric trucks remains marginal in many countries. Success depends on viable operational conditions that integrate accessible charging and affordable costs, not just financial or regulatory incentives. Therefore, it is imperative for authorities to rigorously assess the impact on infrastructure and ensure that aid addresses genuine structural barriers, avoiding blanket concessions that do not drive effective changes in the existing fleet. This case is highly relevant to the debate on greenwashing because it exposes the risk that the ecological transition could be used as a superficial image-cleansing exercise without substantive environmental benefits. If regulatory flexibilities are accumulated without requiring measurable results in emission reductions, the purpose of climate policies is undermined. True sustainability demands that any benefits granted to the sector be strictly linked to verifiable progress in replacing fossil fuels, ensuring that the green narrative translates into concrete actions rather than mere public relations strategies.
