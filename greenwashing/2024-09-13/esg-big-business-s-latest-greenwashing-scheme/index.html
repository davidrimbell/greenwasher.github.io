ESG: Big Business's Latest Greenwashing Scheme

ESG: Big Business's Latest Greenwashing Scheme

The article argues that Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) criteria function primarily as sophisticated greenwashing rather than a genuine strategy to combat the climate crisis. Initially promoted by major financial institutions and polluting corporations, ESG serves as a marketing tool designed to maintain profitability while appearing socially responsible. Despite its widespread adoption, the framework has failed to drive meaningful environmental progress, with many ESG funds underperforming financially and continuing to invest heavily in fossil fuels. This contradiction highlights how capitalism prioritizes shareholder value over ecological survival, rendering ESG an ineffective and often deceptive mechanism for sustainable investing. Furthermore, the political backlash against ESG has accelerated a trend known as "greenhushing," where companies quietly drop sustainability disclosures to avoid right-wing scrutiny. This shift demonstrates that the core issue is not the content of these policies, but their visibility as potential profit threats to established corporate interests. The article concludes that neither ESG nor other forms of "green capitalism" can solve the climate emergency, as they remain subordinate to profit motives and are supported by political actors from both sides of the aisle who ultimately defend corporate wealth over environmental integrity. Consequently, the text asserts that true climate action requires dismantling the capitalist system itself rather than reforming it through investment frameworks. It suggests that relying on market-based solutions inevitably leads to excuses for inaction, such as blaming consumers or ignoring systemic destruction. Instead, the author advocates for a fundamental transformation toward a democratically planned economy, public ownership of key industries, and robust labor movements. Only by prioritizing human needs over profit can society effectively address the urgent scale of the climate crisis.
