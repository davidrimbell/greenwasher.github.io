Greenwashing
ES EN

Companies increasingly rely on third-party sustainability certifications to bolster environmental marketing claims and defend against greenwashing allegations. However, legal experts warn that these certifications do not provide absolute protection, as litigation continues to target companies despite their verified status. The mere presence of a certification does not guarantee immunity from regulatory scrutiny or consumer lawsuits, revealing a significant gap between perceived safety and actual legal exposure. Effective risk mitigation requires rigorous vetting of certification bodies and careful integration of these credentials into marketing materials. Brands must critically evaluate what specific standards a certification represents, how substantiation is performed, and whether the accrediting organization has faced legal challenges. Close collaboration between marketing and legal teams is essential to ensure claims are accurately reflected on packaging, preventing misrepresentations that could trigger class-action litigation or regulatory penalties. Current legal trends highlight vulnerabilities in recycling symbols, chemical safety declarations regarding PFAS, and assertions about carbon offsets or biodegradability. This landscape is relevant to greenwashing because it demonstrates that superficial environmental labeling is increasingly insufficient for defense. As regulators and litigants become more sophisticated, companies face growing pressure to ensure their sustainability narratives are substantively robust rather than merely symbolic, exposing the dangers of relying on unverified or loosely applied eco-friendly branding.
