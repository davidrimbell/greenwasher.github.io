'I told people I was clean eating, but the truth was much worse.'

'I told people I was clean eating, but the truth was much worse.'

Loz Antonenko’s journey from using exercise to process grief to developing orthorexia illustrates how wellness culture can mask disordered eating. Initially seeking empowerment through physical strength, she adopted rigid "clean eating" habits that evolved into a controlling obsession. This experience highlights how the pursuit of an idealized healthy lifestyle can easily become a socially sanctioned form of disordered behavior, where strict dietary rules replace genuine well-being with anxiety and isolation. The article exposes the pervasive nature of greenwashing in the health industry, where harmful diet culture is rebranded as positive wellness. Experts note that messages promoting "clean" or "pure" eating often serve as morally charged marketing tools that disguise toxic perfectionism. By framing restrictive behaviors as virtuous, the industry greenwashes shame and control, encouraging individuals to believe they are achieving health while actually falling into fear-based patterns that erode mental and physical health. Ultimately, this narrative underscores the danger of equating health with aesthetic purity and strict regulation. The shift from body positivity to a new pressure of being "strong and clean" demonstrates how societal ideals are continuously reshaped to maintain consumer engagement through inadequacy. Recognizing this greenwashing is crucial for distinguishing between sustainable, joyful health practices and harmful, obsessive rituals that prioritize appearance and control over actual human well-being.
