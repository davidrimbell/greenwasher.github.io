Alejandro Brown y su visión del greenwashing: “En el día del Ambiente, es una palabra a desterrar”

The article challenges the indiscriminate use of the term “greenwashing,” arguing that the progressive environmental sector frequently employs it as an ideological bias to discredit private-sector efforts. The author contends that this criticism often stems from a moralistic stance that judges the underlying motivations of companies rather than evaluating the actual impact of their actions. This tendency creates a barrier of distrust that hinders the collaboration necessary to address environmental challenges, preventing genuine—even if imperfect—initiatives from thriving. Instead, the article advocates prioritizing continuous improvement and tangible results over the purity of intentions. Companies may adopt sustainable practices for various reasons—whether driven by a fascination with nature, operational necessity, or market strategy—but what matters most is that these actions contribute to a lasting partnership with environmental organizations. The focus should be on supporting producers through a process of adaptation and learning, recognizing that sustainability is a dynamic journey with ups and downs, not a perfect end state that demands purely altruistic motivations. The relevance of this text to the study of greenwashing lies in its inversion of the usual narrative: it suggests that environmentalism itself can fall into a form of “image washing” by promoting symbolic actions that fail to address deep-rooted environmental problems, thereby protecting its own reputation. The author warns that environmentalism should not impose solutions but should instead trust the productive sector’s capacity to generate real change. By rejecting clichés that discredit the other side, the article fosters a framework of mutual trust essential for building practical and effective solutions, shifting the focus from moral criticism to constructive cooperation.
