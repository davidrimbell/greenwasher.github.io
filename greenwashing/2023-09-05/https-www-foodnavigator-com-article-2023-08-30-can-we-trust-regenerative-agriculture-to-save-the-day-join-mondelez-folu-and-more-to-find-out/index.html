Greenwashing
ES EN

The concept of regenerative agriculture is gaining rapid traction among major food and beverage companies as a strategy to enhance soil health and biodiversity. However, the industry currently lacks an official EU-level definition for these practices, creating significant ambiguity in how different supply chain players interpret and implement them. This definitional vacuum raises critical questions about the consistency and authenticity of environmental claims made by corporations. The absence of standardized criteria introduces a substantial risk of greenwashing, where companies may exaggerate or misrepresent their sustainability efforts to appeal to eco-conscious consumers. Without clear, regulatory-backed definitions, there is no reliable mechanism to distinguish genuine ecological regeneration from marketing tactics. Consequently, there is growing concern that consumers might pay a premium for products labeled as regenerative without assurance that the underlying practices truly deliver the promised environmental benefits. This article is directly relevant to greenwashing discussions because it highlights the urgent need for transparency and standardization in sustainability labeling. It underscores how vague terminology can be exploited to create misleading impressions of corporate responsibility. By addressing these gaps, stakeholders aim to establish trust and ensure that the transition toward sustainable food systems is driven by verified ecological impact rather than potentially deceptive communications.
