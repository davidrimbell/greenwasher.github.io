Bluewashing

The personal care industry has shifted toward sea-inspired branding, utilizing terms like “reef-safe” and “ocean-friendly” to market eco-consciousness. However, this trend often represents performative eco-branding rather than genuine sustainability. Because there are no federal standards or clear definitions for these labels, consumers face a regulatory void where marketing optics frequently eclipse actual environmental impact. This phenomenon gained momentum after Hawaii’s 2018 ban on specific sunscreen chemicals, which highlighted the documented harm UV filters cause to coral reefs. Despite this legislative progress, the broader industry continues to promote products that may not adhere to the principles of ocean protection. Critics argue that when sustainability is treated primarily as a marketing function, companies prioritize visual appeal and brand image over substantive ecological action, creating an illusion of responsibility. This article is highly relevant to greenwashing as it exemplifies how vague terminology and aesthetic packaging can deceive consumers. By exploiting the lack of strict regulations, brands create a narrative of ocean stewardship without ensuring their products are truly safe for marine ecosystems. It underscores the critical need for transparent, standardized definitions to distinguish between authentic conservation efforts and superficial marketing tactics that mislead environmentally conscious buyers.
