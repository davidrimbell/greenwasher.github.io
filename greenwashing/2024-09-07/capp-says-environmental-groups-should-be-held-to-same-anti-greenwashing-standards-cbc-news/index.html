CAPP says environmental groups should be held to same anti-greenwashing standards | CBC News

The Canadian oil and gas sector is urging regulators to extend strict environmental advertising standards to non-profit and climate advocacy groups. By demanding parity in truth-in-advertising rules, industry representatives argue that the new federal legislation, which requires substantiation for green claims, should not exclusively target corporations but also hold environmental organizations to the same evidence-based standards. This maneuver serves as a strategic distraction from the core purpose of anti-greenwashing laws, which aim to prevent companies from making unsubstantiated environmental claims. Industry groups, having previously removed climate-related content from their websites due to fears of litigation, are attempting to deflect scrutiny by implying that environmental advocates might also lack scientific backing for their communications, despite their work being grounded in established climate science. This situation is highly relevant to greenwashing as it highlights a defensive tactic used by fossil fuel interests to undermine regulatory credibility. By challenging the validity of environmental messaging, these groups seek to weaken the impact of transparency rules designed to expose misleading corporate sustainability claims. Ultimately, this reflects an ongoing effort to dilute accountability and protect reputations rather than genuinely promoting honest environmental discourse.
