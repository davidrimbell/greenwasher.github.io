'ESG' is fading from corporate lexicon: study

The article concludes that the prominence of ESG terminology in corporate communications has significantly declined since its peak in 2021, driven by political backlash and regulatory complexity. This shift is largely attributed to "greenhushing," where companies intentionally obscure their sustainability efforts to avoid accusations of greenwashing and navigate a controversial political landscape. Consequently, firms are abandoning the specific ESG acronym in favor of safer, broader terms like "responsible business," reflecting a strategic retreat from public branding rather than a cessation of internal practices. This linguistic retreat highlights the delicate balance between corporate transparency and reputational risk. While the explicit use of ESG language has dropped to avoid scrutiny, the underlying commitment to sustainability persists, albeit with less visibility. The trend suggests that companies are prioritizing compliance and operational integration over public relations, recognizing that overt claims of environmental or social responsibility are now more likely to invite criticism than admiration. This indicates a maturation of the sector, where the focus has shifted from marketing ESG as a brand asset to embedding it as a foundational business value. The article is relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how the fear of being labeled a greenwasher can paradoxically lead to reduced transparency. By opting for vague terminology, companies may be attempting to signal ethical conduct without providing the specific data that would invite rigorous scrutiny. This dynamic creates an environment where "greenhushing" replaces "greenwashing," potentially making it harder for stakeholders to assess genuine progress. Ultimately, the piece warns that while ESG is no longer a buzzword, its core principles remain critical, and companies must now earn relevance through demonstrable action rather than linguistic prominence.
