The B.S. Behind the USDA’s New “Climate-Friendly Beef” Label

The B.S. Behind the USDA’s New “Climate-Friendly Beef” Label

The USDA’s endorsement of Tyson’s “climate-friendly” beef highlights a troubling alliance between regulators and major polluters, using taxpayer funds to market products with questionable environmental benefits. This close relationship undermines regulatory oversight and raises serious concerns about the integrity of government-backed sustainability claims. The core issue lies in the lack of transparency regarding the data behind these emissions reductions. Despite official approval, no credible evidence or verifiable methodology exists to support the claimed 10 percent reduction, leaving the validity of the label entirely unsubstantiated. This situation is a prime example of greenwashing, where corporations exploit vague certifications and prestigious partnerships to appear eco-friendly without delivering measurable climate action. It demonstrates how institutional backing can lend false credibility to misleading environmental marketing, confusing consumers and hindering genuine sustainability efforts.
