Greenwashing
ES EN

Shell is identified as the largest employer of influencer marketing for fossil fuels over the past seven years, using high-profile figures to promote low-carbon solutions and biofuels. This strategy represents a sophisticated evolution of greenwashing, where oil and gas giants bypass traditional scrutiny by leveraging the personal credibility of content creators to obscure their environmental impact. The company’s historical engagement with environmentalists and recent promotions of hydrogen fuel highlight a concerted effort to rebrand carbon-intensive operations as climate-friendly through trusted third-party endorsements. Agencies and influencers face growing ethical dilemmas as they navigate these lucrative but controversial partnerships. While regulatory bodies like the ASA are tightening rules against misleading claims, smart PR firms often remain ahead of the law, exploiting ambiguities to secure deals. Influencers are increasingly aware of this dynamic, with many conducting due diligence to protect their reputations and maintain audience trust. However, the commercial incentive to work with major polluters creates a tension between financial gain and the moral responsibility to avoid legitimizing greenwashing tactics. This issue is significant because it demonstrates how greenwashing is shifting from corporate advertisements to the creator economy, where subtle endorsements can be more deceptive than explicit claims. As regulators struggle to keep pace with these nuanced marketing strategies, the intersection of influencer culture and fossil fuel marketing poses a serious threat to genuine climate action. It underscores the urgent need for stricter industry-specific frameworks that compel transparency and ensure influencers are fully informed about the environmental realities of the brands they represent.
