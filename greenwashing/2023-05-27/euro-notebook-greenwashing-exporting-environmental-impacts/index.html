Euro Notebook: 'Greenwashing' = exporting environmental impacts

The article critiques the disconnect between rising consumer food prices and the economic struggles faced by farmers, highlighting how lagging commodity price reductions obscure the true cost of production. This tension is exacerbated by political rhetoric that prioritizes symbolic gestures over substantive food security, often sidelining the practical realities of agricultural sustainability in favor of performative environmentalism. A critical insight emerges from an OECD report revealing that unilateral green policies often fail to address global issues due to "pollution leakage," where environmental burdens are merely shifted to countries with laxer regulations. This approach allows wealthy nations to improve their domestic green credentials while importing cheap food, effectively hiding the environmental impact rather than solving it. The report suggests that current strategies are disconnected, undermining both market competitiveness and genuine ecological progress. This dynamic is highly relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how policymakers and corporations exploit the illusion of environmental responsibility. By adopting fragmented, nationally focused regulations, entities can claim ethical superiority while ignoring the global reality that pollution is borderless. The article exposes how this lack of cohesive strategy serves as a convenient shield for maintaining status quo consumption patterns, thereby masking the true ecological costs behind a veneer of progressive policy.
