Greenwashing: energy companies make false claims about sustainability – they should be held to account - EconoTimes

This article exposes how many corporations adopt Corporate Social Responsibility merely as a marketing tool to gain legitimacy and obscure irresponsible environmental practices, a phenomenon known as greenwashing. Rather than integrating sustainability into their core operations, many businesses engage in responsive CSR, complying only with regulations or stakeholder pressure while continuing destructive activities. This discrepancy between public communication and actual conduct allows companies to mislead the public, often using sophisticated euphemisms to mask the gap between their claimed green credentials and their continued pollution. The relevance to greenwashing is starkly illustrated by major energy firms, such as Shell and ExxonMobil, which publicly commit to net-zero goals and invest in renewable branding while simultaneously expanding fossil fuel exploration. These actions highlight how greenwashing serves to normalize superficial green claims, eroding public trust and hindering genuine climate action. By presenting a sanitized image through social media and partnerships, these entities distract from their significant environmental impact, demonstrating how CSR can be manipulated to block a true green transition rather than facilitate it. However, the article also identifies a potential pathway for change, suggesting that greenwashing scandals can catalyze stricter accountability and strategic reform when civil society remains vigilant. Instances like Volkswagen’s emissions scandal show how public exposure and regulatory pressure can force corporations to pivot toward authentic sustainability. Consequently, combating greenwashing requires more than just exposure; it demands transparent regulations, rigorous monitoring, and proactive civic engagement to ensure that corporate commitments align with measurable, positive environmental outcomes.
