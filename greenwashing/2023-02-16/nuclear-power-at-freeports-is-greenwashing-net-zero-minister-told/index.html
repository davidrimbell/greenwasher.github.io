Nuclear power at freeports is ‘greenwashing’, net zero minister told

The article highlights a critical conflict regarding the definition of green energy, specifically targeting the inclusion of nuclear power in freeports. Critics argue that incorporating small modular reactors constitutes greenwashing because the technology relies on uranium mining and generates long-lasting radioactive waste. This contradicts the genuine environmental aspirations of these zones, exposing the superficial labeling of nuclear as a sustainable solution despite its significant ecological harms. Furthermore, the text emphasizes the need for a just transition for communities currently dependent on nuclear industries. As operations eventually cease, local workers and businesses face economic instability. There is a strong call for utilizing freeports to provide skilled, high-paying alternative employment, ensuring that these areas do not suffer economically when the nuclear era ends. This narrative is relevant to greenwashing as it illustrates how labeling nuclear fission as "green" misleads stakeholders about its true environmental impact. By identifying nuclear manufacturing within green freeports as deceptive practice, the article warns against the broader trend of using attractive environmental labels for technologies that remain harmful. It underscores the importance of distinguishing between actual renewable solutions and those merely marketed as such to secure public support and investment.
