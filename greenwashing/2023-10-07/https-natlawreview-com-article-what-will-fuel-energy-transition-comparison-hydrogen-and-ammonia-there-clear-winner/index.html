Greenwashing
ES EN

The article examines the competing roles of liquid hydrogen and ammonia in achieving net-zero emissions, highlighting how government incentives have accelerated production without a clear consensus on the optimal energy carrier. While hydrogen offers high energy potential, its storage and transportation challenges are significant, whereas ammonia benefits from established global infrastructure and easier handling. This dynamic creates a complex landscape where both fuels are positioned as vital components of the clean energy transition, each serving specific niches from aerospace to maritime shipping. Relevance to greenwashing lies in the tension between ambitious climate goals and market realities. The "ready, fire, aim" approach by producers, driven by tax incentives, risks creating a supply glut before demand is fully realized, as evidenced by sharp price drops in ammonia. This scenario raises concerns about whether these energy solutions are being promoted as universally viable green alternatives when they may face substantial economic and logistical hurdles. Understanding this imbalance is crucial for assessing the sincerity and feasibility of corporate or national net-zero claims. Ultimately, the transition will likely involve a complementary rather than competitive relationship between hydrogen and ammonia, with one potentially acting as a storage medium for the other. However, the uncertainty surrounding future demand and the rapid, incentive-driven expansion of supply suggest that not all advertised "clean" energy projects will succeed. Stakeholders must look beyond superficial green labels to evaluate the actual infrastructure, safety, and market viability of these fuel sources to distinguish genuine progress from potential greenwashing.
