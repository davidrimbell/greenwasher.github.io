Greenwashing
ES EN

The fear of being accused of greenwashing has paradoxically triggered a rise in "greenhushing," where companies downplay their environmental, social, and governance efforts to avoid political scrutiny. However, this retreat contrad strong consumer demand, as significant majorities of both European and American audiences believe corporations have a moral obligation to improve society and view ESG performance as critical to their success. This disconnect suggests that silence risks alienating stakeholders who actively value sustainability. To rebuild trust and communicate authentically, brands must align their visual storytelling with their actual operational priorities and resources. Authentic imagery should reflect everyday consumer interactions rather than relying on superficial green tropes or aspirational ideals. By visually representing the specific areas where a company has made genuine investments, such as supply chain accountability or decarbonization, organizations can demonstrate honesty and avoid appearing inauthentic or deceptive. Ultimately, avoiding greenwashing requires deep stakeholder engagement and a holistic understanding of the community’s needs. Companies must move beyond clichés by listening to consumers and depicting how their sustainability initiatives support individual journeys toward a better future. This approach ensures that marketing materials are grounded in reality, fostering long-term loyalty and proving that sustainability is integrated into the core business rather than used as a mere branding exercise.
