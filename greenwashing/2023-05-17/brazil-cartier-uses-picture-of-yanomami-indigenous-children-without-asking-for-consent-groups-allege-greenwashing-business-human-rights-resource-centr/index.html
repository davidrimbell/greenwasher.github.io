Brazil: Cartier uses picture of Yanomami Indigenous children without asking for consent; groups allege greenwashing - Business & Human Rights Resource Centre

Cartier faced allegations of greenwashing for displaying an unauthorized image of Yanomami children to promote a nonexistent Amazon rainforest preservation initiative. The luxury brand used Indigenous imagery to suggest environmental stewardship, yet the advertised project never materialized, revealing a disconnect between marketing claims and actual conservation efforts. This discrepancy highlights how corporations may fabricate ecological commitments to enhance their public image without delivering tangible benefits. The controversy intensified because the photo was used without the Yanomami’s prior consent, violating international indigenous rights standards. More critically, it underscored the inherent conflict of a gold jewelry company profiting from a resource that drives illegal mining, which devastates the very community it claims to protect. By associating itself with Indigenous culture while contributing to the economic pressures threatening their livelihood, Cartier exemplified the superficiality often found in corporate sustainability narratives. This case is relevant to greenwashing because it demonstrates how companies co-opt indigenous representation and ecological concerns to mask their harmful industry practices. It illustrates the danger of misleading visual storytelling that obscures the reality that demand for luxury goods continues to fuel environmental destruction and cultural exploitation. Such actions erode trust and show how brands exploit social causes for profit rather than engaging in genuine ethical responsibility.
