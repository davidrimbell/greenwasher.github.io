Marcas de impacto para frenar el 'greenwashing'

Marcas de impacto para frenar el 'greenwashing'

The article highlights the growing public distrust toward corporate sustainability claims, which has prompted the European Union to enact specific legislation to combat greenwashing and regulate environmental statements. This regulatory response underscores the urgency of curbing misleading propaganda and demanding transparency, marking a turning point in how the genuine commitment of corporations to global challenges is validated. To restore trust and legitimacy, the text proposes that companies move beyond mere ESG risk management and become “impact brands.” This requires deep alignment between their narrative and actions, operating with a tangible purpose, a regenerative vision, and a systemic approach that benefits society and the environment in the long term, rather than limiting themselves to incremental improvements or superficial marketing. The relevance of this article to the greenwashing debate lies in its diagnosis that the solution is not merely punitive regulation, but a cultural shift toward an authentic business model. By defining the structural characteristics of true impact, the text offers a practical framework for distinguishing genuinely impactful companies from those engaging in image washing, demonstrating that rigorous measurement of transformative impact is the definitive antithesis of environmental deception.
