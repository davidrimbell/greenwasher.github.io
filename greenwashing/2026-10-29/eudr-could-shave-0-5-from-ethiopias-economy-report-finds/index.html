EUDR could shave 0.5% from Ethiopia’s economy, report finds

The EU’s Deforestation Regulation threatens to severely impact Ethiopian smallholders by demanding traceability that many cannot afford. This exclusion from European markets risks a national GDP decline, as four million producers face barriers to compliance. The regulation’s strict geolocation requirements disproportionately harm low-income exporters, creating a significant gap between environmental goals and economic reality for vulnerable farmers. Furthermore, this trade shock may cause perverse environmental leakage, where farmers switch to higher-emission crops or unregulated markets. This unintended consequence undermines the regulation’s core purpose, potentially increasing deforestation rather than reducing it. The report highlights that linking most coffee plots to recent deforestation is factually minimal, suggesting the policy creates unnecessary friction without addressing actual environmental damage. This case is vital to understanding greenwashing because it reveals how well-intentioned environmental policies can mask economic exclusion as ecological responsibility. By framing market access restrictions as sustainability measures, such regulations may serve corporate interests while penalizing small producers. True greenwashing involves using environmental claims to justify practices that disproportionately harm developing economies, shifting burdens onto the most vulnerable rather than addressing systemic issues.
