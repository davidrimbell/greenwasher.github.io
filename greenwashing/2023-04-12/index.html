Etihad ads banned over ‘misleading’ environmental claims
Etihad Airways was found in breach of advertising standards for using misleading "sustainable aviation" claims in Facebook advertisements. The Advertising Standards Authority determined that highlighting isolated efforts, such as reducing single-use plastics and utilizing modern aircraft, was insufficient to substantiate such broad environmental assertions. These incremental steps did not adequately address the substantial carbon and non-carbon emissions inherently produced by air travel. The regulator emphasized that consumers would interpret the term "sustainable aviation" as implying an absolute or near-complete solution to environmental impact. However, no commercially viable technologies currently exist within the industry that can fully neutralize these significant emissions. Consequently, the ads were deemed to exaggerate the positive environmental effects of flying with Etihad, creating a false impression of ecological responsibility that did not align with scientific reality. This case is relevant to greenwashing as it demonstrates how marketers can exploit vague terminology to obscure the heavy environmental cost of their core business practices. By focusing on minor operational adjustments rather than acknowledging the fundamental unsustainability of frequent air travel, companies risk misleading the public. The ruling underscores the critical need for transparency, ensuring that environmental claims reflect actual systemic change rather than superficial marketing tactics designed to appear eco-friendly without making substantial progress.
