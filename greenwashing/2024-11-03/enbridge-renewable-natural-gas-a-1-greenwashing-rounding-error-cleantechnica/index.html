Enbridge Renewable Natural Gas A 1% Greenwashing Rounding Error - CleanTechnica

Enbridge Renewable Natural Gas A 1% Greenwashing Rounding Error - CleanTechnica

The article argues that renewable natural gas (RNG) is fundamentally ineffective for decarbonizing daily energy use, serving primarily as a greenwashing tool for fossil fuel companies like Enbridge. By promoting RNG, these firms create a false narrative of sustainability that allows them to maintain social license and continue lobbying for gas infrastructure, rather than facilitating the necessary transition to cleaner alternatives. This strategic misrepresentation delays the urgent decommissioning of gas utilities and perpetuates reliance on methane, a potent greenhouse gas. Scientific evidence reveals that RNG cannot scale to replace significant portions of fossil fuel demand due to the limited availability of capturable biomass emissions. Furthermore, the production process often involves intentionally generating methane in leaky supply chains, which can result in higher immediate climate impacts than letting organic waste decompose aerobically into carbon dioxide. The minimal volume of RNG actually produced compared to vast fossil gas consumption highlights the disparity between marketing claims and reality, demonstrating that RNG offers negligible emission reductions while masking continued fossil fuel dependence. True decarbonization requires shifting to electrification through wind, solar, and heat pumps, which are vastly superior in efficiency and emission profiles. The only legitimate niche for RNG lies in long-term strategic reserves for rare, extended periods of low renewable energy generation, not in general utility or industrial applications. Recognizing RNG’s limited role exposes the greenwashing tactics employed by gas utilities, which prioritize profit over genuine climate action by exploiting ambiguous definitions of "renewable" energy to resist systemic energy transitions.
