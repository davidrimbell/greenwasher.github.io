India, Israel and the ‘greenwashing’ project taking shape in Kashmir

The establishment of Israeli agricultural centers in Kashmir is interpreted by critics not as a development initiative, but as a strategic mechanism to reshape the region’s demographics and consolidate occupation. Local experts and activists argue that these hubs facilitate a settler-colonial project, effectively exporting Israeli control paradigms to Indian-controlled territory. This move is seen as an attempt to erase indigenous culture and geography while normalizing foreign influence under the guise of agricultural collaboration. This initiative is deeply relevant to greenwashing because it utilizes environmental and agricultural partnerships to mask deeper geopolitical and human rights violations. By framing the presence of Israeli entities as benign technical assistance and economic progress, the arrangement creates a deceptive narrative that obscures the underlying militarization and displacement of local populations. The "green" label of agricultural cooperation serves to sanitize the reality of land appropriation and demographic engineering, similar to practices observed in occupied Palestine. Consequently, these centers function as propaganda platforms that legitize apartheid and settler-colonialism through positive, development-oriented imagery. The collaboration allows Israel to improve its international reputation while supporting India’s efforts to alter the social fabric of Kashmir. Ultimately, the article suggests that such agro-diplomacy is less about genuine farmer benefit and more about providing a respectable veneer for oppressive policies, thereby greenwashing state-sponsored ethnic cleansing and land theft.
