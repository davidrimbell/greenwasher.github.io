Más vigilancia ante publicidad ambiental engañosa

The practice of greenwashing involves companies and governments projecting a false image of environmental responsibility without taking real actions, a phenomenon that severely affects public perception and social justice. Although existing legislation in Mexico seeks to monitor this behavior, these laws prove insufficient to halt misinformation and sustainability reports that legitimize ecological damage without changing production processes. The growth of this deception is directly linked to the rise of sustainability as a market value, incentivizing brands to position themselves as eco-friendly without modifying their core operations. Studies reveal that a significant portion of environmental campaigns are unfounded, prompting countries such as France and the United Kingdom to implement strict legal mechanisms that Mexico has yet to achieve. This issue is crucial to greenwashing because it exposes the gap between eco-marketing and operational reality, generating a crisis of trust. In response, Mexican authorities and Congress are working to strengthen consumer protection laws, requiring that environmental claims be verifiable. This regulatory evolution aims to transform sustainability into a tangible obligation rather than merely a tool for media manipulation.
