Los dos informes clave del juicio Iberdrola vs. Repsol: la eléctrica acusa a la petrolera de gastar nueve millones en 'greenwashing' y esta asegura que sus clientes buscan "ahorro", no energía verde

The legal dispute between Iberdrola and Repsol underscores a pivotal moment in the fight against greenwashing, where corporate competition intersects with environmental accountability. Rather than focusing solely on financial penalties, both parties are engaged in a strategic narrative war. For Iberdrola, the primary objective is to establish a national precedent that exposes misleading ecological advertising, prioritizing reputational influence over monetary gain. Conversely, Repsol aims to minimize reputational damage by defending its identity as a multi-energy company committed to sustainability, seeking to resolve the case swiftly to prevent long-term brand erosion. The core of the dispute hinges on conflicting expert analyses regarding the transparency of Repsol’s advertising. Iberdrola’s experts argue that Repsol employs greenwashing techniques to enhance its image without taking immediate action on emissions, claiming that its marketing creates consumer confusion through deceptive practices. Repsol counters that these accusations lack substantive evidence and asserts that its marketing accurately reflects its transition strategy, emphasizing that consumer loyalty in the energy market is driven primarily by cost savings rather than environmental concerns. This case is crucial for understanding greenwashing because it demonstrates how environmental claims are weaponized in corporate litigation. It reveals the difficulty in defining and proving “ecoposturing,” as legal arguments often rely heavily on subjective interpretations of corporate communication rather than clear regulatory violations. The outcome will likely set important standards for how energy firms must align their public sustainability messaging with their actual operational investments and emission reduction timelines.
