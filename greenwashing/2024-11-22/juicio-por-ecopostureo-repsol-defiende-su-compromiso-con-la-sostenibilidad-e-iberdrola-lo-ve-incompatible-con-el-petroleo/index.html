Juicio por ecopostureo: Repsol defiende su compromiso con la sostenibilidad e Iberdrola lo ve incompatible con el petróleo

This trial marks a significant precedent in Spain regarding corporate greenwashing, pitting Iberdrola against Repsol over advertising claims. The core conflict revolves around whether a fossil-fuel-intensive company can legitimately present itself as a sustainability leader. Iberdrola argues that Repsol’s messaging is deceptive, claiming it misleads consumers by obscuring its heavy reliance on carbon-intensive activities, which contradicts the narrative of being a clean energy innovator. Repsol defends itself by distinguishing between being inherently sustainable and committing to a transition toward that goal. Its legal team asserts that the public is aware of the company’s traditional business and that its investments in renewables justify its evolving identity as a multi-energy firm. They argue that criticizing these efforts ignores the practical necessities of maintaining energy supply and economic stability during the global shift away from fossil fuels. This case is crucial for understanding greenwashing because it highlights the ambiguity in current regulations before EU directives are fully transposed into Spanish law. It forces a legal and societal reckoning with whether corporate communications exploit vague language to mask ongoing environmental damage. The outcome will likely set a standard for how “commitment to sustainability” is evaluated against actual operational realities, potentially curbing misleading eco-friendly branding in heavily polluting industries.
