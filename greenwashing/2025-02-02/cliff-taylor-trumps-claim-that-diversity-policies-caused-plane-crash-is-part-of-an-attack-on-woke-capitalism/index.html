Cliff Taylor: Trump’s claim that diversity policies caused plane crash is part of an attack on ‘woke capitalism’

Cliff Taylor: Trump’s claim that diversity policies caused plane crash is part of an attack on ‘woke capitalism’

The article argues that the rise of political populism, exemplified by Donald Trump’s opposition to environmental and diversity regulations, is fostering a new phenomenon known as "greenhushing." Unlike traditional greenwashing, where companies falsely claim eco-friendly practices, greenhushing involves firms staying silent about genuine sustainability efforts to avoid backlash from critics who label such initiatives as "woke capitalism." This shift creates a climate of fear where businesses prioritize political appeasement over transparent environmental responsibility, potentially stalling progress despite existing investments in green infrastructure. This trend is gaining international traction as major corporations, including large US banks, withdraw from global net-zero initiatives to align with shifting political winds. The pressure is further amplified by regulatory changes in the EU, which is considering simplifying corporate sustainability reporting to boost competitiveness. Consequently, companies may exploit these regulatory relaxations to reduce accountability, creating a loophole where the absence of loud advocacy masks a retreat from actual climate commitments. This environment allows entities to maintain a sustainable image without the rigorous verification or public scrutiny that previously drove meaningful change. This development is critically relevant to greenwashing because it obscures the reality of corporate environmental impact. While greenwashing involves active deception, greenhushing involves strategic silence, making it harder for stakeholders to assess true performance. As political narratives devalue sustainability, the risk increases that companies will use the cover of "avoiding controversy" to delay or abandon genuine decarbonization strategies. Ultimately, this undermines trust in corporate reporting and complicates efforts to hold businesses accountable for their contribution to climate change, shifting the discourse from action to political survival.
