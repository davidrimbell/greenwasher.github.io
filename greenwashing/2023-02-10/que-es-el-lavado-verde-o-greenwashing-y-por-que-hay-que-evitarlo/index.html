¿Qué es el lavado verde o "greenwashing" y por qué hay que evitarlo?

This article examines a subtle form of greenwashing in which brands highlight minor or irrelevant virtues to distract consumers from significant environmental impacts. Although this strategy is often mistaken for mere irrelevance, it is more complex because it manipulates product perception through misleading comparisons or secondary details, thereby cleansing the overall image of highly harmful products. The relevance of this analysis lies in its ability to reveal how the industry exploits the growing interest in sustainability by making partial claims that may constitute fraud or, at best, occupy a thin regulatory gray area. These cases illustrate the current difficulty in distinguishing between legitimate marketing and environmental misinformation, highlighting the need for greater critical scrutiny by consumers. In conclusion, an effective solution rests on education and responsible consumption decisions. By carefully evaluating sustainability claims, consumers can discourage these practices, compelling companies to improve the actual quality of their products. This process requires continuous learning for both regulators and the public, in order to effectively reduce environmental impact and foster genuine transparency in the market.
