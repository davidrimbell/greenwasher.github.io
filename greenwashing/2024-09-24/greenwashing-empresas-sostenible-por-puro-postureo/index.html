Greenwashing: empresas sostenible por puro postureo

Greenwashing: empresas sostenible por puro postureo

More than 80% of Spanish consumers suspect that companies promote sustainability primarily as a marketing tool rather than reflecting a genuine environmental commitment. This widespread skepticism, which is even more pronounced in countries like Portugal, highlights a critical disconnect between corporate messaging and actual practices. The study indicates that consumers are increasingly adept at identifying greenwashing, viewing vague claims of ecological responsibility with deep suspicion unless backed by tangible proof. Despite this distrust, there is a strong desire among the public to act sustainably, with many engaging in recycling, buying second-hand goods, and using shared transport. However, a significant gap exists between these intentions and the ability to verify product claims. Consumers feel hindered by a lack of clear, reliable information, which creates frustration and makes it difficult to distinguish between truly eco-friendly options and those that are merely marketed as such, undermining their ability to make informed ethical choices. This dynamic is highly relevant to greenwashing because it signals a shifting landscape where superficial advertising is no longer sufficient to maintain brand loyalty. As regulations tighten and consumer awareness grows, companies that fail to demonstrate measurable, transparent impacts risk severe reputational damage and legal penalties. The article underscores that avoiding greenwashing is no longer optional but essential for businesses to survive in an era where authenticity and accountability are demanded as prerequisites for trust.
