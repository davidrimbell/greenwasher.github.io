Hey ho, CORSIA has gotta go (too)! - On Line Opinion

Hey ho, CORSIA has gotta go (too)! - On Line Opinion

The article contrasts the transparency of the Trump administration’s resistance to the International Maritime Organization’s carbon schemes with the ICAO’s CORSIA scheme, which advanced with minimal public scrutiny. By highlighting how the aviation sector implements a global market-based offsetting mechanism while avoiding the intense political backlash faced by shipping, the piece exposes how regulatory strategies are tailored to bypass opposition. This discrepancy reveals a critical dimension of greenwashing: the strategic manipulation of policy implementation to achieve climate goals without triggering immediate public or political resistance. Furthermore, the text challenges the economic efficacy and necessity of carbon offsetting by demonstrating that fuel costs already significantly outweigh any potential price increases from CORSIA. Since the actual financial burden on consumers is negligible compared to existing operational expenses, the scheme appears more symbolic than substantive. This disparity suggests that such mechanisms may serve primarily as marketing tools rather than effective environmental solutions, allowing corporations to claim sustainability progress without meaningful operational changes or significant consumer impact. This analysis is relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how international climate agreements can function as reputational management exercises rather than genuine decarbonization efforts. By decoupling the label of "net zero" commitment from tangible economic or environmental realities, these schemes enable industries to maintain high emission levels while projecting an image of responsibility. Understanding this dynamic is essential for recognizing when environmental policies are designed to satisfy stakeholder perceptions rather than address the root causes of ecological damage.
