Greenwashing
ES EN

Greenwashing involves deceptive marketing that falsely portrays non-eco-friendly products as environmentally sustainable. Driven by rising consumer demand for ESG-aligned goods, companies increasingly exploit this trend, leading to a sharp surge in misleading environmental disclosures. This widespread practice capitalizes on buyer willingness to pay premium prices for perceived green benefits, often resulting in significant regulatory scrutiny and public backlash. Recent data reveals an alarming escalation in these deceptive practices, particularly within high-emission industries. Investigations indicate that a substantial portion of major corporations utilize ambiguous language or nature-centric imagery to exaggerate their environmental achievements. This prevalence highlights a systemic issue where marketing tactics outpace actual ecological performance, creating a trust deficit between consumers and brands regarding genuine sustainability efforts. To combat this, regulators have updated guidelines to mandate transparency across a product’s entire lifecycle, from raw materials to disposal. These stricter rules penalize vague claims, minor incremental changes presented as major leaps, and the use of misleading visual cues. Consequently, businesses face heightened legal and reputational risks, shifting greenwashing from a mere branding error to a potential violation of law. Companies must now ensure all environmental claims are concrete, verifiable, and free from exaggeration to mitigate these expanding regulatory dangers.
