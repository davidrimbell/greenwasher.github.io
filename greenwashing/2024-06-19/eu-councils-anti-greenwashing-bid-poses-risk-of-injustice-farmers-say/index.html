EU Council’s Anti-Greenwashing Bid Poses ‘Risk of Injustice,’ Farmers Say

EU Council’s Anti-Greenwashing Bid Poses ‘Risk of Injustice,’ Farmers Say

Over nine hundred farmers globally have warned that the European Council’s recommended Product Environmental Footprint methodology for assessing apparel’s environmental impact unfairly disadvantages natural fibers like wool. They argue that the current metrics heavily favor synthetic, fossil fuel-derived materials, creating a significant risk of injustice for producers of natural textiles. This regulatory framework, despite recent caveats about missing indicators, still threatens to label natural products as less sustainable than synthetics, potentially harming livelihoods dependent on traditional agriculture. The core concern is that these methodological inadequacies facilitate greenwashing by fast fashion brands, which can exploit the system to market cheaper synthetic alternatives as superior eco-friendly options. Critics assert that the absence of crucial metrics such as durability, repairability, and microplastic pollution allows misleading claims to persist. Without a comprehensive understanding of how garments are actually used and worn, the standardized assessment fails to capture the true environmental costs, thereby misinforming consumers and distorting the market in favor of industrial synthetic production over regenerative natural farming. This dispute highlights the critical tension between standardized corporate sustainability metrics and the complex realities of ecological impact. It serves as a stark reminder that greenwashing often thrives in the gap between scientific methodology and material truth. When regulatory tools prioritize convenient, aggregated data over nuanced, lifecycle-specific realities, they can inadvertently validate environmentally harmful practices while penalizing sustainable ones. Consequently, the relevance to greenwashing lies in how rigid frameworks can be manipulated to create a false narrative of sustainability, misleading the public and undermining genuine environmental stewardship.
