Top banks 'bankrolling destruction' in Amazon, says report

Top banks 'bankrolling destruction' in Amazon, says report

Major banks defend their Amazon oil financing by citing robust environmental and social risk policies, yet the analysis reveals a critical disconnect between these stated commitments and actual practices. The institutions highlight enhanced due diligence and adherence to norms, but fail to address the systemic exclusion of Indigenous voices from decision-making processes regarding extractive projects. The core issue is the absence of free, informed consent, meaning Indigenous communities lack veto power over projects threatening their territories and livelihoods. This gap suggests that corporate sustainability frameworks prioritize operational continuity over fundamental human rights, creating a facade of responsibility that masks ongoing harm to vulnerable populations and ecosystems. This discrepancy exemplifies greenwashing, where extensive public relations efforts and generic policy declarations obscure the reality of continued environmental degradation. It highlights how financial institutions can claim ethical alignment while actively funding activities that violate indigenous rights, demonstrating that without enforceable consent mechanisms, sustainability claims remain largely superficial and misleading.
