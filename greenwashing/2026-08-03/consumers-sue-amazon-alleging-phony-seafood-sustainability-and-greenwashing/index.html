Consumers Sue Amazon Alleging Phony Seafood Sustainability and Greenwashing

A class-action lawsuit accuses Amazon of systematically misleading consumers about the environmental sustainability of seafood sold on its platform. The complaint alleges that Amazon markets products using broad, unsubstantiated claims such as “dolphin-safe” and “responsibly sourced,” creating a false impression that these items cause minimal harm to oceans and marine life. This strategy targets eco-conscious shoppers, exploiting their desire to support sustainable practices through purchasing decisions that the lawsuit argues are based on deceptive information rather than genuine ecological benefits. The suit highlights the hypocrisy in Amazon’s approach, which violates its own policies requiring truthful environmental marketing while failing to meet the standards imposed on third-party sellers. Central to the allegations is the misuse of the Marine Stewardship Council’s certification, described as a “pay-for-play” model that allows fisheries with destructive practices, such as heavy bycatch and bottom trawling, to display a misleadingly positive label. By presenting these certifications without necessary disclosures, Amazon allegedly deprives consumers of the ability to make informed choices, effectively charging a premium for products that do not deliver the promised ecological integrity. This case is critical to understanding greenwashing because it illustrates how major retailers can exploit complex supply chains and flawed certification systems to appear sustainable while obscuring significant environmental and labor abuses. The lawsuit emphasizes that traceability does not equal sustainability, noting that many vessels evade tracking and engage in legally dubious methods. By seeking repayments and injunctions, the case underscores the legal risks companies face when using vague environmental claims to mask the reality of their supply chain impacts, demonstrating the tangible harm of deceptive eco-marketing.
