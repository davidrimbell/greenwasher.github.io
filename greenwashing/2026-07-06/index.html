Dominican Republic’s weak biodegradable rules raise prices and harm the environment

Shoppers often face confusing environmental claims. These claims make buying decisions harder. The Dominican Republic recently changed its Solid Waste Law. This change allows foam products labeled as “biodegradable.” Source: Dominican Republic Government Gazette This rule lowered the scientific standards for biodegradability. Now, companies sell long-lasting plastics as eco-friendly. They use vague words to attract buyers who want to help the planet.

This strategy seems good for consumers who care about the environment. Shoppers think they help the earth by buying these items. However, this idea hides big problems. The weak testing rules let non-decomposing plastics enter the trash. These materials stay in the environment longer than normal plastics. Consumers pay more for these products. They get no real environmental benefit. This raises household costs without improving the environment.

Studies show the financial and environmental impact of these practices. Research shows that misleading labels raise prices on basic goods. Source: Journal of Consumer Affairs, 2023 These costs come from corporate money that manufacturers pass on to families. Buyers face a double burden. They pay higher prices and cause environmental harm. The gap between marketing claims and science grows as regulators accept weak standards. Words like “carbon-neutral” or “natural” become empty tools for profit when verification is unclear.

Extended Producer Responsibility mechanisms offer a better alternative. These rules hold manufacturers accountable for their products’ entire life cycle. Source: OECD Environmental Policy Paper, 2022 Governments that use these rules enforce stricter accountability. Consumers benefit from clear testing and real sustainability. Buyers must look past shiny packaging. They must demand rigorous verification. Asking who tests these products and what standards apply reveals the true cost of “eco-friendly” choices. Recognizing these rules empowers consumers. It helps them support policies that prioritize real environmental health over corporate convenience.
