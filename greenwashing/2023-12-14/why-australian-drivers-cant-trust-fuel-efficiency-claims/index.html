Why Australian drivers can’t trust fuel efficiency claims

The High Court ruled that automotive manufacturers and dealers in Australia are not liable for misleading consumers when real-world fuel consumption exceeds official laboratory figures, provided they strictly adhere to mandated testing regulations. This decision establishes that compliance with the technical "letter of the law" serves as a complete defense against claims of deceptive conduct under Australian Consumer Law. Consequently, businesses can display standardized efficiency data without fear of legal penalty, even if that data fails to predict actual driver experiences. This ruling highlights a critical disconnect between standardized regulatory testing and real-world vehicle performance. While laboratories provide a common baseline for comparing models, they cannot simulate every driving condition, leading to significant discrepancies that consumers frequently encounter. The judgment effectively validates the current testing framework, suggesting that the gap between advertised and actual efficiency is an inherent limitation of the system rather than an act of deception by the manufacturer. The decision is particularly relevant to greenwashing because it legitimizes the use of idealized data for marketing purposes, provided the data is technically accurate according to official standards. By allowing companies to rely on favorable laboratory results while ignoring real-world outcomes, the law creates a structural environment where potentially misleading sustainability claims can persist legally. This undermines consumer trust and enables the automotive industry to maintain the status quo, where glossy efficiency metrics do not necessarily reflect genuine environmental performance or operational reality.
