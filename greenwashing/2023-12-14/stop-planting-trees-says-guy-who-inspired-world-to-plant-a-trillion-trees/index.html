Stop Planting Trees, Says Guy Who Inspired World to Plant a Trillion Trees

Ecologist Thomas Crowther uses his past research to expose how corporate and governmental tree-planting initiatives often serve as greenwashing vehicles. He argues that mass plantations are frequently misrepresented as viable climate solutions, allowing entities to avoid reducing actual carbon emissions. By utilizing unreliable evidence regarding the carbon sequestration potential of new forests, industries create an illusion of sustainability that distracts from the urgent need to cut fossil fuel consumption. The narrative shifts to the origins of this trend, tracing it back to a widely publicized study that significantly overstated the Earth’s capacity for tree restoration. Although Crowther later clarified that preserving existing forests is more impactful and corrected the initial data, the damage was done. The resulting global craze enabled major corporations to burnish their green credentials superficially, demonstrating how scientific ambiguity can be exploited to mask continued environmental degradation and delay genuine climate action. Ultimately, the article highlights the contradiction between high-profile conservation pledges and the expansion of fossil fuel infrastructure. Even as officials praise restoration efforts, the host nation of the summit simultaneously plans significant oil expansions, illustrating that temporary carbon storage in nature cannot offset permanent emissions. This disconnect reveals the core danger of greenwashing: using nature-based solutions as excuses to maintain harmful industrial practices, thereby undermining the very goals of climate agreements.
