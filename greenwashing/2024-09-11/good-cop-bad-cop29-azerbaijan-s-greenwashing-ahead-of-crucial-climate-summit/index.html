Good Cop, Bad COP29: Azerbaijan's greenwashing ahead of crucial climate summit

Good Cop, Bad COP29: Azerbaijan's greenwashing ahead of crucial climate summit

The article exposes how Azerbaijan is using the upcoming COP-29 climate summit as a platform for greenwashing while simultaneously suppressing domestic dissent. By hosting a global environmental conference despite having an economy overwhelmingly dependent on fossil fuels and a deteriorating human rights record, the government presents a deceptive facade of sustainability. This stark contrast between public climate commitments and internal repression highlights the disconnect between rhetoric and reality often seen in corporate and state-level environmental claims. The detention of Dr. Gubad Ibadoghlu, a prominent anti-corruption researcher, serves as a focal point for this critique. Authorities have used fabricated charges to silence him after he exposed corrupt deals involving major oil and gas companies. The international silence from these corporations, many of which he worked with on transparency initiatives, underscores a troubling prioritization of resource access over ethical standards and democratic values. This case is crucial to understanding greenwashing because it reveals how authoritarian regimes may leverage climate diplomacy to legitimize their rule while continuing exploitative economic practices. The involvement of fossil fuel executives in the summit’s organization raises serious questions about the integrity of the negotiations. Ultimately, the situation demonstrates that without genuine accountability for human rights and corruption, climate summits can become tools for masking the very industries driving the environmental crisis.
