Eco-friendly? Don Don Donki's move to stop selling plastic bags from October raises concerns by the public

Eco-friendly? Don Don Donki's move to stop selling plastic bags from October raises concerns by the public

Don Don Donki’s decision to stop selling plastic bags in Singapore illustrates the complex dynamics of corporate environmental claims. While the move aligns with broader national sustainability goals, public reaction highlights significant skepticism regarding its actual ecological impact. Critics argue that simply removing bag sales does not address the root causes of plastic waste within the store’s operations. The controversy centers on the argument that this policy constitutes greenwashing by shifting the burden onto consumers rather than reducing overall plastic production. Shoppers point out that Donki’s extensive use of plastic packaging for ready-to-eat foods creates hygiene issues that prevent the reuse of alternative bags. Furthermore, banning standard bags may merely drive customers to purchase more resource-intensive alternatives or rely on plastic bin liners, thereby negating potential environmental benefits. This case is relevant to greenwashing because it showcases how companies can make visible, convenient gestures while maintaining unsustainable practices elsewhere in their business model. It underscores the importance of evaluating the holistic supply chain impact rather than isolated initiatives. True sustainability requires comprehensive reductions in plastic usage across packaging and operations, not just the removal of disposable shopping aids.
