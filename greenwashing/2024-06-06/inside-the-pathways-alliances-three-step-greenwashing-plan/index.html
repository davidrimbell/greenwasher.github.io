Inside the Pathways Alliance’s three-step greenwashing plan

The Pathways Alliance, representing nearly all Canadian oilsands production, employs a calculated strategy to delay effective climate action while maintaining profitability. By heavily advertising a commitment to carbon capture technology, the group creates an illusion of environmental responsibility. This public-facing narrative aims to secure substantial government subsidies for expensive infrastructure projects, shifting the financial burden from private corporations to taxpayers under the guise of climate innovation. Behind this marketing facade, the alliance actively lobbies against rigorous emission reduction policies, including the proposed cap on oil and gas emissions and stricter methane regulations. Despite claiming to support national climate goals, the group consistently advocates for measures that contradict scientific guidance. This approach allows members to weaken regulatory frameworks that threaten their business models, ensuring that climate policies remain insufficient to drive a genuine energy transition away from fossil fuels. This case is critical to understanding greenwashing because it illustrates how the fossil fuel industry has evolved from outright denial to performative engagement. By redefining "net-zero" to exclude emissions from fuel combustion and promoting unproven technologies, the alliance masks its intent to expand production. The strategy demonstrates a shift from opposing climate policy to selectively supporting it only when it offers financial benefits, thereby preserving market power while appearing to cooperate with global climate objectives.
