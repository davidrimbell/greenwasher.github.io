A province seeks to unify with the national government a strategy to certify deforestation-free production for the EU and promotes coordination with the Central Region

The government of Santa Fe is coordinating with national authorities on a strategy to demonstrate to the European Union that its agricultural products originate from non-deforested lands. This initiative aims to comply with new European environmental requirements that mandate the traceability of commodities such as soy and beef, thereby ensuring that Argentine exports do not lose access to key international markets. Priority is given to a state-led validation system through a public, free digital platform, avoiding exclusive reliance on private certifiers. This tool centralizes official data to verify sustainability and the chain of custody, while protecting producers’ confidentiality. The objective is to establish uniform criteria at the federal level, presenting a unified front to the EU that strengthens the credibility and competitiveness of Argentina’s productive sector. This case is relevant to the study of greenwashing because it illustrates how governments can build transparent verification mechanisms to avoid accusations of greenwashing. By officializing traceability and cross-referencing data with land registry records, it distinguishes genuine environmental commitment from superficial marketing. This demonstrates that legitimacy in sustainability requires robust, public, and auditable systems, in contrast to empty statements lacking verifiable technical backing from third parties or state entities.
