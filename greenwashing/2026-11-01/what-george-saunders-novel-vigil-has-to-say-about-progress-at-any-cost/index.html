What George Saunders' novel 'Vigil' has to say about progress at any cost

George Saunders’ novel "Vigil" exposes the psychological mechanisms behind climate denial by fictionalizing a real-world speech that minimized environmental threats. The story centers on K.J. Boone, an oil tycoon who spent his life rationalizing extraction as essential for economic stability and protecting the poor. By exploring Boone’s deathbed regrets, the narrative highlights how corporate leaders use logical-sounding justifications to obscure the catastrophic reality of their actions, effectively shielding themselves from moral accountability. The article is highly relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how businesses construct elaborate narratives to mask environmental harm. Boone’s character represents the archetype of a corporation that prioritizes continuous growth and efficiency over ecological consequences, framing regulatory action as bureaucratic failure. This mirrors real-world greenwashing tactics where companies present themselves as responsible while continuing destructive practices, relying on public apathy and the perception that economic progress is inevitable to avoid scrutiny or change. Saunders extends this critique beyond fossil fuels, warning that similar moral blind spots exist in emerging industries like artificial intelligence. He suggests that acknowledging complicity in harmful systems requires courage, yet many good people continue to participate due to perceived inertia or short-term convenience. The novel serves as a cultural mirror, urging readers to recognize these rationalizations in themselves and their industries, challenging the comfort of complicity and demanding a more honest reckoning with the true costs of modern technological and industrial progress.
