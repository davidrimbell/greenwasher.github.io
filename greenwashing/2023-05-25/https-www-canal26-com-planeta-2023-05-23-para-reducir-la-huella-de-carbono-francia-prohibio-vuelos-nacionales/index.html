Greenwashing
ES EN

France has banned short-haul domestic flights where high-speed rail alternatives are available within 2.5 hours, enforcing a policy aimed at shifting travel from aviation to lower-emission transport. This decisive action targets the significant carbon contribution of air travel, marking a pivotal global step toward reducing greenhouse gas emissions through concrete infrastructure changes. This initiative challenges the aviation industry’s environmental claims by prioritizing systemic measures over voluntary offsets. It exposes the gap between marketing and reality, as many airlines rely on vague sustainability narratives while expanding capacity. By mandating actual modal shifts, the policy counters superficial environmental branding. The relevance to greenwashing lies in its rejection of performative eco-friendly tactics. Instead of allowing airlines to boast about minor improvements, the government demands tangible reductions in carbon footprints. This sets a precedent for holding transportation sectors accountable, ensuring that climate actions reflect genuine progress rather than deceptive marketing strategies designed to appease consumers without substantive impact.
