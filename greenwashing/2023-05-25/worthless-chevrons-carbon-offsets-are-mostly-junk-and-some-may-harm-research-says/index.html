‘Worthless’: Chevron’s carbon offsets are mostly junk and some may harm, research says

An investigation reveals that Chevron’s strategy to claim climate neutrality relies heavily on "junk" carbon offsets and unproven technologies like carbon capture, which fail to meaningfully reduce greenhouse gas emissions. Rather than facilitating a genuine transition to low-carbon operations, these mechanisms allow the fossil fuel giant to continue expanding its oil and gas production while maintaining a facade of environmental responsibility. This approach prioritizes profit over planetary health, effectively enabling business as usual under the guise of sustainability. The specific offsets purchased are often linked to projects with questionable environmental integrity, such as large hydropower dams and forest plantations that do not deliver additional emission reductions. Furthermore, many of these initiatives are associated with severe social and environmental harms in the Global South, including forced displacement and community violence. By leveraging these flawed mechanisms, Chevron obscures the true scale of its impact, shifting the burden of climate mitigation onto vulnerable populations who suffer disproportionately from both the climate crisis and the company’s operations. This case is critically relevant to greenwashing as it exposes how corporate net-zero pledges can serve as deceptive marketing tools rather than actionable commitments. The discrepancy between Chevron’s aggressive expansion plans and its reliance on ineffective offset schemes illustrates a broader industry trend of delaying substantive change. Such practices undermine global climate goals by creating false narratives of progress, thereby delaying the urgent fossil fuel phase-out required to prevent catastrophic warming.
