Tackling greenwashing: BEUC urges ban on “harmful” carbon-neutral food claims

Consumer organizations argue that "carbon neutral" labels on food products constitute greenwashing, misleading shoppers by implying zero emissions when production inherently generates greenhouse gases. This practice creates significant consumer confusion, as most people fail to understand that these claims rely on offsets rather than actual emission reductions. Consequently, these labels risk fostering a false sense of security, allowing polluting industries to maintain harmful practices under the guise of sustainability. The reliance on carbon offsets, such as tree planting, is scientifically flawed and counterproductive, as it delays immediate action on reducing emissions. By offering a cheaper alternative to cutting actual pollution, these claims act as a smoke screen that preserves the status quo in high-emission sectors like dairy and meat. This deception not only undermines genuine climate efforts but also discourages consumers from making more impactful dietary changes, such as shifting toward plant-based options. Regulatory intervention is urgently needed to ban these misleading assertions, which currently thrive due to a lack of standardized verification. Without strict rules, the proliferation of vague eco-labels erodes trust and hinders the transition to truly sustainable consumption patterns. Addressing this issue is critical to ensuring that environmental marketing provides accurate information rather than exploiting consumer goodwill, thereby supporting effective climate action rather than obstructing it.
