Greenwashing
ES EN

The Indonesian government’s construction of Nusantara, its new capital, exemplifies classic greenwashing by juxtaposing grandiose sustainability promises with environmentally destructive realities. Officials market the project as a carbon-neutral "forest city" utilizing renewable energy and smart waste management. However, this pristine image is largely constructed through digital renderings that obscure the current state of massive deforestation, habitat fragmentation, and ongoing excavation in one of the world’s oldest rainforests. Critics highlight that the immediate implementation prioritizes speed over ecological integrity, displacing endangered species like orangutans and ignoring critical animal corridors. Despite vows to rely on clean energy sources, experts warn that short-term reliance on coal power undermines the green narrative. The discrepancy between the idealized vision of a lush, sustainable metropolis and the actual clearing of indigenous lands suggests that environmental claims are being used as a marketing tool rather than guiding principles for development. This case is relevant to greenwashing because it demonstrates how governments can leverage eco-friendly branding to legitimize large-scale infrastructure projects that cause significant harm. By focusing on future aspirations and aesthetic designs while dismissing current ecological costs and indigenous rights, the state creates a misleading impression of sustainability. This disconnect between promotional rhetoric and operational impact highlights the risk of using green labels to mask unsustainable practices and avoid accountability for environmental degradation.
