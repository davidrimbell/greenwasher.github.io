Carbon Offsets Are Unscalable, Unjust, & Unfixable — Joe Romm - CleanTechnica

Joe Romm’s recent research argues that carbon offsets constitute a systemic scam, allowing major polluters to maintain unsustainable emissions while pretending to achieve climate neutrality. Rather than reducing their own carbon footprint, fossil fuel companies and wealthy nations purchase offsets from developing countries, creating a false sense of environmental responsibility. This mechanism effectively serves as a smokescreen, enabling continued pollution under the guise of compensatory actions that rarely result in genuine emission reductions. The article highlights that the vast majority of these offsets are either fictitious or over-credited, meaning the promised environmental benefits do not actually occur. Prominent bodies like the Science Based Targets initiative now recognize that relying on offsets for net-zero claims is primarily a form of greenwashing. By shifting the burden of reduction to other regions instead of cutting domestic emissions, this approach fails to address the root cause of climate change and may even incentivize higher overall global pollution. This narrative is crucial to understanding greenwashing because it exposes how corporate sustainability claims can be structurally deceptive. It illustrates that "carbon neutral" labels often mask inaction rather than signify progress, misleading the public and policymakers about the severity of the climate crisis. Recognizing offsets as a tool for business-as-usual rather than a viable solution is essential for distinguishing authentic climate action from the manipulative marketing tactics used to delay meaningful regulatory and operational changes.
