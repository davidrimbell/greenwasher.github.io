Pradeep S. Mehta | How to balance sustainability, equity and rights of all consumers

The article argues that a just green transition must prioritize equity, ensuring that sustainability efforts do not disproportionately burden developing nations or exclude vulnerable populations from affordable green solutions. It emphasizes that historical responsibility lies with developed countries to finance and facilitate clean technology access, rather than imposing unilateral measures that hinder economic growth in the Global South. Central to this fairness is the fight against greenwashing through robust environmental labeling and strict enforcement against misleading claims. By aligning labels with carbon markets and Extended Producer Responsibility frameworks, governments can build consumer trust and ensure that marketing does not deceive the public about a product’s true environmental impact. Relevant to greenwashing, the text highlights that without transparent criteria and industry accountability, sustainability claims become empty gestures that undermine genuine climate action. The article asserts that protecting consumer rights and preventing deceptive practices are essential for maintaining the integrity of the green economy, ensuring that the shift to sustainable lifestyles is both authentic and accessible.
