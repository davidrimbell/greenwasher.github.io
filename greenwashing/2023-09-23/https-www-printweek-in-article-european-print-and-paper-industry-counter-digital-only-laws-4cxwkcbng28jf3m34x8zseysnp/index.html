Greenwashing
ES EN

This article highlights the insidious nature of greenwashing, where corporations weaponize environmental rhetoric to mask profit-driven digitization. By promoting paperless options under the guise of ecological benefit, companies often exploit the perception that digital is inherently "green," thereby obscuring their primary motive of cost reduction. This practice misleads consumers who are unaware that these "green" campaigns frequently serve as marketing cover for financial efficiency rather than genuine sustainability efforts. Beyond the deceptive marketing, the push for digital exclusion disproportionately affects vulnerable populations, including the elderly and those with limited digital skills. Furthermore, the narrative ignores the substantial environmental advantages of paper, such as its recyclability and use of renewable resources. By framing paper as obsolete or harmful, these campaigns not only marginalize those reliant on physical media but also devalue a sustainable, circular material that plays a significant role in the European economy. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the systematic distortion of truth to drive consumer behavior. Organizations like Two Sides actively challenge companies using unfounded claims like "save a tree" to justify switching to e-billing. This exposes how environmental language is manipulated to create a false dichotomy, suggesting that digital choices are always superior for the planet. Such actions undermine informed consumer choice and perpetuate misconceptions about the true environmental impact of paper versus digital communications.
