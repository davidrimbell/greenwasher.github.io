Eight ways EU greenwashing crackdown will impact fashion sector
The European Union is finalizing strict regulations to combat greenwashing, aiming to protect consumers from deceptive marketing and promote product durability. By banning vague environmental claims like "eco-friendly" without proof, the legislation seeks to eliminate the confusion that currently plagues the market. This shift prioritizes transparency, ensuring that sustainability statements are substantiated and verifiable, thereby fostering genuine informed consumer choices rather than misleading narratives. A key component of these new rules involves tackling planned obsolescence and enhancing awareness of legal guarantees. The EU will introduce labels for producers offering extended warranties, helping shoppers identify longer-lasting goods. Prohibited practices also include discouraging premature replacements and falsifying repairability, which directly addresses corporate tactics that artificially shorten product lifespans for profit. These measures aim to create a fairer landscape where durability is valued over rapid consumption cycles. This development is highly relevant to greenwashing discussions as it directly targets the core mechanisms companies use to exaggerate their environmental credentials. By invalidating unsubstantiated claims and banning offset-based neutrality assertions, the directive removes the flexibility brands previously exploited to appear sustainable without real action. Consequently, businesses can no longer rely on ambiguous terminology to mask poor environmental performance, forcing a fundamental change in how sustainability is communicated and verified.

Evidence suggests these projects frequently shift emissions elsewhere or leak further pollution, undermining their purported environmental advantages.
