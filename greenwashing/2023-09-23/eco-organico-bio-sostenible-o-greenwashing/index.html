¿Eco, orgánico, bio, sostenible o greenwashing?

The article argues that terms such as “eco” or “sustainable” are often merely marketing tools used by large corporations to conceal severe social and environmental harms within their supply chains. This practice, known as greenwashing, manipulates consumers who have growing ecological awareness but are denied the transparency needed to make truly critical and transformative choices. By labeling products with benign terminology, companies enhance their reputation without addressing the underlying issues of pollution and exploitation. The fast fashion industry exemplifies this deception by relying on perceived obsolescence to drive overproduction and overconsumption. This model artificially creates needs, leading to resource depletion, human rights violations, and massive textile waste in landfills. The narrative highlights that, despite the environmental and social costs, these strategies continue to inflate corporate profits while ignoring the scientific consensus that infinite economic growth on a finite planet is unsustainable. In response, grassroots initiatives and movements are emerging to challenge this cycle by promoting product longevity, repair, and second-hand use. These efforts aim to shift the economy away from a throwaway culture toward one focused on durability and social solidarity. The article concludes that recognizing these corporate strategies is vital for empowering citizens to reject greenwashing and demand authentic systemic change rather than superficial labels.
