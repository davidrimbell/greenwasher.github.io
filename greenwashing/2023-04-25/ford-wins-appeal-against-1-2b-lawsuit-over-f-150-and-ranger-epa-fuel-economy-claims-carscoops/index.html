Ford Wins Appeal Against $1.2B Lawsuit Over F-150 And Ranger EPA Fuel Economy Claims | Carscoops

A federal appeals court ruled that the EPA’s authority over fuel economy estimates preempts state-level consumer fraud claims against Ford. This decision upholds the agency’s right to determine testing methods without strict accuracy requirements, shielding automakers from lawsuits alleging inflated MPG figures. The ruling highlights how federal regulatory frameworks can prevent state courts from adjudicating accusations of misleading marketing practices. By prioritizing federal oversight, the court effectively blocked plaintiffs from proving that calculated discrepancies constituted deception under state tort laws, limiting consumer recourse for alleged financial harms. This case is relevant to greenwashing because it demonstrates how legal structures can protect manufacturers from liability regarding environmental performance claims. It underscores the challenge consumers face when regulatory bodies hold exclusive authority to define and validate sustainability metrics, potentially allowing misleading efficiency reports to stand without independent judicial scrutiny.
