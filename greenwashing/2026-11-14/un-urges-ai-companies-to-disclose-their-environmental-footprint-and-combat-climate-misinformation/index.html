UN urges AI companies to disclose their environmental footprint and combat climate misinformation

Artificial intelligence presents a key environmental paradox: although it promotes technological solutions, its high energy consumption, often derived from fossil fuels, contributes to global warming. This direct impact creates an urgent need to assess the true ecological footprint of these technologies, highlighting the necessity of aligning digital advancements with global climate goals to avoid exacerbating the existing crisis. Simultaneously, there is a significant risk of misinformation, as AI can generate false or distorted content about climate phenomena, undermining public trust and weakening the will to take action against climate change. The United Nations emphasizes that information integrity is as critical as operational sustainability, warning that a lack of truthfulness can have devastating consequences for societal responses to disasters and environmental changes. To combat these threats, radical transparency is being promoted, requiring AI companies to publicly disclose their resource consumption and emissions. This approach is fundamental in combating greenwashing, as it prevents corporations from hiding their environmental damage or promoting a false ecological image. Mandatory accountability ensures that the promotion of technology does not come at the expense of scientific truth or environmental protection, restoring the credibility necessary to address the climate emergency.
