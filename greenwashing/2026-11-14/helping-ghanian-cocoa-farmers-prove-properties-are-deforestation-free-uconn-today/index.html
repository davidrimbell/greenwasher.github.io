Helping Ghanian Cocoa Farmers Prove Properties are Deforestation-Free - UConn Today

The European Union’s new deforestation regulation poses significant challenges for small-scale cocoa farmers in Ghana by requiring proof that their land was not deforested after 2020. Unlike large industrial plantations in other countries, these smallholders lack the resources and technical capacity to individually map and monitor their properties. Consequently, while the regulation aims to stop deforestation, it risks excluding vulnerable farmers who cannot navigate complex compliance verification processes, potentially disrupting their livelihoods despite their environmental stewardship. To address this gap, researchers have developed an app-based system in collaboration with a major Ghanaian farmer cooperative. This digital tool allows purchasing clerks to verify compliance by comparing farmer boundaries against satellite deforestation alerts, then issuing QR codes for traceable cocoa bags. This innovation shifts the burden of verification from individual smallholders to the cooperative, digitizing the supply chain and enabling farmers to meet international standards without needing expensive independent mapping or monitoring infrastructure. This initiative is critically relevant to greenwashing discussions because it highlights the tension between well-intentioned environmental policies and their practical implementation. Without accessible verification tools, corporations may claim sustainable sourcing while inadvertently ignoring or excluding small farmers, creating a facade of ethical consumption that ignores supply chain realities. By testing whether monitoring alone or financial premiums drive compliance, this research seeks to ensure that "deforestation-free" claims are genuinely achievable and equitable, preventing greenwashing from becoming a barrier to market access for the world’s poorest producers.
