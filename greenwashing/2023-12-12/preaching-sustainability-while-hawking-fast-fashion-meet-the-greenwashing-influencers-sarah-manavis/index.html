Preaching sustainability while hawking fast fashion – meet the greenwashing influencers | Sarah Manavis

The article argues that influencers have become a primary vehicle for corporate greenwashing, acting as a protective buffer between fast fashion brands and public backlash. By positioning themselves as sustainability ambassadors for companies like Shein and H&M, influencers lend credibility to vague environmental promises. This strategy allows corporations to deflect criticism while promoting halfhearted eco-initiatives, effectively using the influencer’s perceived authenticity to mask the underlying reality of unsustainable production practices and excessive consumption. Furthermore, many influencers engage in a performative contradiction by cultivating an "eco-adjacent" persona while simultaneously profiting from fast fashion through affiliate links and collaborations. These content creators often promote minimalist or sustainable lifestyles to maintain their audience’s trust, yet their revenue models depend on encouraging continuous shopping. This creates a deceptive narrative where environmental consciousness is used as a marketing tool rather than a genuine commitment, allowing influencers to reap financial benefits from the very industries they claim to critique. The piece concludes that influencer-led sustainable brands, such as Tala, rarely offer true environmental solutions. Instead, they adopt defensive, incrementalist language that frames sustainability as a "journey" rather than an immediate standard, often partnering with non-eco-friendly retailers. This approach normalizes greenwashing by suggesting that imperfect efforts are sufficient. The author highlights the fundamental incompatibility between the influencer economy’s need for constant novelty and the true requirements of sustainability, which demand slowing down and reducing consumption.
