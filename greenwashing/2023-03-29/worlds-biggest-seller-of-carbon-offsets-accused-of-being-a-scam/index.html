World’s biggest seller of carbon offsets accused of being a scam

The article argues that the carbon offset industry is fundamentally a deceptive mechanism allowing corporations to maintain high pollution levels by purchasing imaginary environmental credits. Rather than reducing actual emissions, companies use these offsets to create a false narrative of sustainability, effectively outsourcing their responsibility while continuing business as usual. This practice shifts the financial burden onto consumers and working-class drivers, who inadvertently subsidize the very industries driving climate change. A primary example of this failure is the South Pole’s involvement in the Kariba forest project, which was vastly overestimated in its conservation impact. Consequently, major global brands purchased credits that provided negligible atmospheric benefits, meaning their claims of fighting climate change were largely unfounded. Furthermore, the vast majority of the revenue generated by these projects flowed to the offset sellers rather than the local communities tasked with preservation, highlighting a disconnect between corporate greenwashing and genuine environmental or social aid. This revelation is critical to understanding greenwashing because it exposes how the entire market relies on artificial valuation rather than tangible ecological results. The industry functions like a speculative bubble, where confidence is maintained by mandates rather than reality, allowing elites to deflect criticism through costly, ineffective gestures. By treating environmental virtue as a tradable commodity that often does not exist, the system undermines serious climate action and serves primarily as a lucrative shield for continued fossil fuel consumption.
