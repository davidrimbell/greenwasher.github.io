Consumer NZ finds supermarket aisles awash with dubious green claims | infonews.co.nz New Zealand News

A recent investigation by Consumer NZ reveals that greenwashing is widespread among supermarket products in Aotearoa, with nearly every item examined featuring unsubstantiated or misleading environmental claims. The study highlights how vague terminology, such as "planet conscious" or "compostable," allows manufacturers to insinuate eco-friendliness without delivering tangible benefits, often charging premium prices to conscious shoppers. This practice exploits the lack of regulatory enforcement, making it difficult for consumers to distinguish genuine sustainability efforts from marketing spin. The article underscores the critical disconnect between labeling claims and real-world infrastructure, particularly regarding compostable materials. Many products labeled as biodegradable or industrially compostable are impractical for disposal in New Zealand due to the scarcity of appropriate facilities, leading consumers to inadvertently contribute to landfill waste. Additionally, terms like "ocean plastic" are shown to be technically accurate yet misleading, as they often refer to coastal runoff rather than recycled marine debris, further confusing buyers who believe they are making superior environmental choices. This investigation is highly relevant to greenwashing because it demonstrates the systemic failure to protect consumers from deceptive environmental marketing. By exposing how easy it is to use meaningless terms that lack scientific backing or practical application, the article argues for urgent regulatory intervention. It calls for stricter, proactive laws similar to those proposed in the European Union, rather than relying on individual complaints, to ensure transparency and prevent companies from capitalizing on sustainability concerns without accountability.
