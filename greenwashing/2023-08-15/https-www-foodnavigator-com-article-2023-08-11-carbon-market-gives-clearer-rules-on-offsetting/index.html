Greenwashing
ES EN

Major corporations are increasingly distancing themselves from carbon offsetting due to intense scrutiny regarding transparency and credit quality, fearing accusations of greenwashing. This backlash has led industry leaders to prioritize direct emission reductions within their supply chains rather than relying on offsets to claim net-zero status. The resulting hesitation highlights a critical trust deficit, where companies are "greenhushing" their climate efforts to avoid reputational damage associated with potentially misleading environmental claims. To restore confidence, voluntary carbon market bodies are introducing rigorous global benchmarks and certification labels to distinguish high-integrity credits from inferior ones. These initiatives aim to standardize quality assurance, providing buyers with clear indicators of genuine impact. By establishing strict governance and reducing the ambiguity of bilateral transactions, the industry seeks to reassure investors that their purchases finance real, measurable emission reductions, thereby revitalizing the market’s financial viability. Crucially, the article argues that the term "carbon neutral" should be abandoned or strictly redefined to prevent companies from masking their remaining unabated emissions. Experts recommend framing credits as supplementary climate contributions rather than direct compensation for operational pollution. This shift is vital to combat greenwashing perceptions, ensuring that market participants adhere to credible climate leadership standards while encouraging urgent, transparent action toward global temperature goals.
