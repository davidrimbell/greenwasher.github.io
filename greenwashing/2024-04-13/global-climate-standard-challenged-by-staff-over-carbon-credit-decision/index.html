Global climate standard challenged by staff over carbon credit decision

The Science-Based Targets Initiative, widely regarded as the global gold standard for verifying corporate net-zero commitments, faces a severe internal revolt over allegations of greenwashing. Employees and technical advisors have condemned a recent board decision to allow companies to use carbon credits for offsetting significant portions of their value chain emissions. This reversal is described as a “coup” that defies scientific consensus and bypasses adequate consultation, sparking fears that the organization is prioritizing political influence over rigorous climate science. Critics argue this policy shift effectively allows corporations to maintain high pollution levels by purchasing offsets rather than implementing necessary structural changes. By permitting such allowances, the initiative risks becoming a platform where lobbying and conflicts of interest undermine its integrity. Experts warn that this fundamental U-turn signals to the market that companies can buy their way out of genuine responsibility, thereby eroding the credibility of net-zero claims and potentially harming the global effort to limit warming. This controversy is highly relevant to greenwashing as it highlights the tension between standardized certification frameworks and actual emission reductions. If a primary verifier loses its credibility due to compromised governance, it enables businesses to exploit loopholes while appearing compliant with environmental goals. The potential collapse of this standard’s reputation underscores the urgent need for transparent, scientifically grounded accountability to prevent corporate climate pledges from becoming mere marketing tools rather than actionable commitments.
