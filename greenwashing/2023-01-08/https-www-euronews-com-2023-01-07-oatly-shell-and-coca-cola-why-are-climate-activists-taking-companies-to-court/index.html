Greenwashing
ES EN

Climate litigation is surging globally, marking a significant shift in how activists combat environmental damage. This legal momentum is largely driven by growing public awareness of greenwashing, where corporations exaggerate their sustainability efforts. Recent actions by regulators and courts signal that misleading environmental claims will face serious scrutiny, encouraging campaigners to hold both governments and industries accountable through the legal system. The scope of these lawsuits has expanded beyond fossil fuels to include the plastic and food sectors. Companies are being challenged for inaccurately labeling products as recyclable or eco-friendly, such as single-use plastics or plant-based milk alternatives. These cases highlight a broader trend where consumers and NGOs are demanding transparency, forcing brands to either substantiate their green marketing claims or face legal repercussions for negligence and public nuisance. Financial institutions are also becoming primary targets, with banks and investment firms sued for promoting green investments that still fund polluting industries. This evolution demonstrates that greenwashing is no longer confined to product marketing but extends to capital allocation. The rising tide of litigation underscores the urgent need for corporate integrity, as legal precedents establish that misleading environmental narratives can lead to substantial financial liability and mandatory policy changes.
