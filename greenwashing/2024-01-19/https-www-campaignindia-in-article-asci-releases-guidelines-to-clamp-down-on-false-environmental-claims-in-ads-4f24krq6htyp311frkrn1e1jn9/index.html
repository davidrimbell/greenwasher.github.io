Greenwashing
ES EN

The Advertising Standards Council of India has implemented strict guidelines to combat greenwashing, ensuring that environmental marketing claims are reliable, verifiable, and transparent. This regulatory move responds to rising consumer demand for eco-friendly products, aiming to prevent businesses from misleading shoppers with unsubstantiated or exaggerated environmental benefits. The primary goal is to uphold integrity in advertising, allowing genuinely sustainable brands to communicate their efforts accurately while protecting consumers from deceptive practices. A central focus of these rules is the requirement that absolute environmental claims, such as "eco-friendly" or "sustainable," must be backed by robust data and credible accreditations. Disclaimers cannot dilute these broad assertions, nor can visual cues like green colors or nature imagery create false impressions of sustainability without direct evidence. Advertisers must also substantiate comparative claims and ensure that general environmental statements reflect the product’s full lifecycle, preventing the misleading focus on minor positive attributes while ignoring larger negative impacts. These guidelines are highly relevant to greenwashing because they directly address common tactics used to mislead consumers, such as relying on vague labels, exploiting legal compliance as a unique selling point, or making unsupported future promises. By mandating clear disclaimers for "free-of" ingredients and requiring scientific evidence for terms like "biodegradable," the rules close loopholes that allowed companies to exaggerate their environmental footprint. This regulatory framework establishes accountability, ensuring that marketing aligns with actual environmental performance rather than just perception.
