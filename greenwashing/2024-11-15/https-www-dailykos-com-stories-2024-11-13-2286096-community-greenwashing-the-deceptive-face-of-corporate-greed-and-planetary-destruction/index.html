Greenwashing
EN ES

This article exposes greenwashing as a strategic disinformation campaign where corporations prioritize profit over transparency, misleading consumers with false eco-friendly claims. By capitalizing on the growing demand for sustainable products, companies divert attention from their actual environmental harm, allowing them to avoid accountability while continuing harmful practices. The narrative highlights how industries like fashion and fossil fuels use vague terminology and deceptive marketing to mask the reality of their operations, effectively selling the illusion of sustainability to maintain market share. The text draws direct parallels between the fossil fuel and tobacco industries, illustrating how both have historically funded campaigns to sow doubt about the dangers of their products. For decades, these entities have promoted recycling and cleaner alternatives despite internal knowledge proving these solutions are economically unviable or insufficient. This deliberate obfuscation not only protects corporate interests but also shifts the burden of climate impacts onto vulnerable communities, exacerbating global inequality and environmental degradation while the wealthiest entities evade responsibility. Relevance to greenwashing is found in its identification as a systemic tool for corporate evasion rather than isolated marketing errors. To combat this, consumers must leverage their financial and political power by shifting investments away from polluting institutions and demanding stricter regulatory oversight. The article concludes that solving the climate crisis requires dismantling these deceptive narratives through collective action and authentic accountability, as corporations are unlikely to self-regulate when profits are at stake.
