NatWest accused of greenwashing over finance for fossil fuel expansion company

NatWest faces fresh greenwashing allegations after reports revealed the bank assisted BP in raising hundreds of millions for fossil fuel expansion in 2023. This activity directly contradicts the institution’s public pledges to achieve net zero by 2050 and its stated refusal to finance oil and gas majors lacking credible transition plans aligned with the Paris Agreement. Critics argue that providing capital for new oil and gas developments undermines global climate goals, as such expansion inevitably pushes temperature rises beyond safe thresholds. The controversy highlights a systemic issue where major financial institutions label themselves as environmentally responsible while continuing to fund industries that exacerbate climate change. Experts and campaign groups assert that BP’s transition strategy does not genuinely align with international climate targets, yet it still received significant financial support. This discrepancy between public commitments and actual financing practices suggests a disconnect between marketing claims and operational reality, a hallmark of greenwashing that misleads consumers and investors about the true environmental impact of their money. The article’s relevance to greenwashing lies in exposing how banks use vague "transition plan" criteria to justify continued funding of fossil fuel companies. NatWest denies wrongdoing, claiming its risk assessments ensure alignment with its goals, but independent analysis suggests otherwise. By highlighting the gap between stated climate ambitions and actual investment decisions, the report illustrates how corporate sustainability promises can be undermined by ongoing support for high-carbon industries, thereby eroding trust in net-zero claims across the financial sector.
