Greenwashing crackdown puts D&O and liability cover in the spotlight

The Australian Competition and Consumer Commission has introduced strict new guidance requiring businesses to base their environmental marketing on credible, science-based accounting methods. This move aims to prevent greenwashing, which undermines consumer trust and unfairly disadvantages companies with genuine sustainability efforts. Regulators now expect clear, transparent claims that avoid vague language or the misleading equivalence of carbon credits and direct reductions, establishing a higher standard for accountability. Financial and legal risks have escalated significantly as penalties for violating consumer law have doubled, creating substantial liability for corporations. Simultaneously, insurance regulators are actively enforcing standards against misrepresentations in financial products, creating overlapping regulatory pressure. This dual enforcement tracks from both consumer and financial perspectives means that even unintentional greenwashing poses severe legal dangers, exposing directors and officers to significant litigation risks regardless of intent. For business brokers, this regulatory tightening necessitates a proactive approach to risk management and client advisory. Insurers are increasingly scrutinizing governance and the quality of emissions data, making it essential to stress-test clients’ sustainability claims rather than accepting them at face value. As the gap between mandatory disclosures and voluntary marketing claims becomes a trigger for action, brokers must ensure that coverage adequately responds to greenwashing allegations and advise clients to rigorously validate their environmental messaging to mitigate exposure.
