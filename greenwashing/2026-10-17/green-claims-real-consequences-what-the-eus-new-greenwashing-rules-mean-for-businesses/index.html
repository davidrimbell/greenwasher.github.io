Green Claims, Real Consequences: What the EU’s New Greenwashing Rules Mean for Businesses

New EU regulations have immediately tightened restrictions on environmental claims to combat greenwashing, applying to any business marketing to European consumers regardless of its physical location. This legislation bans vague terms like “eco-friendly” without proof of excellence and prohibits describing products as carbon neutral based solely on offsets. Consequently, companies must ensure all sustainability labels are independently verified and that broad claims reflect the entire product, not just specific components. For mergers and acquisitions, these rules necessitate rigorous due diligence on marketing materials rather than production processes. Buyers must assess the financial and reputational risks associated with non-compliant claims, such as the costs of relabeling stock or facing significant turnover-based fines. The directive’s extraterritorial reach means that non-EU sellers, including US exporters, are equally liable if they target EU markets, requiring careful review of all consumer-facing communications and brand names. Financial asset managers and investors also face heightened scrutiny, as the directive intersects with existing sustainable finance regulations. Even if funds are marketed to professionals, individual investors may still be protected as consumers, extending liability to broader firm-level marketing and portfolio companies. Investors must now evaluate how portfolio companies handle green claims, ensuring consistency between fund messaging and the actual practices of the businesses they hold, thereby embedding greenwashing prevention into investment due diligence and monitoring.
