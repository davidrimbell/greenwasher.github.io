Energy & Sustainability Litigation Updates — October 2024

Energy & Sustainability Litigation Updates — October 2024

The dissolution of the SEC’s Climate & ESG Task Force signals a de-prioritization of federal ESG enforcement, though its practical impact remains limited due to prior successful actions and ongoing regulatory efforts outside the task force’s scope. This suggests that while high-profile oversight may lessen, the threat of greenwashing penalties persists through established channels and broader agency initiatives. Simultaneously, California’s rejection of a delay for its new climate disclosure laws highlights the continued push for stricter environmental transparency despite legal challenges. This legislative firmness indicates that state-level mandates will likely proceed, forcing companies to adhere to rigorous reporting standards regardless of federal shifts, thereby increasing compliance complexity for businesses operating within the jurisdiction. Recent litigation against major consumer brands further underscores the ongoing vulnerability of companies to greenwashing claims. By targeting misleading environmental marketing in both private and federal courts, regulators and plaintiffs demonstrate that aggressive scrutiny of product claims remains active, warning firms that deceptive sustainability advertising carries significant legal and reputational risks.
