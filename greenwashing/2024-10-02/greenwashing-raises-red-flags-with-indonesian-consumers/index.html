Greenwashing Raises Red Flags with Indonesian Consumers

Indonesian consumers, particularly millennials and Gen-Z, are increasingly skeptical of corporate environmental claims due to the prevalence of greenwashing. As shoppers become more environmentally conscious, they are willing to pay premium prices for genuinely sustainable products. However, the gap between this growing demand and the reality of deceptive marketing practices has created significant distrust, forcing brands to compete on authentic sustainability rather than superficial "eco-friendly" labels. The core issue lies in the lack of transparency and credible verification for green products. Despite the existence of various ecolabels, consumers often face conflicting information and limited choices, leading to confusion and hesitation. Many products marketed as green still rely on harmful chemicals or generate excessive waste, undermining their environmental value. This disconnect erodes brand-consumer relationships, as shoppers struggle to distinguish between true sustainability and marketing hype without reliable sources of information. This situation is highly relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates the tangible damage caused by deceptive practices: a crisis of trust that hinders the adoption of legitimate sustainable solutions. The article highlights that without stricter regulations and transparent labeling, consumers are forced to rely on anecdotal evidence and brand reputation rather than factual data. Consequently, the fight against greenwashing is not just about protecting the environment, but about restoring the integrity of information required for consumers to make informed, ethical purchasing decisions.
