Oil majors shifting from greenwashing to fossil fuel advocacy, advertising study finds

Major oil companies are actively shifting their public narrative from aspiring climate leaders to defending the continued necessity of fossil fuels. By rebranding oil and gas as essential for national security and economic stability, these firms are normalizing dependence on carbon-intensive energy sources. This strategic pivot indicates that industry commitments to net-zero goals are being diluted in favor of protecting core business interests and shareholder returns, suggesting that previous environmental promises were superficial rather than substantive. The report highlights a concerning evolution in marketing tactics, where companies now promote questionable technologies like carbon capture and natural gas as viable climate solutions. This approach risks prolonging fossil fuel reliance under the guise of innovation, a practice that obscures the reality that these methods may hinder genuine decarbonization. Consequently, the public is being misled into believing that technological fixes and increased extraction can coexist with climate goals, effectively gaslighting consumers about the urgency of transitioning away from dirty energy. This analysis is critically relevant to greenwashing because it exposes how the industry has moved beyond vague sustainability claims into overt "gaslighting." By framing fossil fuels as indispensable and using emotionally resonant themes like family values or energy security, companies manipulate public perception to delay regulatory action. The study reveals a coordinated, global playbook where environmental responsibility is abandoned for defensive rhetoric, demonstrating that the current landscape of corporate communication is designed to mislead stakeholders about the true scale of the climate crisis and the industry’s role in perpetuating it.
