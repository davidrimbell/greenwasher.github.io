Greenwashing
EN ES

This statement argues that Azerbaijan’s hosting of COP29 exemplifies greenwashing by using environmental rhetoric to legitimize a repressive authoritarian regime. The authors assert that the host government masks political persecution and the imprisonment of dissenters with a facade of sustainability. This highlights the critical greenwashing implication where climate conferences are exploited to cleanse the reputations of dictatorial regimes, obscuring severe human rights abuses under the guise of global cooperation and ecological responsibility. Furthermore, the text critiques "green capitalism" as a new form of imperialism that exploits the South Caucasus for resource extraction while displacing local populations. By labeling this trend as a colonial extension, the article reveals how sustainability narratives often serve corporate and geopolitical interests rather than genuine ecological or social justice. This underscores the greenwashing strategy of repurposing extractive industries as "green," thereby masking continued exploitation and violence against indigenous and local communities. Finally, the movement rejects the false binary between Western and Russian influence, demanding accountability for local elites who collude with global powers. It emphasizes that true environmental justice cannot exist alongside authoritarian rule or economic inequality. This perspective is vital for understanding greenwashing because it exposes how climate action is frequently decoupled from social justice, allowing oppressive regimes to profit from the global energy transition while maintaining control through coercion and resource monopolization.
