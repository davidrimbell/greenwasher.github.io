Los créditos de carbono, entre la acción climática y el ecoblanqueo

The article reveals that the majority of voluntary carbon credit programs lack scientific rigor, often failing to deliver genuine climate benefits. Research indicates that these projects frequently overstate their impact and do not effectively prevent deforestation or reduce emissions at the source. Instead, they serve as mechanisms for polluting corporations to maintain their current levels of contamination while projecting an image of environmental responsibility. This lack of integrity enables extensive greenwashing, allowing companies to offset emissions far from their operations without addressing the root causes of pollution. By utilizing these credits, corporations can market themselves as carbon neutral to investors and consumers, effectively masking their continued reliance on fossil fuels. The system is characterized by low transparency and methods that are often biased, turning climate action into a financial tool rather than an ecological solution. The situation is exacerbated by institutional decisions that lower compliance standards, inviting further misuse of these credits to meet indirect emission targets. Consequently, the market not only fails to curb global warming but also reinforces unequal power dynamics in the Global South, where local communities often face human rights violations without consent. This highlights the urgent need for stricter regulations and scientific validation to prevent climate initiatives from becoming mere tools for corporate reputation management.
