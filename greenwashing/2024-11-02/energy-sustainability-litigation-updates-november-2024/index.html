Energy & Sustainability Litigation Updates — November 2024

Energy & Sustainability Litigation Updates — November 2024

The SEC remains highly active in enforcing greenwashing regulations, significantly increasing penalties for misleading ESG claims and inaccurate sustainability statements. This aggressive stance underscores that relying on third-party vendors is insufficient; companies must exercise greater internal diligence to ensure their marketing aligns with actual environmental practices, highlighting the severe financial and reputational risks of deceptive eco-labeling. Simultaneously, California’s climate disclosure laws are advancing, setting a strict compliance timeline that forces greater transparency. This regulatory pressure contrasts with recent legal wins for fossil fuel companies, where courts ruled that general insurance policies do not cover climate-related damages. These diverging trends illustrate the tightening regulatory environment for green claims while simultaneously limiting financial safety nets for entities responsible for environmental harm. Furthermore, the collapse of Missouri’s anti-ESG regulations signals a shift away from restricting sustainable investing considerations. With the state abandoning its appeal, the legal landscape continues to favor the integration of ESG factors rather than prohibiting them. Together, these developments demonstrate a clear regulatory trajectory toward demanding authenticity in green marketing and robust disclosure, exposing greenwashing as a direct liability rather than a permissible marketing strategy.
