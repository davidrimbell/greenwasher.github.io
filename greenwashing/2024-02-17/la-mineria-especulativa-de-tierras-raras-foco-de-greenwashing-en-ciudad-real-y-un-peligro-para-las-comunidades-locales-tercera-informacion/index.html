La minería especulativa de tierras raras, foco de greenwashing en Ciudad Real y un peligro para las comunidades locales - Tercera Información

The Platform Yes to Living Land denounces that an event promoted by a group linked to mining interests constitutes a clear case of greenwashing. The collective firmly rejects participation, arguing that the initiative uses the narrative of the energy transition and the need for critical minerals as an ideological pretext. Its true purpose would be to legitimize and publicize a rare earth mining project previously rejected by authorities and citizens, hiding behind an ecological discourse the pursuit of corporate profits. The organization emphasizes that this maneuver seeks to mask the severe environmental impacts of rare earth mining, such as soil and water contamination, ecosystem destruction, and the generation of toxic radioactive waste. Far from being a sustainable solution, the project is presented as speculative, lacking a real value chain due to the absence of local refining technology and dependence on restrictive international authorizations. Thus, it conceals the reality that the supposed environmental benefit entails ecological costs that are unsustainable for present and future generations. This case is relevant for the study of greenwashing because it illustrates how commercial interests manipulate the discourse of sustainability to legitimize destructive activities. The platform criticizes the "selling" of a favorable environmental narrative while attempting to impose a speculative model that prioritizes European funds and private profits over community well-being. It highlights the importance of distinguishing between genuinely sustainable initiatives and those projects that use the urgency of the climate crisis to justify irreversible damage to the territory.
