Greenwashing
ES EN

The widespread nature of greenwashing has triggered a profound crisis of trust, with majority of consumers across all sectors, including niche industries like pet food, believing brands make false sustainability claims. This pervasive skepticism undermines the very foundation of sustainable consumption, as customers feel deceived by marketing tactics that prioritize premiumization over genuine environmental progress. The data reveals that even industries previously viewed as benign are now scrutinized heavily, indicating that greenwashing is not an isolated issue but a systemic barrier to shifting consumer behavior. This distrust creates a significant disconnect between consumer values and purchasing habits, particularly when economic pressures force buyers to prioritize cost over ethics. Ironically, as consumers become more knowledgeable and passionate about sustainability, they are more likely to detect greenwashing, leading to active rejection of brands or entire product categories. The consequence is a stagnation in the transition to sustainable lifestyles, where the fear of being misled outweighs the desire to support ethical initiatives, effectively paralyzing the market’s ability to drive meaningful change. The article is crucial to understanding greenwashing because it illustrates the tangible business risks: loss of credibility and sales volume. It argues that superficial claims are no longer sufficient in a post-truth environment. Instead, brands must pursue aggressive, third-party-validated commitments and radical transparency. The implication is that integrity, rather than just marketing, is the only viable path to rebuilding consumer trust and enabling the necessary shift toward sustainable consumption patterns.
