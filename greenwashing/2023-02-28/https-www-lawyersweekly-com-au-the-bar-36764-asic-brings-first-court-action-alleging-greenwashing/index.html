Greenwashing
ES EN

ASIC is pursuing legal action against Mercer for allegedly making false and misleading claims regarding its “Sustainable Plus” investment options. The regulator argues that despite marketing these funds as excluding carbon-intensive fossil fuels, alcohol, and gambling, members’ assets were still invested in companies involved in these prohibited industries. This discrepancy suggests Mercer engaged in conduct that could mislead the public about the actual sustainability profile of the products. This case marks a significant shift in regulatory enforcement, representing the first greenwashing proceedings brought by ASIC under recent legislative amendments inspired by the Financial Services Royal Commission. The action demonstrates the regulator’s growing capacity and willingness to hold superannuation trustees accountable for sustainability-related conduct, signaling a departure from past practices where such greenwashing might have gone unchecked. The proceedings highlight the critical relevance of this case to greenwashing, as it underscores the necessity for financial institutions to ensure their marketing claims accurately reflect investment realities. By seeking penalties and injunctions, ASIC aims to deter misleading marketing and restore trust, emphasizing that any promise of specific exclusions must be strictly upheld. This sets a precedent for increased accountability and accurate disclosure in the rapidly expanding market for sustainable financial products.
