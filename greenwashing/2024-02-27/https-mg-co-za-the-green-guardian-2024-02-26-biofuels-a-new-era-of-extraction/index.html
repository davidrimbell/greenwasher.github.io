Greenwashing
ES EN

The article highlights a stark disconnect between the theoretical promises of sustainable aviation fuel and the harsh economic realities faced by African farmers. Major energy companies promise that drought-resistant crops like castor beans will provide additional income without competing with food sources. However, on-the-ground investigations reveal that low yield prices, poor seed quality, and unrealistic agronomic expectations have left farmers with negligible profits and significant losses, undermining the social sustainability claimed by the industry. This discrepancy exposes a critical form of greenwashing where environmental benefits are prioritized over social equity and economic viability. While corporate narratives emphasize climate neutrality and support for arid regions, the actual implementation results in exploitation and disillusionment among small-scale producers. The failure to deliver promised technical support and fair compensation suggests that these biofuel initiatives may serve more as marketing tools for global corporations than as genuine development projects for local communities. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the selective presentation of success metrics. Companies report high engagement numbers and capacity targets while obscuring the dismal actual production volumes and farmer dissatisfaction. By ignoring the structural failures and economic harm inflicted on local farmers, these projects create an illusion of sustainability that benefits global industries at the expense of those bearing the agricultural burden. This case illustrates how "sustainable" labels can mask unethical practices and false narratives about community benefit.
