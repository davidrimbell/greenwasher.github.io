Greenwashing
ES EN

A major investigation reveals that nearly all rainforest carbon credits issued by Verra, the leading global certifier, lack genuine climate mitigation value. The study identifies these credits as "phantom," meaning they do not represent real emission reductions because the projects vastly overestimated the threat of deforestation. Consequently, corporations such as Disney, Shell, and Gucci have relied on these worthless offsets to market themselves as environmentally responsible, potentially worsening global heating by allowing continued pollution under the guise of neutrality. This systemic failure highlights a critical flaw in the carbon offset industry’s verification process, where auditors and certifiers face insufficient independence and significant financial incentives to approve substandard projects. The methodology for "avoided deforestation" relies heavily on unreliable counterfactuals—predictions of what would have happened without intervention—which are notoriously difficult to validate. This lack of rigorous oversight allows the certifier to profit from the volume of credits issued rather than ensuring ecological integrity, creating a structure that prioritizes revenue over actual environmental benefits. The article is highly relevant to greenwashing because it exposes how companies exploit opaque carbon markets to create a false impression of sustainability. By purchasing these flawed credits, major brands engage in misleading communication, claiming to neutralize their carbon footprints without implementing substantive reductions. This undermines public trust and diverts attention from the urgent need for transparency and accountability, demonstrating how greenwashing can persist when verification mechanisms fail to distinguish between real action and empty promises.
