Greenwashing
ES EN

The European Securities and Markets Authority’s proposal for a mandatory ecolabel offers a critical mechanism to combat greenwashing in sustainable finance. By establishing standardized criteria, regulators aim to restore investor trust and transparency, which have been eroded by inconsistent claims and recent downgrades of impact funds. This regulatory intervention addresses the market confusion caused by the lack of a unified definition for what constitutes genuine positive impact, thereby forcing asset managers to demonstrate measurable results rather than relying on vague marketing. A significant challenge lies in the strictness of these proposed standards, particularly the requirement for funds to align a substantial portion of their portfolio with the EU’s green taxonomy. Current data suggests that very few funds meet this threshold, primarily because the taxonomy heavily emphasizes environmental factors while neglecting social and governance aspects. Consequently, there is a risk that an overly rigid label could become irrelevant if it excludes the majority of the market, potentially stifling innovation and discouraging participation in sustainable investing rather than encouraging it. To ensure effectiveness, the solution requires a nuanced approach involving graduated labels and distinct categories for different types of impact, rather than a one-size-fits-all mandate. This balance allows for meaningful differentiation without rendering the certification impractical or obsolete. Ultimately, this article highlights the urgent need for precise, inclusive regulatory frameworks that accurately reflect complex sustainability goals, ensuring that labels genuinely guide capital toward meaningful change instead of serving as mere compliance checkboxes.
