Coutinho hits out at Labour’s ‘mad, bad and dangerous’ clean energy plan

The article highlights a political clash regarding the feasibility and security implications of the UK’s clean energy transition. A government official argues that Labour’s accelerated target for clean electricity is unrealistic and dangerous, fearing it would force reliance on Chinese critical minerals and destabilize energy security. This perspective frames rapid decarbonization as a potential vulnerability, drawing parallels to previous geopolitical dependencies on Russian energy. However, the opposition counters that the current administration’s lack of industrial strategy has already left the country exposed to global supply chain risks and high costs. They position their plan as a solution that prioritizes domestic manufacturing and energy independence. The debate centers on whether speed or caution better serves national security, with accusations flying that the current approach creates "made in China" dependencies while the alternative promises a sovereign, locally-driven green industrial base. This discourse is highly relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how political actors may manipulate environmental narratives for economic or geopolitical advantage. By labeling a competitor’s green policy as "mad" or "dangerous," officials can deflect from their own record while framing their slower approach as responsible. This dynamic often obscures the true sustainability of policies, turning climate action into a partisan tool where claims of security and economic prudence mask strategic failures or deliberate delays in meaningful decarbonization efforts.
