Del ‘greenwashing al ‘techwashing’

The article reveals that techwashing, or the deceptive promotion of artificial intelligence, functions as a direct parallel to greenwashing. Influencers and consultants often sell AI-driven transformation using fabricated success metrics and vague promises, leading to widespread adoption of technologies that are neither understood by users nor genuinely implemented by the companies purchasing them. This creates a facade of innovation that masks the reality that many AI tools remain unused or misunderstood within corporate structures. Experts highlight that this technological hype is frequently weaponized to perpetuate environmentally and socially unsustainable business models. Just as greenwashing obscures a company's true ecological impact with misleading labels, techwashing employs confusing jargon and narratives of transparency to distract from actual lack of progress. Consequently, AI is used to justify mass layoffs or to justify non-compliance with social responsibilities, creating a narrative where technological advancement is presented as a solution to problems it does not resolve, thereby evading accountability for negative societal impacts. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the shared mechanism of using buzzwords to mask substantive failures. The text emphasizes that without strict regulation and increased transparency, companies can exploit the public's lack of technical understanding to maintain opaque operations. The conclusion stresses that combating this phenomenon requires not only legal frameworks with significant penalties but also a citizenry capable of demanding clear explanations of how these technologies are actually used, ensuring that promises of progress are backed by verifiable evidence rather than empty rhetoric.
