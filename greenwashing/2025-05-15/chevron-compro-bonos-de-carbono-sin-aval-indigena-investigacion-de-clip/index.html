Chevron compró bonos de carbono sin aval indígena: investigación de Clip

The investigation reveals how Chevron used carbon credits from an Indigenous project in Colombia to reduce its tax liability, despite claims by affected communities that they neither participated in the project nor received any benefits. This disconnect between the corporation’s conservation narrative and local realities illustrates a key mechanism of greenwashing: the appropriation of sustainability initiatives to improve public image without ensuring genuine rights or transparency for Indigenous peoples. The crisis is exacerbated by systemic flaws in certification, where auditing and development firms share ties that compromise impartiality. The fact that an oil company continues to purchase carbon credits despite court rulings recognizing violations of Indigenous rights demonstrates deliberate negligence, suggesting that carbon market systems prioritize financial legitimacy over social justice and procedural integrity. This case is significant for understanding greenwashing because it shows how bureaucratic complexity and lack of access to information are used to obscure abusive practices. By silencing communities and undermining local governance, corporations turn “conservation” into a tool for image laundering, detaching environmental responsibility from their actual human impact and maintaining power structures that ignore the voices of those who inhabit the territory.
