Iberdrola asume la primera sentencia por ecopostureo y no recurre el fallo favorable a Repsol

Iberdrola asume la primera sentencia por ecopostureo y no recurre el fallo favorable a Repsol

The first final court ruling in Spain on “ecopositioning” has favored Repsol, which was acquitted of the misleading advertising complaint filed by Iberdrola. The court determined that the oil company’s sustainability messages do not mislead consumers, since the Spanish public is universally aware of its business being tied to fossil fuels. The ruling establishes that declaring a commitment to the energy transition is different from claiming to be a sustainable company, allowing traditional firms to announce their evolution without violating laws against misleading advertising. The significance of this judgment lies in its establishment of a key legal precedent: references to sustainability are acceptable if they are framed within the company’s positioning toward international commitments, rather than as direct environmental claims that could confuse the public. With no further appeals possible, this decision stands as the first judicial reference on the matter, providing a specific interpretation of how Spain’s Unfair Competition Law addresses corporate transparency in highly polluting sectors. This case is relevant to greenwashing because it demonstrates that, at present, accusations of deceptive marketing against major energy companies face difficulties gaining traction if the public is already aware of their core business model. It illustrates the legal complexity of regulating corporate communications during the energy transition and warns that, until the European directive is fully transposed into national law, companies may employ future-oriented positioning strategies without necessarily breaking the law.
