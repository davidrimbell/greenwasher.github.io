Designer Peri Drysdale isn't here to greenwash, she's been walking the talk for 40 years

Designer Peri Drysdale isn't here to greenwash, she's been walking the talk for 40 years

Untouched World serves as a critical counter-example to greenwashing by demonstrating that genuine sustainability requires deep operational integrity rather than superficial marketing claims. The brand’s decades-long commitment to local manufacturing, material innovation, and near-total waste diversion contrasts sharply with the industry trend of prioritizing public perception over tangible environmental impact. This approach highlights that true sustainability is a complex, continuous practice embedded in every stage of production, from sourcing to end-of-life recycling, rather than a temporary campaign. The article underscores the danger of vague sustainability labels and mixed-material claims, which often mask environmentally harmful practices. By exposing how seemingly eco-friendly choices, such as blending synthetic fibers with natural ones, can create unrecyclable waste, the brand illustrates the necessity for precise, transparent communication. This focus on educating consumers about the reality of textile waste and microfiber pollution challenges the "lip service" often seen in the fashion industry, urging a move away from careless messaging that misleads the public about a product’s actual ecological footprint. Furthermore, the piece emphasizes the importance of long-term design principles, such as durability and repairability, in combating the disposable culture that fuels greenwashing. Through initiatives that celebrate garment longevity and mentor new designers, Untouched World reinforces that sustainability is intrinsically linked to quality and lasting value. This narrative provides a clear benchmark for authentic eco-friendly business practices, showing how transparency and holistic design can effectively distinguish legitimate efforts from opportunistic marketing tactics.
