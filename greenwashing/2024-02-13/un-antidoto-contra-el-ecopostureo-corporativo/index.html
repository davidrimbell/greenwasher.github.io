Un antídoto contra el ‘ecopostureo’ corporativo

Un antídoto contra el ‘ecopostureo’ corporativo

The article highlights the creation of Social Benefit and Common Interest Companies (SBICs) in Spain as a legislative attempt to distinguish genuine corporate social responsibility from mere marketing fluff. By legally requiring these entities to integrate social and environmental goals into their statutes, the framework aims to separate "good grain" from "advertising chaff." However, the effectiveness of this distinction is currently compromised by the delay in implementing the necessary regulatory framework, leaving the system vulnerable to misuse. This regulatory gap creates a significant risk of greenwashing, allowing companies to adopt the SBIC label for public relations without adhering to rigorous standards. The author warns that without clear rules, the classification could become a loophole for firms to publicize commitments they do not keep, exacerbating the confusion consumers face. This is particularly dangerous given the financial incentives, such as potential tax benefits and public contracting advantages, which motivate businesses to seek certification regardless of their actual impact. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the parallel efforts to regulate environmental claims, such as the EU’s Green Claims Directive. While the SBIC law seeks to establish a middle ground between traditional profit-driven models and high-standard certifications like B Corp, the article argues that transparency remains the critical missing piece. Without strict enforcement and verification, the SBIC designation risks becoming another tool for superficial eco-friendly branding rather than a catalyst for real economic and social transformation.
