Group says Lululemon is 'greenwashing' as its emissions rise, wants competition probe

Stand.earth has petitioned Canada’s Competition Bureau to investigate Lululemon for alleged greenwashing, arguing that the brand’s environmental marketing misleads consumers. The organization highlights a significant discrepancy between Lululemon’s “Be Planet” slogan and its internal data, which reveals that greenhouse gas emissions have doubled since the initiative’s launch. This investigation underscores how companies may exaggerate their sustainability efforts to attract eco-conscious buyers while their actual environmental footprint worsens. The core of the complaint focuses on Lululemon’s supply chain, which relies heavily on fossil fuels for both raw materials and manufacturing. Although the company claims progress in reducing emissions within its direct operations, the vast majority of its carbon output originates in its broader supply network. Critics emphasize that choices such as air freight and coal-powered factories contradict the company’s narrative of climate stewardship, suggesting that the marketing creates a false impression of positive environmental impact. This case is relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates the tactic of decoupling brand messaging from operational reality. By investing in high-visibility sustainability slogans while continuing to expand fossil-fuel-dependent production, Lululemon exemplifies how businesses can profit from rising emissions while appearing to be climate leaders. The investigation highlights the growing scrutiny toward corporate claims that outpace tangible action, warning consumers to look beyond slogans to the actual data regarding supply chain transparency and emission reductions.
