El verde que no siempre es ecológico: el ecopostureo de la industria

El verde que no siempre es ecológico: el ecopostureo de la industria

The article reveals how the widespread use of eco-friendly labels often conceals a deeper reality of greenwashing, where corporate marketing strategies prioritize image over genuine sustainable practices. By exploiting vague terms such as "carbon neutral" or "zero waste," companies obscure questionable environmental impacts, creating a disconnect between their public narrative and actual operations. This deceptive tactic undermines consumer trust and distorts the market for authentic sustainability. Regulatory bodies like the European Union are responding with stricter directives, such as the Due Diligence Directive, which mandates transparency regarding environmental and human rights harms. These measures introduce significant financial penalties for non-compliance, aiming to enforce accountability. However, challenges remain in verifying claims and distinguishing between real commitments and mere publicity stunts amidst complex market dynamics. This topic is crucial for understanding greenwashing, as it highlights the systemic gap between corporate rhetoric and action. It underscores the necessity of independent verification and robust regulation to prevent misleading claims from eroding consumer confidence. Ultimately, meaningful environmental progress depends not only on policy but also on a collective demand for transparency and authentic corporate responsibility that goes beyond superficial branding.
