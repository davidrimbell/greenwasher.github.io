Canada's consumer watchdog has ideas about how to crack down on greenwashing | CBC News

Canada’s Competition Bureau is urgently seeking expanded regulatory powers to combat corporate greenwashing, where companies use misleading environmental claims to appear more sustainable than they are. Commissioner Matthew Boswell argues that current laws are insufficient and has proposed strengthening legislation to require businesses to substantiate broad brand-level claims, such as net-zero targets. This initiative aims to close legal loopholes that currently allow vague or unsupported marketing to persist without accountability, ensuring that general corporate environmental statements are held to the same rigorous standards as specific product claims. The relevance of this push lies in the critical role transparency plays in exposing deception. Environmental advocates argue that current processes are too slow and narrow, failing to adequately address the systemic nature of greenwashing in high-polluting industries. By mandating that companies prove their assertions and ideally make this evidence publicly available at the point of purchase, regulators can empower consumers and watchdogs to verify truthfulness. Without such measures, corporations can continue misinformation campaigns with impunity, undermining genuine sustainability efforts and eroding public trust in environmental commitments. This regulatory evolution is vital because it shifts the burden of proof onto advertisers rather than relying solely on reactive complaints. As investigations into major energy firms proceed, the lack of immediate penalties highlights the need for proactive legislative changes. Strengthening these rules not only aligns Canada with stricter international standards but also creates a necessary deterrent against deceptive marketing. Ultimately, robust oversight is essential to prevent the fossil fuel and energy sectors from exploiting vague terminology, ensuring that environmental claims reflect actual performance rather than strategic PR.
