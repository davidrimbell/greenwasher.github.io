'Greenwashing' fast fashion brands are going all in on the resale market. But consumers are pushing back - The Big Issue

Major fashion experts characterize the introduction of resale platforms by ultra-fast fashion brands as an egregious form of greenwashing. Rather than addressing the root causes of environmental harm, these initiatives serve as a "get-out-of-jail-free card," allowing companies to appear eco-conscious while continuing to encourage excessive consumption. Critics argue this strategy masks the fact that these brands are designed for disposability, making the notion of reselling their low-quality, short-lived garments fundamentally redundant and deceptive. The core issue lies in the inherent contradiction of trying to sustain a business model built on rapid, trend-driven overproduction through circular economy tactics. Industry analysts emphasize that genuine sustainability requires scaling back production and creating durable goods, not merely adding a resale feature to maintain high profit margins. By focusing on surface-level changes, these corporations exploit consumer inexperience, perpetuating a cycle of waste while profiting from the very practices that damage the planet and exploit workers. True accountability cannot be achieved through voluntary corporate initiatives alone, as the current system prioritizes profit over planetary health. The article concludes that meaningful change requires robust legislation, independent auditing, and stricter consequences for polluters. Consumers are urged to remain discerning and reject brands that fail to address the fundamental drivers of waste, recognizing that buying less and choosing quality over quantity remains the most effective individual action against the fast fashion crisis.
