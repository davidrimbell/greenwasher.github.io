Oil and gas companies needed to make green hydrogen a reality

The article challenges the simplistic narrative that oil and gas companies are solely climate villains, arguing that they possess critical infrastructure and expertise essential for the energy transition. While these firms have delayed clean energy adoption, they remain indispensable because hydrocarbons will likely serve as raw materials for plastics and fertilizers for decades. Consequently, a successful shift to net zero requires leveraging their established capabilities in heavy engineering and risk management rather than ignoring their role entirely. A key opportunity lies in the development of green hydrogen, a sector where oil and gas majors have a distinct advantage due to existing experience with hydrogen handling and distribution networks. The author highlights specific investments by companies like Shell and BP in renewable projects, suggesting that those who reinvent themselves as broader energy conglomerates will lead the transition. This evolution involves moving beyond fossil fuel extraction to dominate new markets in green chemicals and synthetic fuels, utilizing their global logistical networks to scale emerging technologies. This perspective is relevant to greenwashing because it reframes corporate environmental strategies from mere public relations exercises to potential operational necessities. Understanding that some major players are adapting their core business models to survive helps consumers and regulators distinguish between genuine, structural reinvestment and superficial eco-branding. It encourages a more nuanced evaluation of these corporations, recognizing that their participation might be vital for achieving global climate goals, even if their past and present practices remain deeply flawed.
