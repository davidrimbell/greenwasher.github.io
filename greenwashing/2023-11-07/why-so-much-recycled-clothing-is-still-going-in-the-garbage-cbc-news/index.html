Why so much recycled clothing is still going in the garbage | CBC News

Major retail brands, including H&M, Nike, and Zara, are frequently criticized for misleading sustainability claims, particularly regarding recycled materials. Investigations reveal that products marketed as eco-friendly often contain negligible recycled content or are made primarily from fossil-fuel-based synthetics. This disconnect between marketing narratives and actual material composition suggests that many green labels are superficial tactics designed to capitalize on consumer demand for sustainability rather than reflecting genuine environmental responsibility. The reliance on recycled polyester, often sourced from plastic bottles, is highlighted as a flawed solution that perpetuates a linear consumption model. Critics argue that this approach diverts plastic from closed-loop recycling systems and ultimately leads to landfill disposal, as current textile-to-textile recycling technologies are not scalable enough to handle blended fibers. Consequently, the promise of a circular economy in fashion remains largely unfulfilled, with the industry struggling to implement effective recycling infrastructure that matches the volume of waste generated. These practices exemplify greenwashing by using vague terminology and selective disclosure to distract consumers from the overall environmental impact of garments. Experts warn that such tactics exploit the lack of industry standardization, allowing companies to highlight minor sustainable attributes while ignoring broader issues like excessive waste and carbon emissions. This confusion not only misleads shoppers into paying premiums for less sustainable products but also undermines efforts by genuinely sustainable businesses, highlighting the urgent need for stricter regulations and transparent labeling standards.
