Greens to call News Corp execs to greenwashing inquiry over gas ads

The Australian Greens are demanding News Corp executives testify before a Senate inquiry regarding allegations that the media conglomerate engaged in greenwashing. The controversy stems from a sponsored series of front-page articles promoting natural gas, which were presented as impartial news stories rather than disclosed advertisements. Critics argue that burying the commercial sponsorship deep within the publications while front-loading the promotional content constitutes a deceptive attempt to disguise fossil fuel propaganda as legitimate journalism. Senator Sarah Hanson-Young asserts that this practice undermines journalistic ethics and risks bringing the profession into disrepute. The inquiry highlights how media outlets can leverage their editorial platforms to advance industry interests under the guise of objective reporting. By failing to clearly disclose the financial relationship with gas companies, News Corp is accused of manipulating public perception and misleading readers about the necessity and benefits of fossil fuels, thereby obscuring the true motivations behind the coverage. This case is critically relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how corporate interests can manipulate media narratives to protect their social license. It demonstrates a sophisticated form of greenwashing where sponsored content is stealthily integrated into editorial pages, blurring the lines between advertising and news. Such practices not only deceive consumers but also hinder informed public debate on climate change and energy transition by amplifying fossil fuel interests through trusted news sources, making it harder for the public to discern unbiased information from commercial promotion.
