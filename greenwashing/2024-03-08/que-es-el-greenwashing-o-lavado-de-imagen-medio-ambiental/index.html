¿Qué es el greenwashing o lavado de imagen medio ambiental?

The article points out that “greenwashing” is an organizational strategy in which companies exaggerate, use vague language, or lie about their environmental benefits to improve their reputation, thereby challenging reality and confusing consumers and investors. This practice commonly manifests through misleading labels lacking genuine certifications, distorting public perception of the true sustainability of products offered in the market. The relevance of this phenomenon lies in the current lack of specific legislation prohibiting such practices. Existing regulations are limited to general consumer protection provisions that do not directly address greenwashing. This legal vacuum allows companies to continue employing these deceptive strategies without facing direct consequences, exploiting regulatory ambiguity to maintain an appearance of corporate social responsibility that does not reflect their actual environmental impacts. To combat this situation, a bill is being promoted that seeks to establish mandatory transparency standards, require specific certifications, and impose fines on those who engage in such falsehoods. The approval of this legislation is crucial to ensure that environmental claims are verifiable, eliminating current impunity and ensuring that companies are held truly accountable for their statements, thereby protecting consumers from harmful advertising illusions.
