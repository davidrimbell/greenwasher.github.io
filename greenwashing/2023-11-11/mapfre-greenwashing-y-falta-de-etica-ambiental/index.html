Mapfre: Greenwashing y falta de ética ambiental

Mapfre has been criticized for its inadequate response to climate change, ranking poorly in international assessments of restrictions on fossil fuels. The company has failed to meet its own pledge to reduce insured emissions and continues to provide significant support for oil and gas projects in Latin America, placing it below the European average. This inaction reflects a lack of environmental ethics and climate ambition, calling into question the credibility of its public commitments. The article’s significance lies in exposing the widespread greenwashing within the insurance sector, where most firms continue to finance the expansion of fossil fuel infrastructure despite this contradicting the scientific goals of the Paris Agreement. Although new coal-fired power plants have become largely uninsurable, the transition remains too slow and superficial. The sector has failed to align with climate science, prioritizing profit over the urgency of the global crisis, thereby perpetuating the very problem it claims to combat. Finally, the mass withdrawal of members from the Net Zero Insurance Alliance illustrates the fragility of voluntary commitments in the face of pressure from fossil fuel interests. Many insurers abandoned the initiative under regulatory threats without achieving their actual targets, demonstrating a disconnect between climate rhetoric and concrete actions. The article concludes that, to be truly sustainable, insurers must align their investments with the 1.5°C target and assume legal responsibility for the costs of climate disasters that communities are now facing.
