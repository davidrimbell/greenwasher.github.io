Greenwashing
ES EN

The article highlights how the chocolate industry uses vague marketing terms like “natural” and unverified certification mimics to exploit consumer demand for authenticity, a practice defined as greenwashing. Experts argue that many companies prioritize superficial storytelling and imagery over substantive ethical practices, creating a blurred line between genuine sustainability efforts and deceptive advertising. This trend misleads shoppers who increasingly seek transparency, allowing brands to profit from perceived moral superiority without implementing meaningful environmental or social improvements. In contrast, the fine cacao sector attempts to establish trust through high premiums for quality and direct traceability, yet it still faces challenges due to a lack of standardized regulations. Even craft chocolate producers struggle to define transparency consistently, revealing that voluntary industry standards are insufficient for guaranteeing ethical compliance. This fragmentation underscores the difficulty consumers face in distinguishing between truly sustainable products and those merely appearing responsible through selective data sharing or vague claims. Consequently, new EU legislation aims to ban misleading environmental statements and unverified eco-labels, directly addressing the core of greenwashing. By requiring proof for generic claims and prohibiting offset-based neutrality assertions, the law seeks to eliminate the chaos of unsubstantiated marketing. This regulatory shift is highly relevant to greenwashing as it legally enforces accountability, forcing companies to substantiate their environmental assertions and preventing the use of ambiguous language to manipulate consumer perception.
