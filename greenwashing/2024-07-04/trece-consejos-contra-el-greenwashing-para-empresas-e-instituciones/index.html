Trece consejos contra el ‘greenwashing’ para empresas e instituciones

The article highlights a new EU directive designed to empower consumers by rigorously regulating environmental claims, thereby forcing companies to adopt transparent and verifiable sustainability strategies. This regulatory shift aims to protect the market from unfair practices and deceptive marketing, ensuring that environmental declarations are not only clear but also backed by objective, measurable data. The focus is on establishing a baseline of honesty that prevents businesses from exaggerating their ecological benefits without substantial proof. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the directive’s direct assault on the core of the practice: presenting products as more environmentally friendly than they truly are. By mandating that claims be specific, substantiated, and free from misleading generic terms, the legislation exposes the gap between marketing rhetoric and actual impact. Companies now face significant risks and potential sanctions if they rely on vague language or false certifications, effectively closing loopholes previously exploited to mask unsustainable business models. To navigate this new landscape, the report outlines key recommendations to avoid accusations of greenwashing, such as avoiding unverified "carbon neutral" labels, disclosing durability limitations, and rejecting fraudulent eco-labels. The overarching message is that true sustainability requires a demonstrable commitment and a public execution plan, rather than superficial communication. As regulations evolve, businesses must prioritize genuine action over performative messaging to maintain credibility and comply with increasingly strict legal standards.
