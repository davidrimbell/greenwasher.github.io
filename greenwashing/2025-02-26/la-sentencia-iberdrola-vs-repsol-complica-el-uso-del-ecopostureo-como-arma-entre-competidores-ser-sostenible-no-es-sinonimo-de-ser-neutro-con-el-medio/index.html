La sentencia Iberdrola vs. Repsol complica el uso del ecopostureo como arma entre competidores: "Ser sostenible no es sinónimo de ser neutro con el medioambiente"

The landmark ruling against Iberdrola’s lawsuit establishes a crucial precedent that shields corporate websites from being classified as direct commercial advertising. By distinguishing between content aimed at investors rather than consumers, the court limits the ability of companies to use competition law against competitors’ sustainability claims. This decision provides significant legal protection for multinationals, making it much harder to litigate greenwashing accusations based solely on statements found in corporate communication channels. The judge’s interpretation defines sustainability as a balance between ecological, economic, and social dimensions, recognizing that companies have the right to pursue environmental goals without immediately compromising economic viability. This nuanced view implies that long-term carbon reduction targets cannot be easily weaponized as evidence of unfair competition, especially when the average consumer is already aware of the company’s traditional business model. Consequently, claims of deception become difficult to prove when the public understands the inherent nature of an industry. This ruling is highly relevant to the greenwashing debate as it creates a safe harbor for corporations to communicate their environmental strategies without fear of immediate legal retaliation from rivals. Since many companies lack detailed, quantitative proof for their transition plans, this judicial protection encourages continued broad sustainability messaging. It signals that while environmental commitments are expected, the threshold for proving that such communications mislead consumers or constitute unfair competition is currently set very high, thereby reducing the legal risks associated with strategic environmental communication.
