PFAS Greenwashing: Company On Company Litigation Strikes

The lawsuit Group SEB v. Caraway represents a landmark shift in environmental litigation by being the first instance where a manufacturer sues a competitor for PFAS-related greenwashing, rather than citizens suing corporations. SEB, which admits to using PTFE in its cookware, alleges that Caraway’s aggressive marketing portraying PTFE products as toxic constitutes false advertising and unfair competition. This unique dynamic forces the court to address contested scientific claims regarding the safety of PTFE, establishing a new precedent where industry peers police each other’s environmental claims rather than external stakeholders. This case highlights the critical legal risks companies face when targeting competitors in their marketing strategies, particularly regarding hazardous substances. By claiming rival products are harmful while asserting their own are safe, companies risk crossing the line from acceptable "puffery" into misleading statements that trigger civil liability under federal and state laws. The dispute underscores that defining the boundaries of safe usage and marketing truthfulness is no longer straightforward, requiring rigorous internal review to avoid litigation from rivals who feel unfairly maligned by specific comparative claims. The relevance to greenwashing lies in its potential to reshape how consumer goods companies manage reputational risk and marketing compliance. It demonstrates that greenwashing is not solely a consumer protection issue but also a competitive weapon, necessitating multidisciplinary oversight involving legal and compliance teams alongside marketing. Ultimately, the court’s determination on the safety of PTFE will set a crucial benchmark for future litigation, influencing how broadly companies can assert the safety of their materials while criticizing competitors’ environmental footprints.
