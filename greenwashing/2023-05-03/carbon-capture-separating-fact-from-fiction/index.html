Carbon capture: Separating fact from fiction

Carbon capture: Separating fact from fiction

The oil and gas industry promotes carbon capture as a vital path to net-zero emissions, securing billions in government subsidies to fund this technology. However, experts argue this approach functions primarily as greenwashing, allowing fossil fuel companies to continue extraction under the guise of environmental responsibility rather than genuinely reducing their carbon footprint. Critics highlight that these projects are financially inefficient and technologically unreliable, with numerous government-backed initiatives failing to deliver results. Rather than serving as a legitimate climate solution, carbon capture is effectively acting as an aid for sustained oil production, enabling companies to externalize the costs of their operations onto taxpayers while maintaining business as usual. This narrative is crucial to understanding greenwashing because it reveals how the industry repackages harmful practices as sustainable innovations. By leveraging public funds and misleading marketing, fossil fuel corporations distract from the urgent need to phase out extraction, exposing a systemic effort to preserve outdated habits while appearing compliant with modern climate goals.
