The author condemns the New England Clean Energy Connect for greenwashing, arguing it falsely markets itself as environmentally beneficial. Despite claims of reducing emissions, the developer avoided verification and successfully lobbied against prior accountability legislation. This secrecy suggests the project’s actual climate impact is negligible or harmful, undermining public trust. Such deception highlights how corporate interests manipulate environmental narratives to justify infrastructure projects. The lack of transparency exposes the gap between marketing claims and verified ecological outcomes. This pattern reveals how greenwashing serves as a strategic tool to bypass rigorous scrutiny and secure approval through misleading narratives. The article remains relevant as it illustrates the urgent need for mandatory post-implementation assessments. By demanding comprehensive greenhouse gas analysis, the proposed bill seeks to restore accountability. It challenges stakeholders to verify claims rather than accept unproven environmental benefits, combating the widespread practice of greenwashing in energy sectors.
