Climate disinformation a ‘public health threat’, warns new WHO report

Health organizations are urging global leaders to classify climate disinformation as a critical public health threat, emphasizing that intentional misinformation severely undermines emergency responses and policy implementation. By sowing doubt, vested interests delay essential actions on air pollution and clean energy, directly jeopardizing human health and compromising the integrity of disaster preparedness. This shift in perspective frames a healthier information environment not as a communication luxury, but as a fundamental condition for achieving climate justice and protecting vulnerable populations from immediate physical harm. The report highlights how disinformation exploits existing inequalities and power structures, transforming necessary public health interventions into polarized culture-war issues. For instance, misleading narratives have obstructed access to disaster aid and weakened support for clean air policies, allowing fossil fuel interests to mask their influence through greenwashing. These tactics distract from scientific evidence, creating confusion that discourages evacuation and reduces public trust in health guidance, ultimately exacerbating the impact of environmental crises on communities already facing systemic disadvantages. Addressing this challenge requires more than just improving public messaging; it demands robust structural safeguards against conflicts of interest and the weaponization of information. Health institutions must integrate tools to detect and counter disinformation into their core frameworks, recognizing that the spread of false narratives is often driven by powerful incentives. Consequently, the article underscores that combating climate disinformation is inseparable from fighting greenwashing, as both rely on obscuring truth to protect harmful industries and maintain the status quo against urgent climate action.
