Week in review: Can US afford to fall behind EU on green fashion legislation?

The article highlights a growing legislative divide between the EU and the US regarding sustainable fashion, emphasizing that the EU is aggressively moving toward stricter environmental standards while the US remains lagging. By proposing bans on generic green claims and implementing extended producer responsibility frameworks, the EU is actively curbing misleading environmental marketing. This regulatory push directly targets the core mechanisms of greenwashing by forcing transparency and holding brands accountable for the full lifecycle of their products. In stark contrast, the US has stalled similar initiatives, such as California’s Responsible Textile Recovery Act, due to delays and stakeholder negotiations. This hesitation leaves a regulatory vacuum where manufacturers can potentially continue using vague environmental claims without rigorous oversight or financial responsibility for waste. The absence of comprehensive federal or state-level mandates in the US allows for continued ambiguity in sustainability reporting, which facilitates deceptive marketing practices that would be prohibited under EU guidelines. This disparity is critical to understanding global greenwashing because it creates an uneven playing field for international fashion brands. Companies operating in both jurisdictions may adopt EU-compliant standards only where legally required, potentially using the lack of US regulation to maintain looser marketing practices domestically. Consequently, consumers in the US face higher risks of encountering unverified environmental claims, illustrating how regulatory fragmentation enables and perpetuates greenwashing across borders.
