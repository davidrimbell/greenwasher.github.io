Conference on Baku’s ‘Greenwashing and Ethnic Cleansing’ Held in European Parliament

The article exposes Azerbaijan’s attempt to use the COP29 Climate Summit to mask severe human rights violations, specifically the ethnic cleansing of Artsakh. By hosting a major environmental forum in an authoritarian state with a poor human rights record, Azerbaijan seeks to create a facade of legitimacy. This disconnect highlights the core mechanism of greenwashing, where environmental prestige is leveraged to divert attention from oppressive political actions and humanitarian abuses. Speakers at the European Parliament conference emphasized that environmental debates must not serve as a cover-up for atrocities. They argued that participating in such events without addressing the detention of Armenian prisoners normalizes these violations. The narrative underscores how climate summits can be manipulated by regimes to improve their international image while simultaneously silencing dissent and committing crimes against minority populations. This situation is relevant to greenwashing as it illustrates the exploitation of global ecological platforms for political rehabilitation. It demonstrates how authoritarian governments utilize high-profile environmental meetings to obscure their track records and evade accountability. The conference serves as a critical intervention, urging the international community to recognize that environmental cooperation cannot exist independently of human rights protections, thereby revealing the deceptive nature of state-sponsored environmental diplomacy.
