Nine in 10 UK marketers want global guidelines on avoiding greenwashing - edie

A recent survey reveals a growing tension among marketers who recognize their companies’ ESG shortcomings yet fear being accused of greenwashing. This anxiety leads many leadership teams to withhold sustainability communications, a phenomenon known as "greenhushing." Consequently, businesses are increasingly reluctant to share their environmental progress, not because they lack genuine efforts, but due to the perceived risk of criticism for potential inaccuracies or exaggerations in their messaging. This defensive posture has evolved into "greensquashing," where companies not only limit communications but also scale back their actual environmental ambitions. High-profile brand rollbacks illustrate this trend, driven by fears of greenwashing accusations, economic pressures, and shifting consumer priorities. The result is a crisis of confidence where genuine sustainability progress is masked, undermining transparency and hindering the broader transition to more sustainable business practices. This article is crucial for understanding greenwashing because it highlights how the fear of being labeled a greenwasher can paradoxically silence legitimate sustainability efforts. It underscores the urgent need for clear, professional guidelines to distinguish between deceptive marketing and honest reporting. Without standardized frameworks to validate claims, the market risks stagnation, where companies prioritize self-protection over meaningful environmental action, thereby weakening the overall integrity of corporate sustainability discourse.
