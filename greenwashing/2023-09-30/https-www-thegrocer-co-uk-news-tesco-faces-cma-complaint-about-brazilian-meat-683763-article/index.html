Greenwashing
ES EN

Campaigners argue that Tesco’s sale of Brazilian meat and its reliance on suppliers linked to illegal Amazon deforestation violate its anti-deforestation pledges and the Green Claims Code. This highlights how indirect supply chain complexities can undermine direct retailer commitments, exposing gaps between stated environmental goals and actual sourcing practices. The incident underscores the difficulty of verifying claims deep within global food networks, where branded products may obscure the origins of ingredients like soy used in animal feed. Such discrepancies illustrate how greenwashing can occur not through deliberate falsehoods, but through insufficient oversight of third-party suppliers, potentially misleading consumers who trust the retailer’s broader sustainability narrative. Relevant to greenwashing discussions, this case demonstrates the critical need for transparent, verified data across entire value chains. It warns that without rigorous monitoring and public accountability, major retailers risk eroding trust and facing regulatory scrutiny for making unverified net-zero and deforestation-free assertions.
