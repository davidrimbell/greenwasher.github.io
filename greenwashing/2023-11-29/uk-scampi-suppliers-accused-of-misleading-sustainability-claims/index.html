UK scampi suppliers accused of “misleading” sustainability claims

UK scampi suppliers accused of “misleading” sustainability claims

Charity Open Seas accuses major UK retailers and seafood companies of greenwashing by marketing langoustines as responsibly sourced. These products originate from a Fishery Improvement Project that failed to address critical environmental issues, such as habitat damage and threats to protected species, within its planned timeframe. The initiative’s stagnation suggests that the sustainability claims promoted by these brands are misleading customers who believe their purchases support genuine ecological conservation. The failure of this management scheme highlights a disconnect between corporate sustainability messaging and actual on-the-ground practices. Despite being located in a well-documented region, the fishery did not meet basic management standards, rendering the improvements insufficient for a developed country. This lack of progress undermines the credibility of the certifications used by supermarkets to justify higher prices or premium positioning to environmentally conscious consumers. This case is relevant to greenwashing because it demonstrates how companies leverage incomplete or failing improvement projects to imply rigorous environmental stewardship. By associating their products with a named initiative, retailers create a perception of responsibility without ensuring meaningful change. Consequently, consumers are deceived into supporting industries that continue to harm marine ecosystems, exposing the gap between marketing narratives and tangible ecological impact.
