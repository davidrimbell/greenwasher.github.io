Greenwashing
ES EN

The article examines the first commercial transatlantic flight using sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) as a emblematic case of greenwashing in the aviation industry. Climate advocates argue that this initiative is a distraction that diverts attention from the urgent need to reduce the total number of flights, presenting a technological solution that fails to address the root cause of the environmental problem. The central criticism hinges on the unviability of scaling SAF production to significant levels. Due to the limited availability of feedstocks such as waste fats, SAF represents an insignificant fraction of global fuel consumption and cannot meet current aviation demand. Experts point out that relying on this substitute is a technological dead end that prevents achieving the emission reductions necessary to avoid climate collapse. The text’s relevance to greenwashing lies in how the industry leverages symbolic events, such as Virgin Atlantic’s flight, to create a false perception of sustainability. By promoting SAF as the sole pathway to decarbonize the sector, airlines and associated companies justify maintaining an unsustainable growth model, deceiving consumers and the public while avoiding more effective measures, such as reducing the volume of travel.
