Greenwashing
EN ES

The article highlights the pervasive issue of greenwashing, where companies misleadingly market products as environmentally friendly using vague terminology and unverified claims. This lack of transparency has eroded consumer trust, creating confusion in the marketplace as shoppers struggle to distinguish genuine sustainability efforts from superficial marketing tactics. The resulting skepticism underscores the urgent need for stricter regulatory frameworks to protect buyers and ensure environmental integrity. To address these abuses, the European Union is implementing a new directive that mandates specific, verifiable, and substantiated environmental claims. By banning generic terms like "eco" or "green" without proof, the regulations require companies to demonstrate the overall environmental friendliness of their products. This shift aims to establish a standardized system where ecological assertions are backed by rigorous evidence and independent verification, similar to the established EU Ecolabel model, thereby reducing the prevalence of fraudulent advertising. This regulatory evolution is highly relevant to the study of greenwashing as it illustrates the transition from self-regulation to enforced accountability. The article demonstrates how independent verification and third-party certifications serve as effective countermeasures against deceptive practices. By forcing companies to prove their sustainability credentials, the EU’s approach directly combats the opacity that facilitates greenwashing, ensuring that environmental labels become reliable tools for informed consumer choice rather than mere marketing devices.
