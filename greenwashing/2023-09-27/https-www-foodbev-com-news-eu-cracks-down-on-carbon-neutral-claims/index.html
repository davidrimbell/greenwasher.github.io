Greenwashing
ES EN

This new EU legislation directly targets the practice of greenwashing by banning unsubstantiated environmental claims, particularly those regarding carbon neutrality, starting in 2026. By prohibiting generic terms like "climate neutral" or "biodegradable" without proof of excellent environmental performance, the law aims to eliminate the ambiguity that allows companies to mislead consumers. This regulatory shift forces businesses to substantiate their sustainability efforts with rigorous evidence rather than relying on vague marketing jargon, thereby restoring trust in eco-friendly product labeling. The article highlights the specific controversy surrounding carbon offsetting schemes, which critics argue act as a smoke screen for insufficient internal emission reductions. By challenging the validity of "carbon neutral" products, such as food items or packaging, the legislation exposes the reality that true neutrality is often unattainable for complex manufacturing processes. This focus on offsetting reveals how companies can appear environmentally responsible while avoiding costly operational changes, effectively deterring genuine ambition under the guise of purchasing credits. Ultimately, these rules are relevant because they seek to correct the market imbalance where greenwashing distracts from actual climate action. By requiring accurate methodologies and transparency, the law empowers consumers to distinguish between meaningful environmental stewardship and deceptive claims. The proposed ban on offset-based neutrality claims challenges major industry players to justify their strategies, signaling a critical step toward holding corporations accountable for their actual carbon footprint rather than their promotional narratives.
