Apple sued over ‘carbon neutral’ claim for watches

Apple sued over ‘carbon neutral’ claim for watches

Apple faces a lawsuit accusing it of misleading consumers by marketing Apple Watches as "carbon neutral." Plaintiffs argue these claims are false because the relied-upon carbon offset projects failed to deliver genuine reductions, as the environmental benefits would have occurred regardless of Apple’s involvement. This case highlights a critical greenwashing risk: using offsets for projects that lack additionality. If companies claim neutrality based on reductions that were already happening, they deceive buyers about their true environmental impact. This undermines trust in sustainability labels and suggests that carbon offsetting can be manipulated to mask actual pollution rather than mitigate it. Relevance to greenwashing lies in the disparity between marketing narratives and operational reality. By potentially relying on invalid offsets, Apple may be engaging in deceptive environmental advertising. This lawsuit emphasizes the need for rigorous verification of carbon claims, ensuring that "neutral" labels reflect real, additional emissions cuts rather than misleading financial transactions.
