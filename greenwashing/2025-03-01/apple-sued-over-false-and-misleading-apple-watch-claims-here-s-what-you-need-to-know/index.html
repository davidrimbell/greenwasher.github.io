Apple sued over 'false and misleading' Apple Watch claims – here's what you need to know

Apple faces a class action lawsuit alleging that its marketing of the Watch Series 9, Ultra 2, and SE 2 as "carbon neutral" is false and misleading. Plaintiffs argue that these claims deceive consumers, who may have avoided purchasing or paying premium prices for the devices had they known the truth. The suit seeks to hold the company accountable for the alleged economic injury caused by these deceptive environmental representations. The core dispute centers on the validity of the carbon offset projects Apple uses to compensate for remaining emissions, specifically initiatives in Kenya and China. The lawsuit contends that these projects lack legitimacy because the claimed carbon reductions were inevitable due to existing legal protections or pre-existing forest cover. Consequently, plaintiffs assert that the offsets do not represent genuine, additional environmental benefits, rendering the "carbon neutral" designation fraudulent. This case is highly relevant to greenwashing as it highlights the critical vulnerability of corporate net-zero claims relying on nature-based offsets. It underscores the broader industry debate regarding the integrity of carbon credit programs, suggesting that offsets often fail to deliver verified emission reductions. The lawsuit exposes how companies may mask unavoidable manufacturing emissions with questionable compensation schemes, a practice that undermines transparency and potentially misleads eco-conscious consumers about a product’s actual environmental impact.
