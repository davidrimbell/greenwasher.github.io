Spoof ad promotes fake mascot ‘Scrubby Greenwash’ to LNG execs

Spoof ad promotes fake mascot ‘Scrubby Greenwash’ to LNG execs

This article details a satirical advertisement created by the activist collective Yes Men to expose the maritime industry’s aggressive greenwashing of liquefied natural gas (LNG). The prank involved placing a fake ad for a fictional marketing agency, GreenCurrent Group, in a trade journal. The ad humorously suggested that instead of addressing the environmental dangers of LNG, companies could simply hire a mascot to scrub away "sad facts," thereby protecting their reputations and profits through misleading marketing rather than genuine energy efficiency or fuel phase-outs. The central implication is that the shipping and cruise sectors are prioritizing image management over climate responsibility, relying on a "dirty smokescreen" of propaganda to convince the public that LNG is a clean solution. By highlighting the industry's willingness to publish such pro-LNG content uncritically, the piece underscores how trade media facilitates the spread of false narratives about fossil fuels. This strategy allows corporations to mitigate reputational risks associated with methane leakage and global warming contributions without making substantive changes to their operations. The relevance to greenwashing lies in its exposure of the disconnect between corporate claims of environmental stewardship and the reality of their fossil fuel investments. Activists argue that such marketing tactics delay meaningful climate action by normalizing LNG as a "clean" alternative, despite scientific evidence to the contrary. The stunt serves as a critique of the creative industry’s role in enabling these lies, illustrating how profit-driven narratives actively obscure the environmental crises caused by expanding fossil fuel infrastructure.
