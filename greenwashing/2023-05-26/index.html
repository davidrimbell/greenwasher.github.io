Greenwashing: energy companies make false claims about sustainability – they should be held to account
This article reveals that many corporations treat Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) merely as a reactive tool to satisfy regulations and stakeholder pressure, rather than a core strategic commitment. By employing ambiguous language and misleading marketing, these entities construct an environmentally responsible facade that masks their continued engagement in harmful practices. This selective disclosure allows them to gain legitimacy and social acceptance while avoiding substantive changes to their business models, effectively decoupling their public image from their actual environmental impact. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the systematic deception inherent in these strategies, where companies publicly claim alignment with global sustainability goals while internally prioritizing fossil fuel exploration and expansion. This discrepancy not only manipulates consumer perception but also actively blocks genuine green transitions by establishing superficial norms. The text highlights how major industries, particularly energy, use sophisticated euphemisms to obscure their carbon footprints, thereby eroding public trust and hindering the collective effort to achieve necessary climate targets through honest accountability. However, the article argues that greenwashing scandals can serve as catalysts for positive transformation when civil society and regulators enforce strict scrutiny. Public exposure and legal pressure can force companies to rethink their strategies, leading to genuine investments in sustainable technologies and improved corporate behavior. Ultimately, combating this deception requires robust transparency, effective regulation, and a proactive societal demand for authenticity, ensuring that CSR evolves from a marketing tactic into a fundamental driver of real environmental stewardship.

Most of Chevron’s carbon offsets are "worthless" or worse, report says
The article concludes that Chevron’s net-zero strategy is fundamentally flawed because it relies on ineffective carbon offsets and carbon capture while ignoring the vast majority of its downstream emissions. This approach allows the company to maintain "business as usual" operations and expand fossil fuel production under the guise of climate responsibility. Experts argue that such frameworks are designed to delay meaningful action rather than achieve genuine decarbonization, effectively undermining efforts to limit global warming to safe levels. A primary mechanism for this deception is the heavy reliance on low-integrity carbon credits, particularly from large hydropower dams and forestry projects that fail to deliver additional emissions reductions. These offsets are often criticized for causing social harm and environmental damage, yet they provide a false sense of progress for corporations. By trading illusory certificates instead of cutting actual pollution, Chevron masks its massive footprint, which dwarfs the emissions of many nations, thereby obscuring the scale of its continued environmental impact. This case is highly relevant to greenwashing as it exemplifies how fossil fuel giants use marketing and complex accounting schemes to appear sustainable while actively lobbying against climate regulations. The discrepancy between Chevron’s public green claims and its minimal investment in low-carbon technologies highlights a systematic pattern of corporate dishonesty. Ultimately, the report demonstrates that without abandoning the expansion of fossil fuel infrastructure, corporate net-zero pledges serve primarily as public relations tools to obstruct necessary climate justice.
