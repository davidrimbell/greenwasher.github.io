Greenwashing
ES EN

Regulatory bodies are intensifying scrutiny on corporate environmental claims to prevent misleading consumers and ensure businesses contribute to national net-zero targets. Recent enforcement actions against major oil, gas, and airline firms demonstrate that broad, unqualified assertions about green transitions are unacceptable when they contradict the reality of high-carbon operations. This strict approach aims to curb greenwashing by requiring that advertising accurately reflects a company’s actual decarbonization progress rather than just its future aspirations. The article argues against the false binary choice between greenwashing and greenhushing, suggesting that transparency is a viable middle ground. Companies can legally communicate their sustainability efforts if they provide precision, include appropriate qualifications, and offer a balanced view that acknowledges current limitations. By explicitly stating where they are on their sustainability journey and highlighting specific, supported actions, businesses can share their credentials without deceiving the public or inviting regulatory intervention. This guidance is crucial for understanding modern greenwashing because it defines how regulators distinguish between irresponsible exaggeration and honest corporate communication. It emphasizes that accuracy and context are key; claims must be clear to consumers with low environmental knowledge and must not hide the negative impacts of core business activities. Ultimately, the piece advocates for "greenspeaking" with confidence through rigorous honesty, precise language, and comprehensive disclosure, establishing a clear standard for legitimate environmental marketing.
