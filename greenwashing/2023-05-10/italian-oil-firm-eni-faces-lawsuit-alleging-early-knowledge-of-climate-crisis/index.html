Italian oil firm Eni faces lawsuit alleging early knowledge of climate crisis

This article details Italy’s first climate lawsuit against Eni, accusing the oil major of greenwashing and misleading lobbying to promote fossil fuels despite understanding their climate risks decades ago. The core of the legal action rests on internal historical reports proving that Eni’s own scientists accurately predicted the catastrophic consequences of rising carbon dioxide levels as early as the 1970s. Yet, simultaneously, the company advertised natural gas as a clean solution, demonstrating a deliberate pattern of concealing scientific truths to protect market interests. The relevance to greenwashing is profound, as this case exemplifies the strategic disinformation tactics used by fossil fuel companies to maintain public trust while expanding production. By highlighting the discrepancy between internal knowledge of climate dangers and external marketing campaigns, the lawsuit exposes how "clean" labels are often employed to deflect scrutiny and delay regulatory action. This mirrors global trends where oil giants are held accountable for prioritizing profit over transparency, turning corporate sustainability claims into tools for deception rather than genuine environmental stewardship. Ultimately, the lawsuit aims to force Eni to slash its carbon emissions significantly, leveraging similar successful precedents set against Royal Dutch Shell in the Netherlands. By including government entities that hold stakes in the company, the case seeks to establish legal accountability for both corporate deception and state complicity. This development signals a growing judicial scrutiny of the fossil fuel industry’s historical and current practices, challenging the narrative that oil companies acted responsibly or without foresight regarding the climate crisis.
