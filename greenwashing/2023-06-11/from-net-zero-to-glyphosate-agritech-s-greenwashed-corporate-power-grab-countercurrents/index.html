From Net Zero To Glyphosate: Agritech's Greenwashed Corporate Power Grab | Countercurrents

The article argues that the mainstream narrative of a “sustainable” food transition is a sophisticated form of greenwashing, designed to protect the profits of agribusiness and big tech corporations. By framing industrial agriculture as the solution to the climate crisis, these entities deflect attention from their historical role in ecological degradation and social inequality. Instead of fostering genuine sustainability, this corporate-led initiative perpetuates a colonial power dynamic, marginalizing small farmers and indigenous practices while reinforcing global economic imances rooted in imperialist histories. Central to this deception is the commodification of carbon and data through so-called “carbon farming.” Corporations use digital platforms to monitor farmers, dictating inputs and practices in exchange for carbon credits that are often fraudulent or ineffective. This system allows polluters to continue harmful operations while selling imaginary environmental benefits to consumers. The reliance on proprietary technology and genetically modified seeds traps farmers in dependency, turning them into data-generating laborers for distant financial interests rather than empowering them as autonomous stewards of the land. This relevance to greenwashing lies in how it uses the urgent language of climate action to mask a profit-driven agenda. The article exposes how “net-zero” claims are frequently based on phantom credits that fail to deliver actual emissions reductions, creating a Ponzi-like scheme that benefits corporate traders over the planet. True environmentalism requires rejecting these techno-fixes in favor of agroecological, locally controlled food systems that prioritize soil health, food sovereignty, and community resilience over corporate expansion and financial speculation.
