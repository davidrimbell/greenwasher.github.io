Greenwashing
EN ES

The author illustrates how corporate sustainability campaigns often function as sophisticated greenwashing tools that mask severe environmental and social harms. By juxtaposing past industrial denialism with modern advertising practices, the narrative reveals how companies use vague promises and weak certifications to create a false sense of progress. This approach allows corporations to maintain lucrative supply chains involving child labor and ecological destruction while presenting a sanitized, responsible image to consumers. The central conflict arises when industry professionals attempt to expose these discrepancies. The author’s experience highlights a systemic intolerance for truth within the advertising sector, where questioning the validity of sustainability claims leads to professional retaliation. This demonstrates that the issue is not merely ignorance, but a deliberate strategy of denial, reinforced by economic incentives that reward complicity and punish those who demand accountability. This story is critically relevant to greenwashing because it exposes the structural mechanisms that enable it. It shows how third-party labels are exploited for plausible deniability and how creative agencies act as facilitators of misleading narratives rather than truth-tellers. By risking their careers to speak out, whistleblowers highlight the urgent need for transparency, proving that without challenging these "polished distortions," the industry continues to legitimize harmful practices under the guise of corporate responsibility.
