Ethical eating or greenwashing? These healthy foods are actually unsustainable

The article argues that popular plant-based milk alternatives often mislead consumers regarding their environmental and health impacts, serving as a prime example of greenwashing where marketing outpaces reality. It challenges the assumption that oat milk is a uniformly sustainable choice, revealing that mass production has led to poor agricultural standards and high sugar content, while some supply chains involve significant transport emissions. Similarly, almond milk is exposed as an ecologically damaging option due to excessive water usage and soil degradation, despite its health benefits. Coconut milk presents a different facade of sustainability. Although its production utilizes nearly the entire fruit with minimal waste, the necessary airfreighting from tropical regions creates substantial greenhouse gas emissions. Furthermore, the expansion of coconut farming is linked to deforestation and biodiversity loss in rainforests, directly contradicting the eco-friendly image many consumers associate with the product. This analysis is relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how specific attributes, such as being plant-based or organic, are used to create a "natural" halo effect. By focusing on single positive metrics like water efficiency for oats or total fruit utilization for coconuts, brands obscure complex negative impacts like carbon-intensive logistics and habitat destruction. Consumers are thereby misled into believing their choices are ethically superior when the broader lifecycle assessments reveal significant ecological and health compromises.
