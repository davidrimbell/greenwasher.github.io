Greenwashing
ES EN

Major food industry players are withdrawing from the voluntary carbon market to focus exclusively on their own supply chains, claiming to avoid greenwashing risks. This shift, however, risks fostering "greenhushing," where companies withhold transparency about broader climate strategies to evade scrutiny. By abandoning carbon credits, firms ignore a critical tool for offsetting unavoidable emissions, potentially slowing the urgent global decarbonization effort needed to limit temperature rises. The article argues that this mindset is dangerously narrow, as the carbon market remains essential for achieving net zero targets. Failing to act now exposes companies to severe operational and financial risks, including supply chain disruptions and facility damage caused by climate change. Research indicates that businesses investing in carbon credits are often decarbonizing at faster rates than those that do not, suggesting that credits and supply chain reductions should be complementary strategies rather than mutually exclusive choices. Relevance to greenwashing lies in the fear of accusations driving inaction. To combat this, companies must prioritize high-integrity projects, such as forest conservation, with rigorous due diligence. The core message is that transparency and the use of all available climate tools are vital; hiding behind minimal compliance or avoiding carbon markets due to reputation concerns ultimately undermines collective efforts to address the climate crisis.
