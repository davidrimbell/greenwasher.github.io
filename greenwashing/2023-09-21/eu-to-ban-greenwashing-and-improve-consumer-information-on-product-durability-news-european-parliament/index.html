EU to ban greenwashing and improve consumer information on product durability | News | European Parliament

The European Union has finalized a crucial update to its consumer protection laws specifically targeting greenwashing and planned obsolescence. By prohibiting vague environmental claims like "eco-friendly" without rigorous proof, the legislation aims to eliminate misleading marketing that tricks consumers into believing products are more sustainable than they actually are. This direct regulatory action prevents companies from using ambiguous terminology to create a false impression of environmental responsibility. Furthermore, the new rules ban claims relying on carbon offsetting schemes and prohibit the manipulation of product durability through software updates or unnecessary consumable replacements. These measures address the critical issue of early obsolescence, ensuring that goods are not marketed as repairable or long-lasting when they are fundamentally designed to fail or require premature replacement. This transparency is vital for combating the deceptive practice of hiding poor product lifespan behind complex commercial communications. Finally, the initiative introduces a harmonized label to highlight extended warranties, making legal guarantee information more visible and encouraging the purchase of durable goods. This directive is highly relevant to greenwashing discussions as it establishes a stricter legal framework for substantiating environmental claims, thereby reducing market chaos and empowering consumers to make informed, truthful purchasing decisions rather than falling victim to superficial sustainability narratives.
