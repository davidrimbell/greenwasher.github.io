Greenwashing
ES EN

The rise of influencer marketing presents a complex challenge for sustainable branding, as creators increasingly desire to promote eco-friendly products but are hindered by significant barriers. The primary obstacle is the fear of greenwashing, where misleading environmental claims damage credibility. Many influencers lack the education or confidence to accurately discuss sustainability, leading them to avoid the topic entirely despite strong consumer interest in their recommendations. Consumer sentiment underscores the high stakes involved, with a majority willing to boycott brands found to have made deceptive sustainability claims. This backlash reflects a broader trend where regulators and shoppers alike are demanding transparency and evidence-based messaging. The discrepancy between consumer awareness and actual knowledge of sustainability labels creates further confusion, making it difficult for influencers to guide purchasing decisions effectively without risking reputational harm. This dynamic is critically relevant to greenwashing because it highlights the systemic risk of unsubstantiated claims spreading through digital channels. To combat this, Unilever and other partners are forming coalitions to provide creators with scientifically backed guidelines and resources. By equipping influencers with accurate information, brands can ensure that marketing efforts drive genuine sustainable behavior rather than exploiting vague "green" narratives, thereby protecting both consumer trust and regulatory compliance.
