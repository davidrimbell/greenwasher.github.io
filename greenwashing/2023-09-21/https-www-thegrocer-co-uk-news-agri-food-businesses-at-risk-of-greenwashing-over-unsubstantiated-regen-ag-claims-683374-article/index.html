Greenwashing
ES EN

Research by the FAIRR network reveals a critical disconnect in the agri-food sector, where a majority of companies promote regenerative agriculture yet lack formal quantitative targets to support these claims. This gap between marketing promises and concrete action highlights a systemic failure to translate sustainability ambitions into measurable progress, leaving significant portions of the industry unprepared for the realities of environmental accountability. The absence of an internationally agreed definition for regenerative agriculture further exacerbates the situation, creating ambiguity that allows for vague and unsubstantiated assertions. Without standardized metrics or specific financial commitments to incentivize farmers, companies risk making claims that cannot be rigorously verified, thereby undermining the credibility of their sustainability narratives and exposing them to increased scrutiny from regulators and investors alike. This situation is highly relevant to greenwashing discussions as new regulations, such as the EU Green Claims Directive, mandate that environmental claims must be fully substantiated or face severe penalties. The discrepancy between promotional rhetoric and verifiable data exemplifies greenwashing, as firms may be misleading consumers about their environmental impact. Consequently, this study underscores the urgent need for transparency and tangible proof of ecological improvement to comply with upcoming legal frameworks and avoid reputational damage.
