Greenwashing, la mentira que puede enterrar la credibilidad de tu marca

The article traces the evolution of corporate environmental responsibility from superficial remediation efforts, termed “responsibility 1.0,” to a holistic approach in which sustainability is embedded in the core business model. It emphasizes that authentic sustainability differs fundamentally from greenwashing, which occurs when companies exploit ecological concerns for commercial gain while their actual practices contradict their public discourse. This distinction is critical because consumers are increasingly sophisticated and vigilant, demanding transparency and genuine social impact rather than mere marketing narratives. Greenwashing poses a severe threat to brand viability, primarily leading to a loss of credibility and reputational crises. When companies are exposed for insincerity, they face diminished sales and market share, as modern consumers scrutinize their actions with an investigative lens. The text highlights that while public awareness of environmental issues has grown significantly, many still struggle to verify product sustainability due to misleading labeling or complex supply chains, creating an environment where deceptive practices are both risky and easily detected by informed buyers. This relevance to greenwashing lies in the shifting power dynamic between corporations and consumers. With increased access to information and regulatory improvements such as new labeling standards, companies can no longer rely on vague environmental claims. Instead, they must align their commercial activities with genuine ecological benefits to maintain trust. The article concludes that true sustainability is not just an ethical imperative but a strategic business investment, as dishonesty quickly erodes consumer confidence in an era where authenticity is the ultimate currency.
