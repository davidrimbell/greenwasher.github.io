Organisations counter greenwashing of ‘monstrous’ Comino development - The Malta Independent

Seven environmental organizations staged a counter-exhibition to challenge Hili Group’s claims that their proposed redevelopment of the Comino Hotel site is sustainable. Critics argue the developer manipulates figures to obscure the severe ecological damage, noting that the project would significantly increase building volume and footprint on untouched land. By highlighting discrepancies between the marketed vision and the physical reality, activists assert that the development threatens the area’s biodiversity and tranquility, proving that true sustainability would require not building at all. The initiative exposes how corporate entities exploit natural heritage for profit, prioritizing elite tourism over public access and environmental preservation. The involvement of a luxury hotel group raises serious concerns about exclusivity, effectively pricing out local residents and transforming a peaceful sanctuary into an exclusive enclave. This dynamic illustrates the broader social costs of such projects, where communal spaces are commodified and removed from the general public, undermining the notion of shared natural heritage. This case is highly relevant to greenwashing as it demonstrates how developers use deceptive marketing to present destructive projects as eco-friendly initiatives. By obscuring the true scale of environmental impact and social exclusion behind a veneer of sustainability, the company attempts to legitimize harmful development. Recognizing these tactics is crucial for identifying when "green" labels are merely superficial tools designed to deflect criticism and secure approval for projects that fundamentally contradict ecological and social well-being.
