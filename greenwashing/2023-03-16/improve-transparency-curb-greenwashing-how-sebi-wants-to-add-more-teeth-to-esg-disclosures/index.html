Improve transparency, curb greenwashing: how Sebi wants to add more teeth to ESG disclosures

Improve transparency, curb greenwashing: how Sebi wants to add more teeth to ESG disclosures

The article highlights the Securities and Exchange Board of India’s initiative to mandate the Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report (BRSR) for the top thousand listed companies, aiming to standardize Environmental, Social, and Governance disclosures. By making these reports compulsory, the regulator seeks to establish a uniform framework that reduces ambiguity in corporate sustainability claims. This structural shift is designed to enhance the reliability of ESG data, ensuring that companies adhere to a consistent set of reporting guidelines rather than relying on voluntary or disparate standards. Central to this regulatory strategy is the emphasis on third-party assurance of sustainability reports to build market credibility. The proposal identifies assurance as the critical mechanism for validating the accuracy of ESG ratings and disclosures. By introducing rigorous verification processes, the framework intends to mitigate the risk of misleading information, thereby forcing companies to substantiate their environmental and social impacts with auditable evidence rather than unverified statements. This development is highly relevant to greenwashing as it directly addresses the core issue of unverified claims. By enforcing mandatory reporting and requiring assurance, the regulatory move aims to eliminate the opacity that allows corporations to exaggerate their sustainability efforts. It forces a transition from superficial marketing narratives to accountable, data-driven transparency, making it significantly harder for entities to engage in deceptive green practices without facing regulatory scrutiny.
