India notifies first emission intensity targets for carbon-intensive sectors

India notifies first emission intensity targets for carbon-intensive sectors

India has introduced its first legally binding emission intensity targets for major carbon-heavy industries, marking a significant shift from voluntary energy-saving schemes to enforceable carbon limits. This regulatory move operationalizes the domestic carbon market, mandating that facilities reduce greenhouse gas emissions per unit of output or face substantial penalties. The framework introduces a tradable credit system where companies emitting below their assigned targets can earn credits, while those exceeding limits must purchase them or pay a fine. This mechanism not only incentivizes immediate decarbonization efforts but also aligns India’s industrial standards with international trade requirements, such as the EU’s Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism. This development is crucial for understanding greenwashing because it transitions corporate climate claims from marketing narratives to regulated compliance. By establishing measurable, legally enforced reduction targets and financial consequences for failure, the rules provide a concrete benchmark against which companies' environmental assertions can be verified. It exposes superficial sustainability claims by contrasting them with mandatory, data-driven operational realities.
