“Cuando todo es sostenible, nada lo es”: Andreu Escrivà, ciencia contra el 'greenwashing'
The article reveals that sustainability has been commodified into a vague marketing tool, allowing corporations to mask their continued environmental destruction under the guise of eco-friendliness. This phenomenon, known as greenwashing, creates a false sense of security where consumers believe simple actions like recycling or buying electric vehicles are sufficient solutions. Consequently, these superficial measures distract from the need for systemic change, enabling businesses to maintain unsustainable practices while portraying themselves as responsible. The core relevance to greenwashing lies in how this deceptive labeling undermines genuine ecological progress. By reducing complex environmental crises to manageable consumer choices, the narrative shifts responsibility onto individuals rather than addressing the root causes: infinite growth in a finite world. The author argues that true sustainability requires abandoning growth-oriented economic models in favor of planned, resource-efficient systems. This critique exposes the hollowness of "green" branding, which often serves as mere cosmetic improvement rather than a structural transformation of production and consumption. Ultimately, the text underscores that without challenging the power of greenwashing, society risks accepting inadequate solutions that fail to prevent severe climate impacts. It advocates for significant structural shifts, such as reducing working hours and implementing equitable resource rationing, to ensure that sacrifices are shared justly across all social classes. The article serves as a critical warning that relying on marketed sustainability rather than radical systemic change will lead to devastating environmental consequences, highlighting the urgent need to distinguish between authentic environmental stewardship and corporate deception.

Sustainable Saskatchewan $1.1M ad campaign: Selling the province or 'greenwashing' the problem | CBC News
The Saskatchewan government’s "Sustainable Saskatchewan" advertising campaign has drawn sharp criticism for utilizing vague terminology to promote resource extraction, a practice experts identify as classic greenwashing. By highlighting specific efficiency metrics and relative emission reductions in agriculture, the campaign constructs a narrative of environmental stewardship that obscures the province’s status as a high emitter. This selective framing allows the government to market itself as a leader in sustainability without addressing the broader, systemic environmental impacts of its core industries. Critics argue that the campaign lacks a substantive definition of sustainability, focusing instead on incremental improvements like methane reduction while ignoring absolute emission cuts and long-term ecological preservation. The dismissal of the Brundtland Commission’s definition, which emphasizes the needs of future generations, reveals a disconnect between marketing rhetoric and genuine sustainable development. Without commitments to decarbonization, biodiversity protection, or alignment with United Nations goals, the term "sustainable" becomes an empty label that misleads consumers and investors about the true environmental cost of Saskatchewan’s products. This case illustrates how governments can exploit the ambiguity of "green" language to deflect scrutiny and maintain business continuity without enacting necessary regulatory or economic sacrifices. The reliance on spin rather than science-based facts undermines public trust and delays the transition to a truly sustainable economy. For consumers, this highlights the critical need to scrutinize marketing claims beyond surface-level metrics, recognizing that true sustainability requires comprehensive action rather than selective publicity.
