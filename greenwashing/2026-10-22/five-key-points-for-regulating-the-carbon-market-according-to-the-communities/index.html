Five key points for regulating the carbon market according to the communities

Colombia’s voluntary carbon market, revitalized by new government policies, is facing a severe legitimacy crisis due to serious irregularities that have affected both the sector’s finances and Indigenous and Afro-Colombian communities. These populations, guardians of biodiversity, have suffered negative impacts as they have been involved in projects where their rights were violated, leading to social fractures and widespread distrust of environmental compensation mechanisms. An analysis of multiple legal conflicts reveals that the current model often prioritizes the interests of corporate developers over territorial autonomy. Communities demand genuine and informed participation at all stages, as well as ownership of the projects, to prevent leaders from being co-opted or clandestine agreements from being signed. This lack of transparency and the use of confidentiality clauses have prevented territories from understanding the economic and operational terms, consolidating a dynamic of covert exploitation. This case is relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how the narrative of sustainability can mask systemic abuses. The disconnect between international promotion of conservation and the local reality of rights violations demonstrates a structural failure in environmental governance. Without strict regulation, absolute transparency, and effective oversight mechanisms, the carbon market risks becoming an instrument of corporate image laundering that perpetuates social injustice under the guise of fighting climate change.
