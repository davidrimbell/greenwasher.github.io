Switzerland: Avoiding greenwashing risks in climate-related claims

Switzerland has significantly tightened the regulatory landscape for corporate climate communication, directly addressing the risks of greenwashing by requiring that all environmental claims be backed by objective, verifiable scientific data. This legal shift places the evidentiary burden squarely on companies, making it essential for organizations to substantiate their sustainability narratives to avoid misleading consumers and facing severe legal consequences. The enforcement framework strictly limits the use of offsetting mechanisms, prioritizing actual emission reductions within a company’s value chain over purchasing external carbon credits. Claims such as "carbon neutral" or "net zero" are scrutinized for their reliance on offsets, with broader, vague terms like "climate positive" deemed currently unsubstantiable under scientific standards, thereby closing loopholes often exploited in greenwashing campaigns. This article is vital for understanding greenwashing because it highlights the transition from voluntary self-regulation to rigorous statutory enforcement, where the burden of proof lies with the communicator. By clarifying acceptable terminology and demanding transparency in methodologies, regulators aim to eliminate deceptive practices, ensuring that corporate environmental messaging reflects genuine impact rather than marketing expediency.
