Ecofascism Is a Rising Threat. We Should Take Modi’s Ascendance as a Warning.

The article argues that India’s global elevation as an environmental leader under Prime Minister Narendra Modi exemplifies severe greenwashing, where superficial ecological branding masks a regime engaged in systemic human rights abuses. International bodies and Western nations have ignored documented evidence of violence and persecution, instead celebrating policies that serve as a facade for what the author identifies as "ecofascism." This phenomenon demonstrates how environmental rhetoric can be strategically weaponized to legitimize oppressive state power and distract from genuine crimes against humanity. Central to this critique is the concept that environmentalism is being co-opted to justify the dispossession and elimination of marginalized groups, including Muslims, Dalits, and Indigenous peoples. The author contends that initiatives such as reforestation, sanitation campaigns, and wildlife protection are utilized as precenses for mass evictions and violence, effectively using "nature conservation" as a euphemism for ethnic cleansing. This mirrors historical fascist strategies where ecological purity was inextricably linked to racial supremacy, suggesting that modern greenwashing serves similar supremacist ends. The piece highlights the dangerous implication that the international community’s complicity normalizes this fusion of environmentalism and authoritarianism. By accepting India’s green credentials despite plummeting environmental performance indices and reports of activist suppression, global institutions enable a model where ecological goals are achieved through brutal exclusion. This collusion not only betrays vulnerable populations but also offers a chilling preview of how future climate policies might be exploited to enforce ethnonationalist agendas under the guise of planetary survival.
