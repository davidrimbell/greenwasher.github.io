Complaint filed against oilsands alliance over ‘false and misleading’ claims

Greenpeace Canada has formally requested an investigation by the Competition Bureau into the Pathways Alliance, accusing the group of greenwashing through its “net-zero” advertising campaign. The complaint argues that the alliance of major oil producers is misleading the public by claiming climate neutrality while their actual emissions continue to rise and their investments in clean technology remain minimal. This campaign is viewed as an attempt to manufacture political support for continued fossil fuel extraction by falsely positioning their oil as environmentally superior to other producers. The core of the allegation lies in the selective accounting of greenhouse gas emissions. The Pathways Alliance’s net-zero claims consider only operational emissions from extraction and processing, deliberately excluding the vast majority of pollution generated when their products are ultimately burned by consumers. By omitting these downstream impacts, the alliance creates a deceptive impression of sustainability. Regulatory guidelines require environmental claims to account for a product’s entire lifecycle, meaning the exclusion of these significant emissions renders the advertising false and misleading under Canadian competition law. This case highlights the growing regulatory scrutiny of corporate environmental marketing. By clarifying on their website that their goals apply only to upstream emissions, the alliance effectively admits to the loophole but continues to rely on broad public advertising that obscures this limitation. The complaint underscores the tension between industry self-reporting and consumer protection, signaling that regulators are increasingly prioritizing the enforcement of accurate lifecycle assessments to prevent companies from gaining unfair market advantages through exaggerated green claims.
