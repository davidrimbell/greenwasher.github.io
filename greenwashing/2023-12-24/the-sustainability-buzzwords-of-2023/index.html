The sustainability buzzwords of 2023

The article identifies a complex landscape of emerging greenwashing tactics, highlighting how the sustainability sector faces scrutiny through concepts like "transition-washing," where vague commitments mask continued reliance on fossil fuels, and "competence greenwashing," where fabricated expertise undermines credible climate action. These phenomena demonstrate that the rush to market sustainability solutions often prioritizes optics over substantive impact, allowing corporations to exploit regulatory loopholes and erode public trust. Simultaneously, companies are increasingly adopting defensive strategies such as "greenhushing" and "green-bleaching" to avoid the severe backlash associated with "green-bashing." By downplaying or omitting sustainability claims entirely, firms attempt to mitigate reputational risks, yet this opacity contributes to a broader crisis of confidence. This trend suggests that without radical transparency and verified evidence, corporate sustainability communications are becoming more about risk management than genuine environmental stewardship, further complicating the verification process for stakeholders. Ultimately, the proliferation of buzzwords and new greenwashing variants underscores the urgent need for humility and candor in corporate reporting. The text argues that acknowledging corporate vulnerability and mistakes is essential to rebuilding credibility, as the current environment of skepticism and overlapping global crises demands rigorous scientific robustness over idealized branding. This relevance to greenwashing lies in its exposure of the gap between marketing narratives and actual operational realities, signaling a critical inflection point where consumers and regulators are no longer accepting superficial claims without concrete proof.
