How Green Claims Hide Real Pollution

Shoppers today face a confusing world. Many companies make false claims about being “green.” They want to look good to the public. At the same time, they want to spend less money. This creates a problem for buyers. People who care about the environment may end up supporting harmful practices.

Big companies use these tricks to gain an advantage. They attract customers who care about nature. But these customers often do not check the facts. For example, a company called Altri promotes its plant in Spain. They call it a model of sustainability. This helps them get government support. But this image hides the truth. The plant takes millions of liters of water from the Ulla River every day. It also releases warm waste water. The company gains protection from rules. But shoppers get a wrong idea. They might think they are helping the earth. In reality, they might be supporting pollution and deforestation.

The gas industry uses similar tricks. They sponsor popular TV shows like MasterChef Australia. This makes gas seem friendly and normal. They link gas to new, cleaner technologies like hydrogen. This creates a fake image of sustainability. It slows down the switch to electric power. Experts say electricity is the true path to a clean future. The gas industry keeps its customers this way. They avoid criticism about climate change. But shoppers are misled. Families watching TV learn that gas is “green.” This hides the fact that gas stops us from using clean energy.

The food industry does the same thing. In Spain, the pork industry paid for a university course. They wanted to promote a story of “triple sustainability.” This hides the damage caused by large pig farms. These farms produce lots of greenhouse gases. They also pollute groundwater with harmful chemicals. The industry protects its reputation. It avoids making big changes. But shoppers face risks. They eat food made in ways that hurt the planet. They might support farms that break rules and threaten health.

We need better rules to fix this problem. New standards can define what “sustainable” really means. These rules are based on science, not just marketing. This helps shoppers make smart choices. They can tell the difference between real eco-friendly products and fake ones. Without these rules, trust disappears. When companies claim to be green while polluting, shoppers cannot tell the truth. It becomes hard to believe any environmental label.

Shoppers must look closely at the facts. Do not just trust big claims. Look for specific evidence. There is a big gap between what companies say and what they do. They use green stories to hide pollution. This tricks the public and breaks trust. Shoppers should demand stricter rules. They must ask for clear labels. This ensures your money supports projects that truly help the earth. It protects you from companies that just pretend to care. We must stay alert. We must check that environmental claims are based on real science.
