The report reveals a profound shift in consumer-brand dynamics, where skepticism toward sustainability claims has transformed greenwashing from a mere reputational risk into a critical communication boundary. Consumers increasingly demand transparency and tangible evidence over narrative, viewing stricter sanctions as essential to combat the widespread erosion of corporate credibility. This distrust extends even to established environmental leaders, indicating that trust in European brands has significantly declined. While sustainability remains a key driver for purchasing decisions, a paradox exists: high intentions to act ethically are often thwarted by price sensitivity, limiting the actual impact of green commitments on buying behavior. The article highlights the urgent need for authentic, data-backed communication to rebuild trust. It underscores that marketers must prioritize verifiable actions over storytelling, as genuine transparency is now the only way to effectively navigate a highly regulated and suspicious market environment.
