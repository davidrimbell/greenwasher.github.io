Un juez estadounidense desestima la demanda contra Mondelez por "greenwashing" en etiquetas de Clif Bars

Un juez estadounidense desestima la demanda contra Mondelez por "greenwashing" en etiquetas de Clif Bars

A US judge dismissed a lawsuit alleging that Mondelez misled consumers by labeling its bars as "carbon neutral." The ruling clarified that the company did not claim the products inherently produced zero emissions, but rather that they held a valid certification from a nonprofit organization aimed at reducing corporate footprints. This distinction is crucial for understanding greenwashing claims, as it separates false inherent benefits from accurate statements about third-party certifications. The judge determined that labeling items with legitimate certifications is truthful and not deceptive, even if the underlying manufacturing process generates significant emissions. This highlights that transparency in how certifications are communicated can protect companies from fraud accusations, provided they do not misrepresent the nature of the environmental benefit. This case is relevant to greenwashing because it establishes legal boundaries between exaggerated eco-claims and compliant marketing. It suggests that including verified certification details on packaging can shield brands from consumer accusations of deception, emphasizing the importance of precise language in environmental marketing to avoid accusations of misleading the public.
