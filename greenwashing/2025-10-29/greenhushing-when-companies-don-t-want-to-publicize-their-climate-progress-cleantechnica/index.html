Greenhushing: When Companies Don't Want To Publicize Their Climate Progress - CleanTechnica

Greenhushing: When Companies Don't Want To Publicize Their Climate Progress - CleanTechnica

The article identifies "greenhushing" as a growing trend where companies deliberately silence their environmental efforts, despite a significant increase in actual climate commitments. This strategic retreat stems from external political pressures, such as regulatory penalties and shifting government priorities, which incentivize silence to avoid backlash. Consequently, many organizations are downplaying their progress in public communications, creating a disconnect between their internal actions and external narratives. This phenomenon is closely relevant to greenwashing because it reflects a dual response to the risks of being accused of it. While some companies retract vague or misleading claims to prevent greenwashing allegations, others remain silent due to fear, political hostility, or resource limitations. This creates a complex landscape where the absence of communication does not necessarily indicate a lack of sustainability efforts, but rather a calculated risk avoidance strategy that obscures the true state of corporate environmental performance. Ultimately, greenhushing undermines the transparency essential for building consumer trust and driving industry-wide change. By hiding successes, companies lose the ability to influence competitors and attract eco-conscious customers, potentially slowing overall environmental progress. The article argues that moving away from strategic silence toward data-driven, credible communication is crucial for maintaining integrity, ensuring that the fight against greenwashing is replaced by genuine, verifiable accountability.
