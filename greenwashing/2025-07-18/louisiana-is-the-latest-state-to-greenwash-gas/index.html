Louisiana is the latest state to greenwash gas

The article identifies Louisiana’s new legislation, which legally reclassifies natural gas as "green energy," as a prime example of corporate greenwashing. By adopting model language from the fossil-fuel-backed American Legislative Exchange Council, the state prioritizes methane infrastructure over truly renewable sources like solar and wind. This semantic shift allows polluting industries to access clean energy funding, effectively masking the environmental harm of high methane leakage with climate-friendly terminology. The implications of this legal redefinition extend beyond rhetoric, significantly impacting market dynamics and climate accountability. Reclassifying gas as green makes fossil fuel projects more attractive to environmentally conscious investors and diverts public subsidies away from sustainable alternatives. This strategy not only perpetuates reliance on carbon-intensive fuels but also undermines the transition to a low-carbon economy by legitimizing dangerous infrastructure under the guise of environmental stewardship. This case is highly relevant to greenwashing because it demonstrates how industry actors manipulate definitions to deceive the public and policymakers. The law exploits the vague concept of "clean" energy to secure financial benefits for the fossil fuel sector while claiming to support sustainability goals. Ultimately, it reveals a coordinated effort to delay genuine climate action by co-opting green language to protect entrenched economic interests rather than reducing planetary warming.
