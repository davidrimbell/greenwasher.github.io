Greenwashing
ES EN

The article questions the genuine sustainability of the rapid expansion of renewable energy in Spain, pointing out that the simplification of environmental authorizations prioritizes the benefits of large foreign investment funds over the protection of rural areas. Critics argue that this administrative leniency transforms the territory into a resource to be exploited, creating a conflict between the urgency of climate action and local environmental and social impacts, thereby revealing a contradiction between green rhetoric and the reality of land management. The relevance of this analysis for greenwashing lies in its exposure of the tensions between institutional discourse and corporate practices. Although the European Union promotes the Green Deal as a fundamental pillar, the war in Ukraine has shown that environmental commitment is flexible and can be displaced by immediate geopolitical and economic interests. This volatility exposes the risk that green initiatives are used primarily as image-building tools or political justifications, rather than reflecting a deep and consistent structural transformation. Furthermore, the article highlights how the influence of institutional investors varies drastically depending on the geopolitical context, with key actors such as BlackRock reducing their support for climate proposals in favor of sectoral stability. This attitude suggests that many companies and funds superficially promote sustainability to maintain their reputation ("greenwashing"), without truly assuming the necessary commitments when circumstances change. The article concludes that the energy transition is being shaped more by economic interests and volatile political stances than by a firm ecological conviction, revealing the fragility of the current green business model.
