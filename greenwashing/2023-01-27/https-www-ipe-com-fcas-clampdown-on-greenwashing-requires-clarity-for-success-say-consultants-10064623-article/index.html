Greenwashing
ES EN

The UK Financial Conduct Authority’s proposed Sustainability Disclosure Requirements aim to combat greenwashing by introducing strict labels and restricting sustainable marketing claims. While industry experts welcome these anti-greenwashing measures as essential for restoring market trust, they emphasize that the rules lack sufficient clarity regarding specific definitions and thresholds. Without precise guidelines, firms may struggle to implement the standards effectively, and consumers might misinterpret the labels, undermining the regulations' core objective of transparency. A critical concern is the alignment of these new rules with a comprehensive UK green taxonomy, which experts argue is urgently needed to provide a robust baseline for measuring sustainability. Consultants warn that the current proposals omit minimum technical standards for metrics, creating risks of inconsistency and comparability issues. Furthermore, there is significant apprehension that applying these regulations only to retail products, while exempting those marketed to institutional investors, would create a dangerous regulatory divergence that complicates the assessment of genuine sustainability across the entire market. Ultimately, these rules are expected to profoundly reshape the sustainable investment landscape by establishing de facto standards that all managers must follow to remain competitive. The introduction of clear labels implies that unlabelled products lack sustainable credentials, which may drive higher market ambition and awareness. However, the success of this shift depends heavily on refining the criteria for various asset classes and ensuring that the regulatory framework is universally applied to prevent loopholes that could otherwise facilitate continued greenwashing.
