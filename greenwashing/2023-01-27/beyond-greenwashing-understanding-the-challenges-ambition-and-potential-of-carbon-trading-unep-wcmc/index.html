Beyond greenwashing: understanding the challenges, ambition and potential of carbon trading - UNEP-WCMC

Voluntary carbon markets have expanded rapidly, allowing companies to claim "net zero" status by purchasing offsets rather than solely reducing emissions. This dynamic creates a significant greenwashing risk, as firms may prioritize buying high-profile, biodiverse credits over genuine decarbonization. The market’s allure lies in the ability to mask continued pollution with narrative-driven projects, potentially diverting attention from the urgent need to phase out fossil fuels and reduce actual emissions at the source. The complexity of verifying carbon sequestration further exacerbates this transparency gap. With numerous certification standards and expensive verification processes, there is ample room for ambiguity and inflated claims. This opaque landscape enables actors to market products as "carbon neutral" while relying on offsets that may not represent permanent or additional carbon removal. Consequently, consumers and regulators struggle to distinguish between authentic climate action and superficial marketing tactics designed to greenwash corporate footprints. Despite emerging regional standards aiming for greater accountability, the fundamental structure of voluntary markets continues to facilitate potential deception. The disconnect between high demand for offsets and the difficulty in accurately measuring their impact suggests that these mechanisms often serve more as public relations tools than effective climate solutions. Recognizing this reality is crucial, as it highlights how carbon trading can be exploited to maintain business-as-usual operations under the guise of environmental responsibility.
