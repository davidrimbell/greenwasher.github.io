Labour to shorten landlords’ net zero deadline by two years

Labour to shorten landlords’ net zero deadline by two years

Labour proposes banning landlords from renting to new tenants by 2028 if they fail to meet stricter energy efficiency standards, aiming for all private rental homes to achieve an EPC rating of C or higher by 2030. This accelerated timeline replaces previous Conservative plans and includes a higher spending cap for insulation and upgrades, signaling a decisive shift toward reducing carbon emissions in the housing sector. Tenants’ rights groups welcome the initiative as a crucial step against fuel poverty, yet caution that without robust protections against eviction and rent hikes, improvements funded by public money could destabilize renters. The policy revival highlights the ongoing political struggle over housing quality, contrasting sharply with earlier government inaction driven by landlord lobbying pressures. This article is highly relevant to greenwashing because it exposes potential discrepancies between policy ambitions and implementation realities. With existing EPC assessments often criticized as unreliable guesswork, there is a significant risk that landlords may claim compliance through superficial or inaccurate reporting rather than genuine efficiency gains. This raises concerns that the stated environmental goals might be met through deceptive practices rather than substantive improvements.
