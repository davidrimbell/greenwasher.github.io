Europa pone en duda la agricultura regenerativa y alerta sobre posibles prácticas de greenwashing

The article highlights a growing concern in Europe regarding the potential for greenwashing through the ambiguous use of "regenerative agriculture." Without clear, legally binding definitions, this term risks becoming a vague marketing tool that allows companies to project an eco-friendly image without committing to verifiable environmental improvements. This lack of standardization threatens to confuse consumers and policymakers, undermining the credibility of genuine sustainable practices. In response, a broad coalition led by IFOAM Organics Europe advocates for strict regulatory frameworks to protect established standards such as organic and agroecological farming. These models already have rigorous certification systems and transparent controls, ensuring that environmental benefits are measurable and trustworthy. The coalition argues that adopting undefined labels could erode public trust and unfairly penalize producers who have invested significantly in biodiversity and soil health, while allowing others to benefit from superficial sustainability claims. Ultimately, the debate underscores the critical need for scientific rigor and accountability in EU agricultural policy. By insisting that future subsidies and incentives be tied to demonstrable outcomes, policymakers aim to prevent the erosion of trust in the food sector. This shift is essential to ensure that the transition toward resilient food systems is driven by real ecological impact rather than misleading narratives, securing both consumer confidence and genuine environmental progress.
