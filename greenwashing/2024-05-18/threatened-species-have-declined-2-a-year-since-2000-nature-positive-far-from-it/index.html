Greenwashing
ES EN

Threatened species have declined 2% a year since 2000. Nature positive? Far from it.

The article argues that the Australian government’s current "Nature Positive" strategy constitutes greenwashing by allowing biodiversity decline to continue while claiming progress. Despite lofty commitments to protect ecosystems, the federal budget allocates negligible funding to nature compared to carbon capture technologies. This financial disparity results in a Threatened Species Index that continues to fall, contradicting the policy’s stated aim to reverse nature loss. The core implication is that the government is redefining success to accept ongoing environmental degradation as acceptable progress. The author highlights a dangerous disconnect between the definition of "Nature Positive" and actual government targets. True nature positivity requires halting and reversing declines by 2030, yet the current trajectory merely slows the rate of collapse without achieving recovery. By accepting a continued downward trend in species health and labeling it as a maintained or improved state, officials are engaging in semantic manipulation. This approach is compared to accepting negative economic growth and calling it stability, illustrating how policy language is being used to mask failure. Ultimately, the text identifies the indefinite deferral of stronger environmental laws as a critical element of this greenwashing campaign. Without robust legal frameworks to enforce restoration, new agencies will lack the power to stop habitat destruction. The article concludes that these weak measures fail to address the urgent need for ecosystem restoration, risking further extinctions and compromising long-term food security and community wellbeing. This reveals a systemic issue where political aspirations are undermined by insufficient action and misleading terminology.
