Greenwashing
ES EN

El ecopostureo tiene los días contados

The article highlights that large food corporations have institutionalized “greenwashing” as an effective commercial strategy, capitalizing on consumers’ growing environmental concerns. Despite the clear need for sustainability, many brands use vague terms such as “natural” or “eco-friendly” without scientific evidence, creating a disconnect between their green image and their actual practices. This deceptive practice confuses the public and distorts the market, prioritizing appearance over genuine reductions in environmental impact, which would entail consuming only what is necessary and reusing resources. The relevance of this issue for the study of greenwashing lies in the emerging legislative responses at both national and European levels to regulate these “green claims.” New regulations aim to classify unverified environmental assertions as unfair practices, imposing severe fines and demanding transparency. This legal context underscores that unsubstantiated advertising language is a tool of manipulation that institutions are seeking to eradicate, recognizing that the lack of clear regulation enables the proliferation of misleading messages that protect polluters while exploiting consumers’ ethical awareness. Finally, a structural solution to corporate deception is proposed: local and cooperative consumption as authentic alternatives. Examples such as Terranostra in the Balearic Islands demonstrate that non-profit models focused on zero-kilometer products and repairability offer a level of transparency impossible to find in conventional corporate marketing. This approach not only avoids greenwashing but also fosters a genuine circular economy, shifting power away from multinational corporations toward communities that prioritize verifiable sustainability over public image.
