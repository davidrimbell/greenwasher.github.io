¿Qué es el greenwashing y por qué las empresas deben evitarlo? | RPP Noticias

¿Qué es el greenwashing y por qué las empresas deben evitarlo? | RPP Noticias

Amid escalating climate crises and record-breaking natural disasters, the private sector is increasingly adopting environmental commitments to appeal to eco-conscious consumers. This surge in corporate sustainability initiatives has inadvertently fueled greenwashing—a deceptive marketing practice in which companies falsely project eco-friendly qualities without substantial foundations. By capitalizing on consumers’ desire for ethical consumption, businesses often create a misleading perception of environmental responsibility, exploiting the urgency of the ecological emergency for commercial gain rather than genuine stewardship. In Peru, regulatory bodies have actively combated this issue by investigating numerous companies for unsubstantiated claims, such as being completely natural or biodegradable. Authorities emphasize that such objective assertions require rigorous, objective proof before publication, highlighting the legal and ethical risks of making careless environmental statements. This enforcement underscores how greenwashing relies on ambiguity and lack of verification, allowing corporations to evade accountability while misleading the public about their actual environmental impact and operational practices. Transitioning from greenwashing to authentic green marketing demands a fundamental shift toward restorative and responsible business models. True sustainability requires embedding environmental care into core operations through strategic action, not just communication. Companies must prioritize ethical production, resource optimization, and honest dialogue with stakeholders. Genuine commitment involves tangible efforts, such as eliminating plastic waste or supporting ecological restoration, ensuring that marketing reflects real corporate behavior rather than serving as a superficial facade to attract environmentally aware customers.
