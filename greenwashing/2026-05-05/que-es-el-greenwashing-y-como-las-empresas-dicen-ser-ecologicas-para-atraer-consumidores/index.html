Qué es el greenwashing y cómo las empresas dicen ser ecológicas para atraer consumidores

The article identifies greenwashing as a deceptive strategy in which companies project a false image of environmental commitment to capitalize on consumer demand for sustainability, without implementing actual changes in their production processes. This phenomenon is increasingly prevalent, particularly in markets with limited regulation, as firms prioritize changing their discourse over transforming their business models to attract ethically minded buyers. To detect this manipulation, experts highlight warning signs such as the use of vague terminology, imagery of nature lacking data support, and the emphasis on minor sustainable actions that overshadow larger negative impacts. True sustainability requires verification through external certifications and transparent, audited reports rather than appealing packaging or superficial claims, emphasizing that transparency and measurable data are the only reliable indicators of genuine environmental responsibility. The relevance of this discussion to greenwashing lies in its broader societal impact: it fosters deep consumer distrust and creates unfair competition against genuinely sustainable companies. By normalizing deception, this practice not only misleads the public but also disincentivizes real ecological change. Ultimately, both companies and consumers share responsibility, as the latter’s reliance on visual cues over verification allows the cycle of mistrust and environmental harm to persist.
