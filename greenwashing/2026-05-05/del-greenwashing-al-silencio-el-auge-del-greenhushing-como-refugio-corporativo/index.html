Del “Greenwashing” al silencio: el auge del “Greenhushing” como refugio corporativo

The article highlights a significant shift from greenwashing to "greenhushing," where companies increasingly silence their sustainability efforts to avoid regulatory scrutiny and accusations of misleading practices. This trend transforms environmental commitment from a competitive advantage into a perceived risk, prompting many firms, particularly in fashion, energy, and banking, to reduce their public communication despite achieving real progress. While this caution stems from stricter European regulations requiring rigorous proof of environmental claims, total silence creates substantial market vulnerabilities. By withdrawing from the conversation, brands risk losing positive consumer perception and ceding ground to competitors who communicate effectively. Furthermore, this retreat can inadvertently breed distrust, as consumers may interpret the lack of transparency as a confession of guilt or a lack of genuine effort. To avoid these pitfalls, the piece argues that the solution is not less communication, but more rigorous and coherent communication. Brands must integrate sustainability and marketing teams to ensure messages are backed by concrete data and align with actual business practices. Ultimately, maintaining transparency about both achievements and ongoing challenges is essential to building credibility and protecting against greenwashing allegations in an increasingly critical market.
