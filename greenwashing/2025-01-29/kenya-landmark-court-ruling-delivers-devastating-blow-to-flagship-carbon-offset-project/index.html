Kenya: Landmark court ruling delivers devastating blow to flagship carbon offset project

Kenya: Landmark court ruling delivers devastating blow to flagship carbon offset project

A landmark Kenyan court ruling has declared key conservancies under the Northern Rangelands Trust’s carbon project illegal, stripping them of legal standing and ordering armed rangers to leave. This judgment directly challenges the foundation of a flagship offset scheme utilized by major corporations like Meta and British Airways, casting doubt on the validity of millions of dollars in generated credits. The decision affirms that these areas were established without Indigenous consent, effectively nullifying the project’s operational basis in significant portions of the affected region. The ruling exposes the systemic failure of certification bodies to enforce legal compliance within the voluntary carbon market. Despite prior warnings regarding the absence of land rights and proper consultation, the primary verifier approved and re-verified the project multiple times. This oversight reveals how verification standards can overlook fundamental property rights, allowing organizations to profit from mechanisms that are legally unsound, thereby undermining the integrity and trustworthiness of the entire carbon credit industry. This case serves as a critical indictment of greenwashing in conservation efforts, illustrating how corporate climate strategies often rely on "fortress conservation" models that displace Indigenous populations while claiming environmental benefits. It highlights the dangerous disconnect between certified carbon offsets and actual human rights, demonstrating that such projects frequently mask exploitation as sustainability. Consequently, this event urges a reevaluation of how carbon markets validate projects, stressing that genuine climate action must respect Indigenous land rights rather than circumvent them for financial gain.
