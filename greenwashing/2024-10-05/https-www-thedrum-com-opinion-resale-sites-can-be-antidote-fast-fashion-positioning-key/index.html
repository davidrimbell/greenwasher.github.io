Greenwashing
EN ES

Fast fashion brands often introduce resale platforms to appear eco-friendly, yet this strategy risks backfiring if it lacks substantive change elsewhere in their operations. Because these companies produce vast quantities of clothing that quickly becomes obsolete due to accelerated micro-trends, resale alone cannot offset the immense environmental damage caused by continuous overproduction. When consumers perceive a disconnect between these marketing efforts and the reality of high-volume waste, they view the initiative as greenwashing rather than genuine sustainability, which can damage brand trust and reputation. This mismatch triggers intense scrutiny, as sustainable messaging that does not align with a company’s actual business model invites higher consumer skepticism. In an era where activists share information rapidly on social media, any perceived hypocrisy can spread quickly, leading to negative word-of-mouth and reduced purchase intentions. The core issue is that highlighting sustainable initiatives while maintaining a model built on overconsumption draws attention to the industry's inherent environmental flaws, making it difficult for brands to control the narrative. Consequently, the article argues that brands must ensure their corporate actions truly align with their sustainability claims before communicating them. Rather than relying on buzzwords or superficial initiatives like resale sites, companies should focus on factual improvements in their supply chains, such as garment longevity and production efficiency. True sustainability requires a holistic approach that addresses the root causes of environmental harm, ensuring that marketing efforts reflect actual operational changes rather than just aesthetic appearances.
