Greenwashing, ecopostureo o lavado verde, la guerra de Iberdrola contra Repsol

Greenwashing, ecopostureo o lavado verde, la guerra de Iberdrola contra Repsol

The Spanish court dismissed Iberdrola’s lawsuit against Repsol, ruling that Repsol’s marketing does not constitute misleading advertising or greenwashing. The judge concluded that Spanish consumers are already well aware of Repsol’s traditional identity as an oil company, meaning its current branding as a multi-energy provider does not deceive the public about its core business. Since the average consumer understands the environmental impact of fossil fuels, Repsol’s promotion of its energy mix is not seen as an attempt to conceal its polluting nature or to manipulate purchasing decisions through false ecological claims. This ruling highlights the legal difficulty of prosecuting greenwashing in the absence of specific, comprehensive legislation defining such practices within a regulatory framework. The court determined that Repsol’s slogans focused on economic savings rather than environmental benefits, and thus did not imply a false commitment to sustainability. Furthermore, the lack of proven direct competition between the two companies in specific sectors such as hydrogen weakened Iberdrola’s standing to sue for unfair competition, underscoring how current commercial laws struggle to address deceptive environmental branding effectively. This case is highly relevant to the greenwashing discourse, as it represents the first major judicial confrontation between major fossil fuel companies in Spain over ecological marketing claims. Although Iberdrola lost the legal battle, the lawsuit successfully drew public attention to the issue of corporations using sustainability rhetoric while continuing to sell fossil fuels. It underscores the urgent need for stricter laws against greenwashing, as current legal frameworks fail to penalize companies for potentially misleading their public image, even in the absence of explicit false advertising.
