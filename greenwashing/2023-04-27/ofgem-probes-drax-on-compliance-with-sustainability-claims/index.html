Ofgem probes Drax on compliance with sustainability claims

Ofgem probes Drax on compliance with sustainability claims

Ofgem, the UK’s energy regulator, has launched an audit into Drax’s sustainability claims following serious allegations of greenwashing exposed by a BBC investigation. The probe centers on whether Drax truly complies with biomass sustainability rules, particularly regarding its sourcing from Canadian virgin forests. This scrutiny arises from accusations that the company contributes to environmental harm by logging old-growth trees rather than using rapid-growing species, challenging the core narrative of its renewable energy credentials. The situation is further complicated by conflicts of interest and questions about the robustness of the certification schemes Drax utilizes. The company’s chief executive previously served on the board of the Sustainable Biomass Program, the very entity Drax uses to prove its sourcing is sustainable. Consequently, critics argue that the regulatory audit, commissioned to protect the regulator from external pressure, may lack independence, raising concerns about whether the current oversight mechanisms are sufficient to verify the environmental claims of major energy producers. This case is highly relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how complex carbon accounting and industry-led certifications can obscure significant ecological damage. Drax continues to assert its operations are carbon-neutral and sustainable, yet it faces renewed demands from its own advisory board to reassess these criteria. The ongoing investigation highlights the tension between corporate environmental narratives and scientific reality, serving as a critical example of how regulatory frameworks may struggle to hold large energy firms accountable for misleading sustainability claims.
