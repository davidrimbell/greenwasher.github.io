Adiós a los productos “sostenibles” y “verdes”: el Gobierno quiere multar con 100.000 euros a las empresas que hagan ecopostureo

The Spanish government is preparing a Sustainable Consumption Law designed to combat greenwashing and eco-posturing, marking a milestone in the fight against misleading environmental claims. The legislation will classify the use of vague or unverified terms as an unfair commercial practice, imposing severe penalties on companies that employ such arguments without solid scientific backing. This measure aims to align with European directives, raising transparency standards and requiring companies to support their ecological claims with public commitments, clear objectives, and verifiable implementation plans. The significance of the article lies in its direct approach to the legal definition of greenwashing, addressing the crisis of confidence generated by the prevalence of unfounded environmental claims in the market. By classifying these practices as unfair competition, the law not only protects consumers but also demands rigorous verification of environmental claims, distinguishing between regulated labels and manipulated commercial messages. This represents a fundamental shift in how corporate sustainability is regulated, shifting the burden of proof onto companies and establishing clear limits on superficial green advertising. In addition to regulating communication, the law incorporates a repairability index for electronic products, promoting durability and the right to repair over premature disposal. This initiative seeks to democratize consumer information, enabling an objective assessment of product lifespans and reducing the environmental impact resulting from excessive consumption. By integrating informational transparency with tangible sustainability mechanisms, the law aims to transform production and consumption models, ensuring that the ecological transition is based on concrete, verifiable actions rather than mere appearances.
