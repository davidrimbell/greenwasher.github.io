Facua-A apoya que Europa frene el 'ecopostureo' y recuerda que "ya hay instrumentos para sancionar" estas prácticas

Facua-A apoya que Europa frene el 'ecopostureo' y recuerda que "ya hay instrumentos para sancionar" estas prácticas

The recent European directive aims to empower consumers by banning misleading eco-claims and requiring scientific evidence for sustainability labels. This legislative shift targets "greenwashing," specifically practices where products market themselves as environmentally friendly without a factual basis, thereby reducing corporate deception. However, enforcement remains critically weak due to insufficient regulatory will and scarce inspection resources. Current penalties are often too negligible to deter companies, allowing them to repeat deceptive practices without significant consequences. The lack of robust oversight mechanisms undermines existing legal tools, making compliance voluntary rather than mandatory. This article highlights that while new laws are necessary to refine definitions of greenwashing, they are ineffective without proper implementation. The relevance lies in exposing the gap between legislative intent and administrative reality. Without adequate funding and strict control, even the best regulations fail to protect consumers from pseudo-ecological marketing, emphasizing that legal frameworks alone cannot combat deceptive business practices.
