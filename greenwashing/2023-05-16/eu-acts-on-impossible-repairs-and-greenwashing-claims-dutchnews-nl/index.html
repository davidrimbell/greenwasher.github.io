EU acts on impossible repairs and greenwashing claims - DutchNews.nl

The European Parliament recently approved legislation prohibiting manufacturers from intentionally limiting product lifespans through design choices that prevent repair or force premature failure. While this move curbs planned obsolescence, consumer advocates argue it remains insufficient. They insist that self-repair rights must be legally protected and that manufacturers should be compelled to extend legal guarantee periods, thereby financially incentivizing the creation of durable, long-lasting goods rather than disposable items. Crucially, the new regulations address digital products by mandating transparency regarding software update durations and their impact on device functionality. This ensures consumers are not misled into buying devices that become obsolete or unsafe due to halted support. The legislation also explicitly bans vague environmental claims like "eco-friendly" without scientific evidence, directly targeting greenwashing tactics that rely on ambiguous marketing to exaggerate a product's sustainability credentials. Despite these advances, significant debates persist regarding carbon offsetting claims, leaving room for companies to market misleadingly "green" services based on offsets rather than actual emission reductions. This ongoing controversy highlights the urgent need for stricter definitions of sustainability in advertising. Consequently, this article is vital to understanding greenwashing, as it reveals how current regulatory frameworks are evolving to distinguish between genuine environmental responsibility and deceptive practices that exploit consumer goodwill.
