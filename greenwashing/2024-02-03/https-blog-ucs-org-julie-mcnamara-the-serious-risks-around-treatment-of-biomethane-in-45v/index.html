Greenwashing
ES EN

The article warns that proposed regulations for the 45V Clean Hydrogen Production Tax Credit contain significant loopholes regarding biomethane and fugitive methane. Without strict guardrails, these exemptions could allow heavily polluting fossil fuel-based hydrogen projects to qualify for subsidies by using offsets, effectively subsidizing pollution under the guise of clean energy. This undermines the policy’s core intent to drive a genuine transition toward truly clean production technologies. This lack of rigor enables a classic form of greenwashing by allowing "blue" hydrogen facilities to artificially lower their carbon intensity scores through questionable accounting methods. By permitting pollution offsets or using weak baseline counterfactuals, the Treasury could inadvertently incentivize industries to increase methane production rather than reduce it. Consequently, taxpayer funds would flow to entrenched fossil fuel infrastructure and industrial agriculture, masking continued environmental harm behind the label of "clean." To prevent this, the author argues that the Treasury must explicitly prohibit pollution offsets and establish rigorous fuel eligibility and tracking requirements. Allowing vague definitions of biomass or fugitive methane risks creating perverse incentives that worsen climate outcomes and exacerbate environmental justice issues. The final regulations must close these gaps to ensure that public dollars actually support genuine emissions reductions rather than legitimizing the fossil fuel status quo through deceptive environmental claims.
