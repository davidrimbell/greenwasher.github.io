‘Be Planet’ or not?: Lululemon accused of greenwashing in US class action

Lululemon faces a class-action lawsuit alleging its "Be Planet" campaign deceptively portrays environmental responsibility while actual greenhouse gas emissions have significantly increased. This discrepancy between marketing messages and operational realities highlights a core greenwashing tactic, where brands emphasize minor sustainable efforts to mask substantial ecological harm caused by practices like heavy reliance on air freight and synthetic materials. The legal challenge is compounded by a formal investigation from Canada’s Competition Bureau, which scrutinizes whether these claims violate consumer protection laws. The potential penalties, tied to global profits, underscore the financial risks associated with misleading sustainability narratives, reinforcing the need for transparency in environmental reporting. This case is relevant to greenwashing as it illustrates the growing legal scrutiny against brands that exaggerate their eco-friendly contributions. It demonstrates how consumers and regulators are increasingly challenging the gap between advertised ethical standards and actual corporate impact, serving as a critical warning to retailers about the consequences of deceptive environmental marketing.
