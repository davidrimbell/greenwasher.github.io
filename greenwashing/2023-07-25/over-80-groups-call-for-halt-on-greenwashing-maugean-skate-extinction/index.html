Over 80 Groups Call for Halt on Greenwashing Maugean Skate Extinction

A coalition of international environmental and scientific organizations is demanding the immediate revocation of sustainability certifications from salmon farms in Tasmania’s Macquarie Harbour. This urgent call coincides with government efforts to prevent the extinction of the Maugean skate, a rare prehistoric species. Despite clear scientific evidence linking industrial fish farming to severe habitat degradation and plummeting skate populations, the responsible companies retain "environmentally responsible" status through major accreditation schemes, highlighting a critical disconnect between certification claims and ecological reality. The core issue lies in the role of these global accreditation schemes in misleading consumers. By maintaining certifications for farms explicitly blamed for driving a species toward extinction, these programs are accused of facilitating greenwashing. Supermarkets rely on these labels to market products as sustainable, effectively decepecting shoppers who believe they are supporting responsible practices. The signatories argue that this misrepresentation allows the industry to continue destructive operations while appearing ethical, thereby eroding trust in sustainability labels and contributing to biodiversity loss. This situation is highly relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how certification bodies can legitimize severe environmental harm through bureaucratic processes rather than actual ecological stewardship. It underscores the danger of relying on private certification systems without rigorous, independent oversight, especially in sensitive ecosystems like World Heritage Areas. The incident serves as a stark warning that without immediate accountability and the withdrawal of credentials for harmful operations, sustainability labels risk becoming mere marketing tools that obscure, rather than resolve, the urgent crises facing vulnerable wildlife and marine environments.
