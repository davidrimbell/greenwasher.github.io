Greenwashing
ES EN

The article exposes how political narratives often distort economic realities by cherry-picking data while ignoring critical context. It demonstrates that claims of historic economic dominance under the previous administration rely on selective timelines, such as excluding the pandemic’s onset, which drastically alters job creation and growth metrics. This selective storytelling creates a misleading impression of sustained success, obscuring the reality that many key economic indicators were comparable to or outperformed by previous or subsequent presidencies. Furthermore, the analysis highlights how broader macroeconomic forces, rather than individual leadership alone, drive national trends. By attributing complex issues like inflation and rising interest rates solely to current policies, these narratives ignore global events such as supply chain disruptions and geopolitical conflicts. This simplification shifts blame unfairly, suggesting that specific leaders are responsible for systemic economic shifts that affect all administrations equally, thereby manipulating public perception of accountability and competence. This content is directly relevant to the concept of greenwashing, as it illustrates a form of "political greenwashing" or "economic greenwashing." Just as corporations may exaggerate environmental benefits to appear sustainable, political actors may sanitize their records by omitting negative context to present a falsely pristine image of performance. Recognizing these techniques helps citizens discern between genuine achievement and manufactured narratives, fostering a more critical and informed understanding of how data can be manipulated to serve ideological or electoral goals.
