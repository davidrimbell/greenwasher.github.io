Greenwashing
ES EN

The article challenges the notion that electric vehicles (EVs) are inherently sustainable, revealing a significant environmental trade-off. The manufacturing process demands extensive energy and mineral extraction, creating a substantial initial carbon debt and raising ethical concerns regarding labor practices and local resource depletion. By highlighting the heavy ecological cost embedded in production, it exposes a critical gap between the marketed image of zero-emission mobility and the harsh reality of its supply chain. Furthermore, the lack of effective recycling systems for batteries and the dependency on the carbon intensity of the local electricity grid complicate the climate benefit analysis. This nuance is vital for understanding greenwashing, as it demonstrates that labeling EVs as purely "green" ignores the upstream pollution and downstream waste issues. Such oversimplification allows companies to capitalize on eco-friendly trends while obscuring the broader environmental footprint associated with raw material sourcing and energy generation. Ultimately, the text suggests that a rigid focus on EVs may be strategically flawed, advocating instead for a diversified approach that includes hybrids and improved combustion technologies. This perspective warns against ideological adherence to a single solution, emphasizing that true sustainability requires evaluating the total lifecycle impact. It underscores the importance of transparency in corporate sustainability claims, urging consumers and regulators to look beyond superficial marketing to the complex realities of resource management and emission reductions.
