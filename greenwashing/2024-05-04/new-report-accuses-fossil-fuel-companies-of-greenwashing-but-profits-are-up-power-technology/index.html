New report accuses fossil fuel companies of greenwashing, but profits are up - Power Technology

New report accuses fossil fuel companies of greenwashing, but profits are up - Power Technology

A bipartisan US legislative report reveals that major fossil fuel giants like Shell, BP, and ExxonMobil have known about the climate impacts of their products since the 1960s. Despite this internal consensus, these companies spent decades actively undermining public understanding of climate science. This disclosure is critical to understanding greenwashing, as it proves that their current environmental narratives are not based on ignorance, but on a deliberate, decades-long strategy of deception to evade responsibility. The document highlights a strategic shift from outright denial to misrepresenting their business plans and energy transitions. While publicly pledging net-zero targets, internal communications show executives privately rejecting these goals to protect existing profitable assets. They actively avoided committing to carbon reductions, suggesting their green certifications are superficial marketing tools rather than reflections of genuine operational change, which directly exposes the disconnect between corporate eco-friendly branding and actual practices. Ultimately, the financial success of these companies underscores the ineffectiveness of current scrutiny. Despite the damaging revelations, they continue to report massive profits and expand fossil fuel production, indicating that greenwashing remains a profitable shield against accountability. This is relevant to greenwashing because it demonstrates how companies can successfully maintain a sustainable image while simultaneously doubling down on the activities that cause the crisis, prioritizing short-term gains over any meaningful commitment to climate action.
