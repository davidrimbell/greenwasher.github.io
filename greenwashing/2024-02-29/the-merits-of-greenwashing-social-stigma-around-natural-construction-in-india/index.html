The Merits of Greenwashing: Social Stigma around Natural Construction in India

India is reviving natural construction to address environmental concerns and reconnect with tradition. This shift challenges the historical dominance of industrial methods, promoting sustainability and ecological harmony. It counters the modern preference for Western-style buildings by embracing vernacular techniques that align with local climates and resources. The resurgence reflects a broader desire to return to roots while meeting global green goals. By utilizing materials like earth and bamboo, architects aim for contextually relevant structures that embody contemporary ideals. This movement seeks to reverse the near-extinction of circular, climate-responsive building practices prevalent in rural India. This article is relevant to greenwashing because it highlights the genuine integration of traditional, low-impact methods into modern architecture. Unlike superficial eco-labeling, this approach prioritizes authentic, localized sustainability over aesthetic trends. It demonstrates how cultural heritage can drive real environmental benefits, distinguishing true sustainability from deceptive marketing.
