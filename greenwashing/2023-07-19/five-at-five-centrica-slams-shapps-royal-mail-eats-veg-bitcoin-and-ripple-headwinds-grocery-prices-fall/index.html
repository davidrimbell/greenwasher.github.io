Corporate leaders challenge government claims regarding hydrogen’s viability, while Royal Mail shifts to vegetable oil fuels. These moves highlight how firms rebrand alternatives to fossil fuels as sustainable solutions. Such narratives risk obscuring the complex realities of infrastructure and energy sources. This selective framing exemplifies greenwashing, where companies exaggerate environmental benefits to improve public perception without delivering substantial ecological impact. This case matters because it reveals how political and corporate statements often downplay limitations. Understanding these discrepancies helps consumers and regulators distinguish genuine sustainability efforts from misleading marketing tactics designed to exploit green interest.
