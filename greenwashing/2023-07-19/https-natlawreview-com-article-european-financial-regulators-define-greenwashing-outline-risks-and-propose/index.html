Greenwashing
ES EN

The European supervisory authorities have established a unified, high-level definition of greenwashing to address inconsistent practices across the financial sector. This consensus defines the issue as misleading sustainability claims that fail to reflect an entity’s true environmental profile. By creating a shared understanding, regulators aim to harmonize efforts and improve consistency in combating deceptive marketing within European financial markets. Reports from the banking, insurance, and securities sectors highlight that unsubstantiated claims pose significant reputational, operational, and strategic risks to firms while eroding consumer trust. These risks are exacerbated by a lack of high-quality data and mature regulatory frameworks. Consequently, market participants face substantial challenges in ensuring their sustainability disclosures are accurate, balanced, and adequately substantiated to avoid regulatory penalties and loss of credibility. This development is crucial to greenwashing discourse as it moves the industry beyond vague interpretations toward concrete supervisory standards. The unified definition serves as a foundational step for more robust enforcement and clearer consumer protection, signaling a shift toward stricter accountability. As these authorities prepare final reports, their standardized approach influences how financial institutions must structure their governance and communications to comply with emerging sustainable finance regulations.
