Australian petroleum lobby advertisement banned over 'cleaner' natural gas claim

The Australian advertising watchdog banned an industry campaign claiming natural gas is “50 per cent cleaner,” ruling the unsubstantiated comparison violated environmental advertising codes. This decision highlights the critical importance of transparency in green claims, as the advertisement failed to specify what baseline the gas was cleaner than. By omitting clear context, the campaign created a vague impression of environmental friendliness that regulators deemed misleading. The ruling identifies this vague branding as greenwashing, emphasizing that gas exploration and transport emit significant greenhouse gases, particularly methane, which has a potent warming effect. Critics argue that labeling gas as “green” ignores the severe environmental damage caused by its extraction and logistics. The ban underscores that scientific nuances cannot be reduced to blanket statements without precise substantiation, protecting consumers from deceptive marketing that obscures the fossil fuel industry’s true climate impact. This case represents a pivotal moment for regulating greenwashing in Australia, signaling stricter enforcement against misleading environmental assertions by the oil and gas sector. As global bodies urge a halt to new fossil fuel extraction to meet net-zero targets, such regulatory actions challenge the narrative of gas as a necessary “bridging fuel.” The decision reinforces the need for rigorous oversight to ensure energy transition discussions remain grounded in accurate science rather than industry-driven marketing.
