Fake Twitter profiles, Wikipedia editing and PR battles: Inside the push to greenwash the COP28 climate summit - Local News 8

The article highlights significant concerns that the UAE’s hosting of COP28 and the appointment of a fossil fuel CEO as president reflect a coordinated effort to distract from its lack of genuine climate ambition. Critics argue that appointing the head of a state-owned oil company to lead global climate negotiations creates a profound conflict of interest, suggesting that the summit’s primary goal may be to maintain the status quo rather than drive the necessary transition away from fossil fuels. This structural controversy undermines the integrity of the negotiations, as the leader of the process is directly tied to the industry the summit aims to regulate. Compounding these governance issues is an alleged covert influence campaign involving digital manipulation and public relations spending. Investigations reveal attempts to alter Wikipedia entries to sanitize the president’s image and the use of artificial accounts to amplify pro-UAE narratives online. These tactics indicate a strategic use of greenwashing techniques to manufacture consent and obscure the country’s continued expansion of oil production, effectively using sophisticated disinformation to shield its economic interests from scientific scrutiny and public criticism. This situation is relevant to greenwashing because it demonstrates how nations can leverage high-profile environmental events to rebrand themselves as sustainability leaders while simultaneously investing in fossil fuel infrastructure. The disparity between the UAE’s ambitious PR assertions and its actual policies—such as increasing oil output despite setting net-zero targets—exemplifies the core mechanism of greenwashing: presenting a misleadingly positive environmental image to deflect accountability. It serves as a critical warning about how powerful interest groups may infiltrate and manipulate international climate processes to delay meaningful action.
