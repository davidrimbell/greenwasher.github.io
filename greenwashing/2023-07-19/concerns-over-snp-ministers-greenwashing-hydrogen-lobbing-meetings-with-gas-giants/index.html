Concerns over SNP ministers' 'greenwashing' hydrogen lobbing meetings with gas giants

The article highlights how the Scottish Government’s enthusiastic promotion of hydrogen as a major economic and climate solution risks enabling greenwashing by the fossil fuel industry. Despite green hydrogen being the only environmentally sustainable option, the government has pledged significant public funds to an industry heavily reliant on expensive, less efficient blue and grey hydrogen derived from methane. By labeling these fossil-fuel-based variants as "low carbon," officials are inadvertently legitimizing polluters' claims that their extraction methods are climate-friendly, thereby obscuring the significant environmental damage caused by methane leakage and carbon capture limitations. Furthermore, the close ties between ministers and oil and gas lobbyists raise serious concerns about regulatory capture and the influence of corporate interests on public policy. Critics argue that the government is prioritizing the marketing hype of companies like BP and Shell over independent evidence, which suggests that direct electrification via heat pumps is a far more efficient and cost-effective pathway to decarbonization. This strategic focus allows fossil fuel giants to maintain their business models for decades longer than necessary, framing hydrogen as a necessary transitional fuel while continuing to lobby against more immediate, practical renewable energy solutions. This situation is critically relevant to greenwashing because it demonstrates how state entities can amplify corporate disinformation by funding and endorsing technologies that appear green but are fundamentally tied to fossil fuel infrastructure. The classification of harmful fossil-fuel hydrogen as low-carbon creates a misleading narrative that allows polluters to continue extracting resources while claiming climate action. Consequently, public resources are diverted toward inefficient technologies that delay genuine emission reductions, serving the financial interests of the oil and gas industry rather than the urgent needs of the climate crisis.
