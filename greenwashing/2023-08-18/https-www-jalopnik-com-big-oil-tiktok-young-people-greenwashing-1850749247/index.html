Greenwashing
ES EN

Major oil corporations like Shell and ExxonMobil are strategically leveraging social media platforms such as TikTok and Instagram to rebrand their image among Generation Z. By collaborating with lifestyle influencers whose content does not naturally align with fossil fuels, these companies aim to normalize their presence and shift public perception. This approach transforms standard advertising into subtle cultural integration, ensuring that young audiences associate these brands with everyday convenience and positive community interactions rather than environmental degradation. This marketing strategy goes beyond promoting specific fuel products; it represents a calculated effort to secure the social license to operate by winning the trust of a digitally native demographic. Research indicates that these campaigns reach billions globally, exploiting the high engagement rates of younger users. The primary implication is that oil giants are attempting to embed themselves into the social fabric of youth culture, thereby diluting scrutiny of their core business models and fostering a sense of familiarity that discourages critical questioning of their environmental impact. This practice is a quintessential example of greenwashing, as it uses the guise of low-carbon transition and community support to mask continued heavy investment in fossil fuels. By projecting an image of responsible sustainability through influencer partnerships, companies distract from the reality of their substantial carbon footprints. Ultimately, this narrative manipulation seeks to maintain profitability and public acceptance without making substantive changes to their reliance on harmful energy sources, thereby deceiving consumers about their true environmental contributions.
