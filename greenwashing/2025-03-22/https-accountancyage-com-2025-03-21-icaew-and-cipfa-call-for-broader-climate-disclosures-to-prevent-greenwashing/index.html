Greenwashing
EN ES

Chartered accountancy bodies ICAEW and CIPFA argue that the International Public Sector Accounting Standards Board must broaden its proposed climate disclosure standards to effectively prevent greenwashing. They warn that the current narrow definition, which limits reporting to policies explicitly designed for climate outcomes, creates significant loopholes. By allowing entities to exclude policies based solely on their stated objectives, the current framework risks obscuring the true environmental impact of public sector activities, thereby undermining transparency and accountability. Instead, these organizations advocate for the inclusion of all material policies that contribute to national climate targets, regardless of their primary goal. This broader scope captures both positive and negative climate impacts, offering a more accurate picture of government efforts and necessary trade-offs. Restricting disclosures to only climate-focused policies ignores the significant secondary effects of initiatives aimed at other sectors, such as economic growth. Including these policies ensures comprehensive reporting that meets user needs and prevents the manipulation of disclosure boundaries. Implementing these changes requires a phased approach rather than delays, allowing public sector entities time to upskill their workforce and establish robust data collection processes. Since much of this information is subjective, the ability to assure these reports is critical for building trust. Ultimately, expanding the definition of reportable policies is essential to drive genuine climate progress, ensuring that public sector reporting reflects reality rather than enabling greenwashing through selective disclosure.
