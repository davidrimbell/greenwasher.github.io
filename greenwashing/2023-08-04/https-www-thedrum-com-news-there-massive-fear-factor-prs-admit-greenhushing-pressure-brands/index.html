Greenwashing
ES EN

This article identifies "greenhushing" as a growing corporate trend where companies deliberately withhold communication about genuine environmental efforts. Driven by a fear of scrutiny, accusations of insufficient action, and the volatile economic climate, businesses are choosing silence to avoid reputational damage. This caution represents a significant shift in corporate communication, moving away from transparency toward risk avoidance despite having positive initiatives. The primary implication of this behavior is the loss of valuable brand equity and public trust. By staying silent, companies miss critical opportunities to engage employees, attract talent, and foster industry-wide collaboration. Experts argue that admitting setbacks and sharing progress openly, even when imperfect, builds stronger stakeholder relationships and inspires collective progress toward sustainability goals, whereas silence stifles innovation and accountability. Relevant to greenwashing, this phenomenon highlights the complex spectrum of environmental misrepresentation. While greenwashing involves exaggerating impact, greenhushing is a failure to communicate truthfully, which undermines the credibility of the entire sector. To combat both issues, PR professionals are urged to upskill in sustainability regulations and rigorously fact-check claims. Building a robust, legally sound narrative prevents misleading assertions while encouraging authentic engagement, ensuring that honest efforts are recognized rather than hidden.
