Greenwashing
ES EN

The UNEP’s new Sustainable Fashion Playbook argues that addressing production impacts alone is insufficient to combat greenwashing, as the core issue remains the industry’s promotion of excessive consumption. Communications professionals are urged to shift narratives away from novelty and identity-driven purchasing, helping consumers break the cycle of disposability by making sustainable habits appear more appealing than the thrill of buying new items. Regulatory efforts to standardize environmental claims, such as the EU’s Green Claims Directive, are essential for preventing deception, yet they carry the risk of oversimplification. If frameworks become too rigid, they may mislead consumers with binary judgments like "natural is good," ignoring complex realities like microfiber shedding. Consequently, effective communication must move beyond technical labeling to provide nuanced, truthful context that empowers informed decision-making without relying on misleading shortcuts. Ultimately, the article highlights that marketing has historically fueled unsustainable trends, creating consumer cynicism toward "purpose-led" branding. To genuinely tackle greenwashing, the industry must redefine status and success through storytelling that supports policy goals rather than exploiting emotional desires. Without redirecting these cultural narratives toward circularity, even accurate environmental claims will fail to drive the systemic behavioral changes necessary for true sustainability.
