La catástrofe del Rana Plaza y el greenwashing en la moda

Ten years after the Rana Plaza disaster, Greenpeace reveals that the fast fashion industry’s widespread promotion of sustainability is largely greenwashing. Although major brands market their own eco-labels and claim ethical production, their practices remain exploitative and environmentally damaging. This deceptive marketing masks the continued use of toxic chemicals, poor labor conditions, and a lack of supply chain transparency, demonstrating that current efforts are superficial rather than substantive. The core issue lies in the massive overproduction of synthetic textiles, which generates enormous waste and relies on fossil fuels. With production volumes soaring and recycling rates negligible, the industry contributes significantly to pollution in the Global South. This model prioritizes profit over planetary health, creating mountains of non-recyclable clothing that destroy ecosystems and livelihoods, thereby contradicting the environmental benefits brands claim to offer. This report is critical to understanding greenwashing because it exposes the gap between corporate rhetoric and reality. It highlights how companies use vague labels to distract consumers while avoiding responsibility for their environmental and social impact. True sustainability requires a radical shift toward durable, repairable, and second-hand models, supported by strict regulations and radical transparency, rather than mere marketing spin.
