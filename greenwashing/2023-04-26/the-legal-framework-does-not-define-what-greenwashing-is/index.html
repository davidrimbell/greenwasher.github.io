“The legal framework does not define what greenwashing is”

“The legal framework does not define what greenwashing is”

The article argues that labeling the current ESG landscape as solely greenwashing is an oversimplification that ignores the complex reality faced by asset managers. While many investors perceive products as misleadingly marketed, the author contends that much of this discrepancy stems from unintentional gaps rather than deliberate deception. The core issue is not necessarily malicious intent, but rather the structural difficulties in aligning public perception with regulatory transparency standards, leading to widespread misunderstanding of what constitutes greenwashing. A significant driver of this perception is the lack of reliable data and harmonized metrics, particularly for non-liquid assets where ESG rating agencies are scarce. Because regulatory frameworks like the SFDR mandate transparency without defining strict classification thresholds, asset managers struggle to verify and communicate their sustainability commitments accurately. This ambiguity allows for varied interpretations of an asset’s "greenness," causing funds that are compliant with disclosures yet lack deep ESG integration to be unfairly scrutinized as greenwashing practices. To mitigate these risks and improve authenticity, the author advises integrating ESG strategies from the very inception of a financial product, rather than treating it as an afterthought. Asset managers must invest in internal assessment tools and collaborate with sector-specific regulatory experts to navigate the technical complexity of compliance. By prioritizing rigorous data collection and transparent early-stage planning, firms can better align their operations with regulatory expectations, thereby reducing the opacity that fuels accusations of greenwashing. This perspective is crucial for understanding greenwashing, as it shifts the focus from simple blame to the systemic challenges of data scarcity and regulatory complexity that enable misleading perceptions.
