Opinion: Cal Fire’s forest management undermining California’s climate goals

Opinion: Cal Fire’s forest management undermining California’s climate goals

Cal Fire’s management of California’s state forests exemplifies institutional greenwashing by prioritizing industrial logging under the guise of climate conservation. The agency employs flawed carbon accounting models that systematically underestimate emissions from felling old-growth trees while overestimating the speed and extent of regrowth. This approach ignores the immediate release of stored carbon and the long-term viability of regenerating forests under current climate conditions, effectively misleading the public about the environmental impact of these operations. By labeling sustainable timber harvesting as an ecological benefit, Cal Fire masks activities that actually degrade forest resilience and biodiversity. Recent logging proposals target healthy, old second-growth stands that naturally resist fire and sequester significant carbon, instead creating dense, flammable juvenile stands. This practice not only fails to mitigate wildfire risk but exacerbates it by drying out soils and promoting invasive undergrowth, contradicting the agency’s stated mission to protect public lands and reduce climate threats. This case is relevant to greenwashing as it demonstrates how a public agency, typically exempt from corporate scrutiny, utilizes misleading narratives and opaque scientific data to justify environmentally harmful practices. By presenting extractive logging as a climate solution, the agency erodes public trust and delays genuine, science-based stewardship. It highlights the urgent need for transparent, accurate assessments of forest health and carbon storage, proving that greenwashing is not exclusive to the private sector but persists in public policy where political convenience overrides ecological reality.
