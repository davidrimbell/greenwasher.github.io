Ecologists critique govt’s tree planting drive as ‘greenwash’ – 6 June 2026 | O Heraldo

The article highlights how the Green NH66 Mission serves as a prime example of greenwashing by prioritizing high-profile public relations over genuine ecological restoration. Environmentalists argue that the initiative is a superficial formality, characterized by the destruction of ancient, shade-providing trees for infrastructure development, only to be replaced by unsuitable exotic species that fail to support local biodiversity or wildlife. This approach disregards the complexity of natural ecosystems, substituting functional forests with ornamental plantings that offer little long-term environmental benefit. Further undermining the project’s credibility is the alleged mismanagement of compensatory funds and a lack of necessary infrastructure for tree survival. Experts point out that mass planting without prior preparation, such as securing water sources or protecting saplings, leads to high mortality rates, effectively turning the initiative into a symbolic gesture rather than a sustainable effort. The misuse of funds for administrative costs instead of actual reforestation exacerbates human-animal conflicts as indigenous food sources vanish, demonstrating the disconnect between policy execution and ecological reality. Ultimately, the article asserts that large-scale roadside plantations cannot offset the irreversible damage caused by mega-infrastructure projects, which contribute to land sealing and severe groundwater depletion. The prevailing narrative that simple planting drives can mitigate environmental loss is described as a dangerous fallacy that distracts from root causes like desertification and climate change. The text calls for a shift toward credible, localized strategies involving community engagement and rigorous maintenance, rather than uncoordinated mass plantings that merely mask ongoing ecological degradation.
