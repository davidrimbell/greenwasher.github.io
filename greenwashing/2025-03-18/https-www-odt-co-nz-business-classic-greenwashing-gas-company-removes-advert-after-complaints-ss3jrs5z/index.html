Greenwashing
EN ES

Clarus withdrew an advertisement claiming its gas was renewable after the Advertising Standards Authority addressed complaints of greenwashing. The ad misled consumers by implying a fully renewable product, whereas the supply was actually a blend of a small amount of biomethane with the majority of fossil fuel gas. This incident highlights how ambiguous marketing can deceive the public about the environmental impact of energy sources. The core issue lies in the disparity between consumer perception and reality. While the company produces some renewable biomethane from organic waste, it constitutes only a tiny fraction of their total gas network. By not clearly communicating this blending process, the advertisement created a false impression of sustainability, which is a hallmark of greenwashing tactics used to maintain consumer trust and market share. This case is relevant to understanding greenwashing because it demonstrates how partial renewable contributions are used to market largely fossil-fuel-dependent services as eco-friendly. It underscores the importance of transparency in energy marketing, ensuring consumers are not misled into believing they are supporting a fully clean energy transition when the reality involves significant continued reliance on potent climate-warming fossil fuels.
