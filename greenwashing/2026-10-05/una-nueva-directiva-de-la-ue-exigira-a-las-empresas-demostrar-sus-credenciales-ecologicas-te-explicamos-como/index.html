Una nueva directiva de la UE exigirá a las empresas demostrar sus credenciales ecológicas. Te explicamos cómo

The new European directive aims to eliminate greenwashing by banning vague environmental claims and requiring that sustainability labels be backed by independent certifications. This forces companies to reassess their communications—not to silence their commitment, but to ensure that their statements are verifiable and transparent, thereby eliminating the distrust generated by misleading marketing. Corporate silence, although intuitive in the face of legal risk, carries significant costs, as consumers perceive a lack of communication as inaction. Conversely, companies that invest in robust verification systems build a defensible foundation for their claims. Third-party certification acts as a crucial layer of governance that distributes responsibility and provides the evidence needed to distinguish genuine commitment from greenwashing. In conclusion, the regulation empowers consumers by demanding honesty and data, transforming credible certification into an essential strategic tool. Rather than being a liability, this independent verification seal enables companies to demonstrate due diligence and protect themselves from scrutiny, establishing trust as the new standard for competing in a market where every environmental claim must be substantiated by evidence.
