ASCI proposes guidelines to check on greenwashing
The Advertising Standards Council of India has proposed draft guidelines to combat greenwashing by mandating evidence-based environmental advertising. These regulations require absolute claims like "eco-friendly" to meet high substantiation standards, ensuring transparency and preventing misleading marketing practices that exploit consumer goodwill. Crucially, advertisers must evaluate claims based on a product’s full life cycle, prohibiting selective highlighting of minor benefits while ignoring larger environmental harms. This approach directly addresses common greenwashing tactics where companies emphasize positive attributes of specific components to distract from the overall negative ecological impact of their products or services. Furthermore, the guidelines restrict aspirational future claims and enforce strict rules for carbon offset disclosures. By requiring clear certification sources and scientific backing for biodegradability, the initiative aims to restore trust in green marketing. This is vital for consumers seeking authentic sustainability, as it establishes a framework for accountability and reduces deceptive environmental representations in the marketplace.

Climate investing: Are ESG ratings holding back green growth?
The article argues that green investing is failing to effectively address climate change primarily due to flawed and non-standardized ESG rating systems. Senior banking executives and experts highlight that these ratings often prioritize a company’s resilience to external risks rather than its actual environmental impact. This fundamental disconnect allows companies to achieve high scores without delivering genuine ecological benefits, leading to significant capital misallocation and the erosion of trust in sustainable finance. This ambiguity creates a fertile ground for greenwashing, where corporations can parade superficial credentials to attract investment without committing to substantial planetary impact. The lack of international standards and transparent methodologies means that ratings can be misleading, as evidenced by controversial comparisons where polluters scored higher than green technology firms. Consequently, investors face increased risks of supporting initiatives that do not align with their sustainability goals, undermining the credibility of the entire green economy transition. Regulatory bodies are now recognizing these dangers and moving to impose stricter oversight on ESG data providers. However, the immediate relevance to greenwashing lies in the current absence of unified standards, which allows rating agencies to dilute the accuracy of their signals through competition and opacity. Until a clear, rigorous framework is established to differentiate true sustainability performance from mere corporate posturing, the risk of misleading investors will remain uncomfortably high.

This discrepancy is highly relevant to understanding greenwashing because it exposes the discrepancy between marketing narratives and substantive political advocacy. WMW | Air pollution standard backed by MEPs is ‘worse than useless’
The proposed Euro 7 standard fails to meaningfully reduce air pollution, effectively serving as a tool for greenwashing rather than genuine environmental protection. By maintaining previous nitrous oxide limits for petrol cars and weakening testing conditions for trucks and vans, the legislation allows dirty vehicles to be marketed as clean while regulators permit them access to low-emission zones and tax breaks. This regulatory rollback results from intense industry lobbying based on misinformation, significantly delaying implementation and relaxing limits that were originally designed to prevent thousands of premature deaths. Consequently, the new standard offers negligible improvements over its predecessor, creating a false sense of progress while ignoring critical pollutants and real-world driving conditions. T&E urges lawmakers to rename the draft as ‘Euro 6 F’ or withdraw it entirely, highlighting the danger of deceptive labeling in emissions standards. This article is relevant to greenwashing because it exposes how weak regulations enable carmakers to falsely advertise vehicles as environmentally friendly, misleading consumers and policymakers about actual air quality impacts and public health risks.
