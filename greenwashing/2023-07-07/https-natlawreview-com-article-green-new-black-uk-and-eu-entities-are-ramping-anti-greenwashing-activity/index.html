Greenwashing
ES EN

European regulators are intensifying their crackdown on greenwashing in the fashion sector through strict new directives. The primary goal is to eliminate deceptive environmental claims by mandating greater transparency regarding manufacturing processes, labor conditions, and material sourcing. This regulatory push aims to ensure that sustainability labels reflect genuine ecological efforts rather than mere marketing tactics. To support this transparency, the European Commission proposes frameworks like the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation and a Digital Product Passport. These tools are designed to track a product’s lifecycle impacts, enabling consumers and businesses to verify environmental assertions. The underlying implication is a shift toward mandatory, verified data, making it significantly harder for brands to obscure their true environmental footprint or make unsubstantiated "green" promises. However, industry groups warn that overly rigid requirements might harm craftsmanship and competitiveness. They advocate for balanced regulations that consider the entire value chain. This tension is crucial for understanding greenwashing, as it highlights the conflict between ambitious policy goals and practical implementation, showing how vague or unenforceable standards can inadvertently sustain misleading practices if not carefully designed.
