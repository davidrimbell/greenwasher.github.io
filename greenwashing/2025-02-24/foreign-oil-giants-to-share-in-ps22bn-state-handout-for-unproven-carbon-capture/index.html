Foreign oil giants to share in £22bn state handout for 'unproven' carbon capture

The article highlights a controversial UK government decision to allocate billions in taxpayer subsidies to develop carbon capture and storage (CCS) technology, a strategy heavily promoted by major oil and gas companies. Critics, including climate campaigners and fuel poverty advocates, argue this move constitutes a significant greenwashing con. They contend that the technology is unproven and risky, yet the financial burden will be passed directly to consumers through increased energy bills, effectively using public money to sustain the profitability and operations of fossil fuel giants that have already amassed enormous profits. This initiative is particularly relevant to the concept of greenwashing because it allows energy corporations to position themselves as leaders in climate action while continuing their core business of extracting and burning fossil fuels. By securing state funding for CCS, companies like BP and Equinor can market their activities as "net zero" solutions, potentially distracting from the lack of tangible progress in reducing emissions. The funding model, which relies heavily on consumer levies rather than corporate investment, suggests a strategy of offloading the costs of experimental technology onto the public, thereby protecting corporate assets while creating an illusion of environmental responsibility. Ultimately, the article illustrates how greenwashing operates through policy lobbying, where governments endorse technological fixes that benefit polluters financially without requiring immediate reductions in fossil fuel consumption. The narrative underscores the ethical concern of rewarding companies with massive state handouts for technologies that have failed to deliver reliable results over decades. This dynamic risks delaying genuine decarbonization efforts by creating a false sense of security, allowing the industry to continue contributing to climate crisis while presenting a veneer of sustainable innovation funded by the very households they exploit.
