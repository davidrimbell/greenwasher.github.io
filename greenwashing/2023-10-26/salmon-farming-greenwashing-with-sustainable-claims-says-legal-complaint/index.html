Salmon farming 'greenwashing' with 'sustainable' claims, says legal complaint

The article highlights a formal complaint against the Scottish salmon farming industry for falsely marketing its products as “sustainable.” Environmental groups argue that the term misleads consumers who associate sustainability with positive or neutral environmental impacts. However, the industry’s practices, including heavy chemical use, reliance on wild fish for feed, and harm to local ecosystems, contradict these claims, suggesting a deliberate attempt to obscure the negative ecological footprint of farmed salmon production. Central to the greenwashing allegation is the industry’s use of vague language to imply environmental responsibility without substantive proof. Trade bodies promote products as meeting “sustainable development” standards and emphasize nutritional benefits while ignoring critical issues like parasite dispersal, antibiotic overuse, and high mortality rates. This discrepancy between marketing narratives and operational realities serves to manipulate consumer perception, leveraging the desire for ethical consumption to sell a product that causes significant ecological damage. This case is highly relevant to greenwashing as it exemplifies how industries exploit ambiguous terminology to gain social license and protect economic interests despite proven environmental harm. The complaint underscores the regulatory gap where companies can continue making misleading sustainability claims, eroding public trust. It also reflects a broader trend where the fashion and food sectors face scrutiny from consumer protection agencies for deceptive green marketing, urging stricter enforcement to prevent the dilution of meaningful environmental standards.
