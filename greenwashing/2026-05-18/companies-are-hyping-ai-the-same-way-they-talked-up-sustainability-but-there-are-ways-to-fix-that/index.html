Companies are hyping AI the same way they talked up sustainability, but there are ways to fix that

The article identifies "AI washing" as a deceptive marketing trend where companies overstate their artificial intelligence capabilities to boost valuation, drawing direct parallels to historical greenwashing practices. This phenomenon allows businesses to capitalize on investor hype while obscuring the lack of substantive technological advancement or significant societal benefits, creating a cycle of misinformation similar to the unsustainable claims seen in corporate environmental reporting. The persistence of this deception stems from four critical failures: the absence of standardized metrics, incomplete disclosure frameworks regarding material impacts, a lack of rigorous third-party verification, and insufficient legal enforcement. Without common benchmarks and independent audits, companies can easily mask poor performance or ethical risks, while the minimal penalties for false advertising make deceptive marketing a rational, profitable business strategy rather than a reputational hazard. This analysis is highly relevant to understanding greenwashing because it demonstrates how unregulated innovation sectors replicate the same accountability gaps that plagued sustainability efforts for decades. The author argues that AI is advancing faster than regulatory frameworks can adapt, suggesting that without mandatory standards and robust penalties, AI washing will likely eclipse greenwashing in scale and harm. The piece serves as a cautionary tale, highlighting that voluntary corporate commitments are insufficient and that durable political support for strict enforcement is essential to prevent widespread corporate deception.
