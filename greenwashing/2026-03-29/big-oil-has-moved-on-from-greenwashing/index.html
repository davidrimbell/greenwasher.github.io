Big Oil has moved on from ‘greenwashing.’

The article reveals a significant strategic shift by major oil companies from traditional greenwashing to "gaslighting," where they no longer attempt to appear sustainable but instead sow doubt about the necessity of transitioning away from fossil fuels. By reframing their continued expansion as essential for energy security and economic stability, these corporations have moved away from highlighting minor green initiatives to aggressively defending fossil fuel dependence as a pragmatic necessity. This narrative change aims to confuse the public regarding the true environmental impact of their operations and the urgency of climate action. This rhetorical pivot coincides with a broader societal retreat from climate urgency, allowing oil giants to position natural gas and oil as indispensable for modern life, including supporting technological advancements like AI. Research indicates that these companies often discuss renewables only to highlight their perceived flaws or to legitimize further fossil fuel infrastructure, such as using wind power to energize oil platforms. Consequently, their messaging serves to maintain their "social license to operate" by portraying fossil fuels as a balanced, responsible component of any energy future, thereby discouraging public support for a rapid phase-out. This analysis is critically relevant to understanding greenwashing because it illustrates how the tactic has evolved from superficial eco-marketing to active misinformation. Rather than merely exaggerating green credentials, companies are now systematically undermining the scientific consensus on the need to stop building fossil fuel infrastructure. This shift demonstrates how corporate influence can distort public perception of energy realities, complicating efforts to address climate change by framing necessary decarbonization as an economic risk rather than an environmental imperative.
