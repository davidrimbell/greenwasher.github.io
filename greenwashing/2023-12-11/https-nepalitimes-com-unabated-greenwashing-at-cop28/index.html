Greenwashing
ES EN

The article critiques COP28 as a spectacle of greenwashing where major fossil fuel exporters prioritize political theater and incremental adjustments over the urgent, systemic phase-out of petroleum. By hosting the conference in a leading oil-exporting nation and allowing industry representatives to shape discourse, the summit exemplifies how climate negotiations are manipulated to delay meaningful action. The focus on semantic tricks like "unabated" emissions and unproven geo-engineering solutions, such as carbon capture, serves to protect incumbent interests rather than address the accelerating climate crisis. The author highlights that current fossil fuel consumption far exceeds the limits required to meet even outdated warming targets, rendering incremental calibration insufficient. The push for technologies like solar radiation modification is portrayed as a dangerous distraction that ignores the immediate physical realities of climate breakdown, such as record-breaking temperatures and ice loss. This reliance on speculative solutions by the world’s largest polluters signals a deliberate lack of political will to reduce carbon footprints, effectively continuing a pattern of delayed responsibility that has persisted since early climate agreements. This global inaction carries devastating implications for vulnerable nations, which face existential threats from rising seas and melting glaciers despite contributing minimally to the problem. The text argues that developing countries must prioritize domestic renewable energy independence, such as hydropower, to ensure economic survival rather than relying on inadequate international compensation. The relevance to greenwashing lies in exposing how the rhetoric of international cooperation masks the continued expansion of fossil fuel infrastructure, offering victims of climate change false hope while the root causes of destruction remain unaddressed.
