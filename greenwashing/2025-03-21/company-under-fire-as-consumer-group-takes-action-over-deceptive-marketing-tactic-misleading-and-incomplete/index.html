Company under fire as consumer group takes action over deceptive marketing tactic: 'Misleading and incomplete'

Company under fire as consumer group takes action over deceptive marketing tactic: 'Misleading and incomplete'

Swiss regulators are investigating running shoe brand On for alleged greenwashing regarding its "Cyclon" subscription program. The Romandy Consumers Association claims the initiative is misleading because, despite promises to recycle worn-out shoes, no footwear has actually been recycled. This marks one of the first enforcement actions under a new Swiss anti-greenwashing law, highlighting the critical gap between marketing circular economy concepts and their actual implementation. This case underscores how fabricated sustainability claims deceive eco-conscious consumers and undermine genuine environmental progress. When companies exaggerate their green efforts, it erodes public trust and prevents individuals from making truly responsible purchasing decisions. The investigation serves as a vital test case for ensuring that all environmental assertions are objectively verifiable, rather than mere marketing tactics designed to manipulate buyer behavior. Relevant to greenwashing, this lawsuit demonstrates increased governmental oversight aimed at holding brands accountable for their environmental promises. It emphasizes the necessity of strict regulation to stop false claims that mislead consumers into supporting wasteful practices under the guise of sustainability. Ultimately, this development reinforces the importance of transparency and third-party verification in the fashion industry to ensure that sustainability efforts are authentic and effective.
