Plástico vs papel: ¿Qué envolturas son más ecológicas? | Teletica

This article reveals how the public perception that cardboard is inherently more eco-friendly than plastic constitutes a common form of greenwashing. Companies and marketing campaigns exploit the favorable reputation of paper, concealing their own significant environmental impacts, such as high emissions during production and transportation. This misleading narrative diverts attention toward a false dichotomy, when in reality both materials carry considerable environmental burdens and complex waste disposal chains. The study’s relevance lies in debunking myths about recyclability. Although cardboard is technically easier to process in many places, its greater weight increases transportation emissions, and mixed packaging, such as paper cups with linings, proves difficult to recycle. On the other hand, conventional plastic is rarely recycled and often becomes contaminated with harmful substances, while bioplastics are promoted as a green solution despite not being free from chemical risks and problems linked to their raw materials, such as deforestation. Finally, the text concludes that focusing on choosing between paper or plastic is a dangerous distraction that allows an uncontrolled waste culture to persist. The true critique of greenwashing here is its inability to promote real systemic change. Instead of seeking apparently “green” packaging alternatives, the solution involves reducing overall consumption and adopting reuse models, thereby challenging the narrative that merely substituting materials is sufficient to resolve the environmental crisis.
