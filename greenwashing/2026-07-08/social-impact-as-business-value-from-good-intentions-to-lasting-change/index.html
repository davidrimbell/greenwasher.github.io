Social impact as business value: from good intentions to lasting change

The article argues that credible social impact requires moving beyond performative corporate social responsibility toward measurable, long-term commitments. Generali Vietnam’s CEO emphasizes that trust is built not through goodwill or short-term campaigns, but through alignment with core values, consistent support, and transparent reporting. This approach shifts the business focus from merely generating financial profit to addressing systemic social challenges, thereby redefining long-term value as intrinsically linked to community well-being and sustainable growth. A major critique of current corporate engagement is its tendency toward fragmentation and short program cycles, which often fail to address the complexity of issues like early childhood development. The piece advocates for system-based approaches that combine business resources with the technical expertise of partners like UNICEF and government bodies. By integrating efforts across healthcare, education, and policy, companies can avoid isolated interventions and ensure their contributions are technically sound, locally relevant, and capable of creating lasting structural change rather than temporary relief. This distinction is crucial for understanding and combating greenwashing, as it highlights the difference between superficial branding and genuine accountability. Greenwashing often relies on the scale and visibility of activities, whereas this model prioritizes the quality of outcomes and honest measurement of progress. By demanding consistency and long-term partnership over quick wins, businesses can demonstrate authentic responsibility, proving that their social impact is a strategic investment in societal resilience rather than a marketing tactic designed to mask environmental or social shortcomings.
