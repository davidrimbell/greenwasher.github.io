Luxury to fast fashion: How green European brands really are – DW – 11/24/2025

Luxury to fast fashion: How green European brands really are – DW – 11/24/2025

Fashion companies have significantly increased their sustainability rhetoric to address growing consumer concern over environmental and labor abuses. However, an analysis of major European brands reveals that only about half of their stated goals have been met. This gap between promise and performance highlights a systemic issue where voluntary corporate pledges often fail to translate into tangible ecological improvements, leaving the industry’s substantial carbon footprint and pollution largely unaddressed. The industry’s reliance on ambiguous language and vague metrics facilitates greenwashing, allowing brands to mask their continued dependence on fossil-fuel-derived synthetics. Many commitments are either unverified or defined so loosely that consumers cannot hold companies accountable. Furthermore, the silence from luxury brands, who lack transparency despite having fewer price constraints, suggests a strategic avoidance of scrutiny. This opacity prevents genuine comparison and allows misleading claims to persist, undermining efforts to establish sustainable fashion as the norm. Ultimately, the article argues that self-regulation is insufficient to combat greenwashing, necessitating strict legislative oversight. While new European reporting directives aim to standardize disclosures, political delays threaten progress. The core implication is that without binding laws and rigorous evidence requirements, sustainability claims remain marketing tools rather than operational realities, perpetuating environmental harm under the guise of responsibility.
