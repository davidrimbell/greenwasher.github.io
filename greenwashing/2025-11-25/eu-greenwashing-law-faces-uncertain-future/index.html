EU greenwashing law faces uncertain future

EU greenwashing law faces uncertain future

The proposed EU Green Claims Directive remains stalled, with its future hinging on the exclusion of micro-enterprises from its scope. This exemption is a critical pivot point, as these small businesses constitute the vast majority of EU companies. While the directive has not been formally withdrawn, the lack of agreement between political bodies suggests delayed implementation, creating significant uncertainty for lawmakers and businesses awaiting clearer regulatory standards. Despite this legislative limbo, existing consumer protection laws are already active and strictly enforced across member states. These rules prohibit vague environmental terms and unsubstantiated climate neutrality claims, signaling that the regulatory landscape for green marketing is tightening rather than relaxing. National authorities are increasingly investigating misleading messages, meaning companies face immediate compliance pressures even in the absence of new specific legislation. This situation is highly relevant to greenwashing because it demonstrates that the fight against misleading environmental claims is already underway through existing enforcement mechanisms. Businesses cannot rely on regulatory delays as an excuse for vague marketing. Instead, they must proactively build transparent, verifiable evidence for all sustainability assertions. Whether due to current laws or future directives, the imperative for scientific substantiation and independent verification is becoming the new standard for avoiding penalties and maintaining trust.
