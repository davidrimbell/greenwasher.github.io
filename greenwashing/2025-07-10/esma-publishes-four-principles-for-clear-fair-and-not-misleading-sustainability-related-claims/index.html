ESMA Publishes Four Principles for Clear, Fair and Not Misleading Sustainability-Related Claims

ESMA Publishes Four Principles for Clear, Fair and Not Misleading Sustainability-Related Claims

The European Securities and Markets Authority recently released thematic guidance warning financial market participants that complex sustainability claims risk misleading investors, thereby facilitating greenwashing. This regulatory intervention highlights the urgent need for transparency and accuracy in environmental, social, and governance disclosures to prevent both intentional deception and inadvertent misinterpretation by market participants. The guidance establishes four core principles: claims must be accurate, accessible, substantiated, and up-to-date. By prohibiting vague language, cherry-picking data, or omitting relevant negative aspects, ESMA aims to ensure that sustainability profiles are presented fairly and consistently. These standards require clear evidence and logical reasoning behind every assertion, demanding that firms avoid oversimplification while providing sufficient detail for investors to understand the true nature of a product’s ESG credentials. This development is critically relevant to greenwashing because it signals intensifying regulatory scrutiny across the asset management sector. The overlap with existing frameworks like the UK FCA’s guide demonstrates a coordinated global effort to curb misleading practices. Consequently, firms face mounting pressure to adapt their communication strategies and substantiate their claims rigorously, as regulatory bodies expect the industry to have already aligned with these heightened standards of honesty and clarity.
