Brazil: Carbon credit projects allegedly profiting people and businesses fined for destroying the Amazon, says Reuters; incl. companies' comments - Business & Human Rights Resource Centre

Global companies invest heavily in Brazilian conservation projects to purchase carbon credits, yet investigations reveal these initiatives often benefit entities previously fined for illegal deforestation. By funding landowners and firms penalized for unauthorized logging and timber violations, corporations indirectly support the illicit trade responsible for destroying the Amazon rainforest, despite claims that their specific pledges remain intact. This paradox highlights a critical flaw in the voluntary carbon market’s verification processes. Major multinationals rely on accredited firms and registries to ensure quality control, assuming these mechanisms filter out bad actors. However, the presence of convicted offenders in project ownership structures demonstrates that current standards may fail to prevent financial flows from supporting illegal activities, undermining the environmental integrity buyers believe they are purchasing. The article is vital for understanding greenwashing because it exposes how corporate climate actions can mask complicity in ongoing ecological destruction. It illustrates a scenario where companies exploit green credentials to offset emissions while their investments sustain the very actors destroying the source of those offsets, revealing a systemic failure to align financial incentives with genuine conservation outcomes.
