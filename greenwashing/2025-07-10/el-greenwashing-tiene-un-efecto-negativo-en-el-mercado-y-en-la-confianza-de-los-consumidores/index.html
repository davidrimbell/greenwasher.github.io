“El ‘greenwashing’ tiene un efecto negativo en el mercado y en la confianza de los consumidores”

Greenwashing constitutes deceptive marketing that falsely claims environmental sustainability in order to exploit consumer concerns. It relies on vague or unsupported statements, misleading buyers who are genuinely seeking eco-friendly options. This practice highlights the gap between corporate messaging and actual environmental impact, serving as a primary example of how brands manipulate perceptions of responsibility without substantive commitment. The repercussions for companies are severe, encompassing significant legal penalties and lasting reputational damage. As regulations tighten, particularly in regions like the European Union, firms face stricter requirements for verified data and independent audits. Losing consumer trust is often irreversible, demonstrating that transparency and accountability are no longer optional but essential for maintaining market presence and avoiding sanctions. Ultimately, greenwashing erodes overall market confidence, causing skepticism that harms both deceptive brands and genuinely sustainable businesses. When consumers feel deceived, they may abandon responsible consumption habits, creating a cycle of distrust. This dynamic underscores the critical need for rigorous standards and honest communication, as credible sustainability efforts require verifiable proof to preserve consumer faith and drive genuine market change.
