New European environmental regulation could strengthen the international competitiveness of Argentine forestry companies

The article analyzes the European Union Deforestation Regulation (EUDR), highlighting that its entry into force marks a paradigm shift by requiring full traceability and legal compliance, not only in relation to illegal deforestation. In the absence of a binding global governance framework for forests, this European regulation acts as a de facto standard that could influence future international legislation, significantly raising the barriers to entry for sustainable trade. It is argued that, far from being a threat, compliance with these requirements represents a strategic opportunity for Argentine forestry companies, particularly in the context of the MERCOSUR–European Union agreement. Argentina’s competitive advantage lies in its high percentage of production certified under private standards (FSC and PEFC), which facilitates the demonstration of the due diligence required by the EU, providing a clear advantage over regional competitors with less advanced technological capabilities and risk management practices. This situation is relevant to the debate on greenwashing because it exposes the tension between genuine transparency and corporate image washing. The article warns that the requirement for georeferenced data and strict traceability can generate significant operational costs and confidentiality challenges, underscoring the need to verify that sustainability claims are not merely declarative but are backed by a verifiable and robust data infrastructure.
