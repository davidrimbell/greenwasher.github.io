COP31: More than just greenwashing

The article argues that COP summits are not genuine efforts to save the climate, but rather sophisticated platforms for greenwashing that facilitate the reorganization of the global economy. These events serve as pretexts for imperialist monopolies and extractive industries to establish new financial and technological dependencies, particularly through carbon trading and the restructuring of energy supply chains. By framing the climate crisis as a market opportunity, these summits allow powerful corporations to dictate terms that benefit their capital accumulation while forcing dependent nations to monetize their natural and human resources. The author highlights Turkey’s participation as a prime example of this hypocrisy, where the government promotes superficial environmental campaigns while simultaneously expanding destructive mining and fossil fuel activities. The narrative of a "green transition" is used to justify massive investments in electrification and renewable infrastructure, which are actually designed to secure market access and loans for capitalist interests. This dual approach allows states to appear environmentally responsible on the international stage while aggressively pursuing policies that exacerbate ecological degradation and social exploitation domestically. Ultimately, the piece contends that the capitalist system inherently prioritizes short-term profit over long-term ecological survival, making genuine sustainability impossible within the current framework. The ongoing climate negotiations mask a deeper process of resource plunder, where the burdens of environmental collapse are shifted onto the working class and nature. The article concludes that because systemic change is not forthcoming from these institutions, resistance and organized opposition are the only viable paths to challenging the exploitative structures underpinning both capitalism and the global climate regime.
