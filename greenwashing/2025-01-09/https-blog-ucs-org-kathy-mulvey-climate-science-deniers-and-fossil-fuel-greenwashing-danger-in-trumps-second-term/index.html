Greenwashing
ES EN

The article argues that President Trump’s administration provides fossil fuel corporations with an opening to rehabilitate their public image through strategic greenwashing. By placing climate science deniers in key government positions, the administration allows companies like ExxonMobil to feign acceptance of climate science while simultaneously expanding production and delaying genuine action. This political alignment enables the industry to regain social license, presenting itself as part of the solution while continuing to exacerbate the climate crisis through misleading narratives and double standards. A primary focus is the deceptive nature of corporate environmental, social, and governance (ESG) claims, which often mask continued reliance on fossil fuels. The piece highlights how major energy firms manipulate metrics, such as focusing on emissions intensity per unit rather than total absolute emissions, to create a false impression of progress. This sophisticated disinformation campaign, rooted in historical tactics of creating doubt, allows corporations to appease climate-conscious investors and policymakers without committing to the rapid phaseout of hydrocarbons necessary to avert catastrophic warming. This phenomenon is critical to understanding greenwashing because it reveals a coordinated effort by the fossil fuel industry to influence policy and perception from within. Rather than accepting climate solutions, these entities use political connections and deceptive reporting to obstruct meaningful regulation and maintain profitability. The article warns that accepting these corporations as viable partners in climate action is dangerous, as their historical record of deception and current strategic maneuvers aim to preserve fossil fuel dominance under the guise of environmental responsibility.
