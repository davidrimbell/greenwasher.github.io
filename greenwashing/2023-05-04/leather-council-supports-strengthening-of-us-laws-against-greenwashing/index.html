Leather Council supports strengthening of US laws against greenwashing

Leather Council supports strengthening of US laws against greenwashing

The Leather and Hide Council of America (LHCA) advocates for stricter Federal Trade Commission regulations on environmental marketing claims to combat pervasive greenwashing. They argue that current guidelines fail to address deceptive terminology used by competitors selling synthetic alternatives, urging the FTC to adopt lifecycle assessments to ensure transparency and accuracy in sustainability reporting. LHCA contends that petroleum-based products, often marketed under vague labels like "vegan" or "eco-leather," mislead consumers by masquerading as environmentally friendly. By obscuring their plastic origins, these manufacturers exploit the growing demand for sustainable goods, creating market confusion that unfairly disadvantages naturally derived materials and distorts consumer purchasing decisions based on false premises. This issue is critical to understanding greenwashing because it highlights how industry actors exploit regulatory gaps to deflect environmental blame. LHCA warns that this deception not only harms the leather industry but also exacerbates climate change by promoting waste-intensive disposal methods for hides and accelerating the production of polluting, micro-plastic-shedding synthetic materials, ultimately causing greater ecological damage than natural alternatives.
