Mijo, ciruela, leche vegetal y legumbres, entre los alimentos más saludables... y sostenibles

Mijo, ciruela, leche vegetal y legumbres, entre los alimentos más saludables... y sostenibles

This study reveals that individual dietary decisions have a significant environmental impact, demonstrating that sustainability need not compromise nutritional health. The researchers conclude that it is possible to follow the Spanish dietary guidelines while minimizing one’s carbon footprint through informed choices within each food group, such as opting for tap water over bottled water or selecting pelagic fish instead of deep-sea species. The relevance of this work to the debate on greenwashing lies in its data-driven scientific approach, which debunks simplistic and often misleading narratives. By providing precise metrics of environmental impact, the report avoids the vague generalizations that characterize many eco-marketing strategies, instead offering concrete tools for consumers and companies seeking to act with transparency. Thus, the study underscores that true sustainability lies in balance and trade-offs, not in the extreme elimination of entire food groups. By recommending food alternation and nutrient-by-nutrient substitution, it counters misinformation that promotes “free-from-all” diets as magic solutions, reaffirming that environmental responsibility requires complexity and specific knowledge, not just vague labels.
