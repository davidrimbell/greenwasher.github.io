Dozens of Indigenous herders file lawsuit against carbon project in Kenya

Dozens of Indigenous herders file lawsuit against carbon project in Kenya

This article highlights a critical case of greenwashing where environmental credentials mask severe human rights violations. Indigenous communities in Kenya have filed a lawsuit against the Northern Rangelands Trust, arguing that their ancestral lands were commercialized into a carbon credit project without free, prior, and informed consent. The lawsuit challenges the legitimacy of offsets sold to major corporations, asserting that the project systematically undermines pastoralist livelihoods and concentrates opaque financial control within the organization rather than the local people. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the discrepancy between the project’s marketed “climate-neutral” image and its reality of land dispossession. Experts and activists describe the initiative as fraudulent, noting that international certification bodies have already suspended credits due to credible evidence of illegality and abuse. The narrative of community-led conservation is exposed as a facade, where local leaders are reduced to symbolic figures while armed rangers enforce restrictions that threaten the survival of the herdsmen. Ultimately, this case demonstrates how carbon markets can exploit Indigenous rights under the guise of climate action. It serves as a stark warning to buyers of such credits, urging them to question whether their purchases support genuine ecological balance or simply fund the “fraudulent labeling” of stolen land. The ongoing legal battle underscores the urgent need for transparency and accountability, revealing that true climate justice cannot coexist with the systemic erasure of Indigenous sovereignty and livelihoods.
