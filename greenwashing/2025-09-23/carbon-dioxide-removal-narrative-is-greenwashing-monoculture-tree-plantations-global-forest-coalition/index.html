Carbon Dioxide Removal Narrative is Greenwashing Monoculture Tree Plantations - Global Forest Coalition

Carbon Dioxide Removal Narrative is Greenwashing Monoculture Tree Plantations - Global Forest Coalition

This article argues that Carbon Dioxide Removal initiatives are predominantly a greenwashing strategy employed by the industrial tree plantation sector to legitimize their expansion. By rebranding monoculture plantations of exotic species as “carbon sinks,” corporations obfuscate the severe environmental damage these operations cause to biodiversity, water resources, and local communities. The narrative exploits public concern about climate change to justify activities that are fundamentally detrimental to the ecological crisis they claim to address. The authors highlight that nearly all commercialized carbon removal credits stem from afforestation and reforestation projects, which effectively function as industrial plantations. Major pulp and paper corporations leverage these claims to secure financing and expand their operations, often receiving support from prominent financial institutions. This mechanism allows the industry to monetize their destructive practices, masking the reality that carbon storage in such monocultures is ephemeral and that these projects drive land appropriation and social conflict. Ultimately, the piece exposes how the carbon removal framework serves the interests of the wood products and biomass sectors rather than genuine climate mitigation. By integrating Carbon Dioxide Removal narratives with commercial expansion, companies like Arauco and Aperam use voluntary carbon markets to legitimize the destruction of indigenous territories and landscapes. The article concludes that resisting this rebranding is essential, as these plantations exacerbate the climate and biodiversity crises they purport to solve.
