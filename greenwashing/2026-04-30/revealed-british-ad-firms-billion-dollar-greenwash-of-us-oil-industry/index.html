Revealed: British ad firm’s billion-dollar greenwash of US oil industry

A recent investigation reveals that WPP, a major British advertising conglomerate, has facilitated nearly $1 billion in advertising spend for ExxonMobil, Chevron, Shell, and BP in the US since the Paris Agreement. Despite adopting a 2022 policy refusing work that frustrates climate goals, the company provided essential creative and strategic services for these oil giants, effectively shaping public perception to support fossil fuel expansion rather than the transition to renewables. This partnership constitutes a clear case of greenwashing, as the advertisements employed deceptive strategies to obscure the companies’ actual environmental impact and divert attention from their continued reliance on fossil fuels. By crafting narratives that equate fossil fuels with renewable energy solutions or highlighting speculative green technologies, WPP helped legitimize misleading claims, thereby delaying meaningful climate policy and undermining global efforts to limit warming. The article’s relevance to greenwashing lies in exposing the critical role of the advertising industry in amplifying corporate deception. It highlights how creative agencies, often insulated from the immediate realities of climate science, actively construct and disseminate the glossy, misleading imagery that allows polluters to maintain their social license to operate while simultaneously increasing production, thus complicating accountability and regulatory oversight.
