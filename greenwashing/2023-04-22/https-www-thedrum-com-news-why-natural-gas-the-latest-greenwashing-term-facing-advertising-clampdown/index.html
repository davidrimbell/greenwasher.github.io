Greenwashing
ES EN

Advocacy groups are urging regulators to align green advertising standards with international norms, specifically targeting fossil fuel companies. They demand that renewable energy marketing correspond directly with actual investments, aiming to prevent corporations from overstating their environmental contributions. This push seeks to ensure that promotional efforts accurately reflect corporate actions rather than masking the reality of continued fossil fuel reliance. A primary focus is cracking down on misleading terminology such as “natural” or “green” gas, which falsely implies environmental friendliness for potent greenhouse gases. Regulators are also being asked to clarify “net zero” claims and tighten rules on carbon offsets. These measures aim to expose insufficient evidence behind sustainability pledges, forcing companies to substantiate their promises with concrete plans rather than vague marketing language that confuses consumers. This regulatory push is highly relevant to greenwashing because it addresses the systemic deception used by major polluters to maintain public trust. By demanding stricter enforcement, activists hope to hold advertising agencies accountable for facilitating climate misinformation. This global effort, mirrored by similar actions in the UK and Europe, underscores the urgent need to dismantle false narratives that hinder genuine climate action and protect consumers from deceptive eco-claims.
