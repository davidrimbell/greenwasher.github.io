Greenwashing
ES EN

A prominent lobbying firm is being paid millions to enhance the reputation of Azerbaijan, an authoritarian state heavily dependent on fossil fuels, ahead of the UN climate summit. This arrangement has sparked accusations that the firm is facilitating greenwashing by helping the regime obscure its environmental impact and poor human rights record. The controversy highlights how oil-rich nations leverage public relations to appear environmentally responsible while continuing to exploit natural resources. The lobbying company employs former political figures and ex-oil industry executives to manage narrative development and media training for the summit host. Critics argue this creates a severe conflict of interest, as the firm also represents major fossil fuel corporations. By providing strategic communications services, the firm arguably aids in disguising the disconnect between the host country’s climate rhetoric and its actual reliance on petrochemical wealth. This case exemplifies the broader issue of greenwashing, where PR strategies are used to create a false impression of sustainability. It demonstrates how lobbying firms can act as complicit partners, helping polluting states and industries mitigate reputational damage rather than addressing climate harm. The situation underscores the urgent need for transparency in climate negotiations and raises questions about the integrity of international environmental summits hosted by non-democratic, oil-dependent regimes.
