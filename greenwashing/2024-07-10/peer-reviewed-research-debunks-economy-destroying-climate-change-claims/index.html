Peer-Reviewed Research Debunks Economy-Destroying Climate Change Claims

Peer-Reviewed Research Debunks Economy-Destroying Climate Change Claims

The article critiques a paper published in the American Journal of Economics and Sociology, which argues against urgent climate action by claiming there is no current evidence of increased harm from global warming. The authors, who have ties to industry groups like CLINTEL, assert that adaptation is preferable to mitigation, as rapid fossil fuel elimination would devastate the economy and harm the poor without addressing a non-existent crisis. This narrative is highly relevant to greenwashing because it exemplifies how industry-backed actors use selective economic data and pseudoscientific framing to delegitimize environmental regulations. By portraying climate science as an exaggerated "panic" and promoting the idea that business-as-usual is beneficial, these actors create a false equivalence between environmental protection and economic suicide. This strategy shifts the debate from scientific consensus to speculative cost-benefit analysis, allowing corporations to delay meaningful decarbonization while maintaining their fossil fuel dependence. Ultimately, the text serves as a case study in how greenwashing operates through intellectual manipulation rather than product claims. It demonstrates how organizations can co-opt academic platforms to spread doubt and justify inaction, effectively washing their image by aligning themselves with "rational" economic arguments. This approach obscures the reality of climate impacts, protecting corporate interests under the guise of protecting prosperity, and highlights the need for scrutiny regarding the funding and affiliations behind such publications.
