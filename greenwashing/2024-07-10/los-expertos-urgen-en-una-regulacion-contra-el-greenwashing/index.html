Los expertos urgen en una regulación contra el 'greenwashing'

The article underscores the urgent need for robust regulation to combat greenwashing, which is increasingly recognized as an unfair business practice that harms both consumers and genuinely sustainable companies. Experts at the ICAM forum emphasize that the new European directive aims to establish stricter standards, making it progressively more difficult for corporations to engage in deceptive environmental marketing while protecting those truly committed to ecological transitions. A central theme is the discrepancy between evolving legal frameworks and public awareness. While some experts express optimism about growing societal consciousness regarding sustainability, others argue that consumers remain largely uninformed and skeptical, often assuming marketing messages are false despite lacking specific knowledge. This gap suggests that current voluntary measures are insufficient, necessitating mandatory transparency where companies must substantiate their claims with concrete data and avoid omitting material information. The narrative concludes that the new directive is essential for fostering corporate responsibility and creating a level playing field. Even if some firms engage in greenwashing unintentionally due to complexity, the upcoming regulations will provide clearer guidelines, ultimately ensuring that environmental claims are truthful and verifiable. This shift is critical for rebuilding trust in green marketing and accelerating a legitimate ecological transition.
