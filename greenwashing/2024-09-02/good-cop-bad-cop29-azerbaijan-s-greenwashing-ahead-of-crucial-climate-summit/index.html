Good Cop, Bad COP29: Azerbaijan's greenwashing ahead of crucial climate summit

Good Cop, Bad COP29: Azerbaijan's greenwashing ahead of crucial climate summit

The article illustrates how authoritarian regimes may exploit climate summits to manufacture an image of legitimacy while systematically suppressing internal dissent. Azerbaijan’s imprisonment of anti-corruption researcher Dr. Gubad Ibadoghlu, timed strategically around hosting COP29, suggests an attempt to silence critics and control the narrative. This crackdown on journalists and activists undermines the very civil society participation essential for genuine democratic climate action, raising serious doubts about the summit’s inclusivity and fairness. This situation highlights the disconnect between a nation’s green branding and its reality as a fossil-fuel-dependent economy. Azerbaijan presents itself as a partner in global energy transition, yet it remains heavily reliant on oil and gas, with plans to expand exports. The presence of major energy executives in the summit’s organization further complicates these claims, indicating that vested interests in continued fossil fuel use may overshadow genuine environmental commitments. The case is relevant to greenwashing as it exposes the hypocrisy of using environmental conferences to mask human rights abuses and economic corruption. Major corporations and governments participating in COP29 face criticism for prioritizing access to resources over ethical accountability. Ultimately, the article argues that Azerbaijan’s "green" facade is merely a strategic tool to legitimize an authoritarian regime, revealing how climate diplomacy can be weaponized to obscure systemic failures rather than address them.
