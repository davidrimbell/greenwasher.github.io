Straight talk on Canada’s new greenwashing laws: What mining companies need to know

Straight talk on Canada’s new greenwashing laws: What mining companies need to know

Recent amendments to Canada’s Competition Act introduce stringent anti-greenwashing provisions, fundamentally altering the regulatory landscape for businesses operating in or headquartered in the country. These laws mandate that all environmental and social claims be substantiated by adequate, proper testing, with enforcement powers now active and private litigation rights expanding in 2025. This shift demands immediate compliance from sectors like mining, where vague or unverified sustainability assertions can result in severe financial penalties and lasting reputational harm. To navigate these requirements, companies must anchor their sustainability communications in internationally recognized methodologies and rigorous evidence, rather than relying solely on local approvals or aspirational language. Implementing robust due diligence and maintaining comprehensive documentation of data and third-party verifications are essential steps to demonstrate reasonable care against deceptive marketing accusations. Furthermore, organizations must carefully scrutinize how information from regulatory filings is repurposed for public marketing, ensuring every public claim withstands scientific and legal scrutiny. This regulatory evolution is critical to the greenwashing discourse because it moves beyond voluntary guidelines to enforceable legal accountability, closing the gap between marketing narratives and verified environmental performance. By requiring specificity and proof over vague assertions, these measures protect stakeholders from misleading information and force industries to demonstrate genuine operational improvements. Ultimately, compliance is no longer optional but a fundamental requirement for maintaining social license, investor confidence, and trust in an era of heightened ESG scrutiny.
