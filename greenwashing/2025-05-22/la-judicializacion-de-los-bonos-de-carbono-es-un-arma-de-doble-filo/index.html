"La judicialización de los bonos de carbono es un arma de doble filo"

Colombia’s carbon bond market operates with opacity and lacks effective state regulation, exposing Indigenous and Afro-descendant communities to projects that disregard their rights. This dynamic generates socio-environmental conflicts, where the absence of transparency and the shift of decision-making authority from the state to the judiciary have created a vulnerable environment for peripheral territories. To address these gaps, the concept of “green judicialization” has been promoted, with courts attempting to compensate for governmental inaction. However, this approach is a double-edged sword: while it can protect rights, it is also frequently used by corporate actors to silence community resistance through criminal lawsuits and legal intimidation, thereby legitimizing regressive and oppressive practices rather than ensuring environmental justice. The relevance of this analysis to greenwashing lies in how the model commercializes conservation under a facade of sustainability, concealing real abuses against local populations. The positive narrative of climate protection masks the lack of informed consent and inequitable distribution of benefits, revealing that the financial success of these carbon bonds is often built on the exploitation and silencing of those who hold natural resources.
