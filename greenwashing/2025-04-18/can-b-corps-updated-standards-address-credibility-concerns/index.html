Can B Corp’s updated standards address credibility concerns?

Can B Corp’s updated standards address credibility concerns?

B Corp certification, once seen as a reliable seal of ethical quality, faces growing scrutiny for allowing major corporations with serious human rights and environmental controversies to maintain their status. To restore credibility, B Lab has introduced rigorous new standards that replace the previous flexible points system with mandatory minimum requirements across seven critical areas, including climate action and human rights. This shift aims to ensure holistic accountability, preventing companies from excelling in one metric while neglecting others, thereby addressing long-standing criticisms about the scheme’s lax enforcement. However, the effectiveness of these changes in preventing greenwashing remains debatable. While the new rules demand greater transparency and specific actions like publishing pay gaps and climate transition plans, they lack strict penalties for failure. Companies are only required to document "lessons learned" when they fall short, rather than facing immediate decertification. Critics argue this approach creates insufficient consequences for non-compliance, allowing potentially ill-intentioned businesses to use the B Corp label as a marketing shield without being held to truly high ethical standards. To address these integrity gaps, B Lab is transitioning to mandatory third-party verification for all applications, a move driven by both external pressure and the need for independent oversight. Despite this structural improvement, the uncertainty surrounding how external verifiers will handle enforcement and the delayed timeline for full implementation leaves the scheme’s current value questionable. Ultimately, B Corp’s survival depends on proving that its growing membership size is matched by genuine rigor, rather than merely serving as a credential for greenwashing.
