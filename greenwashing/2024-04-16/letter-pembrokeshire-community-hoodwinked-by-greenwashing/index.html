Letter: "Pembrokeshire community hoodwinked by greenwashing"

The author argues that local planning committees practice tokenism by ignoring public input, thereby failing democratic standards. This procedural failure allows officials to bypass existing policies and approve controversial projects, such as a single wind turbine, despite local opposition and heritage restrictions. Greenwashing is identified as the mechanism used to hoodwink the community, framing the project as beneficial renewable energy while disregarding its actual destruction of quality of life, wildlife, and landscapes. This narrative obscures the lack of tangible benefits for residents, masking environmental and social costs under the guise of sustainability. This case is relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how corporate and institutional actors manipulate perception to secure approvals. By prioritizing a sustainable image over substantive community concerns and factual evidence, they legitimize harmful developments, demonstrating how greenwashing serves as a tool to evade accountability and democratic oversight in favor of unchecked development.
