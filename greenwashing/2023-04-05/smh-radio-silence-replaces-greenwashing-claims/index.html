SMH: Radio Silence Replaces Greenwashing Claims

Stricter regulations against misleading environmental claims are causing a significant shift in corporate behavior, moving the industry from active greenwashing to "green hushing." Companies are increasingly silencing their previous climate commitments to avoid legal repercussions rather than genuinely reducing their environmental impact. This trend highlights how regulatory pressure can inadvertently encourage opacity, as firms prioritize legal safety over transparency. The removal of climate reports and net-zero pledges by major entities like AustralianSuper demonstrates a retreat from public accountability, suggesting that companies may be protecting themselves from liability rather than adapting their operations. This development is crucial to understanding greenwashing because it reveals a new, less visible form of deception. By staying silent instead of lying, corporations evade scrutiny while maintaining business-as-usual practices, making it harder for consumers and regulators to assess true environmental progress and hold entities accountable for their actual ecological footprint.
