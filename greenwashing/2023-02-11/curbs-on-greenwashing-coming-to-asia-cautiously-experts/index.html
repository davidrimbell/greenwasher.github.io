Curbs on greenwashing coming to Asia, cautiously: experts

Curbs on greenwashing coming to Asia, cautiously: experts

While multinational corporations face mounting legal challenges for greenwashing in Europe and the US, this trend has yet to significantly materialize in Asia due to evolving regulatory frameworks and less active civic engagement. Although major Asian economies now mandate sustainability reporting, the immediate impact on corporate accountability remains limited as regulators process this new data. Consequently, the burden of policing misleading claims currently falls more heavily on consumers and investors rather than strict legal enforcement, creating a delayed timeline for meaningful change compared to Western markets. However, the financial incentives for exaggerating environmental credentials remain strong, driving companies to adopt various subtle greenwashing tactics to attract capital and talent. These tactics include hiding behind industry alliances, highlighting minor green initiatives to distract from larger environmental harms, or shifting blame onto consumers. Because businesses often lack internal sustainability expertise, they resort to vague communications that distort market pricing and divert funds away from genuine sustainability goals, ultimately compromising long-term ecological objectives. This situation is relevant to greenwashing because it highlights a critical gap between disclosure requirements and actual behavior. The article demonstrates that mere reporting is insufficient without enforceable standards and consumer scrutiny, warning that the region is not yet at "peak greenwash." It underscores the urgency for Asian firms to move beyond superficial marketing claims and embed authentic sustainability practices, as the lack of rigorous enforcement allows deceptive practices to persist while undermining investor confidence and global climate efforts.
