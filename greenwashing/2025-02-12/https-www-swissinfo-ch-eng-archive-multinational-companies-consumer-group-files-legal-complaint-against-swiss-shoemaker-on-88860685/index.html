Greenwashing
EN ES

Swiss authorities have formally accused sports brand On of greenwashing, highlighting a stark contradiction between marketing claims and reality. Despite advertising their Cyclon shoe as infinitely recyclable, public investigation revealed that tens of thousands of pairs sold had not actually been recycled. This discrepancy led consumer protection agencies to file a legal complaint, arguing the advertising misled the public about the product’s environmental sustainability. On defends its practices by asserting that the majority of returned shoes are repurposed into new components or other industrial products, with none ending up in landfills. The company claims it has made corrective adjustments to its website after initial discussions with regulators. However, authorities remain unconvinced, maintaining that the gap between the "100% recyclable" promise and the actual lack of recycling infrastructure constitutes deceptive marketing rather than legitimate green innovation. This case is highly relevant to greenwashing because it demonstrates how vague environmental claims can mask a lack of tangible action. It underscores the critical importance of verifiable proof in eco-marketing, especially under new laws banning unproven climate impact claims. The situation illustrates the broader challenge consumers face in distinguishing between genuine sustainability efforts and superficial branding designed to exploit eco-conscious trends without committing to substantive change.
