NGOs slam Arla for ‘greenwashing’ its reputation as ‘climate leader’

The report accuses Arla Foods of greenwashing by promoting an image of climate leadership while failing to meet UN guidelines for effective climate action. Despite ambitious public pledges to reach net zero, the company’s actual strategies rely heavily on unproven "silver bullet" technologies and weak accounting methods, such as carbon credits, rather than addressing the root causes of its emissions. This discrepancy suggests that Arla prioritizes its public relations and lobbying efforts over genuine environmental impact, effectively obscuring its lack of substantial progress. A central criticism is Arla’s failure to establish concrete targets for methane reduction, which constitutes the majority of its farming emissions. Instead of tackling this primary source of pollution, the co-op focuses on innovations like feed additives and biogas, which the report argues offer minimal real-world reduction potential. By ignoring these critical methane issues, Arla’s current policies make it highly unlikely that it will achieve its stated climate goals, undermining its claim to be a sustainable industry leader. Furthermore, the report highlights that Arla’s transition toward plant-based alternatives is negligible, with non-dairy products representing a tiny fraction of its revenue. The company continues to prioritize industrial dairy production, benefiting from structures that favor large farms while claiming to support traditional agriculture. This narrative exposes a contradiction between Arla’s marketed sustainability and its business model, revealing that the co-op is more focused on maintaining its status quo and market dominance than on delivering meaningful climate solutions.
