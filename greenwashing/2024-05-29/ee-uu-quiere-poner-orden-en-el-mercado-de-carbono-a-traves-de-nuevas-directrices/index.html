EE. UU. quiere poner orden en el mercado de carbono a través de nuevas directrices

EE. UU. quiere poner orden en el mercado de carbono a través de nuevas directrices

The U.S. government is establishing new guidelines for voluntary carbon markets, seeking to legitimize mechanisms that critics often dismiss as greenwashing. Janet Yellen will present principles designed to strengthen the integrity of the system, arguing that private financing is indispensable for achieving global climate goals. This approach reflects an official stance that prioritizes the integration of capital into the fight against climate change, despite existing controversies. The significance of these guidelines lies in their attempt to counter accusations of greenwashing, which emerged after investigations revealed the overestimation or nonexistence of many emission reductions. By requiring that credits be linked to genuine greenhouse gas removal and by increasing transparency, the administration aims to distinguish between real offsets and those that merely serve to improve corporate image without delivering real environmental impact. However, official support for these instruments continues to spark debate. While proponents view these markets as a "gold mine" for financing projects in the Global South, skepticism persists regarding their actual effectiveness. The initiative is crucial for understanding the current tension between market-based models and the need for authentic climate action, marking a turning point in how corporate environmental responsibility will be regulated.
