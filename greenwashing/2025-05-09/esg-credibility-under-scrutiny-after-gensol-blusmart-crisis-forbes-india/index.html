ESG Credibility Under Scrutiny After Gensol, BluSmart Crisis - Forbes India

ESG Credibility Under Scrutiny After Gensol, BluSmart Crisis - Forbes India

The BluSmart and Gensol Engineering scandal illustrates how financial misconduct and poor governance can expose the hollowness of sustainability branding. When companies heavily marketed as leaders in electric mobility are found guilty of fund diversion and corporate fraud, it reveals that their environmental claims often serve as a smokescreen. This disconnect between green rhetoric and unethical business practices suggests that ESG labels are sometimes deployed to distract from underlying moral failings, turning sustainability commitments into mere marketing tools rather than genuine operational standards. This case highlights a critical gap in the ESG framework, particularly the neglect of the "Governance" component in favor of environmental narratives. The incident underscores that operating in a sustainable sector does not automatically guarantee holistic adherence to ethical principles. It demonstrates that weak internal controls and lack of oversight can severely undermine the credibility of sustainability efforts, proving that true accountability requires rigorous examination of financial integrity and board responsibilities alongside environmental goals. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the erosion of public trust and the resulting investor skepticism. These events show that without transparent verification and independent assurance, ESG claims are vulnerable to being dismissed as deceptive practices. Consequently, the industry faces pressure to adopt stricter regulatory measures and deeper due diligence to distinguish genuine sustainability from superficial branding, ensuring that the broader movement for ethical investing remains credible and not tainted by fraudulent activities.
