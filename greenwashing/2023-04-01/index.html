Currently, the lack of uniform definitions has caused the "greenium"—the yield advantage investors once accepted for sustainable bonds—to disappear. By introducing a clear, high-bar standard, the regulation is expected to revitalize investor demand for genuinely sustainable assets. This shift will force issuers to prove their claims more thoroughly, creating a competitive race to secure the new EU label and potentially restoring financial incentives for authentic green financing. However, the high threshold poses immediate challenges, as most existing bonds, particularly those issued by sovereigns, do not yet meet the new criteria. This discrepancy creates reputational and financial risks for investors holding legacy assets that fail to align with the upcoming standards. The regulation’s global recognition remains uncertain, but its implementation will likely push issuers to cautiously avoid overclaiming alignment, thereby tightening the overall market’s definition of sustainability.
