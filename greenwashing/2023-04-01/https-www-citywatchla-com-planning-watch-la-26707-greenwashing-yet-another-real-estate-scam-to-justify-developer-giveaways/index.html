Greenwashing
ES EN

The article argues that prominent California legislation, while framed as a solution to climate change, effectively facilitates greenwashing by real estate investors and developers. Proponents claim these laws increase housing supply and reduce emissions, but the author contends these are deceptive narratives used to mask the true intent: deregulating land use to benefit wealthy interests. By exempting new housing projects from rigorous environmental review, the laws allow developers to present high-cost, car-dependent luxury apartments as climate-friendly solutions, despite their lack of actual sustainability benefits. A central mechanism for this deception is the removal of the California Environmental Quality Act protections, which previously served as a critical tool for measuring a project’s true climate impact. Without mandatory environmental monitoring or verification of general plan requirements, there is no mechanism to ensure that newly built housing actually reduces greenhouse gas emissions. The article highlights that many of these high-end units attract tenants who continue to rely on private vehicles, rendering the touted "transit-oriented" benefits hollow and misleading. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the systemic enforcement gaps that make such deception viable. Because violations result only in minor civil fines rather than criminal prosecution, the financial incentives for greenwashing far outweigh the penalties. This weak regulatory framework allows corporations to exploit public concern about climate change without facing significant consequences, illustrating how policy can be manipulated to legitimize environmentally harmful practices under the guise of social progress.
