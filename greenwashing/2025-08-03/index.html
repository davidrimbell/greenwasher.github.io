Beyond Green Labels: Why Compostable Plastics Fail in Landfills

Fashion and packaging companies often use greenwashing. This trick changes how people see products. Terra Sol Studio shows the truth about this. They explain that “compostable” plastics often do not break down in normal landfills. This gap between marketing and reality confuses buyers.

Greenwashing helps consumers feel good. It lets people feel ethical when they buy things. Labels like “biodegradable” or “carbon-neutral” give quick comfort. This feeling makes people keep buying single-use items. But this approach has big problems. It distracts us from fixing waste systems. We need more industrial composting facilities. Without these, “compostable” plastics stay in the environment just like regular trash.

Studies show these issues clearly. A 2015 study by the University of California, Irvine, found that very few compostable plastics break down in home compost piles. Another example is H&M’s “Conscious Collection.” It faced criticism for vague claims that lacked outside verification. These cases show how nice branding hides the true cost to the environment.

Consumers must realize that greenwashing shifts blame. It moves responsibility from companies to individuals. This lets companies keep bad practices while looking good. This stops us from making real progress toward a circular economy. Buyers who trust simple labels may accidentally support pollution. True accountability needs strict standards and clear supply chains. We must look past shiny packaging. We need to understand the real life cycle of products. Our environmental efforts depend on this careful check.
