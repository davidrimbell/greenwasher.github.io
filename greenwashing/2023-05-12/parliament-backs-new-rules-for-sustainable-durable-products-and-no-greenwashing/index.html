Parliament backs new rules for sustainable, durable products and no greenwashing

The European Parliament has approved a directive aimed at empowering consumers for the green transition by banning misleading environmental claims and generic terms like "eco-friendly" or "natural" unless strictly substantiated. This legislative move directly targets the pervasive greenwashing practice of using vague, unsubstantiated slogans to create a false impression of sustainability, ensuring that marketing messages reflect verifiable ecological realities rather than marketing hype. Furthermore, the proposal seeks to combat early obsolescence by prohibiting design features that intentionally limit product lifespan and restricting manufacturer control over third-party repairs and accessories. By mandating transparent information regarding repair options and introducing labels that highlight durability, the legislation forces companies to prioritize longevity and quality over planned waste, thereby holding them accountable for the actual environmental impact of their production cycles. This initiative is crucial for combating greenwashing because it establishes a legal framework that demands evidence-based environmental transparency and physical product durability. By replacing ambiguous marketing with certified, substantiated claims and enforcing repairability, the directive dismantles the opacity that typically allows corporations to exaggerate their green credentials, ultimately aligning consumer choices with genuine sustainable practices.
