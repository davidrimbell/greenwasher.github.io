PFAS Greenwashing: Company On Company Litigation Strikes

The Group SEB v. Caraway lawsuit marks a significant shift in greenwashing litigation by representing the first instance where one corporation sues a rival over deceptive environmental marketing regarding PFAS. Unlike traditional cases brought by citizens against manufacturers, this action highlights competitive risks, where companies may face legal retaliation for aggressively characterizing competitors’ products as toxic while promoting their own alternatives as safer. Central to the dispute is the contention that Caraway’s marketing falsely implied all PTFE-containing cookware is harmful, damaging Group SEB’s market share despite their defense that PTFE is safe under normal use. This case forces a judicial examination of consumer safety claims that has largely been avoided in previous litigation. The court’s determination on whether PTFE is truly safe will set a critical precedent for how marketing statements are evaluated, distinguishing between acceptable "puffery" and legally actionable falsehoods. This case is highly relevant to greenwashing awareness as it demonstrates that environmental claims carry substantial legal liability even within B2B or corporate-to-corporate disputes. It underscores the necessity for companies to integrate legal, compliance, and risk assessments into marketing strategies, rather than relying solely on creative teams. By showing that aggressive positioning against rivals can trigger complex greenwashing lawsuits, the case warns that environmental marketing boundaries are narrower and more litigious than previously understood.
