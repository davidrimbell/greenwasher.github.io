Greenwashing
ES EN

La ‘cheguevarización’ de la crisis climática

The article argues that greenwashing is a serious obstacle to climate action, as corporations exploit environmental jargon for marketing purposes without implementing tangible sustainable practices. This deceptive strategy creates a false sense of progress among consumers, diluting the perceived urgency of the crisis and allowing high-emission industries to maintain their harmful operations under a veneer of ecological responsibility. Regulatory efforts are currently underway in regions such as Europe and Chile to combat this issue by demanding verifiable proof for sustainability claims and establishing objective classification systems. These measures aim to restore transparency and hold companies accountable, moving beyond vague terminology to enforce standards that genuinely reflect environmental impact and prevent the trivialization of ecological issues. The text emphasizes that consumer vigilance alone is insufficient without urgent, strict legal frameworks backed by severe penalties. Given the immediate severity of global climate and biodiversity crises, society cannot afford delays in implementing robust sanctions against greenwashing. Immediate legislative action is crucial to ensure that environmental concerns are treated with the gravity they deserve, rather than being exploited as fleeting marketing trends.
