Greenwashing
ES EN

The article highlights intensifying regulatory scrutiny of ESG claims, driven by anticipated updates to the Federal Trade Commission’s Green Guides. This regulatory shift signals that vague terminology like "net zero" and recyclability claims are becoming high-risk areas for litigation. Companies face mounting pressure to ensure their sustainability marketing aligns strictly with consumer understanding and factual evidence, rather than relying on ambiguity. A critical implication is the legal danger of failing to disclose material ESG risks, which can trigger lawsuits from stakeholders. The case against Coca-Cola illustrates how sustainability campaigns can be labeled as greenwashing if they mask significant environmental harms, such excessive plastic pollution. Although courts may dismiss purely aspirational claims if they cannot be measured, this does not provide safe harbor, as regulators continue to challenge goals that appear illusory. Relevance to greenwashing lies in the growing legal framework distinguishing between genuine action and deceptive marketing. Firms must substantiate every claim, particularly in complex supply chains like chocolate or coffee, where opacity fuels accusations of greenwashing. The trend indicates that vague or unverified environmental promises are increasingly likely to result in costly litigation and reputational damage, forcing brands to prioritize transparency over marketing flair.
