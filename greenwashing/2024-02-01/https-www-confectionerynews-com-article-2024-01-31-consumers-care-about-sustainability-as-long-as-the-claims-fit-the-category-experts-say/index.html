Greenwashing
ES EN

This article highlights the strategic risk of greenwashing by demonstrating that consumers actively scrutinize sustainability claims, with many skeptical of brands’ genuine commitment. While there is strong demand for eco-friendly products, the research reveals a disconnect: few brands actually qualify as truly sustainable in consumer eyes. Consequently, companies that fail to align their marketing with authentic, credible practices risk misleading audiences, turning potential loyalty into distrust when perceived efforts lack substance. Relevance to greenwashing lies in the finding that authenticity is the primary defense against accusations of deceptive marketing. Brands must carefully curate claims that fit their specific category and product reality rather than using generic, formulaic statements. When companies stack multiple claims, growth accelerates, but only if these assertions are genuine and relevant. This suggests that superficial "claim stacking" to create a green halo without underlying operational changes is increasingly detectable and ineffective, as consumers look for coherence between messaging and actual brand behavior. Furthermore, the industry faces challenges in standardizing terminology to prevent consumer confusion, which can inadvertently signal vague or misleading practices. By moving toward shared industry standards and specific, meaningful language, companies can reduce the ambiguity often exploited in greenwashing. Ultimately, the article argues that sustainable growth depends on transparent, tailored communication that reflects real environmental and social impacts, rather than superficial labels designed solely to capture premium pricing.
