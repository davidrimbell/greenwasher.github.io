The path to truly green investments is fossil-free - EUbusiness.com | EU news, business and politics

The Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation (SFDR) currently suffers from structural flaws that breed confusion and facilitate greenwashing. Although intended to enhance transparency for sustainability-focused investors, Articles 8 and 9 lack clear, measurable indicators. This ambiguity allows financial actors to mislabel funds as environmentally friendly without rigorous standards, making it difficult for private investors to distinguish genuine sustainable investments from those supporting controversial industries. The core issue lies in the inability of current disclosure rules to prevent investments in fossil fuel expansion. Without mandatory exclusion criteria, "light green" funds may still finance companies expanding coal, oil, or gas operations. This regulatory gap undermines the EU’s net-zero commitments and creates an uneven playing field. Advocates argue that true sustainability requires strict boundaries, specifically the exclusion of any entity engaged in fossil fuel expansion, rather than relying on vague definitions that enable deceptive marketing practices. This article is highly relevant to greenwashing because it exposes how regulatory gaps are exploited to create misleading environmental claims. By highlighting the urgent need for the upcoming SFDR review to implement concrete, measurable standards and utilize trusted exclusion databases, the briefing offers a blueprint for combating deceptive labeling. Strengthening these rules is essential to ensure that ESG investments genuinely contribute to decarbonization rather than providing a veneer of sustainability for polluting activities.
