The article details the international cooperation agreements reached by Colombia at COP31, highlighting strategic alliances with Singapore, the Dominican Republic, the World Resources Institute (WRI), and the Global Environment Facility (GEF). The central objective is to strengthen carbon markets under clear rules ensuring environmental integrity, protect marine ecosystems through conservation corridors, and secure financing to prioritize water, biodiversity, and communities. These initiatives aim to consolidate transparent mechanisms that prevent the manipulation of environmental data and guarantee that investments in carbon and conservation deliver real, measurable impacts. Technical collaboration with recognized organizations such as the WRI strengthens the country’s capacity to implement evidence-based policies, aligning ecological protection with the sustainable development of coastal communities and water security. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the fact that these cooperation frameworks establish rigorous standards for transparency and environmental integrity. By formalizing clear rules for carbon markets and promoting joint auditing of ecosystems, Colombia reduces the risk of companies or governments using misleading eco-labels. This institutionalized structure is crucial for distinguishing genuine environmental actions from greenwashing, ensuring that international commitments translate into tangible, verifiable benefits.
