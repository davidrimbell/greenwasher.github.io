'Invisible Forests': Study Reveals Carbon Credit Scam

The article argues that Australia’s safeguard mechanism is failing because fossil fuel corporations rely heavily on flawed Human Induced Regeneration offsets to comply with climate laws rather than reducing actual emissions. Research reveals these projects grossly underperform, with average forest cover reaching only a fraction of required levels, meaning the offsets do not deliver the environmental benefits claimed. This systemic failure allows major coal, gas, and mining entities to continue expanding operations and increasing pollution under the guise of carbon neutrality. Consequently, the current framework effectively permits unlimited fossil fuel expansion by allowing companies to "cook the books" through invalid credits. The research highlights that despite these offsets, total industrial emissions have barely decreased, demonstrating that the policy does not protect the planet or prevent dangerous climate tipping points. The situation is exacerbated by new projects, such as Woodside’s massive gas proposal, which would release billions of tonnes of emissions while leveraging discredited offset schemes to mask their environmental impact. This case is highly relevant to greenwashing as it exposes how corporations use misleading environmental claims to maintain public trust and business continuity while continuing harmful activities. By purchasing offsets that lack genuine ecological value, companies create an illusion of sustainability without making substantive changes to their pollution levels. The article calls for banning fossil fuel entities from buying these offsets, emphasizing that true climate action requires stopping expansion and cutting emissions directly, rather than relying on deceptive financial instruments that obscure the ongoing ecological crisis.
