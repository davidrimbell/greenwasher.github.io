‘Phantom forests’ in Australia’s arid outback used to create carbon credits meant to offset emissions, report claims

Researchers assert that Australia’s carbon offset system is being exploited by fossil fuel companies through the certification of “phantom forests” that show negligible real-world regeneration. By purchasing these low-integrity credits, industrial polluters can legally maintain high emission levels while claiming to offset their environmental impact, effectively bypassing the need for direct reductions in pollution. The study reveals that many semi-arid projects certified for forest regeneration are unlikely to ever support significant tree growth, yet continue to generate billions of dollars in carbon credits. This discrepancy highlights a critical flaw in the national safeguard mechanism, which allows unlimited offsets to count toward compliance. Consequently, the policy fails to drive the necessary immediate cuts in emissions, instead incentivizing companies to pay for cheap credits rather than addressing the root causes of their carbon footprint. This issue is central to the greenwashing debate because it demonstrates how environmental accounting mechanisms can be manipulated to create a false narrative of sustainability. When offsets lack scientific validity, they serve as a marketing tool that masks continued environmental degradation, allowing corporations to appear green while contributing to climate change. The reliance on such dubious credits undermines public trust in corporate climate commitments and delays the urgent transition to genuine emission reductions.
