Greenwashing
ES EN

The article argues that banning fossil fuel advertising is an urgent and necessary step to combat the climate crisis, drawing a direct parallel to the successful global restrictions on tobacco promotion. Just as the World Health Organization’s Framework Convention on Tobacco Control effectively reduced smoking by outlawing deceptive marketing, a similar ban on fossil fuel communications is needed to counter the industry’s systematic greenwashing. This comparison highlights that both sectors rely on misleading narratives to sustain public acceptance of harmful products, making advertising bans a critical tool for public health and environmental protection. A key lesson from the tobacco precedent is the necessity of defining prohibitions broadly to close loopholes and prevent corporate evasion. The tobacco treaty encompasses all forms of indirect promotion, including sponsorships and corporate social responsibility campaigns, recognizing that these tactics are often used to sanitize brand images. For fossil fuel companies, this means that claims of being "low-carbon" or "green" are equally deceptive and should be regulated, as they create false impressions about environmental impact while obscuring the continued expansion of fossil fuel extraction and usage. Furthermore, the article emphasizes the importance of shielding policy-making from corporate interference, a principle established in tobacco regulations through Article 5.3. Currently, international climate negotiations lack robust protections against fossil fuel lobbying, allowing the industry to influence outcomes despite its conflict of interest. Implementing similar safeguards, alongside advertising bans, is essential to prioritize public safety over commercial interests. This approach challenges the notion that harmful commercial speech is protected free speech, asserting that governments have a duty to prevent deceptive practices that endanger society.
