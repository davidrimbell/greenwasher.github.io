Greenwashing
ES EN

Regulators like the Competition & Markets Authority are increasingly scrutinizing food and drink brands for greenwashing, driven by consumer demand for sustainable practices. However, the complexity of environmental impact often leads to misguided claims. A packaging change intended to be eco-friendly can inadvertently increase waste in other areas, highlighting how incomplete understanding of supply chains can result in actions that contradict genuine sustainability goals. This challenge is exacerbated by a significant knowledge gap among marketing teams. Many professionals lack the expertise to verify the real-world effects of their environmental claims, leading to skepticism from consumers who suspect commercial motives over ethical ones. Consequently, general assertions like "eco-friendly" are becoming risky and ineffective, as savvy shoppers demand specific, evidence-based details to distinguish between genuine effort and superficial marketing. To mitigate greenwashing risks, the industry must prioritize education and transparency over vague promises. Brands should collaborate with sustainability specialists to ensure their claims withstand scrutiny and accurately reflect complex trade-offs. By acknowledging the ongoing journey toward a circular economy and providing clear, specific information, companies can rebuild trust and communicate their role in positive environmental change more effectively.
