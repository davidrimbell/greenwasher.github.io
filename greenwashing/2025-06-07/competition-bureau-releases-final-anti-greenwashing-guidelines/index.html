Competition Bureau releases final anti-greenwashing guidelines

The Canadian Competition Bureau’s final anti-greenwashing guidelines establish that businesses may make environmental claims provided they are not false or misleading and are substantiated using internationally recognized methodologies. Rather than creating a rigid list of permissible statements, the regulation emphasizes a holistic assessment of marketing impressions, requiring rigorous testing of claims to ensure accuracy. This framework shifts the burden onto companies to prove the validity of their environmental assertions rather than seeking pre-approval for specific phrasing. The regulatory approach deliberately distinguishes consumer protections from securities law, refusing to treat greenwashing checks as gatekeeping for private citizens or limiting safeguards to investors. Consequently, the government maintains that courts, not the Bureau, should interpret legal terminology. This stance aims to protect ordinary consumers from deceptive marketing without extending the stricter evidentiary standards of capital markets to general commercial advertising, thereby balancing consumer safety with business flexibility. These guidelines have triggered significant industry backlash and self-censorship, as energy firms and banks withdraw climate communications and sustainability metrics due to uncertainty over compliance. This reaction highlights the core relevance to greenwashing: the new rules aim to prevent companies from using vague environmental claims to delay genuine climate action. However, the resulting silence from major sectors illustrates the tension between combating deception and allowing legitimate industry discourse, warning that poorly defined boundaries may stifle transparency rather than enforce accountability.
