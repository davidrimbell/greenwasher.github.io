Countries must be allowed to prepare own plans for SAF: India

India argues that global mandates for Sustainable Aviation Fuel by 2050 are premature, asserting that nations must develop strategies aligned with their specific national plans and capabilities. This stance emphasizes that rigid volumetric targets may conflict with the flexible, aspirational nature of long-term global goals, which should recognize diverse state circumstances rather than imposing uniform obligations. The article is highly relevant to greenwashing because it highlights the tension between aspirational corporate and international net-zero pledges and the practical feasibility of implementing them. By resisting binding fuel mandates while supporting broad climate goals, India exposes how easily high-level sustainability commitments can become superficial marketing tools if they lack concrete, context-specific implementation frameworks or adequate financial support from developed nations. Furthermore, the push for ICAO assistance in balancing sustainability with other aviation priorities reveals how complex operational realities are often sidelined in broad green narratives. This underscores the risk of greenwashing, where broad net-zero targets are adopted for prestige, potentially obscuring the significant gaps between rhetorical commitment and the tangible, often underfunded, actions required to truly decarbonize the industry.
