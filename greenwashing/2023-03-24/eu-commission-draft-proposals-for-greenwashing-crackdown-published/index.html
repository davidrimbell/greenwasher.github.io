EU Commission draft proposals for greenwashing crackdown published

The European Commission has proposed new regulations to combat greenwashing by mandating that all voluntary environmental claims be independently verified and substantiated with scientific evidence. This initiative aims to restore consumer trust and ensure that products marketed as green genuinely meet high sustainability standards. By requiring rigorous proof, the rules prevent companies from making vague or unfounded assertions, thereby protecting buyers from misleading information and ensuring they can make informed, eco-conscious decisions. A central goal of this legislation is to create a fair competitive landscape for businesses. Currently, the lack of uniform standards allows some companies to gain market share through deceptive marketing, unfairly disadvantaging those who invest in authentic environmental improvements. The new framework ensures that genuinely sustainable enterprises are accurately recognized and rewarded by the market, fostering healthy competition based on real ecological performance rather than clever but empty advertising tactics. Furthermore, the proposal addresses the chaos caused by the proliferation of diverse environmental labels, which often confuse consumers and breed distrust. To streamline the market, new public labeling schemes will be banned unless developed at the EU level, and private labels must demonstrate superior environmental ambition with prior approval. By regulating these labels to ensure they are transparent, reliable, and regularly reviewed, the directive seeks to eliminate the current landscape of conflicting signals, providing clear and comparable information to support a truly circular and sustainable economy.
