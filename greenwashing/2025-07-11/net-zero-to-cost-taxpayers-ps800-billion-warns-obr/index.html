Net Zero to Cost Taxpayers £800 Billion, Warns OBR

The Office for Budget Responsibility projects that Britain’s Net Zero transition will incur massive fiscal burdens, estimated at hundreds of billions of pounds over two decades. This staggering cost stems from significant public investments in green infrastructure and the substantial loss of revenue from declining fuel duties as electric vehicles replace petrol and diesel cars. Critics argue these projections rely on implausibly optimistic assumptions from the Climate Change Committee, suggesting the true financial impact is likely far higher. Skeptics point to specific errors in estimating renewable energy costs, such as ignoring expensive floating offshore wind technologies and underestimating solar and onshore wind expenses, which significantly distort the perceived affordability of the transition. This discourse is crucial to understanding greenwashing, as it highlights the discrepancy between politically promoted low-cost environmental narratives and the harsh economic realities. By exposing the gap between projected costs and actual market prices, the article reveals how simplified or optimistic reporting can mislead the public about the true sacrifices required for climate goals, a hallmark of deceptive green marketing.
