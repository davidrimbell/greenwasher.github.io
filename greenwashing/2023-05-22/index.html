AI vs. Psychology: How to Spot Fake Eco-Claims

Shoppers face tricky marketing plans that hide the real environmental cost of products. This confusion makes it hard for buyers who want truly sustainable items. This situation has both good and bad sides for people who are thinking about a purchase.

Technology helps in a positive way. It gives shoppers the power to check if claims are true. The Science Foundation Ireland’s Future Innovator Prize funds a project that uses artificial intelligence to look at sustainability reports. This system spots misleading claims that companies often hide behind vague words. This clear information helps shoppers tell the difference between real ethical practices and corporate spin. As a result, buyers can make smart choices that match the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals. Having access to true data lowers the risk of supporting fake practices. It also helps the market for truly organic and biodegradable products.

However, there are bad sides too. Companies use psychology to manipulate shoppers. They create a fake sense of urgency and make items seem scarce. They sell products as limited-time offers to push sales through pressure, not environmental benefit. This strategy skips rational thinking and encourages wasteful buying. Shoppers who fall for these tricks often buy things without asking about their real impact on nature. This confuses the market and breaks trust in all eco-friendly claims. It keeps harmful environmental practices going under the guise of responsibility. Buyers might feel they are making a green choice, but the product actually adds to the problems they want to solve.

These two sides show how complex modern shopping is. Technology gives tools to see through lies, while psychological traps make shoppers easy targets. Buyers must ask for the transparency that AI can provide. They must also resist the fake urgency that marketing tries to force on them. Understanding these tricks ensures that buying choices remain truly ethical and sustainable. This awareness protects the future we want to leave for the next generation.
