Greenwashing
ES EN

The article reveals that Sultan Ahmed Al-Jaber, president of COP28 and CEO of a major oil company, orchestrated efforts to edit his Wikipedia profile to obscure conflicts of interest. His team removed information about lucrative pipelines and critical financial reports, inserting praise instead. This manipulation highlights a direct attempt to control the public narrative and hide the disparity between his leadership in climate negotiations and his role in expanding fossil fuel production. This case is crucial for understanding greenwashing because it demonstrates how industry actors exploit authoritative platforms to sanitize their reputations while undermining global climate goals. By prioritizing carbon alternatives over emission reductions and using PR tactics to silence criticism, Al-Jaber exemplifies the strategy of capturing climate processes to serve fossil fuel interests. This creates a dangerous precedent where corporate influence supersedes scientific urgency, potentially derailing effective climate action. The incident underscores the urgent need for stricter regulations limiting fossil fuel industry influence at climate summits. With numerous lobbyists already present at previous conferences, the manipulation of digital records signals an escalation in deceptive practices. Consequently, this situation calls for immediate intervention by international bodies to establish clear rules that prevent the hijacking of climate negotiations, ensuring that commitments to zero emissions are not compromised by industry hypocrisy.
