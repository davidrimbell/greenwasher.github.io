Reclaiming Earth Day: The fight against greenwashing

Reclaiming Earth Day: The fight against greenwashing

The article highlights a student-led teach-in that explicitly rejects corporate greenwashing, framing Earth Day as an opportunity for radical climate justice rather than superficial consumer changes. Organizers argue that corporate narratives distract from the root causes of environmental degradation, specifically the fossil fuel industry and capitalism. By criticizing the commodification of activism, the event positions genuine climate action as inherently linked to social justice, asserting that individual gestures like recycling are insufficient without systemic structural change. A central theme is the intersectionality of the climate crisis, linking environmental destruction to global conflicts, imperialism, and economic exploitation. Speakers connected issues such as the conflict in Palestine and industrial agriculture in India to local environmental harm, illustrating how marginalized communities bear the brunt of ecological collapse. This perspective challenges the notion that climate change is an isolated issue, demonstrating instead that the defense of human rights and indigenous lands is inseparable from the protection of the planet. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the explicit exposure of how institutions and corporations mask their environmental impact through performative activism and political influence. The event underscored how powerful entities use greenwashing and astroturfing to maintain the status quo, while simultaneously calling out university ties to military funding and fossil fuel dependence. By exposing these mechanisms, the article serves as a critique of how "green" messaging is often used to obscure ongoing ecological damage and perpetuate inequality, urging a shift toward collective, intersectional action.
