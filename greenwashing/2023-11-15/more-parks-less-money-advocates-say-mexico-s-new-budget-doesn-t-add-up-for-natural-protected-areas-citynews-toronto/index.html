More parks, less money: Advocates say Mexico's new budget doesn't add up for natural protected areas | CityNews Toronto

More parks, less money: Advocates say Mexico's new budget doesn't add up for natural protected areas | CityNews Toronto

Environmental activists in Mexico accuse the López Obrador administration of greenwashing by rapidly designating numerous new protected areas to create a favorable environmental image, while simultaneously defunding the agencies responsible for safeguarding them. This strategy appears contradictory given the government’s long-standing support for fossil fuels and resistance to renewable energy, suggesting that the expansion of protected zones is primarily a cosmetic effort rather than a genuine commitment to conservation. The financial reality undermines these declarations, as the environmental budget has faced significant cuts, resulting in drastically reduced funding per hectare of protected land. With resources shrinking while the number of designated areas explodes, agencies are forced to borrow from other departments or rely on military oversight for projects like the controversial Maya Train. This misallocation of funds means that claimed investments in climate adaptation often support industrial developments that harm the very ecosystems they supposedly protect. This article is highly relevant to greenwashing because it exemplifies how organizations or governments can use superficial environmental gestures, such as creating protected areas on paper, to mask unsustainable practices. It highlights the critical distinction between labeling and action, demonstrating that without adequate financial backing and consistent policy enforcement, environmental credentials are merely performative. The case illustrates how "green" branding is used to simulate responsibility while actual resources are diverted to contradictory economic interests.
