'Greenwash shambles': No ports money for chaotic move to 'green' fiasco ferries

The article exposes a significant disconnect between the environmental promises made for Scotland’s new ferries and the messy reality of their deployment, highlighting a classic case of greenwashing. Despite being marketed as sustainable vessels powered by liquefied natural gas (LNG) to support climate goals, the project has been plagued by massive delays, cost overruns, and the abandonment of dedicated fueling infrastructure. The government’s decision to rely on slow, weather-dependent tanker refueling at sea undermines the claimed operational efficiency and reliability, while casting doubt on the environmental benefits due to poor infrastructure planning. More critically, environmental organizations argue that LNG is not a genuinely green solution because of high methane leakage. Methane is a potent greenhouse gas that traps heat far more effectively than carbon dioxide in the short term. Critics emphasize that the specific engines chosen for these ferries have the highest rates of methane slip, meaning the fuel’s net climate impact may be worse than traditional options. By promoting LNG as a clean alternative while ignoring or downplaying these emissions, authorities are presenting a misleading image of sustainability that does not align with scientific consensus on reducing short-term warming. This case is relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how political and industrial actors can label fossil-fuel-based technologies as "green" to secure funding and public approval, despite lacking robust evidence of actual environmental benefits. The rhetoric of contributing to climate goals is used to mask the reality of continued reliance on volatile fossil fuels and the absence of necessary supporting infrastructure. Ultimately, the situation reveals how "sustainable" labeling can be exploited to obscure the true ecological cost of energy choices, misleading stakeholders about the genuine progress toward decarbonization.
