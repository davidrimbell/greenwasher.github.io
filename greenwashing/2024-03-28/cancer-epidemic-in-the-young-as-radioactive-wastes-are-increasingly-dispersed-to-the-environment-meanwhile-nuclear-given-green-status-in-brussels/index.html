Greenwashing
ES EN

Cancer “epidemic” in the Young as Radioactive Wastes are Increasingly Dispersed to the Environment meanwhile Nuclear given “green” status in Brussels.

The article exposes the nuclear industry’s pervasive greenwashing by revealing how decommissioning is euphemistically framed as "restoration" while actually facilitating the widespread environmental dispersal of radioactive waste. By manipulating regulatory hierarchies, facilities legally release previously prohibited materials into the public realm, contradicting the clean, sustainable image promoted by governments and corporations to justify continued nuclear expansion. This environmental degradation is highlighted by a growing cancer epidemic among the young, yet public discourse conveniently shifts blame to individual lifestyle choices rather than systemic exposure to radiation and toxins. This narrative manipulation absolves the industry and state actors of responsibility, allowing them to obscure the direct link between increased radioactive pollution in air, sea, and land and severe public health crises affecting vulnerable populations. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the political alliance between state bodies like the IAEA and the nuclear sector, which successfully rebrands dangerous energy sources as essential climate solutions. By focusing opposition solely on carbon metrics rather than the full toxic consequences of nuclear power, activists inadvertently allow the industry to secure political legitimacy. This strategic silence enables the "nuclear mafia" to expand its agenda, disguising ecological and health dangers as a necessary transition to net-zero emissions.
