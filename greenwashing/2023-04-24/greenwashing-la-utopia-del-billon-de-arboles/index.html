¿Greenwashing? La utopía del billón de árboles

The article critiques massive corporate reforestation pledges, such as those associated with Marc Benioff’s initiatives, by highlighting their potential to serve as greenwashing rather than genuine climate solutions. Despite the enthusiastic commitment of leaders and billions in funding, the campaign often focuses on counting planted trees rather than ensuring their survival or ecological impact. Experts argue that using tree planting to offset corporate emissions allows businesses to maintain high pollution levels under the guise of "net zero," creating a marketing image of sustainability without implementing necessary reductions at the source. Furthermore, the feasibility of these global reforestation goals is questioned due to severe spatial limitations and methodological flaws. Scientists point out that the amount of land required to meet these ambitious targets exceeds available space, potentially leading to monocultures that harm biodiversity and displace local communities. Additionally, the assumption that young trees will permanently store carbon is flawed, as many die young or are harvested before reaching maturity, releasing the stored carbon back into the atmosphere. This disconnect between promises and practical reality undermines the effectiveness of these projects in mitigating climate change. Ultimately, the article emphasizes that active tree planting is frequently a costly distraction from more effective conservation strategies like protecting existing forests and restoring ecosystems naturally. The lack of transparent data on tree survival rates and the focus on superficial metrics reveal how corporate social responsibility campaigns can obscure true environmental accountability. This case is relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how complex ecological challenges are simplified into attractive, countable initiatives that prioritize public relations over substantive, verifiable environmental progress, misleading stakeholders about the actual impact on global warming.
