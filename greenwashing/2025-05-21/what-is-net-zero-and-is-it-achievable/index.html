What is Net Zero and is it Achievable?

What is Net Zero and is it Achievable?

The article argues that the concept of "net zero" is fundamentally flawed because it allows for continued greenhouse gas emissions by relying on offsets and removals rather than achieving absolute reductions. This approach creates a dangerous loophole where wealthy nations can maintain high domestic emission levels by funding projects in developing countries, effectively bypassing the need for genuine, widespread decarbonization. Furthermore, the author claims that current climate models fail to account for powerful natural variables, such as volcanic activity and the self-perpetuating cycle of atmospheric water vapor. By ignoring these uncontrollable natural processes, the push for net zero is depicted as an oversimplified solution that ignores the complexity of Earth’s climate system, rendering the targets largely ineffective against actual temperature changes driven by natural forces. This perspective is highly relevant to greenwashing because it characterizes "net zero" not as a scientific necessity, but as a rhetorical tool used by elites to distract from the lack of tangible progress. It suggests that the prevailing narrative is designed to legitimize inaction by the global ruling class, framing the crisis as solvable through manageable offsets and political agreements while actual emissions continue to rise, thereby masking the absence of a true environmental commitment.
