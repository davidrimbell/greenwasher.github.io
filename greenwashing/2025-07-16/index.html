Why Higher Prices Don’t Mean Sustainable Products

People often see products labeled as “sustainable” in stores or online. Shoppers hope their purchases help create a cleaner future. But some companies use tricky pricing to take advantage of this kindness. A study from the University of Cambridge shows that firms often charge more for items that do little for the environment. This creates a trap for buyers. Many shoppers believe that a higher price means the product is more ethical.

Buying these items makes shoppers feel good. They think they are supporting green energy or fair labor. This happy feeling helps brands sell more products. However, there is a bad side to this. The extra money usually goes into company profits. It rarely funds real environmental projects. A report by the European Commission says many “green” products lack proof of their environmental benefits. This means consumers get no real ecological benefit from their spending.

This situation hurts trust in the market. When shoppers learn that high prices do not mean better environmental performance, they doubt all green claims. A study in the Journal of Consumer Research shows that seeing fake green claims lowers consumer confidence in eco-friendly labels. This doubt hurts honest companies. These truthful brands struggle to compete with others that use marketing to raise prices without offering real benefits.

Consumers must check prices carefully. They should look at product details too. Shoppers should demand clear data to prove environmental claims. Regulators also have an important job. They must enforce accurate labeling. The European Commission’s Green Claims Directive tries to stop misleading environmental statements. Shoppers can support real sustainability by checking certifications. They should also research how transparent a brand is. True environmental responsibility needs honesty, not just a higher price.
