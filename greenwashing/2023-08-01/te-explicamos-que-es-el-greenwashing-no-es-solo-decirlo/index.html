Te explicamos qué es el ‘Greenwashing’: ¡No es solo decirlo!

The central issue highlighted is the dangerous gap between public climate commitments and actual actions, a phenomenon known as greenwashing. When governments and corporations announce ambitious environmental goals without implementing rigorous strategies, these declarations become mere marketing tools or political gestures rather than effective solutions. This disconnect prevents real progress, as announced intentions often mask a lack of substantive effort to address the climate crisis. The relevance to greenwashing is evident in how entities exploit the perception of environmental responsibility to boost their image while continuing harmful practices. Research demonstrates that most major companies claiming net-zero emissions are on track to reduce only a fraction of their actual carbon footprint, revealing that their plans often exclude significant portions of their supply chains. This discrepancy allows organizations to benefit from positive public perception and potentially higher profits without bearing the true cost of decarbonization, thereby misleading stakeholders and delaying necessary ecological changes. To combat this, genuine climate action requires strict adherence to transparency and comprehensive planning rather than superficial announcements. Effective strategies must include public, updated transition plans, the elimination of fossil fuel use, and accountability through standardized reporting. Ultimately, achieving critical temperature limits demands that society and institutions move beyond rhetoric, ensuring that every commitment is backed by verified, substantial actions that prioritize nature and people over corporate gain.
