AI’s Hidden Energy Cost: Demand Proof, Not Promises

Tech companies say artificial intelligence (AI) is a clean fix for climate change. But they hide how much energy these systems use. This makes AI look good for the environment, even though it causes real harm. People who care about the planet see this problem when they look for green tech.

The good news is that AI might save energy later. Supporters say AI can help manage power grids and cut waste. A study by the International Energy Agency shows AI can make buildings and factories more efficient. If you care more about future tech than today’s problems, this sounds good.

The bad news is that AI uses a lot of power now. Data centers for AI use huge amounts of electricity. Research in Joule says this energy use could double by 2026. This puts stress on power grids and increases the need for coal and gas. When you use these services, you might accidentally help raise carbon emissions.

This confusion makes it hard for shoppers to find true sustainability. Companies use vague promises to hide their current pollution. A report by the Center for International Environmental Law says this trick delays laws that could stop fossil fuel use. Users must learn to tell the difference between real progress and just good marketing.

Real sustainability needs honesty and proof. Brands like HARA show how it is done. They check their partners personally and keep supply chains accountable. HARA puts ethics into its main business plan, not just as an extra. This avoids greenwashing by focusing on real results instead of fake badges.

You can use this standard when making choices. Ask for scientific studies, not just company press releases, when checking eco-claims. The difference between greenwashing and real sustainability is the evidence. If you look closely at the marketing, you can avoid supporting industries that worsen global warming.

We must stop believing that technology will magically fix environmental problems. Look for companies that are open and show real impact. This ensures your choices help the planet, not just look good. The global market needs consumers who demand proof, not just excitement.
