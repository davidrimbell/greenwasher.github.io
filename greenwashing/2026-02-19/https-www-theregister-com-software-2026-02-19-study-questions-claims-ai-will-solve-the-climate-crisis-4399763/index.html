Greenwashing
ES EN

The article reveals a significant discrepancy between the climate benefits claimed by AI advocates and the environmental reality of generative AI. Research indicates that the industry is conflating traditional AI, which may offer modest efficiency gains, with the highly energy-intensive infrastructure required for generative models. This strategic confusion serves as a classic example of greenwashing, where companies deflect criticism of their massive carbon footprints by promoting unverified long-term benefits that do not apply to the specific technologies driving current emissions. Furthermore, the analysis exposes a severe lack of credible evidence supporting the narrative that AI will solve the climate crisis. The vast majority of claims regarding AI’s environmental advantages rely on corporate publications or lack citations entirely, with only a fraction grounded in peer-reviewed academic research. This suggests that the industry is fabricating a narrative of net-positive climate impact to justify the rapid expansion of fossil-fuel-powered data centers, misleading policymakers and the public about the true ecological cost of these technologies. This investigation is crucial to understanding greenwashing because it highlights how corporations manipulate scientific ambiguity to maintain their operations without accountability. By linking unsubstantiated theoretical benefits to immediate, tangible environmental harm, the AI sector is attempting to legitimize infrastructure that exacerbates global warming. The report urges advocates to reject this misleading discourse and focus on preventing the construction of new fossil-fuel-dependent facilities, emphasizing that current claims are not just wrong, but potentially intentional disinformation campaigns designed to delay necessary regulatory action.
