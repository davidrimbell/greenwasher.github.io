Monday briefing: What can be done to offset the environmental cost of cruise ships?

The article highlights the cruise industry’s rapid expansion and escalating environmental footprint, revealing how massive new vessels rely heavily on polluting fossil fuels. Despite growing passenger numbers, the industry continues to prioritize size and capacity over sustainability, resulting in significant air pollution and carbon emissions that far exceed those of other common modes of travel. A central focus is the prevalence of greenwashing, particularly the industry’s misleading classification of liquified natural gas (LNG) as a clean fuel. Critics argue that this designation ignores substantial methane leakage and offers a false sense of environmental responsibility, allowing companies to market unsustainable practices as eco-friendly initiatives while avoiding the higher costs of genuine clean energy solutions. This issue is relevant to greenwashing because it exposes how luxury industries exploit regulatory loopholes and ambiguous labeling to deflect criticism. The report calls for stricter transparency, taxes, and real decarbonization measures to counter these deceptive narratives, emphasizing that without accountability, the sector will continue to dump pollution on society under the guise of sustainability.
