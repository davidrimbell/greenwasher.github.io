Ten years of sustainability at Concordia | News

The article examines Concordia University’s long-standing Sustainability Action Plan, revealing a stark contrast between its ambitious public commitments and its operational reality. While the institution markets itself as environmentally responsible and has achieved certain ecological targets, it has simultaneously failed to address critical social sustainability issues, such as student affordability and financial precarity. This disparity highlights a core tension in higher education sustainability efforts: the prioritization of measurable environmental metrics over the human well-being of the student body. This imbalance fuels accusations of greenwashing, as the university appears to use its environmental initiatives as a marketing tool to attract students while neglecting their fundamental economic needs. Critics point to the selective divestment from fossil fuels and the pursuit of high-level sustainability certifications as superficial gestures that mask a deeper institutional failure to support those they serve. The argument suggests that true sustainability cannot exist without addressing the social and economic barriers that prevent students from accessing education, making the current approach feel performative rather than transformative. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the definition of sustainability as merely ecological, ignoring the broader social context required for genuine responsibility. By focusing on visible environmental wins while classifying affordability as "not pursued" due to lack of data, the university exemplifies how organizations can create a false image of holistic responsibility. This case illustrates the danger of separating environmental goals from social justice, suggesting that such decoupling is a common tactic used to maintain a positive public image without implementing substantive, equitable changes.
