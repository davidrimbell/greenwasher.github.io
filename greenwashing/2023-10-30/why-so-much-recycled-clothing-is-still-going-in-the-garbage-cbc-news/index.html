Why so much recycled clothing is still going in the garbage | CBC News

Major retail brands are misleading consumers with vague sustainability claims, particularly regarding recycled materials, which constitute a significant form of greenwashing. Investigations reveal that products labeled as eco-friendly often contain minimal recycled content or are still predominantly made from fossil fuel-based plastics. By highlighting small sustainable features while obscuring the overall environmental impact, companies distract shoppers from the reality that these items are essentially single-use plastics destined for landfills, thereby perpetuating a linear consumption model rather than a circular one. The term "recycled" is frequently used inaccurately to imply true environmental benefit, when in practice it often refers to downcycled plastic bottles that cannot be effectively turned back into new clothing. Experts argue that this marketing exploits consumer desire for sustainability, misleading them into believing they can endlessly consume new garments knowing they will be recycled. However, the technology for textile-to-textile recycling is not yet scalable, meaning most "recycled" fashion contributes to waste accumulation rather than reducing it, despite the lower carbon footprint of recycled polyester compared to virgin materials. This widespread greenwashing harms both consumers, who pay premiums for ineffective solutions, and legitimate sustainability efforts by diluting the value of authentic environmental progress. Regulatory bodies have already begun challenging these practices, noting that vague terms like "conscious" fail to explain actual sustainability benefits. As a result, companies face increasing scrutiny and promises to reform labeling, yet the discrepancy between marketing narratives and the actual recyclability of blended fibers remains a critical issue, undermining trust and slowing meaningful industry change.
