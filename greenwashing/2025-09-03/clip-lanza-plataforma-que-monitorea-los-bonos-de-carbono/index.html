This article highlights a critical investigative platform exposing systemic flaws in Colombia’s carbon credit market, revealing how corporate sustainability claims often mask harmful realities. The central conclusion is that the market suffers from severe opacity and lack of regulation, allowing powerful entities to exploit environmental mechanisms without adequate oversight or accountability to affected local populations. The investigation identifies major issues including double counting of emissions reductions and the marginalization of indigenous and Afro-Colombian communities. These groups frequently lack knowledge about projects affecting their territories and face social fractures caused by corporate agreements that bypass legitimate local governance, demonstrating how greenwashing thrives on information asymmetry between project developers and beneficiaries. Regulatory delays further exacerbate these vulnerabilities, leaving significant gaps in transparency and consumer protection. This case is relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how companies can purchase carbon offsets to appear environmentally responsible while contributing to social injustice and ecological mismanagement. The platform provides essential public data to challenge these misleading narratives and demand genuine corporate accountability.
