Greenwashing in India: key rules and regulatory insights

Greenwashing in India: key rules and regulatory insights

This article highlights how greenwashing allows companies to fabricate environmental responsibility for competitive advantage, prompting India to establish robust regulatory frameworks to counter deceptive marketing. The Central Consumer Protection Authority and the Advertising Standards Council have introduced strict guidelines demanding transparency, requiring all sustainability claims to be substantiated by verifiable scientific evidence and credible certifications. This shift ensures that advertisements accurately reflect a product’s environmental impact, protecting consumers from vague or exaggerated promises. In the financial and food sectors, regulators like SEBI and the FSSAI impose rigorous disclosure and verification requirements to prevent misleading investment and labeling practices. These measures mandate independent third-party assessments and detailed transparency regarding supply chains, effectively closing loopholes that previously allowed firms to obscure their true ecological footprint. By enforcing accountability across industries, these regulations aim to eliminate the gap between marketing rhetoric and actual sustainable performance. Relevance to greenwashing lies in exposing the mechanisms that enable corporate deception and detailing the evolving legal recourse available to hold entities accountable. By clarifying the specific responsibilities of advertisers and endorsers, and outlining penalties for violations, the article demonstrates the transition from unregulated self-regulation to enforced compliance. This context is crucial for understanding how policy is being used to dismantle the structural advantages of greenwashing and restore integrity to environmental claims.
