Tribunal en Alemania prohíbe a Apple publicidad verde engañosa sobre compensación en Paraguay - Mundo - ABC Color

Tribunal en Alemania prohíbe a Apple publicidad verde engañosa sobre compensación en Paraguay - Mundo - ABC Color

A regional court in Frankfurt ruled that Apple is prohibited from marketing its smartwatch as carbon-neutral, agreeing with the environmental group Deutsche Umwelthilfe that such advertising constitutes greenwashing. The tribunal found the claims misleading because Apple’s reliance on offsetting projects for residual emissions failed to meet the long-term durability standards expected by consumers. Specifically, the court criticized Apple’s reforestation initiatives for lacking sufficient long-term security, noting that some trees are scheduled for removal within a few years rather than preserving carbon storage for decades. This legal precedent highlights the danger of using short-term or questionable nature-based solutions to justify immediate "neutral" status, exposing the gap between corporate sustainability narratives and actual environmental impact. This case is highly relevant to greenwashing as it demonstrates how regulators are cracking down on vague climate claims tied to external offsets rather than genuine emission reductions within production. It signals a shift toward stricter accountability, forcing companies to substantiate sustainability assertions with verifiable, long-term strategies rather than marketing tactics that obscure their true carbon footprint.
