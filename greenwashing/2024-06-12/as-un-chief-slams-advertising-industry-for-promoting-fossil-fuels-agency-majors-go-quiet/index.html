As UN chief slams advertising industry for promoting fossil fuels, agency majors go quiet

As UN chief slams advertising industry for promoting fossil fuels, agency majors go quiet

UN Secretary-General António Guterres has urgently called for the advertising industry to cease enabling planetary destruction by working with Big Oil, comparing fossil fuel marketing to the regulated tobacco industry. He argues that major agencies must immediately stop acquiring new fossil fuel clients and develop strategies to drop existing ones, asserting that promotional efforts for carbon-intensive sectors directly undermine global climate goals and accelerate environmental degradation. Despite this high-profile appeal, leading global advertising groups largely resist fully divesting from dirty energy, often citing compliance with the Paris Agreement as justification for continuing work with major oil and gas corporations. This resistance highlights a critical greenwashing dynamic: agencies frequently claim ethical alignment while representing clients whose actual emission reduction targets remain weak or insufficient. By maintaining these lucrative relationships, major brands legitimize the climate promises of fossil fuel companies, even when those promises fail to reflect reality or address full supply chain emissions. This situation underscores the urgent need for regulatory intervention, as industry experts note that agencies will continue seeking fossil fuel revenue as long as it remains legal. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the symbiotic relationship between media and energy giants, where sophisticated branding creates an illusion of sustainability to delay the transition to clean energy. Until governments implement strict bans on fossil fuel advertising, the industry will likely continue facilitating the perception that dirty energy is compatible with climate neutrality, thereby obscuring the truth of their environmental impact.
