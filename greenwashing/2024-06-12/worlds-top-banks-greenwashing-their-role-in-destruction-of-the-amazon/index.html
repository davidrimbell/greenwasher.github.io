World’s top banks ‘greenwashing their role in destruction of the Amazon’

This report exposes how major global banks engage in greenwashing by maintaining environmental, social, and governance policies that claim to protect the Amazon while simultaneously funding fossil fuel projects that degrade it. The investigation reveals that over 70% of the rainforest falls outside the scope of these banks’ protective guidelines, creating a stark contradiction between their stated ethical commitments and their actual financial practices. The discrepancy arises because these institutions predominantly finance oil and gas companies through general corporate bonds rather than specific project loans. This structural choice allows them to bypass rigorous environmental screening, effectively obscuring the link between their capital and activities that harm Indigenous communities and biodiversity. Consequently, their policies function more as reputation management tools than meaningful safeguards, enabling continued investment in destructive industries despite public pledges to the contrary. The findings are critical to understanding greenwashing because they illustrate how vague definitions and financial structuring are used to hide significant ecological damage. By highlighting the failure of current ESG frameworks to cover the majority of the Amazon, the report demonstrates that superficial compliance does not equate to genuine responsibility, urging a shift toward comprehensive geographic exclusions to ensure financial interests do not undermine global climate and conservation goals.
