Carbon capture project is ‘Band-Aid’ to greenwash $10bn LNG plant, locals say

This article highlights the Rio Grande LNG project in Texas, where developer NextDecade claims to be constructing the world’s greenest facility by integrating carbon capture and storage (CCS). The company markets this technology as a solution to make natural gas exports climate-safe, positioning itself as a clean energy leader committed to net-zero goals. This narrative is designed to reassure investors and the public that expanding fossil fuel infrastructure aligns with global climate objectives, despite scientific warnings against new fossil fuel developments. Critics, including local activists and environmental groups, argue this is a prime example of greenwashing. They point out that CCS only addresses a tiny fraction of the project’s total emissions, while methane leaks and other pollutants continue to devastate local ecosystems and public health. Furthermore, opponents question the technical feasibility of the proposed CCS systems, noting a lack of geological studies and the industry’s history of relying on unproven technology to justify continued extraction and export of fossil fuels. The relevance to greenwashing lies in how CCS is used as a marketing tool rather than a viable climate solution. By selling carbon credits and labeling the project as "clean," the industry attempts to legitimize new fossil fuel production. Experts describe this as "greenwash on top of greenwash," as it prioritizes financial gains and political optics over actual emission reductions, deceiving the public into believing that harmful industrial expansion is compatible with climate safety.
