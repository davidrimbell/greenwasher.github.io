UK faces net zero crisis unless full hybrids are banned by 2030, warns motoring body

The UK government’s proposed ban on petrol and diesel cars by 2030, with consultations extending hybrid availability until 2034, has sparked debate over genuine environmental progress. Experts warn that allowing full hybrids, which rely heavily on internal combustion engines, undermines the transition to zero-emission transport. This policy distinction is critical to understanding greenwashing, as promoting hybrids as a sustainable alternative can mislead consumers. Critics argue such measures erode public trust by blurring the lines between transitional technologies and true electric mobility, potentially delaying the adoption of authentic zero-emission vehicles. Consequently, the debate highlights how regulatory ambiguity enables industries to appear eco-friendly without achieving substantial emissions reductions. It serves as a pertinent example of how seemingly progressive policies may facilitate greenwashing by validating insufficient technological shifts rather than mandating decisive climate action.
