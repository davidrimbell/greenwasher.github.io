Caltrans and VTA Widening Winchester Blvd./I-280 Interchange in San Jose - Streetsblog San Francisco

The article argues that the proposed $153 million improvement to a San Jose freeway interchange is primarily a greenwashing tactic designed to justify massive car-centric infrastructure expansion. While officials highlight minor enhancements for pedestrians and cyclists, such as replacing a dark tunnel with a brighter overcrossing, these changes serve only to mask the project’s core objective: adding a new freeway flyover ramp and widening car lanes. This strategy prioritizes vehicle capacity over genuine sustainable mobility, using superficial "green" improvements to distract from the environmentally destructive nature of the expansion. Furthermore, the project relies on outdated planning metrics that ignore the reality of induced demand, falsely claiming that increased road capacity will reduce pollution and congestion. In reality, making driving easier inevitably encourages more driving, leading to higher emissions and traffic volume. The analysis reveals that the benefits to active transportation are minimal or negative, including the removal of existing bike lanes to accommodate car ramps and a lack of significant safety improvements for walkers. This approach demonstrates a fundamental disconnect between stated environmental goals and actual engineering decisions. This case is critically relevant to understanding greenwashing because it exemplifies how transportation agencies rebrand car-oriented infrastructure as progress for all modes. By bundling small, cosmetic upgrades for cyclists and pedestrians with major highway expansions, authorities create a narrative of inclusivity and environmental stewardship that does not reflect the true impact on climate and safety. It highlights the danger of using tokenistic improvements to legitimize projects that exacerbate global warming and undermine the shift away from fossil-fuel-dependent transportation systems.
