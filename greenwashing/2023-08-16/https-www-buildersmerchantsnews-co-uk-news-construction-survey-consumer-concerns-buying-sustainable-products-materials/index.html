Greenwashing
ES EN

Consumers increasingly desire sustainable home products but face significant barriers, primarily regarding price and trust. A major concern is that many sustainability claims may be misleading, leaving buyers uncertain about the authenticity of environmental credentials. This hesitation highlights a critical gap between consumer intent and market reality, where the fear of deception outweighs the desire for eco-friendly purchases. The complexity of sustainability terminology further confuses shoppers, who often struggle to verify claims or understand the specific language used by businesses. Despite this confusion, a strong majority intend to research the environmental impact of appliances and materials before purchasing. However, they rely on fragmented sources like websites and social media, which may not provide consistent or verified information, creating an environment ripe for deceptive marketing practices. This article is vital to understanding greenwashing because it reveals the precise vulnerabilities companies exploit to mislead buyers. The research underscores that without transparent, verified evidence and consistent messaging across all channels, brands risk losing credibility. It serves as a warning that genuine ESG commitments and clear proof points are essential to combat consumer skepticism and prevent accusations of greenwashing in the competitive home improvement sector.
