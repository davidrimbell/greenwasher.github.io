Greenwashing
ES EN

The article argues that the recent climate summit in the UAE, led by a national oil company executive, exemplifies systemic greenwashing where fossil fuel interests dominate global policy. By allowing major oil-producing nations to host proceedings and exert influence, the resulting agreement vaguely called for "transitioning away" from fuels rather than an immediate phase-out. This outcome validates the strategy of powerful lobbyists who successfully diluted accountability, ensuring that continued fossil fuel expansion remains politically acceptable despite scientific warnings. Big Oil companies further perpetuate this deception through misleading marketing campaigns that highlight unproven technologies, such as algae biofuels and carbon capture, while aggressively expanding actual oil and gas production. Executives manipulate public perception by investing millions in advertisements that suggest a genuine shift toward sustainability, even though their core business remains focused on extracting more petroleum. This dual approach allows corporations to maintain high profits and public trust while avoiding the substantial investments required for a real transition to renewable energy. This narrative is critically relevant to understanding greenwashing because it reveals how industry actors exploit vague terminology and technological hype to obstruct meaningful climate action. The disconnect between public relations claims and actual corporate behavior demonstrates that greenwashing is not merely about superficial branding, but a strategic effort to delay regulatory action and preserve the status quo. Recognizing these tactics is essential for distinguishing genuine environmental commitments from the empty promises designed to protect fossil fuel profits.
