22 brands called out for greenwashing in 2023

Asia is increasingly confronting greenwashing, though regulatory frameworks lag significantly behind Western standards. While South Korea issued its first fines and nations like Singapore and Australia are tightening advertising codes, penalties remain negligible compared to the scale of deception. This regulatory hesitation is often attributed to cultural tendencies to avoid conflict and a lack of robust legal precedents, leaving many companies with minimal incentive to accurately disclose their environmental impact despite emerging guidelines from industry associations. Consequently, corporations frequently exploit vague terminology and omission tactics to mask their true environmental footprint. Major entities in the fossil fuel, automotive, and aviation sectors have been repeatedly challenged for claiming "carbon neutrality" or "sustainability" without substantiation, or by cherry-picking minor wins while ignoring systemic harms. Common strategies include rebranding polluting industries as "green," using ambiguous net-zero pledges for the distant future, and promoting partial solutions like sustainable fuels while downplaying continued high emissions, thereby misleading consumers about the severity of their ecological contributions. This trend underscores the critical relevance of greenwashing to public trust and corporate accountability. The rise in reported cases highlights a global shift where regulators and civil society are actively dismantling misleading narratives, even as some brands retreat into "greenhushing" to avoid scrutiny. For stakeholders, this landscape necessitates a critical examination of sustainability claims, revealing that without rigorous verification and stringent penalties, marketing often obscures rather than illuminates genuine environmental progress.
