Coca-Cola y el greenwashing que mancha la historia de la COP28

Coca-Cola y el greenwashing que mancha la historia de la COP28

Coca-Cola has solidified its position as the world’s largest plastic polluter, maintaining its leadership in this harmful category for five consecutive years despite its massive efforts to improve its image. The multinational employs extravagant advertising strategies and ambiguous voluntary commitments to create a facade of environmental responsibility, while its core problem—plastic pollution—remains unaddressed at its root. This disconnect between its propaganda and operational reality reveals a systematic image-washing strategy designed to deceive the public and dilute its culpability. The article highlights how this corporation funds and promotes ineffective recycling systems, such as Ecoembes in Spain, which inflate real recycling figures and shift waste to countries with weaker regulations, like Turkey. Rather than reducing plastic production, the company fosters a false circular model that does not solve the origin of the problem, generating microplastics and creating dependence on costly and symbolic cleaning processes. The lack of transparency in its methods and the variation of its actions across regions demonstrate that its initiatives are superficial and lack rigorous external auditing. This situation is crucial for understanding greenwashing, as it illustrates how major polluters use communication and false collaboration to avoid legal and reputational accountability. Criticism from organizations like Greenpeace and pressure from investors underscore that the sustainability slogan is a tool of distraction, not a solution. The Coca-Cola case serves as a warning about how image-washing allows companies to continue destructive practices while appearing to lead the fight against climate change, thereby eroding the credibility of global events they sponsor.
