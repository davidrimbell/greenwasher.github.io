Greenwashing
ES EN

Greenwashing has become entrenched in Colombia as a deceptive corporate practice that uses marketing to project an ecological image without taking real action against climate change. Characterized by false or exaggerated claims about sustainability, this phenomenon undermines genuine environmental commitment and pushes sustainable practices to the background, thereby necessitating stricter regulatory frameworks to protect consumers and the environment. The article’s relevance lies in how greenwashing erodes public trust and distorts the real fight against the climate crisis. By creating a facade of responsibility, companies evade the necessary structural transformation, perpetuating environmental damage while appearing to make progress. This disconnect between advertising narratives and actual operations poses a significant risk to corporate reputation and hinders the transition toward a truly sustainable economy. To combat this issue, legal regulation and education for both consumers and businesses are being promoted. Buyers are encouraged to become more critical, seeking independent and verifiable certifications, while companies must adopt transparency, measurable goals, and clear policies. The article concludes that although deception persists, there is a positive movement toward authentic practices driven by technology and greater corporate awareness, which are essential for advancing toward genuine sustainability.
