Holding brands accountable: misleading claims and consumer protection in the Philippines

The article highlights how deceptive marketing practices, ranging from false safety claims to environmental misrepresentation, fundamentally threaten public trust and safety in the Philippine marketplace. By exploiting the gap between consumer expectations and corporate reality, companies undermine the regulatory framework designed to protect citizens, turning individual transactions into broader risks for health, financial stability, and environmental integrity. Greenwashing serves as a critical illustration of this erosion of trust, where corporations fabricate ecological progress to appeal to conscious consumers without making substantive changes. This deception not only misleads individuals but also delays urgent environmental solutions and distorts fair competition, penalizing businesses that genuinely adhere to sustainability standards while allowing polluters to benefit from unjustified market advantages. The persistence of such misleading practices demonstrates the inherent limits of government regulation alone, as digital commerce allows deceptive claims to proliferate faster than enforcement mechanisms can respond. Consequently, the article argues that robust consumer protection requires a cultural shift toward active vigilance, where informed buyers actively verify claims and report violations, serving as the essential first line of defense against corporate deception and greenwashing.
