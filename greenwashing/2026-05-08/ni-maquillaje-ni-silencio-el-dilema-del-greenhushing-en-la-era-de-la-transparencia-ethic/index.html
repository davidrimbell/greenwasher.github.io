Ni maquillaje ni silencio: el dilema del greenhushing en la era de la transparencia | Ethic

Ni maquillaje ni silencio: el dilema del greenhushing en la era de la transparencia | Ethic

The article identifies "greenhushing" as a corporate reaction to stricter environmental regulations and heightened social expectations, where companies choose to conceal or downplay their sustainability initiatives to avoid criticism or sanctions. Although this caution stems from fears of greenwashing, it represents a shift in communication strategy that prioritizes silence over demonstrating genuine commitments, driven by an increasingly stringent regulatory environment. However, this withdrawal from discourse carries significant strategic consequences. By failing to actively manage their narrative, brands lose the ability to influence consumer perception—a key factor given the growing interest of buyers in environmental impact. Silence does not neutralize public evaluation; rather, it empties the communication landscape, allowing third parties to define the company’s reputation and hindering the development of positive, differentiated mental associations around sustainability. To remain relevant, communication must move beyond both excess and absence, relying on verifiable evidence, structural coherence, and transparency about progress and limitations. This approach integrates sustainability with marketing, demonstrating that credibility is earned by acknowledging the complexity of balancing economic, social, and environmental considerations. Ultimately, greenhushing limits brand value in a market where participating in the conversation is essential for competitive success.
