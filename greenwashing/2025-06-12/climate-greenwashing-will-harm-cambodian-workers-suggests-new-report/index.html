Climate ‘greenwashing’ will harm Cambodian workers, suggests new report

The report reveals that major fashion brands are engaging in greenwashing by prioritizing aggressive decarbonization pledges while completely neglecting the welfare of garment workers in key manufacturing hubs like Cambodia. This disconnect highlights a superficial commitment to sustainability, where environmental goals are pursued without addressing the human rights violations that often accompany industrial practices. By ignoring the social dimension of climate action, these corporations present a misleading image of ethical responsibility, effectively using green rhetoric to mask their failure to protect vulnerable labor forces. Furthermore, the absence of just transition policies means that workers bear the disproportionate burden of climate-related risks, such as extreme heat and flooding, without adequate training, financial support, or job security. Factories are adopting greener technologies, yet employees are excluded from the decision-making process and left vulnerable to health hazards and income instability. This lack of inclusion transforms decarbonization efforts into a dangerous shortcut that exacerbates economic insecurity and workplace dangers, demonstrating that true sustainability cannot exist without empowering the workers who make the products. This article is relevant to greenwashing because it exposes how companies can claim environmental leadership while failing to implement comprehensive strategies that integrate social equity. It illustrates that claims of sustainability are hollow when they omit critical components like worker protection and fair labor practices. Ultimately, the report urges brands to move beyond hollow slogans and embrace a holistic approach that ensures the fashion industry is both clean and fair, thereby dismantling the deceptive narratives that often obscure the true cost of fast fashion on people and the planet.
