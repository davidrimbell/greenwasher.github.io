Greenwashing
ES EN

The article exposes how the UAE is leveraging the COP28 summit to greenwash its fossil fuel interests, using fake social media bots to amplify this narrative. The Emirati president of the summit simultaneously heads a national oil company, highlighting a profound conflict of interest. By promoting vague language that prioritizes "zero-carbon alternatives" over explicitly phasing out fossil fuels, the strategy creates a pathway for the industry to maintain influence while appearing committed to climate action. Central to this greenwashing is the promotion of Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS) technology, which critics view as a risky delay tactic rather than a genuine solution. Evidence from failed projects in Norway demonstrates that subsurface storage carries significant, unpredictable geological risks that are often hidden from public discourse. Despite these dangers, the push for CCS allows oil and gas companies to justify continued production under the guise of technological mitigation, undermining efforts to reduce emissions at the source. This relevance to greenwashing lies in the systematic obfuscation of corporate impact through manufactured consensus and selective transparency. While new rules require lobbyist disclosures, the UAE’s approach of engaging fossil fuel executives as essential partners mirrors historical tactics used by industries like tobacco to legitimize harmful practices. Ultimately, the article illustrates how strategic communication and ambiguous policy goals can mask the reality of continued fossil fuel expansion, deceiving the public about the urgency and efficacy of current climate strategies.
