How Fossil Fuel Lobbyists Hijack Climate Action With False Promises

The Fake Promise of Clean: Why Your Good Intentions Are Being Used Against You

We teach our children to care for the Earth. We tell them to recycle and save water. We want them to choose the “eco-friendly” products. We hope to leave them a clean and safe world. But the truth is confusing. Words like green, organic, and biodegradable are everywhere. They have lost their true meaning.

A hidden system tricks us. Big companies and governments use false claims. They want to hide harmful practices. They make us feel good about buying their products. But these products do not help the planet. This betrayal hurts. It feels like a lie.

This problem is bigger than just plastic bottles. It is now part of global energy and politics.

UN Secretary-General António Guterres recently criticized the fossil fuel industry. He called their Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS) technology an “immoral distraction.” Companies spend billions on these complex fixes. They want to keep drilling for oil and gas. At the same time, they pretend to be eco-friendly. This distracts us. It hides the real cause of climate change: burning fossil fuels. This trick makes the industry look sustainable. It lets them grow instead of shrink.

This trick is not just in business. It is also in international politics. Look at Saudi Arabia and the UAE. Saudi Arabia holds carbon credit auctions. They call this a step toward “net-zero.” Critics say it is just a public relations stunt. It hides their reliance on oil. The UAE led the COP28 climate summit. The president of that summit also led a national oil company. This is a clear conflict of interest. They used vague words like “zero-carbon alternatives.” They avoided saying “stop using fossil fuels.” They used bots on social media to spread their message. This is similar to tactics used by the tobacco industry. They try to make harmful habits look normal.

For regular shoppers, this causes daily confusion. Airlines like Delta Airlines claim to be “carbon-neutral.” Now, they face lawsuits. Why? They use unreliable carbon offset markets. There are no strict rules for these markets. Companies can lie about their impact. They buy offsets that might not reduce emissions. This is false advertising. They sell you the feeling of doing good. They do not deliver the reality.

This trend also affects tourism. In the Middle East, some travel companies issue fake carbon reports. They want to look eco-friendly. They do not make real changes. They rely on marketing labels. They do not fix waste or close the loop on resources. This is pure greenwashing. They use words like sustainable to hide a lack of commitment. True sustainability needs big changes. It is not just a PR statement.

We can learn from unexpected sources. Look at Blacklisted News. Their views may be controversial. But they offer a useful lesson. They compare corporate greenwashing to media bias. Just as companies lie about the environment, some news sources lie for power. They want you to think for yourself. Do not just accept what you hear. Check the facts. Resilience comes from evidence, not simple stories.

As mothers and grandmothers, we have a special role. We teach our children to look past the label. Now, we must do the same for the world. We must remember that green is not always good. Natural is not always safe. When a company promises a complex fix, be careful. They might be distracting you. They want to avoid real change.

A better future does not come from fake eco-friendly convenience. It comes from demanding the truth. We must check claims. We must reject lies that protect harmful practices. We must ask, “What is the real cost?” and “Who benefits?” By doing this, we protect our money. We also protect the future we want to build.
