Bruselas avisa a 20 aerolíneas de que se exponen a sanciones por ofrecer a los clientes recargos en los billetes para compensar emisiones

The European Commission and consumer protection authorities have warned twenty airlines about misleading practices that could lead to sanctions, prohibiting the notion that passengers can offset their CO2 emissions through voluntary payments. This intervention aims to combat “ecoposturing,” highlighting that offering the option to neutralize the environmental impact of flights via additional fees creates a false impression of sustainability, which is prohibited under the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive. Specifically, authorities are investigating the indiscriminate use of terms such as “green” or “sustainable fuel” without scientific justification, as well as the presentation of unverified emissions calculators. Authorities are questioning whether these claims are backed by solid evidence, emphasizing that consumers should not be misled into believing that their financial contributions fully counteract the emissions generated by their flights—a practice that distorts climate reality and exploits the public’s good faith. This case is crucial in the fight against greenwashing because it establishes a firm regulatory precedent: environmental claims must be supported by verifiable science and cannot be ambiguous. By demanding responses within a short timeframe and threatening coercive measures, the EU reinforces that commercial communication on sustainability cannot serve as a tool for deceptive marketing, requiring companies to base their claims on real data and preventing individual solutions from masking the lack of clear corporate commitments.
