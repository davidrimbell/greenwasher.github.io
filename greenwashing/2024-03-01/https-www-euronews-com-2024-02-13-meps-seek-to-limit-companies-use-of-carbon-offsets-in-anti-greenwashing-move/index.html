Greenwashing
ES EN

European lawmakers are advancing stringent regulations to combat greenwashing by severely restricting how companies can utilize carbon offsetting. The proposed directive mandates that offsetting can only address residual emissions that cannot be reduced through internal measures, effectively forcing firms to cut their own emissions by 90 to 95 percent first. This approach prevents businesses from using offsets as a primary excuse for inaction, ensuring that climate neutrality claims reflect genuine operational changes rather than financial transactions that allow continued high pollution levels. The regulations also require strict transparency, obligating companies to provide science-based, measurable commitments with verifiable interim targets before making environmental claims. By separating offsetting details from a company’s actual carbon footprint and requiring independent verification, the law aims to stop misleading consumers who might otherwise believe that purchasing offsets equates to zero emissions. This shift targets the growing trend of major corporations pledging net-zero status while relying heavily on market-based solutions instead of concrete decarbonization efforts. This legislative move is critically relevant to greenwashing because it addresses the core deception of the industry: the false equivalence between buying certificates and actual environmental stewardship. By closing loopholes that allow companies to claim climate neutrality while maintaining significant emissions, the EU sets a rigorous global standard for corporate accountability. It protects consumers from deceptive marketing and forces a transition from superficial green claims to verifiable, substantial reductions in greenhouse gas output.
