By contrasting university-based theories with private-sector engineering practices, the text claims that real-world physics proves gases cool rapidly and do not retain thermal energy, thereby invalidating the greenhouse effect theory entirely. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the exposure of potential ideological fraud within environmental policy advocacy. Legal community flags concerns over proposed freeze on greenwashing litigation
The Australian government proposes a three-year ban on private climate-related lawsuits, shielding companies from litigation while they adapt to new mandatory disclosure rules. This moratorium aims to prevent corporate reticence but raises significant alarm among legal and environmental groups, who argue it obstructs accountability and justice for alleged greenwashing practices. Critics warn this pause could halt ongoing legal actions against firms like Santos, potentially stalling efforts to enforce Australia’s strict emission reduction targets. By granting regulatory bodies exclusive enforcement power, the policy risks undermining the immediate legal consequences necessary to curb misleading climate statements during the transition period. This tension highlights the critical intersection of corporate regulation and greenwashing accountability. Without accessible private litigation, victims of deceptive environmental claims may lack effective recourse, weakening the integrity of financial disclosures and hindering genuine climate action despite new reporting standards.

Parliament backs new rules for sustainable, durable products and no greenwashing | News | European Parliament
The European Parliament has approved a directive aimed at combating greenwashing by strictly regulating environmental marketing. The legislation bans vague, unsubstantiated claims such as "eco" or "carbon neutral" and prohibits companies from relying solely on carbon offset schemes to justify their environmental impact. By requiring detailed evidence for any sustainability assertion and restricting labels to official certifications, the law seeks to eliminate misleading practices and create transparency in green marketing. Beyond truth in advertising, the proposal addresses product longevity to counteract premature obsolescence. Manufacturers will be forbidden from designing goods that malfunction early or restricting repairs through proprietary parts. Consumers will gain clearer information regarding repair options and costs before purchasing, ensuring they can choose durable items. This shift incentivizes producers to focus on quality and lifespan rather than planned obsolescence, directly challenging the business model that relies on frequent replacements. This initiative is highly relevant to greenwashing as it establishes legal boundaries against deceptive environmental storytelling. By dismantling the "jungle" of false claims and promoting verifiable sustainability, it protects consumers from manipulative marketing. The directive ensures that green transitions are driven by genuine product improvements rather than superficial labels, holding companies accountable for both their ecological statements and the physical durability of their goods.
