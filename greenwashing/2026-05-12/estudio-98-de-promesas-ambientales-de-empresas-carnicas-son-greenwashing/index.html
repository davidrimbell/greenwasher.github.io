Estudio: 98% de promesas ambientales de empresas cárnicas son greenwashing

A study reveals that 98% of the environmental claims made by major meat and dairy corporations constitute greenwashing. These statements, often imprecise or based on unverified future projections, create a false perception of ecological commitment. This deception allows the industries to maintain polluting practices without facing genuine accountability, while misleading consumers and stakeholders about their actual environmental impact. The research highlights that almost none of these claims are backed by scientific evidence, underscoring the severity of the problem. The use of vague language and the lack of verifiable data erode corporate credibility. By failing to provide concrete evidence, these companies hinder transparency and avoid the accountability necessary for a genuine environmental transition. This finding is crucial for understanding greenwashing, as it exposes the systematic disconnect between green marketing and operational reality. It serves as a warning about how the industry uses sustainability as a public relations tool rather than an ethical practice. Understanding this dynamic is essential to combat misinformation and demand standards of truthfulness in the environmental promises made by the global food sector.
