Scottish salmon farming industry employs greenwashing tactics, new report finds

Scottish salmon farming industry employs greenwashing tactics, new report finds

The report concludes that Scottish farmed salmon is being heavily greenwashed, with industry marketing creating a misleading narrative of high welfare and environmental sustainability. Despite positioning the product as premium and responsibly produced, the analysis reveals that none of the major companies adequately protect fish welfare or the environment. This significant reality gap suggests that consumers are being sold a mistruth about the true ecological and ethical costs of intensive salmon farming. A primary driver of this deception is the strategic use of marketing tools to obscure the industrial nature of production. By removing the word “farmed” from Protected Geographical Indication labels and utilizing ambiguous branding like “hand-reared” or confusing wild-versus-farmed distinctions, companies distance themselves from their actual practices. These tactics create consumer confusion and allow high mortality rates, excessive chemical usage, and poor sea lice management to be masked by sophisticated branding and third-party supplier networks. This article is highly relevant to greenwashing because it highlights how regulatory changes and marketing spin can disguise harmful environmental impacts. It demonstrates the disconnect between certified claims and on-ground realities, such as massive fish deaths and pesticide use. The case illustrates how industries may exploit labeling loopholes and emotional appeals to maintain a sustainable image while continuing destructive practices, thereby deceiving eco-conscious buyers and undermining genuine conservation efforts.
