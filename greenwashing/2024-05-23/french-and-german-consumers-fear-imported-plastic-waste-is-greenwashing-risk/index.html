French and German consumers fear imported plastic waste is greenwashing risk

French and German consumers fear imported plastic waste is greenwashing risk

Majority consumers in Germany and France view importing waste for recycled plastic production as greenwashing, driven by deep suspicions regarding environmental integrity and consumer safety. This skepticism suggests that true sustainability relies on domestic recycling loops rather than global waste trade, highlighting a critical disconnect between corporate sourcing strategies and public perception. The findings are particularly relevant to greenwashing as they expose how brands may exploit complex global supply chains to market recycled content falsely. By shifting responsibility through international waste flows, companies risk accusinging themselves of deceptive practices when consumers perceive these methods as undermining local environmental goals and potentially compromising health standards. Furthermore, regulatory shifts banning exports to non-OECD nations aim to resolve these trust issues by enforcing stricter standards on imported recyclate. However, if domestic capacity lags, the resulting scarcity of compliant material could exacerbate greenwashing risks. Brands must therefore prioritize transparent, local sourcing to maintain credibility and avoid the pitfalls of misleading sustainability claims in an increasingly scrutinized market.
