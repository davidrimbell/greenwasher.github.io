Greenwashing
ES EN

Authorities in the EU, UK, and USA have finally established rigorous legal frameworks, such as the Green Claims Directive and Code, to combat vague environmental marketing. These regulations mandate that sustainability claims be truthful, substantiated, and transparent, shifting the burden of proof onto businesses. This legislative evolution aims to empower consumers with accurate information, ensuring their spending power drives genuine environmental progress rather than misleading narratives. However, this regulatory tightening has inadvertently triggered "green-hushing," where companies remain silent on their net-zero goals to avoid legal scrutiny. While this avoids the risks of greenwashing, it creates a dangerous vacuum of information. Both practices are equally harmful to the transition toward sustainable consumption, as they prevent public accountability and obscure the actual progress companies are making behind closed doors. This dynamic is critical to understanding greenwashing because it highlights that silence is not merely a neutral stance but a potential evasion tactic. The article emphasizes that meaningful sustainability communication requires both rigorous evidence and clear disclosure. By enforcing strict standards, regulators hope to eliminate cliché, unsubstantiated claims while encouraging companies to openly demonstrate their full lifecycle environmental impact, thereby restoring trust in corporate environmental promises.
