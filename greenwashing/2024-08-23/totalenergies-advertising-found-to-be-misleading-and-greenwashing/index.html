TotalEnergies advertising found to be misleading and greenwashing

The Advertising Regulatory Board ruled that TotalEnergies’ claim of supporting “sustainable development” was misleading because it contradicts its core business of exploiting fossil fuels. This decision highlights how corporations often use vague environmental language to mask harmful industrial practices, directly illustrating a classic greenwashing tactic where actions do not align with marketing messages. The ruling establishes a critical precedent in South Africa, affirming that companies cannot use promotional partnerships with conservation bodies to obscure their environmental impact. By prohibiting the use of specific sustainability claims in this context, the body reinforces that truthful advertising requires transparency about a company’s actual contribution to ecological integrity rather than superficial associations. This case is relevant to greenwashing as it demonstrates growing regulatory resistance to false eco-friendly narratives from the fossil fuel industry. It underscores the urgent need for stricter advertising standards and consumer vigilance to prevent the obscuring of climate-related harm, ensuring that environmental commitments are genuine rather than merely promotional tools for continued exploitation.
