Las ONG abandonan la alianza de las aerolíneas y las acusan de ecopostureo con los biocombustibles

The withdrawal of the NGOs Transport & Environment and Ecodes from the Spanish Alliance for Sustainable Air Transport serves as a clear example of how civil society organizations can expose corporate greenwashing by highlighting the industry’s preference for less impactful transition solutions. By leaving the alliance, these entities refuse to legitimize an approach that prioritizes bio-based biofuels, which they consider a distraction that keeps the sector locked into a pattern of dependence on fossil hydrocarbons. The underlying conflict centers on the unequal promotion of synthetic fuels, which are regarded as the only truly scalable and sustainable long-term solution for genuine decarbonization. The NGOs argue that the alliance’s excessive tilt toward the interests of the biofuel industry creates technological paralysis, obscuring the urgency of investing in net-zero-emission alternatives and avoiding so-called “eco-posturing,” where environmental commitment is displayed without the depth required to achieve it. This situation is relevant to the debate on greenwashing because it illustrates the risk that public-private partnerships may become spaces for legitimizing mediocre strategies rather than catalysts for radical innovation. By refusing to become complicit in this dynamic, the NGOs underscore the critical importance of distinguishing between limited transition solutions and transformative technologies, warning that an imbalance in this regard could significantly hinder real progress toward an ecological and equitable aviation sector.
