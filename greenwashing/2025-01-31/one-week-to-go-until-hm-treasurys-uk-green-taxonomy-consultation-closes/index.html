The UK government’s proposed Green Taxonomy aims to establish clear standards for sustainable investments, directly addressing the pervasive issue of greenwashing. By defining which economic activities genuinely support environmental objectives, this framework seeks to provide financial market participants with a reliable reference tool to distinguish legitimate sustainability efforts from misleading claims. A primary implication of this initiative is the potential to channel capital more effectively toward true sustainability goals. However, the complexity of implementation remains a significant concern, with stakeholder feedback being mixed. The consultation highlights the need to balance rigorous standards with usability, ensuring the taxonomy integrates seamlessly with international frameworks without creating unnecessary regulatory burdens. This article is relevant to greenwashing because it demonstrates how regulatory definitions can serve as a defense against deceptive marketing. By mandating clear criteria for sustainable finance, the UK aims to reduce ambiguity that companies often exploit. Ultimately, the success of the taxonomy depends on its ability to enforce accountability while remaining adaptable to evolving environmental science and market needs.
