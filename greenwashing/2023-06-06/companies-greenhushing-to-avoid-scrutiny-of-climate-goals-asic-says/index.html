Companies ‘greenhushing’ to avoid scrutiny of climate goals, Asic says

Australian regulators warn that "greenhushing," where companies hide climate goals to avoid scrutiny, is effectively a form of greenwashing. This practice allows firms to claim strong ESG credentials without verification, creating a misleading green halo while avoiding the hard work of implementation. By staying silent, corporations evade accountability, making it difficult for stakeholders to assess their true environmental impact. The lack of transparency hinders the sharing of knowledge and prevents meaningful progress toward global climate targets. While some companies publicly support international agreements, their underlying business models often contradict these commitments, particularly in the fossil fuel sector. This discrepancy undermines investor confidence and obscures the reality that existing infrastructure alone threatens to exceed climate limits. Regulators emphasize that robust ESG reporting is essential for transparency and informed investment decisions. They identify vague language, unwarranted labels, and unsupported net-zero targets as primary examples of deceptive behavior. Ultimately, hiding behind silence does not protect companies from criticism; instead, it exacerbates the problem by obscuring the gap between stated policies and actual actions.
