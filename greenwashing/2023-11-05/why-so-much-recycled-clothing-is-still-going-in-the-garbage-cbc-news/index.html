Why so much recycled clothing is still going in the garbage | CBC News

Why so much recycled clothing is still going in the garbage | CBC News

Major retailers like H&M, Nike, and Lululemon are aggressively marketing products as sustainable through recycled materials, yet investigations reveal these claims are largely misleading. A significant portion of these "green" labels are unsubstantiated, often highlighting minor recycled components while ignoring that the majority of the garment remains virgin synthetic plastic derived from fossil fuels. This disconnect creates a false narrative of environmental responsibility, suggesting that consuming these items is a viable ecological choice when it is not. This discrepancy is a textbook example of greenwashing, where vague terminology and partial truths distract consumers from the broader environmental harm. By focusing marketing efforts on small sustainable elements, such as recycled polyester blends, brands obscure the fact that these garments are still largely plastic-based and destined for landfills. Experts argue that this strategy exploits consumer goodwill, allowing companies to charge premium prices for products that offer negligible environmental benefits compared to their overall carbon and waste footprint. The prevalence of such deceptive practices harms both consumers and genuinely sustainable companies. Consumers are misled into paying more for items that do not align with their environmental values, while the illusion of circularity undermines the urgent need for systemic change. True textile-to-textile recycling remains technologically difficult and scarce, meaning most "recycled" fashion is merely a marketing spin on plastic waste rather than a solution. Consequently, the current industry trend exacerbates waste and hinders progress toward actual sustainability standards.
