'A case of greenwashing': How the Philippines' energy transition comes at the cost of lives, biodiversity

'A case of greenwashing': How the Philippines' energy transition comes at the cost of lives, biodiversity

The article reveals that the Philippines’ push for "green" transition minerals is causing severe environmental and social harm, directly challenging the sustainability of the global renewable energy sector. Mining operations have devastated forests, contaminated water sources, and encroached upon critical biodiversity zones. This extensive ecological damage undermines the very climate mitigation goals the green energy transition aims to achieve, suggesting that the current approach relies on exploiting natural resources rather than truly preserving them. Furthermore, the extraction boom disproportionately impacts indigenous communities, violating their land rights and forcing them to bear the brunt of climate risks like floods and landslides. The report highlights how the government’s failure to uphold free, prior, and informed consent allows mining companies to operate on ancestral lands under the guise of environmental progress. This systemic disregard for human rights demonstrates how the green agenda is being used to legitimize exploitation, turning communities into collateral damage for corporate and national energy goals. This case is a prime example of greenwashing, where the label of "eco-friendly" masks ongoing destruction and injustice. By framing resource-intensive mining as a necessary step for climate action, authorities are obscuring the true costs paid by vulnerable populations and ecosystems. The article emphasizes that without genuine justice and indigenous leadership, the shift to green energy remains a deceptive narrative that perpetuates colonial patterns of resource extraction rather than fostering a truly sustainable future.
