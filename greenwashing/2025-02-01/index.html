How to Spot Fake Eco-Labels and Avoid Greenwashing Scams

People in the early stages of shopping often face greenwashing. This is when companies lie or exaggerate about how good they are for the earth. This trick helps brands make quick money. It also makes their image look better. They do this without spending real money to change how they work.

A study from the University of Texas at Austin shows that people trust vague eco-labels. This lets companies gain customers fast. But this has big problems for users. It creates confusion. It also breaks trust when claims are not true.

Research from the University of Michigan says something worse happens. When people find out about greenwashing, they feel betrayed. They stop trusting all environmental claims. This makes it hard to make smart choices later. Users also risk buying products that do not help the planet. This wastes money and hurts nature.

For example, a case study by the European Commission showed how misleading packaging tricks buyers. People choose less sustainable options because they are fooled. To avoid these risks, users must look for proof. Do not just believe slogans.

Tools like TrueGreen (https://truegreen.ai) help check claims. They compare statements to strict laws. These tools expose lies. They also help companies follow rules. By using data to verify claims, users can tell the difference. They can see real efforts from empty promises. This ensures your purchases help the environment, not just marketing tricks.
