Teesside University Collaborates with Industry Partners to Advance Net Zero Goals

Hydrogen is critical for net zero goals, but its volatility complicates safe storage and transport. This research investigates chemical hydrides as a safer, more efficient alternative storage medium. By enabling production at industrial hubs, the study aims to reduce energy demands and facilitate widespread distribution across the UK. The initiative supports a just transition to net zero by minimizing costs and maximizing societal benefits. Collaborating experts emphasize achieving emissions reductions efficiently, ensuring that the infrastructure developments align with consumer interests and network stability while accelerating the shift to sustainable energy. This article highlights greenwashing risks by showcasing how new technologies are often marketed as instant green solutions. While promising, such innovations require rigorous validation to ensure they deliver genuine environmental benefits rather than serving as mere public relations tools for decarbonization narratives without addressing underlying practical challenges.
