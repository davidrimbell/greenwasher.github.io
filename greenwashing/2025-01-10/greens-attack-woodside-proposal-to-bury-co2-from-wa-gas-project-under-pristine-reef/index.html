Greens attack Woodside proposal to bury CO2 from WA gas project under pristine reef

Woodside Energy’s proposal to store carbon dioxide from the Browse gas project in undersea formations off Western Australia has sparked intense controversy, with environmental groups and political opponents labeling it a classic example of greenwashing. Critics argue that this scheme allows the fossil fuel industry to mask significant ongoing emissions under the guise of environmental responsibility, effectively using carbon capture as a license to continue polluting rather than addressing the root causes of climate change. The core concern lies in the perceived inadequacy and risk of the proposed solution. Opposition leaders and conservationists emphasize that dumping carbon offshore is a high-cost, high-risk strategy that has historically failed globally. They contend that the technology is unreliable, prone to leaks, and threatens endangered marine species, thereby failing to deliver the promised emission reductions while exposing sensitive ecosystems to potential disaster. This article is relevant to the discourse on greenwashing because it illustrates how companies may employ complex, unproven technologies to create an illusion of sustainability. By framing a controversial fossil fuel expansion with a carbon storage narrative, the project attempts to align itself with climate goals while maintaining business-as-usual operations, highlighting the gap between corporate environmental claims and actual ecological impact.
