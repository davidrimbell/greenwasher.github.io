FranklinTill lists nine principles for a shift to regenerative materials

FranklinTill lists nine principles for a shift to regenerative materials

The article outlines principles for textile designers to combat greenwashing by shifting from mere sustainability to true regeneration. It argues that genuine ecological responsibility requires actively healing ecosystems rather than just maintaining the status quo. By understanding the full lifecycle of materials, brands can avoid superficial claims and ensure their products contribute positively to both the environment and society, thereby establishing credibility against deceptive marketing practices. Key strategies include enriching communities, restoring biodiversity, and ensuring radical transparency. The text emphasizes that regenerative practices must improve livelihoods, protect indigenous heritage, and utilize waste streams to create circular economies. Crucially, it highlights the role of technology in tracking material origins, allowing brands to verify their environmental impact. This traceability is essential for distinguishing legitimate efforts from greenwashing, as it provides undeniable proof of ethical sourcing and production standards. Ultimately, this framework is relevant to greenwashing because it provides a concrete, multi-dimensional definition of integrity that goes beyond vague "eco-friendly" labels. By demanding specific outcomes like soil health and fair labor, it creates a high bar for authenticity. This approach empowers consumers and industry professionals to identify and reject vague sustainability claims, forcing brands to substantiate their environmental and social contributions with tangible, verifiable data and holistic design strategies.
