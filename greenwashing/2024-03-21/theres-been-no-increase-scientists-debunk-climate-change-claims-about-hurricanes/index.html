‘There’s Been No Increase’ Scientists Debunk Climate Change Claims About Hurricanes

The article argues that attributing increased hurricane severity to climate change is a manipulative narrative designed to push a political agenda rather than reflect scientific reality. Authors contend that while official agencies and leaders frequently blame human-caused warming for extreme weather, historical data actually suggests hurricanes are becoming smaller and less intense over time. This discrepancy highlights a critical greenwashing tactic: leveraging public fear of environmental disaster to validate specific policy outcomes, even when observational evidence contradicts these alarming claims. Experts in the text assert that the perceived increase in hurricane damage is largely due to coastal development and increased infrastructure value, not worsening storm physics. By focusing solely on wind speed and central pressure while ignoring storm size, agencies allegedly obscure the fact that modern hurricanes are more compact. This selective presentation of data serves to maintain the illusion of escalating climate threats, thereby justifying heightened regulation and alarmism under the guise of scientific accuracy, despite evidence showing stable or improving safety metrics regarding climate-related disasters. Relevance to greenwashing lies in the deliberate distortion of environmental risks to serve ideological goals. The piece exposes how organizations may use sensationalized messaging and incomplete data sets to create a false sense of urgency about climate impacts. By framing natural variability and economic factors as symptoms of catastrophic global warming, these entities engage in deceptive communication practices that mislead the public, ultimately prioritizing political narratives over transparent, data-driven understanding of meteorological trends.
