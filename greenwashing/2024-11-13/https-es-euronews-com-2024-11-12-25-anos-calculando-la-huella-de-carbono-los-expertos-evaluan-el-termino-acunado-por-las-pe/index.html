Greenwashing
EN ES

The article argues that while the concept of the "carbon footprint" successfully raised individual awareness, it has inadvertently shifted the burden of environmental responsibility onto consumers rather than holding major corporations and policymakers accountable. Researchers highlight that this focus on personal lifestyle choices has failed to drive the systemic political and industrial changes necessary to combat climate change, allowing polluters to avoid the more complex and costly task of reducing their own emissions. This dynamic is particularly relevant to greenwashing because it reveals how the term was popularized by oil giants like BP to distract from their own role in environmental degradation. By promoting calculators and messages that emphasize individual action, these companies effectively created a smokescreen, enabling them to deflect scrutiny and delay necessary regulations. This strategy allows them to maintain their business models while appearing to support sustainability, a classic tactic of greenwashing where public relations campaigns obscure the true scale of corporate pollution. Furthermore, the lack of standardized definitions for carbon footprints creates confusion that corporations can exploit, while experts advocate for broader metrics like the ecological footprint. These alternative measures account for resource depletion and waste, offering a more complete picture of environmental impact than carbon emissions alone. The shift towards these comprehensive indicators is crucial for exposing the limitations of individual-focused narratives and demanding genuine systemic accountability from the entities that have the greatest power to effect real change.
