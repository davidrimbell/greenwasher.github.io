Protesters condemn COP29 host Azerbaijan’s greenwashing of humanitarian crimes | News

Protesters condemn COP29 host Azerbaijan’s greenwashing of humanitarian crimes | News

The article exposes Azerbaijan’s strategic use of COP29 as a vehicle for greenwashing, masking its status as an authoritarian petrostate and ongoing human rights abuses behind a performative environmental image. By hosting the UN climate conference while simultaneously expanding fossil fuel production and engaging in ethnic cleansing in Nagorno-Karabakh, the government attempts to legitimize its regime through ecological diplomacy rather than genuine sustainability. Critics argue that this dual strategy allows Azerbaijan to benefit from international legitimacy and investment while concealing crimes against humanity. The presence of a former oil company executive leading the conference underscores the deep entanglement between state interests and the fossil fuel industry, suggesting that the event serves to sanitize the nation’s reputation rather than address the climate crisis or its humanitarian impacts. This case is critically relevant to greenwashing as it demonstrates how environmental summits can be exploited to distract from severe ecological and ethical violations. It highlights the danger of accepting state-led environmental pledges without scrutinizing their political context, showing how green narratives can effectively obscure authoritarian control and the displacement of indigenous populations under the guise of global cooperation.
