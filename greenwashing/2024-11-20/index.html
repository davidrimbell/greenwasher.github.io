Scientists warn 'vague' net zero rules could derail climate goals
Vague net-zero guidelines risk accelerating global warming by allowing nations to rely on natural carbon sinks rather than eliminating fossil fuel emissions. This misuse of forests and oceans compromises their ability to stabilize temperatures, effectively undermining the Paris Agreement’s critical 1.5°C target. True climate stability requires a shift to "geological net zero," ensuring all emissions are permanently captured and stored. Without adopting this rigorous standard, countries continue to offset ongoing pollution, failing to address historical carbon buildup and perpetuating environmental damage. This scenario exemplifies greenwashing, where ambiguous terminology obscures the reality of continued fossil fuel dependence. By prioritizing superficial offsets over genuine decarbonization, governments and corporations deceive the public, delaying necessary action and jeopardizing global climate goals.
