Azerbaijan Greenwashes Authoritarianism At COP29| Countercurrents

Azerbaijan Greenwashes Authoritarianism At COP29| Countercurrents

The article argues that Azerbaijan’s hosting of COP29 serves primarily as a mechanism for greenwashing, allowing the regime to mask its authoritarian governance and fossil-fuel dependency with superficial climate commitments. Despite signing international agreements, the government lacks the regulatory frameworks or political will to implement meaningful sustainability measures, prioritizing the expansion of oil and gas exports over genuine environmental stewardship. This discrepancy is highlighted by the country’s systemic governance failures and severe repression of civil society. With a concentrated power structure that silences dissent and imprisons activists, Azerbaijan cannot credibly lead on climate justice or human rights. The regime’s strategy appears focused on image management and legitimizing its rule on the global stage, rather than addressing the urgent need for energy transition or democratic accountability. This case is crucial to understanding greenwashing as it exemplifies how authoritarian states exploit high-profile international forums to deflect criticism and legitimize their regimes. By leveraging the prestige of events like COP29, governments can project an image of environmental responsibility while simultaneously stifling the civic space necessary for transparent, effective climate action. It underscores the danger of hosting such summits in nations with poor human rights records, as it risks validating policies that prioritize regime survival over planetary and social well-being.
