Addressing Greenwashing in Ghana’s Green Transition: Enhancing public awareness and consumer protection against false eco-claims - MyJoyOnline

This article argues that Ghana’s vital green transition is at risk of being derailed by widespread greenwashing, a deceptive practice where corporate and government actors falsely market their environmental credentials. By exaggerating sustainability efforts or hiding significant ecological damage behind vague eco-friendly labels, these entities mislead investors and consumers, ultimately undermining genuine climate action and diverting resources away from legitimate solutions. The prevalence of greenwashing in Ghana stems from weak regulatory frameworks and a lack of independent verification standards, allowing companies to use misleading buzzwords without accountability. This regulatory gap is exemplified by stark contradictions, such as the government’s ambitious climate pledges at international forums coexisting with severe domestic environmental degradation caused by illegal mining and unchecked industrial pollution. Such inconsistencies erode public trust and distort the market, making it difficult for truly sustainable businesses to compete. To safeguard the integrity of the green economy, the article proposes urgent policy interventions, including a national law mandating strict, third-party verification of all environmental claims. By implementing severe penalties for false advertising and establishing a national certification authority, Ghana can align its practices with global best practices. These measures are essential to ensure that sustainability commitments translate into tangible environmental benefits rather than serving merely as superficial branding exercises.
