Lawmakers Question California Cap and Trade Policies, Citing ProPublica Report

Legislators have formally demanded a review of California’s cap-and-trade program after investigations revealed that its forest offset component likely issues millions of carbon credits with negligible or nonexistent climate benefits. The core issue lies in the program’s reliance on these offsets to meet emission reduction targets, which allows major polluters to purchase cheap permits instead of making substantial, expensive operational changes to cut their own greenhouse gas output. The investigation highlights that a significant portion of these forest credits are effectively "ghost" credits, generated through flawed rules that ignore geographic nuances and include projects that were never at risk of deforestation. By allowing non-additive preservation efforts to count toward strict climate goals, the system undermines scientific integrity. This creates a market where questionable offsets substitute for genuine emissions reductions, threatening the credibility and effectiveness of the state’s broader climate strategy. This situation is critically relevant to greenwashing because it demonstrates how regulatory frameworks can be manipulated to create an illusion of environmental progress without delivering actual ecological improvements. When companies buy credits for forests that provide no real, additional carbon storage, they market their carbon neutrality based on false premises. This not only misleads consumers and investors but also perpetuates high emissions by keeping the financial incentive to genuinely reduce pollution artificially low, thereby delaying urgent climate action.
