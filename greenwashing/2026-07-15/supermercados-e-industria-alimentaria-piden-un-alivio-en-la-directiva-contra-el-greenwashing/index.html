Supermercados e industria alimentaria piden un alivio en la directiva contra el 'greenwashing'

The new European EmpCo directive marks a crucial milestone in the fight against greenwashing, establishing strict limits on the environmental labeling of products. Its primary objective is to protect consumers from misleading claims and ensure that eco-friendly terms reflect a genuine commitment. By prohibiting the use of vague adjectives such as “sustainable” or “eco” without rigorous external verification, the regulation aims to restore trust in environmental claims and prevent companies from exploiting public concern about climate change through superficial marketing. The implementation of these rules has generated significant friction between authorities and the business sector. Major industry and retail associations argue that the current requirements are too stringent and costly, particularly due to the immediate compliance deadline. These entities warn that strict enforcement could force the mass destruction of existing stock, causing severe economic damage. This tension highlights the challenge of balancing regulatory ambition with the practical viability of the supply chain. The relevance of this article to greenwashing lies in how European policy is closing the loopholes that previously allowed deceptive advertising. By requiring official certifications and restricting the communication of artificial climate offsets, the legislation eliminates the ambiguity that often favored questionable practices. However, the current debate over flexibility in enforcement demonstrates that the transition toward a truly transparent economy remains a complex process, subject to intense negotiations to avoid unintended collateral consequences in the market.
