Watchdog bans Shell adverts for 'misleading' low-carbon claims

The Advertising Standards Authority ruled that Shell’s advertisements were misleading because they failed to disclose that fossil fuel extraction still constituted the vast majority of the company’s operations. By highlighting specific renewable energy products and investments without context, the ads created an exaggerated impression of Shell’s overall commitment to low-carbon activities, thereby omitting material information about its significant environmental impact. Shell argued that the campaign aimed to raise awareness of its transition efforts, claiming consumers already understood its core business. However, regulators determined that this omission misled the public regarding the scale of the company’s green credentials relative to its continued reliance on oil and gas. The ruling emphasizes that marketing focusing on future ambitions or minor renewable segments is deceptive when it obscures the reality of a corporation’s dominant carbon-intensive business model. This case is a critical benchmark for combating greenwashing, as it establishes that omitting the proportion of high-emission activities from promotional materials constitutes fraud. It signals stricter enforcement against energy majors attempting to rebrand themselves as sustainable while continuing to expand fossil fuel infrastructure. Consequently, advertisers and corporations must ensure that claims about green initiatives are balanced with clear disclosures about their primary revenue sources to avoid regulatory penalties and public distrust.
