Greenwashing
ES EN

Sustainability has become a powerful driver for consumer loyalty and premium pricing in the food industry, yet this trend is overshadowed by widespread greenwashing. Many environmental claims lack supporting evidence or are vaguely worded, creating a significant trust gap. As regulators move to eliminate misleading messaging, companies risk severe reputational damage if they cannot substantiate their environmental assertions with rigorous proof. To address this, the European Union is implementing a stringent Directive on Green Claims to ensure all environmental statements are reliable, comparable, and verifiable across member states. This regulatory shift aims to protect consumers and establish a fair market environment by enforcing transparency. Although the timeline for full implementation extends into the future, food producers are urged to act immediately rather than waiting for enforcement, as compliance becomes a critical component of brand integrity and legal safety. Simultaneously, the UK is intensifying scrutiny through its Green Claims Code, with authorities targeting household and food products for potential deception. Experts advise manufacturers to adopt a proactive strategy by identifying specific claims, validating their accuracy, and ensuring all promotional materials are truthful. With regulators taking a hard-line stance on both intentional and unintentional greenwashing, substantiating environmental benefits is no longer optional but essential for avoiding penalties and maintaining consumer trust.
