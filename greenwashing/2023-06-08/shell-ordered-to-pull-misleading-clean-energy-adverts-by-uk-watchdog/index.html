Shell ordered to pull 'misleading' clean energy adverts by UK watchdog

The Advertising Standards Agency has ordered Shell, Petronas, and Repsol to withdraw advertisements that significantly overstate their commitment to renewable energy. By showcasing electric vehicle infrastructure, wind turbines, and biofuels, these fossil fuel giants created a misleading impression of their operations. The rulings indicate that consumers are likely to underestimate the vast majority of these companies’ investments in carbon-intensive activities, thereby confusing their marketing narratives with their actual environmental impact. This case highlights the core mechanism of greenwashing: emphasizing small, "green" aspects of a business while omitting or downplaying its dominant role in fossil fuel extraction. Although Shell allocated only a tiny fraction of its capital to clean energy, its ads suggested a significant transition. Similarly, Petronas and Repsol implied that sustainable solutions were central to their identity despite being major sources of greenhouse gas emissions. The regulators concluded that these campaigns lacked the necessary context to accurately reflect the companies’ true carbon footprints. These decisions are highly relevant to the greenwashing discourse as they demonstrate increasing regulatory scrutiny of "transition" marketing. By ruling that such imagery misleads the public into viewing these firms as sustainable, the ASA challenges the industry’s strategy of using advertising to rehabilitate its image. This precedent suggests that highlighting minor environmental efforts without proportional evidence is unacceptable, potentially deterring other energy majors from relying on vague claims of sustainability to distract from their ongoing environmental damage.
