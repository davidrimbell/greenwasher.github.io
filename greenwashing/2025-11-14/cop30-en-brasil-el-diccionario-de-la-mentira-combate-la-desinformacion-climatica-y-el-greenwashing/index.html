COP30 en Brasil: el “Diccionario de la mentira” combate la desinformación climática y el greenwashing

The COP30 in Belém marks a significant shift in global climate policy by explicitly addressing the fight against misinformation and greenwashing as central components of climate action. Organizers and civil society groups have framed the summit as a forum for truth, recognizing that deceptive narratives and disinformation campaigns are actively undermining the credibility of scientific consensus. By acknowledging that robust climate decisions require a debate based on facts rather than falsehoods, the conference highlights the critical need to protect the integrity of information alongside traditional negotiations on emissions and financing. At the heart of this initiative is the "Dictionary of Lies," a tool developed by civil society to help journalists and citizens identify specific tactics used to distort reality. These strategies include direct attacks on scientific credibility, astroturfing, and the appropriation of progressive causes to mask environmental harm. The dictionary serves as an educational resource to decode sophisticated disinformation structures, enabling stakeholders to demand greater transparency and resist narratives that perpetuate the status quo of fossil fuel industries. This focus on communication integrity is vital to understanding greenwashing, as it reveals how corporate actors use marketing and false expertise to appear environmentally responsible while continuing harmful practices. By exposing these mechanisms, the COP30 underscores that combating greenwashing requires more than regulatory changes; it demands public awareness and media literacy. Ultimately, the summit demonstrates that dismantling the power of disinformation is essential for ensuring that climate policies are driven by evidence rather than manipulated by those seeking to avoid accountability.
