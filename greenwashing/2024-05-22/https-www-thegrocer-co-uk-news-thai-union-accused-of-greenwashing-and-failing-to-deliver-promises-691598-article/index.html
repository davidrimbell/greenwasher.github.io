Greenwashing
ES EN

Thai Union is accused of greenwashing by charity Shark Guardian, which highlights a significant disconnect between the company’s public sustainability promises and its actual practices. The investigation suggests that the seafood giant uses its corporate social responsibility initiatives as a smokescreen, prioritizing communication strategies over tangible environmental and social progress. This discrepancy raises serious concerns about the integrity of the company’s claim to be a leader in sustainable fisheries. The report specifically criticizes the efficacy of Fishery Improvement Projects and reliance on Marine Stewardship Council certification, arguing these mechanisms lack credibility amidst declining global fish stocks. Furthermore, the charity claims that critical issues like human trafficking are being minimized within these frameworks, with risk assessments postponed indefinitely. These practices are viewed not as genuine conservation efforts, but as industry-funded tools designed to distance the corporation from problematic suppliers and obscure its role in supply chain abuses. This case is highly relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how large corporations can leverage complex supply chains and third-party certifications to create a facade of sustainability. By promoting narratives of progress without delivering measurable results, companies can mislead investors and consumers. The accusations emphasize the danger of accepting corporate self-reporting at face value and underscore the need for independent, rigorous auditing to ensure that sustainability claims reflect reality rather than serving as mere public relations tools.
