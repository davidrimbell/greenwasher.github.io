Sostenibilidad de las empresas, entre escepticismo y greenwashing

Sostenibilidad de las empresas, entre escepticismo y greenwashing

Companies are rapidly advancing their sustainability strategies, but this corporate maturity coincides with a significant rise in consumer distrust. The perception of *greenwashing* has skyrocketed, particularly among younger generations that demand transparency and rigor. This gap between companies’ genuine efforts and the credibility granted to them by the market is at the heart of the problem, as a lack of authentic trust can undermine the reputational benefits organizations seek to achieve. The risk of falling into ambiguous practices that confuse public opinion is now one of the top concerns for executives. To mitigate this negative perception, firms must prioritize accurate measurement and clear disclosure of data regarding their environmental and social impacts. The article’s relevance lies in demonstrating that *greenwashing* is no longer just a moral accusation, but a tangible business risk that affects customer loyalty, especially when consumers actively verify brands’ actual performance before making purchases. Finally, the text underscores operational urgency: sustainability must be viewed as a strategic investment focused on profitability, not as a cost. By implementing efficient measures and circular models based on solid data, companies can drastically reduce their emissions and meet their climate goals. The implicit conclusion is that the only defense against *greenwashing* is verifiable action and double materiality, integrating environmental and social criteria into every business decision to operate within current planetary boundaries.
