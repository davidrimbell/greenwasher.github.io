MEPs to vote on new rules over labelling goods

MEPs to vote on new rules over labelling goods

The European Parliament has overwhelmingly approved a directive to ban misleading environmental claims on products, marking a significant legislative step against greenwashing. This move aims to restore consumer trust by prohibiting vague terms like "natural" or "biodegradable" unless they are backed by official certification from public authorities, thereby shifting the burden of proof onto manufacturers. The legislation specifically targets unsubstantiated durability labels and carbon offsetting claims, such as tree planting, which were previously used to mask unsustainable practices. By forcing companies to provide detailed evidence of product lifespan and repairability, the rules intend to encourage the production of longer-lasting goods and reduce consumer confusion regarding sustainability assertions. Although the ban currently applies only to individual products rather than broader corporate statements, it represents a crucial regulatory shift. By criminalizing unfair commercial practices related to climate and environmental benefits, the directive seeks to protect shoppers from deception and ensure that "green" marketing reflects genuine, verifiable environmental responsibility rather than empty rhetoric.
