MEPs adopt new law banning greenwashing and misleading product information | News | European Parliament

MEPs adopt new law banning greenwashing and misleading product information | News | European Parliament

This new EU directive fundamentally combats greenwashing by prohibiting vague environmental claims like “eco-friendly” or “climate neutral” unless backed by rigorous proof. By banning unsubstantiated assertions and offset-based neutrality claims, the law aims to eliminate deceptive marketing that misleads consumers about a product’s actual environmental impact. The legislation also addresses the confusion caused by numerous private sustainability labels, restricting their use to officially recognized public schemes. This standardization ensures that eco-labels are credible and comparable, preventing companies from exploiting poorly regulated certification systems to appear more sustainable than they truly are. Furthermore, the focus shifts toward durability and repairability, directly tackling premature obsolescence. By banning unfounded durability claims and misleading repairability representations, the directive encourages a move away from throwaway culture. This relevance to greenwashing lies in holding corporations accountable, ensuring that sustainability marketing reflects tangible, long-term product performance rather than superficial, misleading narratives.
