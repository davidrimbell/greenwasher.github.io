EU Parliament Votes to Tighten Rules on Green Claims

The European Parliament has overwhelmingly approved a new directive that strictly bans vague environmental marketing claims, directly targeting the core practice of greenwashing. By prohibiting unverified adjectives like "eco-friendly" or "natural," the legislation forces businesses to substantiate their sustainability assertions with concrete evidence. This shift moves the market away from ambiguous, feel-good messaging toward transparency, ensuring that consumers are no longer misled by superficial labels that lack scientific backing or rigorous verification. A particularly significant aspect of this regulation is the prohibition of "carbon-neutral" claims derived from carbon offsetting schemes. By rejecting the credibility of offset-based neutrality, the directive highlights the gap between marketing narratives and actual emission reductions. This stance validates long-standing criticisms that relying on offsets allows companies to greenwash their operational footprint, thereby protecting the integrity of genuine sustainability efforts and preventing corporations from buying their way out of accountability. Furthermore, the mandate requires retailers to disclose product repairability at the point of sale, combating the hidden greenwashing of planned obsolescence. While it does not outright ban short product lifespans, forcing transparency about repairability empowers consumers to make informed choices against wasteful consumption. This measure, combined with the crackdown on misleading ecolabels, signals a broader institutional commitment to aligning corporate communication with tangible environmental performance, making greenwashing a legally risky and commercially untenable strategy for companies operating in the EU market.
