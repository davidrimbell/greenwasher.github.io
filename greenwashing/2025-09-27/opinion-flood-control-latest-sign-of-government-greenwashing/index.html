[OPINION] Flood control latest sign of government ‘greenwashing’

[OPINION] Flood control latest sign of government ‘greenwashing’

The article argues that the Philippines’ massive climate budget allocation is being exploited through greenwashing, where the appearance of environmental action masks systemic corruption. By channeling the majority of funds into infrastructure projects like flood control and road construction under the guise of climate resilience, agencies misleadingly label standard or corrupt practices as eco-friendly. This tactic diverts resources away from genuine ecological protection and allows officials to conceal malfeasance behind a facade of sustainability, undermining the integrity of climate policy. This misallocation is facilitated by significant weaknesses in the Climate Change Expenditure Tagging system, which lacks robust independent oversight and quality assurance. The current framework allows agencies to self-tag projects, leading to a concentration of power in a single department and enabling the classification of questionable infrastructure as climate adaptation measures. Without stricter verification processes, such as mandatory probabilistic analysis and external audits, the system fails to ensure that public funds actually support environmental justice or effective climate resilience. The relevance to greenwashing lies in how this practice erodes public trust and damages the nation’s reputation by prioritizing political and corporate interests over actual ecological health. The author contends that true climate action requires good governance and a shift toward nature-based solutions rather than infrastructure-heavy spending. Unless the state addresses corruption and strengthens accountability mechanisms, these "green" budgets will continue to serve as tools for fraud rather than meaningful climate mitigation.
