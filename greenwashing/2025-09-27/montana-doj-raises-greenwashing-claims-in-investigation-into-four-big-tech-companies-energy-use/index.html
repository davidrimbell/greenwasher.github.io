Montana DOJ raises greenwashing claims in investigation into four Big Tech companies’ energy use

Montana DOJ raises greenwashing claims in investigation into four Big Tech companies’ energy use

This investigation highlights a critical greenwashing tactic where major tech firms claim to be powered entirely by renewables through the purchase of unbundled Renewable Energy Certificates (RECs). Attorneys general argue this practice is deceptive because purchasing certificates does not guarantee that the specific electricity consumed is clean or that fossil-fuel generation is directly displaced. This discrepancy allows companies to present a misleadingly green image while potentially continuing to rely on carbon-intensive grid power. The allegations extend beyond marketing to include serious concerns about grid reliability and energy security. Critics contend that these tech giants are driving up electricity demand for AI and data centers while locking up stable baseload power sources like nuclear energy. By incentivizing a shift toward intermittent wind and solar for the broader market, these practices may force the retirement of reliable coal and gas plants, thereby increasing the risk of blackouts for local communities and undermining grid stability. This development is highly relevant to understanding greenwashing as it exposes the gap between corporate sustainability branding and actual environmental impact. The investigation serves as a warning that without transparent, granular data on electricity sources, companies can exploit market mechanisms like RECs to mask their true carbon footprint. It underscores the urgent need for stricter regulations and honest accounting to prevent businesses from claiming net-zero achievements while contributing to energy insecurity and continued fossil fuel dependence.
