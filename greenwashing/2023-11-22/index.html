Simultaneously, the UAE promotes "fossil fuel solutionism," a delay tactic that frames the oil and gas industry as an essential partner in climate solutions. This narrative allows major polluters to maintain influence over climate policy, ensuring their continued participation in decision-making processes that should prioritize rapid decarbonization over corporate profitability. This case is critically relevant to understanding greenwashing because it demonstrates how environmental marketing extends beyond simple carbon footprint claims to include ethical and social manipulation. By intertwining performative allyship with industry self-interest, the UAE illustrates a sophisticated form of disinformation that obscures the true urgency of the climate crisis. It highlights how actors can use inclusive rhetoric to legitimize polluters, thereby eroding public trust and obstructing meaningful climate action under the guise of unity and progress.

Australia: Santos and Tokyo Gas to collaborate on carbon-neutral e-methane in Australia
Santos and Tokyo Gas are collaborating to develop carbon-neutral e-methane in Australia, aiming to provide a low-cost decarbonization solution for industries that currently lack viable alternative technologies. By producing this synthetic fuel, they intend to offer a direct substitute for natural gas that can be utilized within existing infrastructure, thereby avoiding the prohibitive costs of building new industrial processes or transport networks. This initiative is highly relevant to the greenwashing debate because it highlights a common tactic where fossil fuel companies promote minor "new energy" ventures to mask their continued reliance on fossil fuels. By leveraging established gas networks for e-methane, the company frames its existing operations as part of a green transition. This strategy risks allowing consumers to perceive a business as sustainable while it continues to depend heavily on traditional hydrocarbon extraction and distribution systems. Furthermore, the focus on hard-to-abate sectors suggests an attempt to extend the lifespan of gas infrastructure under the guise of climate action. If e-methane is primarily used to justify continued fossil fuel use rather than representing a significant, scalable reduction in emissions, it exemplifies how companies might use technological optimism to delay genuine operational decarbonization. The narrative of "affordable, reliable lower-carbon energy" may thus obscure the limited environmental impact compared to true renewable alternatives.

Fidel Salazar: Del “greenwashing” al “greenghosting”
The article highlights a growing media bias that systematically ignores corporate sustainability initiatives in favor of scandalous negative news. This selective disinformation creates a cycle where responsible business actions are hidden, leaving the public with an incomplete and skewed perception of corporate responsibility. The relevance to greenwashing lies in this opaque environment, which allows companies to hide genuine progress or, conversely, masks the lack of substantial action behind vague narratives, making it difficult for stakeholders to distinguish between true commitment and superficial marketing. To combat this, corporations must prioritize being over appearing, ensuring their sustainability efforts are deeply integrated into their core business strategy rather than treated as isolated charitable events. Consistency, transparency, and a willingness to admit ongoing challenges and imperfections are essential. By demonstrating serious commitment through facts and data rather than sporadic publicity stunts, companies can provide the verifiable evidence needed to prove they are not just greenwashing, but actively advancing their environmental and social goals alongside financial performance. Simultaneously, journalists need better training to understand the technical aspects of sustainability to fulfill their role as vigilant observers. The piece argues that sustainable development is not a fleeting trend but an integral part of modern business strategy. When media and public audiences value environmental and social balances as highly as financial metrics, a more balanced narrative emerges. This shift is crucial for exposing greenwashing by ensuring that both positive actions and negative impacts are scrutinized with the same rigor, fostering a transparent ecosystem where accountability cannot be easily concealed.
