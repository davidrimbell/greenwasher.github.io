Greenwashing
ES EN

The article argues that the UAE’s presidency of COP28 is characterized by deliberate disinformation strategies designed to protect fossil fuel interests, specifically through deflecting blame and suppressing dissent. By employing whataboutism, officials shift scrutiny away from their own extensive investments in oil and gas by pointing to the historical actions of other nations. This tactic creates a false equivalence, suggesting that because other polluters exist, the UAE’s conflict of interest should be overlooked, thereby obstructing accountability for the host nation’s primary role in driving the climate crisis. Furthermore, the UAE promotes a narrative of individual responsibility to distract from systemic industrial change. Spokespeople emphasize personal lifestyle adjustments, such as reducing meat consumption and avoiding plastics, while ignoring the need to halt fossil fuel expansion. This mirrors tactics used by major polluters to blame consumers rather than producers, effectively shifting the burden of climate action onto individuals who lack the power to enact the large-scale societal transformations necessary to address global warming. Finally, the host nation attempts to silence valid criticism by dismissing activists’ concerns as mere complaints and misrepresenting their arguments through straw man fallacies. The article highlights evidence of media restrictions and an aggressive stance against dissent, indicating that the UAE is prioritizing reputation management over honest climate negotiations. These actions reveal a profound lack of good faith, underscoring how petrostates can manipulate international forums to delay meaningful policy changes while maintaining their economic dependence on carbon-intensive industries.
