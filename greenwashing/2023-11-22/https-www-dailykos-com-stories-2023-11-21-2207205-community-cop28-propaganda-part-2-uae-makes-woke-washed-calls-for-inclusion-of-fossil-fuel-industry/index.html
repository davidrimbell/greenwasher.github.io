Greenwashing
ES EN

The article exposes how the UAE employs "woke-washing" to mask its fossil fuel interests by co-opting progressive social justice language. By claiming to prioritize inclusivity and solidarity during COP28, the host nation creates a misleading appearance of alignment with values it fundamentally opposes. This tactic serves to deflect criticism regarding severe human rights violations and the suppression of dissent, effectively exploiting social movements to sanitize the image of the petrostate and protect its economic gains. Simultaneously, the UAE promotes "fossil fuel solutionism," a delay tactic that frames the oil and gas industry as an essential partner in climate solutions. COP President Sultan Ahmed Al-Jaber argues for the inclusion of high-emitting industries in negotiations, suggesting that least-carbon-intensive fuels are part of the necessary energy mix. This narrative allows major polluters to maintain influence over climate policy, ensuring their continued participation in decision-making processes that should prioritize rapid decarbonization over corporate profitability. This case is critically relevant to understanding greenwashing because it demonstrates how environmental marketing extends beyond simple carbon footprint claims to include ethical and social manipulation. By intertwining performative allyship with industry self-interest, the UAE illustrates a sophisticated form of disinformation that obscures the true urgency of the climate crisis. It highlights how actors can use inclusive rhetoric to legitimize polluters, thereby eroding public trust and obstructing meaningful climate action under the guise of unity and progress.
