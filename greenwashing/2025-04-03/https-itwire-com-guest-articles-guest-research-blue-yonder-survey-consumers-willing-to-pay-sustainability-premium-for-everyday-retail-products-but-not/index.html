Greenwashing
EN ES

Despite a strong consumer desire to align purchases with sustainability, a significant trust gap remains between intent and action. While most shoppers value eco-friendly practices, a mere fraction believe brands are communicating their initiatives truthfully. This widespread skepticism highlights the pervasive nature of greenwashing, where vague or exaggerated marketing erodes credibility and prevents consumers from making confident ethical choices. Cost acts as a critical barrier, particularly when consumers suspect that sustainability premiums are justified by genuine value rather than marketing spin. Shoppers are generally willing to pay more for everyday items like food and cleaning products but remain hesitant regarding high-cost durable goods. This hesitation suggests that without transparent proof of authentic environmental benefits, consumers view higher prices as a potential risk of deception rather than an investment in quality. Ultimately, the survey underscores that consumer enthusiasm alone cannot drive sustainable markets; brands must provide verifiable evidence of their claims. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the urgent need for transparency and quantifiable metrics. Without clear, authentic communication and supply chain visibility, companies risk losing consumer trust, proving that sustainability must be demonstrated through action, not just advertising, to overcome the stigma of empty promises.
