OceanSaver censured by ASA over environmental claims

OceanSaver censured by ASA over environmental claims

OceanSaver was censured by the Advertising Standards Authority for making unsubstantiated environmental claims about its laundry and cleaning products. Despite arguing that its dissolvable film is not plastic, the watchdog found insufficient evidence that the products are truly plastic-free, zero-microplastic, or harmless to marine life. This ruling highlights how companies can misleadingly use technical distinctions to bypass stricter regulatory definitions of plastic and microplastics. The investigation further exposed inaccuracies in the brand’s biodegradability assertions and savings statistics. OceanSaver claimed significant plastic prevention based on sales volume, yet failed to prove these alternatives actually avoid long-term ecological harm or provide robust lifecycle substantiation. This demonstrates the gap between marketing narratives suggesting total environmental safety and the scientific reality required to verify such bold assertions. This case is highly relevant to greenwashing as it illustrates the risks of vague, unverified eco-labels and the misuse of scientific terminology to appear sustainable. It underscores the critical need for rigorous, lifecycle-based evidence behind environmental claims. By penalizing OceanSaver, regulators signal that consumer trust depends on transparency and precise, proven data rather than simplified, potentially deceptive messaging.
