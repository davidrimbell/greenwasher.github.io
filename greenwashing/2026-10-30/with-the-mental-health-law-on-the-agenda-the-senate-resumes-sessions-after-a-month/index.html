With the Mental Health Law on the agenda, the Senate resumes sessions after a month

The Argentine Senate is resuming its activities with an agenda that includes the controversial reform of the Mental Health Law and the creation of a Regime for Incentives on Relevant Investments. This new regulatory framework aims to fill a legislative gap by offering tax benefits for medium-scale investments, positioning itself between incentives for mega-projects and support for small businesses. Simultaneously, the regulation of the carbon market will be addressed, a mechanism designed to allow the purchase and sale of credits linked to greenhouse gas emissions in order to mitigate climate change. These legislative initiatives reflect the State’s efforts to structure specific environmental and financial markets, although the political priority lies in negotiating the mental health legislation due to the lack of full consensus. The relevance of this article to greenwashing lies in the creation of a previously unregulated carbon market. Such systems can serve as tools for ambiguous marketing if they do not guarantee real and verifiable reductions, allowing companies to offset their emissions without necessarily changing their polluting practices. This raises the risk that environmental damage may be legitimized through financial instruments lacking ecological rigor, generating a false perception of corporate sustainability.
