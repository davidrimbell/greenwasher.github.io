'It's ridiculous': Liberal senator criticises calls to abandon net zero

'It's ridiculous': Liberal senator criticises calls to abandon net zero

Senator Andrew Bragg warns he will resign from the Liberal Party’s frontbench if the opposition abandons Australia’s commitment to the Paris Agreement, arguing that withdrawing would alienate voters and betray future generations. His stance highlights the internal conflict within the party regarding its climate policy, as some members face pressure to drop the net zero target entirely. Bragg insists that maintaining international climate obligations is essential for Australia’s global standing and moral responsibility, framing any retreat as a failure to reduce emissions for the benefit of future cohorts. This tension is exacerbated by the National Party’s unanimous vote to abandon its support for a specific net zero mandate, seeking a cheaper and fairer approach to emissions reduction. This move has spurred demands from some senior Liberals to discard both the 2050 goal and the net zero policy. However, Bragg and other moderates resist absorbing these views, contending that Australia cannot isolate itself from international decarbonization standards. The leadership remains cautious, allowing the Nationals to maintain their position while navigating the deep divisions that threaten party unity ahead of critical policy meetings. The article is highly relevant to greenwashing because it exposes the fragility of political commitments to net zero targets when faced with economic pressures and coalition dynamics. If the Coalition were to dilute its climate stance or continue supporting fossil fuels under the guise of "fairer" solutions, it could engage in greenwashing by maintaining rhetorical support for climate action while undermining substantive policy. The potential abandonment of rigorous emissions goals risks misleading the public about the party’s actual dedication to environmental stewardship, revealing a gap between stated aspirations and strategic intent.
