Radio silence replaces greenwashing claims

Major Australian corporations and superannuation funds are increasingly withdrawing their public climate commitments, a phenomenon termed “green hushing.” This strategic retreat is driven by stricter regulatory scrutiny and the heightened risk of legal action for alleged greenwashing. Entities like AustralianSuper and BHP are removing specific emissions targets and reports, citing the need to navigate a rapidly changing legal landscape and avoid liability for misleading claims. The core issue arises from the tension between corporate sustainability ambitions and the severe consequences of regulatory enforcement. While companies initially publicized these goals to satisfy shareholder and consumer demand, the recent crackdown by authorities such as ASIC and the ACCC has created a chilling effect. Firms are now prioritizing legal safety over transparency, effectively downgrading their ambitious targets to mere aspirations to mitigate the risk of lawsuits for non-compliance or deceptive practices. This trend is critically relevant to greenwashing because it reveals how the threat of penalties can paradoxically encourage a lack of transparency rather than genuine accountability. When companies use the fear of litigation as an excuse to lower their environmental standards, they obscure their actual progress and hinder meaningful climate action. Greenwashing is not just about making false claims; it also involves the strategic silencing of information, which prevents stakeholders from holding corporations truly responsible for their environmental impact.
