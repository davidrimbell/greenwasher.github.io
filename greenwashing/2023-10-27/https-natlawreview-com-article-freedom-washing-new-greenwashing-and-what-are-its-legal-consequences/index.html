Greenwashing
ES EN

Companies risk severe legal consequences for "freedom-washing," defined as making misleading or incomplete claims about their efforts to combat modern slavery in supply chains. This practice parallels greenwashing, where organizations overstate their ethical performance toappease conscious consumers. Regulators are increasingly scrutinizing these representations, viewing inaccurate disclosures not just as reporting failures, but as potentially deceptive conduct that harms market integrity and consumer trust. Multiple Australian regulatory bodies, including the ACCC and ASIC, are expanding their enforcement powers to target false sustainability claims. The ACCC’s guidance on environmental assertions applies equally to modern slavery disclosures, while ASIC actively monitors financial product statements for misrepresentations regarding ESG credentials. This heightened oversight signals that organizations cannot rely on vague or optimistic language; they must provide accurate, evidence-backed transparency to avoid significant pecuniary penalties and enforcement actions. Furthermore, upcoming amendments to the Modern Slavery Act may introduce criminal offenses for submitting materially false statements or failing to maintain adequate due diligence systems. The convergence of stricter domestic laws and emerging international sustainability reporting standards means that misleading claims carry escalating risks, including director liability and shareholder litigation. This article is crucial for understanding how regulatory frameworks are evolving to close loopholes in corporate ethics reporting, ensuring that social responsibility claims are held to the same rigorous truthfulness standards as environmental ones.
