JERA’s claim of ‘CO2-free fire’ is greenwashing, campaigners say | The Asahi Shimbun: Breaking News, Japan News and Analysis

JERA, a major Japanese electricity producer, faces allegations of greenwashing for advertising "CO2-free fire" through ammonia co-firing with fossil fuels. Critics argue this marketing creates a misleading impression of environmental responsibility, as the proposed fuel mix yields negligible carbon reduction and fails to align with critical global warming limits. Furthermore, the environmental impact is ignored because ammonia production and transport generate significant emissions, rendering the claim scientifically baseless and deceptive to consumers. This incident highlights a stark regulatory disparity between Japan and Europe. While the European Union enforces strict guidelines requiring substantiation for eco-friendly claims, Japan’s current consumer protection laws primarily target products rather than corporate advertising. This regulatory gap allows companies to use vague, unverified environmental slogans without facing immediate legal consequences, creating an environment where misleading corporate branding can flourish unchecked compared to the rigorous scrutiny seen in Western markets. The relevance of this case to greenwashing lies in its demonstration of how vague terminology can obscure substantial carbon footprints. As global trade partners like the EU tighten anti-greenwashing regulations, Japanese firms risk losing credibility and market access if they fail to substantiate their environmental assertions. Ultimately, this situation underscores the urgent need for Japan to align its advertising standards with international expectations to prevent corporate misleading practices and ensure genuine progress toward sustainability goals.
