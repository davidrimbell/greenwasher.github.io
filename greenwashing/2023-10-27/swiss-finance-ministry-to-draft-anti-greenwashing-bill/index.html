Swiss finance ministry to draft anti-greenwashing bill

Switzerland’s Federal Department of Finance is drafting an ordinance to strictly regulate sustainable financial products, aiming to eliminate greenwashing by establishing rigorous transparency standards. This regulatory push stems from a government mandate that defines sustainability solely through the achievement of specific goals, rather than mere risk reduction or performance optimization. By requiring clear disclosure of management processes and independent verification, authorities seek to ensure that labels accurately reflect positive environmental and social impacts. The proposed framework prioritizes impact investing and active ownership, setting a high bar for what qualifies as "sustainable." Consequently, many current products relying on ESG integration or exclusions without tangible outcomes may no longer meet these criteria. This shift implies a significant restructuring of the market, as only investments actively contributing to defined sustainability objectives will be deemed compliant, forcing a clear distinction between superficial claims and genuine contributions. Industry associations argue that flexible self-regulation is superior to rigid statutory rules, yet they acknowledge that their frameworks must align with the government’s principles to avoid formal legislation. The potential exclusion of the majority of existing sustainable funds from the label highlights the severe consequences of stricter definitions. This development is highly relevant to greenwashing discussions, as it demonstrates how regulatory clarity can dismantle misleading marketing and enforce accountability in sustainable finance.
