Government releases guidelines to prevent greenwashing by brands

New government guidelines strictly regulate greenwashing, prohibiting brands from using vague environmental terms like “eco-friendly” without credible, verifiable evidence. This shift forces companies to substantiate all sustainability claims with independent certification or scientific data, effectively ending the era of unsubstantiated marketing tactics that previously misled consumers. The regulations mandate full transparency, requiring advertisers and endorsers to disclose all material information via accessible formats like QR codes. By clarifying whether claims relate to production, packaging, or disposal, the rules ensure that comparative environmental benefits are backed by relevant data, preventing brands from highlighting only favorable aspects of their impact while ignoring negative consequences. These measures align with international best practices, aiming to shield consumers from deception while fostering a marketplace built on integrity. Rather than stifling genuine environmental efforts, the guidelines promote truthfulness by penalizing misleading ads. This relevance to greenwashing lies in establishing clear accountability, ensuring that sustainability is defined by substantive action and transparent disclosure rather than superficial branding.
