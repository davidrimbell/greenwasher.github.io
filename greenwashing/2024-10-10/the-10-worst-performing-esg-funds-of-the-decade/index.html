The 10 worst-performing ESG funds of the decade

The article highlights the polarization surrounding ESG investing, framing it as both a politically charged target and a source of skepticism due to rampant greenwashing. Critics on the right view ESG as ideological "woke capitalism" that compromises financial returns, while detractors on the left argue that many funds merely market themselves as sustainable without delivering substantive impact. This divide underscores the complexity of integrating environmental, social, and governance factors into investment decisions, where genuine risk assessment often clashes with superficial branding. At the core of the greenwashing critique is the distinction between using ESG data for material financial analysis and using it as a marketing tool. Experts argue that true ESG integration helps identify tangible risks and opportunities, such as climate exposure or labor practices, rather than simply excluding "dirty" companies or chasing high scores. Greenwashing occurs when asset managers prioritize optics over substance, offering vague sustainability claims that fail to reflect the holistic web of factors necessary for sound investing, thereby misleading investors about the actual impact and performance of their portfolios. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the industry’s struggle to validate its claims against growing consumer and regulatory skepticism. The narrative suggests that poor performance in some ESG funds is often a result of these "faux" strategies, where firms optimize for labels rather than real-world outcomes. Consequently, investors are increasingly urged to look beyond marketing hype and understand whether ESG is being used as a rigorous analytical framework or merely as a buzzword to justify higher fees and achieve desired optics, revealing the critical gap between perceived sustainability and actual practice.
