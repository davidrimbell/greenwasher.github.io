Greenwashing
EN ES

Amnesty International accuses the Azerbaijani government of blatant greenwashing by using COP29 to mask a severe deterioration of human rights. The organization argues that hosting a global climate summit while simultaneously dismantling civil society and suppressing dissent creates a deceptive facade. This hypocrisy undermines the core principles of climate justice, which require respect for fundamental freedoms and active participation from all sectors of society. The crackdown involves the arbitrary detention of hundreds of individuals, including journalists, activists, and environmental defenders, who are imprisoned on politically motivated charges. Authorities have also targeted the relatives of these critics and enacted restrictive laws to stifle the work of non-governmental organizations. This systematic repression ensures that critical voices are silenced ahead of and during the summit, preventing genuine dialogue and accountability regarding both human rights and environmental policies. This case is critical to understanding greenwashing because it highlights how nations with poor human rights records exploit international environmental conferences to improve their image without implementing substantive change. By prioritizing diplomatic optics over civic liberties, governments can co-opt the moral authority of climate action to legitimize authoritarian practices. Recognizing this link is essential for holding host countries accountable and ensuring that climate summits do not become tools for obscuring systemic abuses.
