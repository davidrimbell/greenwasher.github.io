Anger at UK’s ‘bonkers’ plan to reach net zero by importing fuel from North Korea

The UK government’s reliance on imported biomass to achieve net zero emissions faces severe criticism for being scientifically implausible and ethically questionable. Critics argue that the resource model underpinning this strategy is flawed, as it depends on unrealistic assumptions about global crop yields and relies on unlikely sources like North Korea and Afghanistan. This highlights a disconnect between climate policy and practical ecological reality, suggesting the strategy is designed to maintain high energy consumption rather than promoting genuine reduction. The article is highly relevant to greenwashing because it exposes how the classification of biomass as "low-carbon" may be a deceptive narrative. Burning wood releases significant carbon immediately, and the assumption that forests will regrow quickly enough to offset these emissions is disputed by many scientists. Furthermore, the failure of rigorous oversight, evidenced by previous regulatory fines for inaccurate data, suggests that sustainability labels are being applied without credible verification, misleading the public about the environmental benefits of these energy sources. Ultimately, this approach risks undermining the credibility of the UK’s entire climate strategy by betting on unproven technologies and dubious supply chains. The reliance on biomass, coupled with the promise of carbon capture technology that does not yet exist at scale, creates a false sense of security. This case illustrates how governments may use complex, unverified models to justify fossil-fuel-adjacent industries, potentially delaying the transition to truly renewable energy and obscuring the urgent need for reduced overall energy consumption.
