Greenwashing
ES EN

The article reveals that the fashion industry has superficially adopted the concept of sustainability, using vague terminology to attract environmentally conscious consumers. This practice, known as greenwashing, involves exaggerating or fabricating ecological attributes without real evidence, thereby deceiving the public and distorting the true definition of sustainable development. The lack of clear legal regulation allows brands to exploit this ambiguity, marketing products as “eco-friendly” or “green” when their actual environmental impacts remain significant. The consequences of this corporate deception are serious, as they erode consumer trust and harm companies that operate with transparency. When consumers discover the falsehood of green claims, they not only experience social embarrassment but also lose faith in the brands involved. Driven by competition for “conscious” customers and social pressure, this phenomenon creates an environment in which greenwashing becomes an empty marketing strategy, discouraging genuine innovation in sustainable materials and processes. The article’s relevance lies in its call for regulatory and educational action. Governments and international bodies are responding with directives that ban generic labels lacking scientific backing, requiring measurable and comparable data. This underscores that combating greenwashing demands independent, science-based oversight rather than corporate self-definition. The article concludes that true transparency and consumer education are essential to distinguish authentic sustainability from deceptive marketing, thereby promoting a transition toward a genuine circular economy.
