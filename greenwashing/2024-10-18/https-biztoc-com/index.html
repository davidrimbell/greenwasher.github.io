Greenwashing
ES EN

The article highlights corporate strategic shifts amid complex geopolitical tensions, such as energy volatility from Middle East impasses and evolving US regulatory landscapes. These macroeconomic pressures directly influence market stability and corporate decision-making, creating an environment where public relations narratives are heavily scrutinized by investors and consumers. In this volatile context, sustainability and corporate responsibility claims face heightened skepticism. When companies announce major investments or operational changes, the underlying motivations are often questioned. This skepticism is central to understanding greenwashing, as entities may exploit global uncertainty to position themselves as environmentally conscious without substantive action, aiming to secure investor confidence or market share during turbulent times. Relevance to greenwashing lies in the intersection of regulatory changes and corporate messaging. As the US rolls back environmental rules while tech giants grapple with safety ethics, the gap between policy and practice widens. This disparity allows firms to engage in superficial sustainability branding, misleading stakeholders about their true environmental impact while benefiting from the ambiguity of evolving regulations and ongoing global conflicts.
