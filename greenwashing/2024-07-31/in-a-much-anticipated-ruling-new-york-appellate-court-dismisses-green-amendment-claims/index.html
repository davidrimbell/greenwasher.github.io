In a Much-Anticipated Ruling, New York Appellate Court Dismisses Green Amendment Claims

A New York appellate court recently dismissed environmental rights claims against a landfill operator, the state, and New York City. This decision significantly limits the scope of the Green Amendment by rejecting arguments that regulated industries constitute state action. Consequently, private entities can no longer be directly sued for violating the constitutional right to a healthful environment, creating a substantial barrier for community groups challenging permitted facilities. The court further ruled that the amendment does not allow citizens to compel government agencies to enforce regulations against private parties. By characterizing such lawsuits as requests for mandamus relief, the judiciary affirmed that enforcement discretion belongs to agencies and is not subject to judicial compulsion. This effectively shields both private operators and regulatory bodies from direct liability for operational failures or inadequate oversight under the new constitutional provision. This ruling is critical to understanding greenwashing because it restricts the legal mechanisms available to hold polluters and regulators accountable for environmental harm. Without the ability to sue private entities or force state enforcement, communities lack leverage to challenge misleading environmental claims or inadequate mitigation efforts. The decision empowers regulated industries by reducing litigation risks, potentially enabling them to continue operations with fewer legal repercussions for environmental degradation.
