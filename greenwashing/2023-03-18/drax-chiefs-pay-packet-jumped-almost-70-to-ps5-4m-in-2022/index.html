Drax chief’s pay packet jumped almost 70% to £5.4m in 2022

The article highlights a stark contradiction between Drax’s corporate rewards and its environmental claims, illustrating the core mechanism of greenwashing. Executives received massive pay packages driven by soaring profits, which were largely subsidized by taxpayers under the pretense of being a low-carbon energy provider. This financial success, built on public funds, underscores how companies can exploit regulatory definitions of "green" energy to justify substantial bonuses even during periods when consumers face severe economic hardship. Central to this controversy is the criticism that Drax’s biomass operations, which involve burning imported wood pellets, are not genuinely renewable. Critics argue that this process can actually increase carbon emissions and harm ecosystems, yet the company continues to receive significant subsidies because it labels itself as sustainable. This discrepancy between marketing and ecological reality allows the firm to maintain its lucrative subsidy status, effectively monetizing a false narrative of environmental responsibility while contributing to the climate crisis. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the exposure of how green credentials are used as a financial shield. By securing taxpayer support through misleading environmental claims, Drax protects its profitability despite operational practices that contradict its eco-friendly image. The article demonstrates how such deception not only misleads the public and policymakers but also enables executives to reap extraordinary rewards at the expense of both the climate and struggling households, revealing the dangerous gap between corporate branding and actual environmental impact.
