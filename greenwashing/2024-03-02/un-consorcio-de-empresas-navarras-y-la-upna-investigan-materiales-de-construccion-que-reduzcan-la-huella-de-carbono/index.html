Un consorcio de empresas navarras y la UPNA investigan materiales de construcción que reduzcan la huella de carbono

The ENERCON project proposes a transformative construction paradigm that utilizes eco-active, photocatalytic materials, which actively reduce atmospheric carbon when exposed to sunlight. By integrating these technologies into urban infrastructure, the initiative aims to create zero-emission spaces where architecture plays a proactive role in environmental restoration, moving beyond passive sustainability to active pollution elimination. To ensure credibility, the project develops a digital platform leveraging blockchain-like technology to certify the origin and impact of each decontamination action. This system connects producers of green carbon credits with buyers, establishing a transparent market for verified ecological benefits. By monitoring processes through sensor networks, the initiative seeks to validate the circularity of these efforts, ensuring that environmental claims are backed by rigorous, traceable data. This case is highly relevant to greenwashing because it attempts to solve the core problem of unverifiable environmental claims through technological transparency. Greenwashing often thrives on vague assertions of sustainability; however, ENERCON’s reliance on certified, monitored data creates an audit trail that distinguishes genuine ecological impact from mere marketing. It demonstrates how concrete verification mechanisms can prevent companies from exaggerating their environmental benefits, thereby protecting consumers and regulators from misleading practices.
