Greenwashing
ES EN

The article highlights how the cold chain industry frequently engages in greenwashing by marketing gel-based ice packs as sustainable solutions, despite their harmful environmental impact. These packs often contain polymers that clog drainage systems and break down into microplastics, directly contradicting marketing claims that suggest they are safe for household use or plant watering. This disconnect between marketing and reality not only damages ecosystems but also exposes brands to significant reputational risks as consumers become increasingly skeptical of hollow eco-claims. Moving beyond superficial fixes, the piece argues for a genuine shift toward water-based ice packs, which offer a safer, more transparent alternative that aligns with circular economy principles. While water-filled options are easier to dispose of and recycle, the industry still faces challenges regarding food safety standards and the misconception that paper or bioplastics are inherently superior. True sustainability requires rigorous scrutiny of supply chains and product lifecycle management, ensuring that materials are truly reusable or recyclable rather than perpetuating a throwaway culture under the guise of environmental responsibility. This issue is critical to understanding greenwashing because it demonstrates how companies can exploit consumer trust in sustainability trends while delivering products that cause actual ecological harm. By hiding behind vague "eco-friendly" labels for hazardous materials, businesses mislead the public and delay necessary industry-wide reforms. The article underscores that without transparency and accountability, the push for sustainability risks becoming another marketing tactic rather than a driver of real environmental progress, ultimately eroding consumer confidence and hindering genuine climate action.
