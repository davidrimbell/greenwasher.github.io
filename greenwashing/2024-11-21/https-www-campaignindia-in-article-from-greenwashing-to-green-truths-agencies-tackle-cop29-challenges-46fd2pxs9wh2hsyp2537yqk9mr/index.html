Greenwashing
EN ES

The article argues that greenwashing is becoming unsustainable as governments implement stricter regulations and consumers demand greater transparency regarding environmental claims. With scrutiny at record highs, ambiguous terms like "net zero" are increasingly viewed as deceptive if they rely on offsets rather than actual emission reductions. This shift forces brands to move beyond superficial messaging, as misleading claims now carry significant regulatory and reputational risks. A primary driver of greenwashing is the disconnect between marketing and sustainability departments, which often results in exaggerated or incomplete narratives. To combat this, companies must foster interdisciplinary collaboration and ensure advertising agencies possess genuine expertise in climate science and regulatory compliance. By aligning creative storytelling with accurate sustainability data, organizations can avoid the pitfalls of siloed operations and ensure their communications reflect real corporate actions. Ultimately, transparency serves as a key competitive advantage, building trust with eco-conscious consumers who are skeptical of traditional advertising. Brands that openly share their progress, challenges, and specific reduction strategies differentiate themselves from competitors relying on vague promises. This article highlights that authentic, fact-based climate communication is no longer optional but essential for maintaining legitimacy and leadership in the face of intensifying global scrutiny.
