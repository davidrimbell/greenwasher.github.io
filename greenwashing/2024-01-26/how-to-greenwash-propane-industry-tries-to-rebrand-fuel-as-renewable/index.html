‘How to greenwash’: propane industry tries to rebrand fuel as renewable

‘How to greenwash’: propane industry tries to rebrand fuel as renewable

The fossil fuel industry is systematically rebranding propane, a byproduct of natural gas and crude oil, as a "clean" and "renewable" energy source despite scientific consensus that it is a greenhouse gas emitter. Internal lobbying records reveal that industry executives explicitly strategize to manipulate public perception, aiming to dissociate the fuel from its fossil origins to appeal to climate-conscious demographics. This coordinated effort prioritizes favorable consumer image over factual accuracy, leveraging significant advertising budgets to create a misleading narrative that obscures the environmental harm caused by burning these hydrocarbons. This campaign constitutes a classic example of greenwashing, where companies use vague or deceptive marketing to appear environmentally responsible while continuing harmful practices. Although the industry promotes the theoretical existence of "renewable" propane derived from plant oils, such alternatives constitute a negligible fraction of total supply and are not currently scalable solutions. By emphasizing these minor exceptions and ignoring the primary fossil fuel reality, the industry dilutes the urgency of transitioning to truly sustainable energy sources, effectively delaying necessary regulatory action and consumer awareness regarding the climate crisis. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the industry’s deliberate exploitation of information asymmetry to maintain market share for fossil fuels. By flooding media platforms with advertisements that lack nuance about emissions and sustainability, the lobbying group aims to influence both consumers and policymakers, framing a harmful product as a viable climate solution. This strategy mirrors historical tactics used by other fossil fuel sectors to legitimize their operations, demonstrating how corporate spending can distort public understanding of energy transitions and impede genuine progress toward net-zero emissions goals.
