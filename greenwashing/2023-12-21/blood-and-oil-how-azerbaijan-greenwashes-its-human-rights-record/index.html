Blood and Oil—How Azerbaijan Greenwashes Its Human Rights Record

The article argues that hosting COP29 in Azerbaijan constitutes a severe act of greenwashing, allowing a petro-dictatorship to leverage a prestigious global environmental forum to mask its poor ecological and human rights record. By exploiting the conference’s moral authority, the Azerbaijani regime aims to legitimize its heavy reliance on fossil fuels and obscure the severe environmental devastation caused by its oil and gas industry, including widespread pollution and ecological damage. This strategy represents a dangerous precedent where authoritarian leaders weaponize environmentalism to justify ulterior political motives and military aggression. The author highlights how Azerbaijan has previously used false claims of eco-terrorism to cover up ethnic cleansing and the use of chemical weapons, demonstrating how climate gatherings can be manipulated to whitewash crimes against humanity. This mirrors similar tactics used by other nations, such as the UAE, to divert attention from their own abuses while maintaining international credibility. The piece emphasizes that human rights and environmental stewardship are inseparable, warning that institutions like the United Nations undermine their mission by ignoring these links. Allowing a country with a history of aggression and repression to host such summits sends the wrong message, suggesting that environmental goals can be pursued regardless of a nation’s ethical conduct. Consequently, the article calls for strict adherence to human rights and rule of law as prerequisites for hosting international climate conferences, ensuring that these platforms remain genuine vehicles for justice rather than tools for propaganda.
