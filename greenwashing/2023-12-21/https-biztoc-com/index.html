Greenwashing
ES EN

Global markets face significant pressure as soaring Treasury yields revive fears of aggressive Federal Reserve tightening, dragging down major indices like the Nasdaq and Dow. This financial instability is exacerbated by geopolitical tensions, including stalled US-Iran diplomacy and an extended but unresolved US-China trade truce, which contribute to volatility in oil prices and broader economic uncertainty. Simultaneously, global debt has reached a record high, intensifying fiscal pressures and raising concerns about the sustainability of current economic models amidst rising borrowing costs. The housing sector reflects this strain, with mortgage rates climbing sharply and worsening affordability for buyers and renters alike. Corporate performances are mixed, with some companies like Merck and BlackBerry seeing positive developments, while others, such as Vistry and Starbucks, announce profit slashes or closures due to weak demand and cost-cutting necessities. These shifts highlight a fragile economic environment where consumer spending power is being eroded by inflation and higher interest rates. This article is relevant to greenwashing because the escalating energy crisis and soaring fuel costs create fertile ground for companies to exaggerate environmental commitments. As oil prices surge due to geopolitical conflict and supply disruptions, firms may exploit public anxiety about energy security to promote superficial sustainability initiatives. This allows corporations to distract from their actual carbon footprints by aligning with broader societal concerns about stability and resource scarcity, thereby masking unsustainable practices under the guise of responsible energy management.
