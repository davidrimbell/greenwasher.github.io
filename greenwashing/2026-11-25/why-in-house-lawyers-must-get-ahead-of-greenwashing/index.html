Why in-house lawyers must get ahead of greenwashing

As transitional protections for climate reporting expire, the regulatory burden shifts significantly toward in-house legal teams and directors. Organizations must now demonstrate that their sustainability disclosures are substantively compliant, rather than merely claiming reasonable efforts were taken. This change mandates rigorous governance structures, clear accountability, and verifiable data systems, leaving little room for the informal processes that may have sufficed during the initial implementation phase. The article highlights the severe financial and reputational consequences of greenwashing, noting recent multi-million dollar penalties imposed on major corporations. These legal precedents establish that companies must possess reasonable grounds for their environmental claims at the time they are made. Regulators are increasingly focused on ensuring that corporate messaging does not outpace the underlying evidence, emphasizing that the burden of proof rests entirely with the organization to justify its statements. To mitigate these risks, legal teams must implement structured frameworks for tracking and verifying all public environmental claims. This involves maintaining a comprehensive register of assertions across all channels, aligning marketing materials with governed sustainability data, and integrating legal review into pre-publication workflows. By ensuring that every claim, particularly those using terms like “net zero,” is backed by documented evidence and clear pathways, organizations can defend against accusations of misleading conduct and strengthen their overall compliance posture.
