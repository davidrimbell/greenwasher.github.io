«El 'greenwashing' de los biocombustibles trata de hacernos creer que son limpios» - Ethic

The article highlights greenwashing as a critical obstacle to genuine decarbonization, particularly within the transport sector. By presenting technologies like biofuels as clean alternatives to electric vehicles, companies obscure the severe health impacts of pollutants such as NOx. This deceptive marketing not only misleads consumers but also protects incumbent fossil fuel interests, delaying the necessary transition to zero-emission technologies despite their proven superiority for road transport. Relevant to greenwashing, this practice erodes public trust and creates an uneven playing field where sincere efforts to reduce emissions are undermined by false claims of neutrality among technologies. The text argues that equating harmful biofuels with electricity is factually incorrect and dangerous, as it prioritizes corporate profit over public health. Without accurate information, consumers cannot make informed choices, allowing industries to maintain outdated business models under the guise of sustainability. The relevance lies in exposing how greenwashing stifles political and technological progress. By emphasizing the health risks associated with these "clean" fuels, the article underscores that greenwashing is not just a marketing issue but a public health crisis. It calls for stricter regulations and reliance on impartial civil society organizations to distinguish real commitments from marketing, ensuring that decarbonization efforts actually protect citizens and the environment rather than serving corporate interests.
