¿Los aguacates son malos para el medio ambiente? Las razones por las que esta fruta tan popular es criticada por expertos

The global boom in avocado production reveals a fundamental contradiction between its image as a healthy superfood and its devastating environmental impacts. Although the fruit itself is nutritious, its mass production relies on intensive monocultures that deplete water resources in regions already affected by drought and promote deforestation, threatening local biodiversity and the water sovereignty of neighboring communities. This dynamic illustrates how demand from distant markets distorts agricultural practices in producing countries, displacing traditional diversified farming systems with export-oriented models that are vulnerable and contaminated by pesticides. Although reliance on maritime transport has a smaller carbon footprint than commonly believed, it exposes the food system to logistical disruptions, while soil degradation and the loss of natural habitats highlight the hidden negative externalities behind the popularity of this commodity. The relevance of this case to greenwashing lies in how avocados are marketed as an ecological or sustainable choice, obscuring the reality of their supply chain. Overlapping with "healthy" marketing are human rights abuses and organized crime, demonstrating that positive labeling can serve to mask systemic harms. The article warns against the need to question corporate sustainability narratives that ignore the real social and ecological costs of intensive production.
