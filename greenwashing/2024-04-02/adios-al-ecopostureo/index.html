Adiós al Ecopostureo

The article explains that growing consumer interest in sustainability has fueled “ecoposturing,” a strategy in which companies use superficial marketing to appear eco-friendly without implementing genuine practices. This phenomenon relies on misleading claims and designs that evoke nature, concealing the fact that corporate actions remain harmful or contradictory to their advertising messages. To identify these scams, it is crucial to recognize patterns such as the excessive use of vague terms, insignificant green initiatives alongside other harmful practices, and aesthetic changes in packaging to reinforce a false image of environmental responsibility. These techniques manipulate public perception, creating a disconnect between what is being sold and the company’s actual operational reality, thereby confusing those who seek genuinely sustainable products. The text’s relevance to ecoposturing lies in its warning about the serious consequences of this misinformation, which diverts attention from real efforts and generates public distrust, as demonstrated by the Volkswagen scandal. Finally, the author questions consumer responsibility and advocates for strict regulations and public leadership, insisting that environmental protection should not be reduced to a commercial tool.
