Greenwashing para una ciudad privada en Punta Colorada

The article exposes "Punta La Paz," a luxury real estate megaproject on the coast of Baja California Sur that employs greenwashing to conceal its environmental impact. By rebranding from previous failed ventures and establishing a conservation foundation, developers project an image of ecological stewardship. This strategic marketing aims to legitimize the construction of a high-end private city in a sensitive area, attempting to reconcile exclusive real estate objectives with superficial environmental commitments. The core mechanism of this greenwashing involves promoting community and ecological benefits, such as coral restoration and beach cleanups, while simultaneously seeking permits to displace local communities and degrade natural habitats. Developers leverage partnerships with conservation NGOs and government agencies to build credibility. This approach creates a deceptive narrative in which environmental responsibility is used as a tool to gain social license, rather than reflecting a genuine commitment to preserving the region's fragile ecosystems. This case is relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how the luxury real estate sector obscures aggressive development behind a facade of sustainability. It highlights the conflict between profit-driven megaprojects and genuine environmental protection, where ecological actions are undertaken primarily to facilitate construction rather than to preserve nature. The eventual withdrawal of some civil society partners upon learning the true extent of the development underscores how these alliances often serve as strategic tools for legitimacy rather than representing authentic collaboration.
