Greenwashing
ES EN

The article highlights a critical gap in the sustainable food sector: while major corporations are promoting "regenerative agriculture" to enhance soil health and biodiversity, the lack of an official definition at the EU level creates significant ambiguity. This absence of standardized criteria means different supply chain actors interpret the term inconsistently, raising concerns about how these practices are actually implemented and verified across global networks. Without clear regulatory boundaries, there is a substantial risk that companies could engage in greenwashing by making misleading environmental claims. The core argument suggests that establishing an EU-level definition would not only clarify expectations but also help mitigate the potential for deceptive marketing. By defining what constitutes genuine regenerative practices, stakeholders can ensure that sustainability efforts are transparent and verifiable, thereby protecting consumers from superficial ecological branding. This issue is directly relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how vague terminology can be exploited to project an image of environmental responsibility without necessarily delivering measurable ecological benefits. As the food industry transitions toward more sustainable sourcing, production, and consumption models, the potential for abuse increases if standards remain loose. Therefore, rigorous definitions are essential to maintain integrity in sustainability claims and prevent the dilution of genuine environmental progress.
