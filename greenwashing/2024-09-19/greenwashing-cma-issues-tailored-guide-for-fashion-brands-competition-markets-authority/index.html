Greenwashing: CMA issues tailored guide for fashion brands | Competition & Markets Authority

The UK’s Competition and Markets Authority has released a comprehensive compliance guide and issued warnings to seventeen prominent fashion brands, signaling a stringent shift toward regulating environmental marketing. This regulatory push aims to eliminate ambiguity in sustainability messaging, ensuring that terms like "sustainable" or "eco-friendly" are supported by clear, specific evidence rather than vague generalizations. By establishing a level playing field, the CMA intends to protect consumers from deception while holding companies accountable for truthful representation. This intervention is critical to combating greenwashing because it targets the most common tactics used to mislead shoppers, such as labeling entire products as recycled when only components are, or grouping items into "green" ranges without transparent criteria. The authority highlights that misleading claims not only damage consumer trust but also violate consumer protection laws. The recent landmark agreements with major retailers demonstrate that superficial or incomplete environmental disclosures are no longer acceptable, forcing the industry to adopt rigorous standards for describing product lifecycles and material compositions. The stakes have been elevated by impending legal changes that grant regulators the power to impose substantial fines on non-compliant businesses. This creates a strong financial incentive for fashion companies to audit their claims and align with the Green Claims Code before stricter penalties take effect. Ultimately, this development underscores that sustainability marketing must be substantiated by facts; without genuine transparency and accuracy, brands risk severe legal and financial consequences, marking a turning point for honest environmental communication in the fashion sector.
