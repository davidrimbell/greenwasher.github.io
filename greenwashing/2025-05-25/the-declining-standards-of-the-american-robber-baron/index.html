The declining standards of the American robber baron

This article argues that while historical industrial magnates utilized philanthropy to effectively greenwash their reputations, today’s tech billionaires avoid such charitable camouflage, relying instead on the corrosive effects of unchecked media influence. The author contrasts the legacy of oil and steel titans, who built legacies alongside their wealth, with modern figures who prioritize disruption and minimal regulation over social responsibility. The central critique focuses on how tech leaders like Mark Zuckerberg and Elon Musk have dismantled content moderation and fact-checking standards, aligning their platforms with anti-normative political movements. By claiming their services are merely communication tools rather than media entities, they evade accountability for spreading misinformation and hate speech, thereby reshaping public discourse in ways that benefit their business models rather than societal well-being. This dynamic is relevant to greenwashing because it reveals a shift from environmental PR to narrative control. Whereas past oligarchs used charity to mask industrial harm, current moguls engage in a form of intellectual greenwashing by presenting their deregulated platforms as neutral conduits of free speech. This obscures the reality that their actions actively degrade information quality and democratic norms, substituting genuine ethical stewardship with the illusion of open access.
