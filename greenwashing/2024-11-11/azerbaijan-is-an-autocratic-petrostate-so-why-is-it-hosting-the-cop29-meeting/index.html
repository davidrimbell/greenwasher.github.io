Azerbaijan is an autocratic petrostate. So why is it hosting the COP29 meeting?

The selection of Azerbaijan, a major fossil fuel exporter with an authoritarian leadership, to host COP29 raises significant concerns regarding the integrity of global climate diplomacy. The government’s public embrace of renewable energy appears largely strategic, aimed at securing European gas imports following sanctions on Russia rather than addressing the urgent need to phase out carbon-intensive industries. This positioning allows the state to maintain its economic reliance on oil and gas while projecting an image of modern environmental stewardship to the international community. Critics argue that these efforts constitute classic greenwashing, as domestic actions contradict the country’s progressive rhetoric. Environmental monitoring is suppressed, and local communities near extraction sites suffer from health issues that authorities ignore. Furthermore, data indicates an increase in gas flaring, suggesting that official commitments to reduce emissions are insufficient and lack genuine enforcement or transparency. The disparity between the state’s narrative of sustainability and the reality of ongoing ecological damage highlights a deliberate attempt to mask continued fossil fuel dependence. This situation is critical to understanding greenwashing because it illustrates how host nations can leverage high-profile international forums to legitimize their polluting industries. By framing fossil fuel exports as a necessary bridge to a green future, governments can deflect criticism and secure short-term economic gains without committing to meaningful decarbonization. The Azerbaijan case serves as a stark warning that hosting climate summits does not guarantee environmental progress, but can instead become a tool for reputation management while the climate crisis deepens.
