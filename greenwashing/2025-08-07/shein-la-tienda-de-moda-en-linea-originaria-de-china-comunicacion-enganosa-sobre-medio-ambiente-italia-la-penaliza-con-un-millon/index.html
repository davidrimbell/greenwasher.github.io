Shein, la tienda de moda en línea originaria de China: comunicación engañosa sobre medio ambiente, Italia la penaliza con un millón

Italy’s competition authority has imposed a substantial fine on Shein for disseminating misleading environmental claims regarding the circularity and recyclability of its products. The regulator found that vague, generic, or outright false statements about eco-friendly materials and sustainability practices amounted to deceptive advertising. By emphasizing significant environmental benefits without clarifying their actual relevance in light of the brand’s massive production volume, the company engaged in practices that misled consumers about the true ecological impact of its merchandise. This enforcement action is particularly pertinent to the issue of greenwashing, as it targets the fast fashion sector—an industry notoriously associated with high levels of pollution and disposable consumption patterns. The authority stressed the heightened duty of care required from companies operating in such environmentally damaging sectors. Consequently, the ruling underscores that marketing efforts cannot obscure the fact that a small subset of “eco” products does not justify broad claims about the entire brand’s sustainability credentials, thereby exposing the disconnect between promotional narratives and operational reality. Ultimately, this case demonstrates that greenwashing is not merely an exaggeration in marketing but a serious breach of consumer trust and commercial ethics. It validates allegations made by consumer associations that companies employ deceptive tactics to drive compulsive purchasing under the guise of environmental responsibility. The decision signals a stricter regulatory stance against ecological posturing, reinforcing that transparency and accuracy are essential. This highlights the growing importance of holding corporations accountable for specific, verifiable claims rather than allowing vague assertions to manipulate eco-conscious consumers.
