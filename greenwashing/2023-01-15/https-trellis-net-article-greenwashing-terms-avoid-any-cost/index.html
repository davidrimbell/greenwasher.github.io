Greenwashing
ES EN

The article warns that vague or unsubstantiated environmental claims constitute greenwashing, which risks severe reputational damage, consumer backlash, and legal penalties. As more buyers seek eco-friendly products, corporations face significant pressure to ensure their marketing accurately reflects their sustainability practices. Failure to align claims with reality not only erodes trust but also embarrasses consumers, leading to potential boycotts and loss of brand integrity. Regulatory bodies like the U.S. Federal Trade Commission are actively updating guidelines to crack down on misleading terminology such as "natural," "biodegradable," or "recyclable" when used without specific, verifiable context. The piece highlights that general statements implying no negative environmental impact are increasingly scrutinized, urging companies to provide clear, prominent, and scientifically backed details. This shift aims to prevent "wishcycling" and other deceptive practices that leave consumers with products that cannot be responsibly disposed of. Relevant to greenwashing, the article illustrates how the gap between marketing rhetoric and actual corporate behavior creates liability. It emphasizes that sustainability professionals often fail to communicate critical compliance standards to marketing teams, resulting in widespread non-compliance. By adopting precise, evidence-based language and avoiding trendy but hollow buzzwords, businesses can mitigate legal risks and build genuine consumer confidence, rather than falling into the trap of aspirational advertising that lacks substantive proof.
