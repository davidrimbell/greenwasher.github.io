Fashion’s Greenwashing Problem: How Canadian shoppers might be misled by eco claims - National | Globalnews.ca

The article highlights how the fashion industry’s urgent response to consumer demand for sustainability has inadvertently created a landscape rife with greenwashing. Companies frequently use vague, appealing terminology like "eco-conscious" or "natural" to mislead shoppers into believing their products are environmentally friendly, despite often relying on polluting synthetic materials. This lack of clear standards allows misleading brands to exploit the complexity of global supply chains, making it difficult for consumers to distinguish between genuine ethical practices and deceptive marketing tactics. To combat this ambiguity, truly sustainable brands are turning to rigorous third-party certifications, such as B Corp status, to prove their commitment to transparency and ethical labor. These companies emphasize that authentic sustainability involves comprehensive operational changes, including local manufacturing and fair wages, which naturally result in higher price points. This stands in stark contrast to fast fashion models that encourage excessive consumption and disposal, underscoring that true environmental responsibility requires significant financial investment and supply chain visibility rather than superficial labeling. Ultimately, the piece argues that without stricter government regulations and enforcement against false claims, greenwashing will persist, leaving consumers vulnerable to manipulation. The author suggests that while education is vital for raising awareness, robust legal frameworks are necessary to hold corporations accountable. The most effective strategy for reducing environmental impact remains avoiding overconsumption altogether, reinforcing that the most sustainable garment is already owned, rather than one marketed as newly "green."
