Investors continue to prioritise climate action despite lacking trusted information | ThaiPR.NET

The core conclusion of this report is that while climate action is increasingly viewed as a commercial imperative, severe distrust pervades corporate sustainability reporting due to pervasive greenwashing. Investors recognize climate change as a top-five priority for businesses, yet they suspect that unsupported claims are prevalent in corporate disclosures. This disconnect highlights a critical failure in transparency, where companies fail to provide verifiable data that aligns with their public commitments, thereby undermining stakeholder confidence. Greenwashing remains a significant issue because investors lack confidence in current reporting mechanisms and third-party ratings. The survey reveals that a vast majority of investors believe corporate reports contain unsubstantiated claims, creating an environment where skepticism replaces trust. This lack of reliability prevents capital from flowing efficiently toward genuinely sustainable initiatives, as stakeholders cannot distinguish between authentic efforts and superficial marketing tactics designed to appease demand without substantive action. To combat greenwashing, the article argues for mandatory, high-quality independent assurance and standardized global reporting frameworks. Investors are calling for stricter regulations and robust audit standards to ensure data integrity and independence. By enforcing these measures, companies can bridge the trust gap, ensuring that sustainability claims are credible. Ultimately, reducing greenwashing is essential not only for ethical accountability but also for maintaining investor confidence and attracting capital in an era where economic performance and sustainability are inextricably linked.
