COP31 becomes longest-ever UN climate summit, EU rejects 'greenwashing of fossil fuels'

COP31 negotiations collapsed into unprecedented overtime, revealing deep fractures in global climate diplomacy. The summit’s prolonged delay highlights a critical failure in multilateralism, as delegates struggled to reconcile ambitious climate targets with the political will required to enact them. The absence of explicit fossil fuel phase-out language in the final draft signals a retreat from the necessary urgency, suggesting that geopolitical compromises often override scientific reality. This stagnation underscores how political inertia can dilute international agreements, leaving the world unprepared for the escalating climate crisis. The contentious inclusion of carbon capture technology illustrates a major greenwashing trend, where fossil fuel interests promote ineffective solutions to maintain business-as-usual operations. Backed heavily by oil-producing nations, this technology is criticized for being costly and incapable of delivering genuine emission reductions. By embedding such controversial measures into the framework, the agreement risks legitimizing carbon capture as a viable alternative to systemic change, thereby distracting from the immediate need to abandon fossil fuels entirely. Reframing emissions reduction around electrification without mandating renewable sources further obscures the truth. This approach allows countries to increase electricity consumption while potentially relying on non-renewable grids, creating a false narrative of sustainability. Such ambiguity enables governments and corporations to claim progress without making substantive changes. Ultimately, the summit’s outcome demonstrates how complex negotiations can be manipulated to produce weak, ambiguous texts that appear cooperative but fail to deliver tangible environmental benefits, perpetuating the cycle of greenwashing in international policy.
