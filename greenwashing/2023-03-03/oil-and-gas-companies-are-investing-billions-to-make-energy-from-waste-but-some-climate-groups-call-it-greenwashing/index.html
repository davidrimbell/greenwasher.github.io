Oil and gas companies are investing billions to make energy from waste but some climate groups call it greenwashing

Oil and gas giant Enbridge is investing heavily in a company that converts food waste into renewable natural gas, a move that critics label as classic greenwashing. While the investment aims to position Enbridge as a leader in the energy transition by reducing landfill emissions, climate advocates argue that promoting methane-based solutions contradicts the urgent need to eliminate fossil fuel combustion entirely. They contend that renewable natural gas is merely a costly interim fix that distracts from more effective, proven alternatives like electrification. The core controversy lies in the discrepancy between Enbridge’s public sustainability narrative and its actual business practices. Although the company highlights its small-scale investments in renewable technologies, its primary operations remain focused on expanding fossil fuel infrastructure and securing long-term gas exports. This strategy allows Enbridge to maintain its traditional revenue streams while using eco-friendly projects to improve its image, effectively obscuring the reality that the vast majority of its emissions come from customers burning the gas it delivers. This case is highly relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how large emitters use niche, low-carbon projects to create a facade of environmental responsibility without addressing their core business model. By leveraging subsidies and marketing renewable natural gas as a climate solution, fossil fuel companies can continue expanding their reach under the guise of decarbonization. This tactic misleads consumers and policymakers, delaying the necessary shift away from hydrocarbons and undermining genuine climate action efforts.
