Greenwashing
ES EN

The article exposes six sophisticated greenwashing tactics, including greencrowding, where polluters hide behind alliances to delay real action, and greenlighting, where companies highlight minor eco-friendly products to distract from their overall environmental damage. These strategies allow corporations to maintain a sustainable image while continuing harmful practices, such as lobbying against climate regulations or relying heavily on fossil fuels. Other deceptive methods include greenshifting, which blames consumers for carbon emissions despite industry dominance, and greenrinsing, where firms frequently lower or delay sustainability targets to avoid scrutiny. Greenhushing involves intentionally under-reporting green credentials to evade regulations, while greenlabelling uses misleading packaging and unsubstantiated claims to create a false impression of eco-friendliness. These tactics collectively manipulate public perception and undermine genuine environmental efforts. This analysis is crucial for understanding greenwashing because it reveals how marketing evolves to bypass increasing consumer skepticism and new regulations. By identifying specific deceptive frameworks, the article empowers individuals and regulators to scrutinize corporate claims more effectively, ensuring that environmental labels and promises reflect tangible actions rather than strategic obfuscation.
