Yes, there is a lot of greenwashing, but Cop summits are our best chance of averting climate breakdown | Ashish Ghadiali

Yes, there is a lot of greenwashing, but Cop summits are our best chance of averting climate breakdown | Ashish Ghadiali

The article portrays the UN climate process as increasingly hijacked by corporate interests, specifically highlighting the disproportionate influence of fossil fuel lobbyists at Cop29 compared to delegates from vulnerable nations. This dynamic has transformed summits into global stages for greenwashing, where diplomatic rhetoric masks the continued expansion of oil and gas deals by host nations, thereby undermining the integrity of international climate negotiations. Despite this corruption, the narrative acknowledges that the multilateral framework remains an essential vehicle for climate justice, offering a unique platform for developing nations to voice their needs. Although recent political shifts, such as electoral wins for anti-climate forces, threaten to stall progress, the process has historically achieved critical milestones like the recognition of the 1.5C threshold and mechanisms for loss and damage finance. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the stark contrast between the performative diplomacy of summits and the reality of escalating emissions. The article argues that while the system is flawed and risks being co-opted for commercial gain, the transition toward a sustainable economy is already underway through broader market and diplomatic shifts, suggesting that abandoning the process would allow greenwashing narratives to dominate without resistance.
