Bruselas declara la guerra al ecopostureo y pone a España bajo presión por el greenwashing para frenar engaños ecológicos

The European Commission has launched infringement proceedings against Spain and nineteen other countries for failing to timely transpose the new regulations aimed at combating greenwashing. This measure responds to the urgent need to curb “eco-posturing” practices, where companies use ambiguous labels and messages to appear sustainable without any real backing. By not adapting its legislation, Spain is under pressure to address these legislative gaps and avoid potential sanctions from the Court of Justice of the European Union. The main objective of this European offensive is to restore consumer trust, as consumers feel misled by opaque environmental claims. The new directive requires that any ecological claim be technically justified, banning terms like “green” or “sustainable” when they lack a scientific basis. Additionally, the regulation seeks to combat premature obsolescence by obliging companies to clearly inform about the durability and repairability of their products, thereby fostering a genuine circular economy. This article is crucial for understanding greenwashing because it illustrates the evolution of companies from merely manipulating public perception to adhering to strict and verifiable regulations. The fight is no longer just about intent, but about traceability and mandatory transparency. By criminalizing the lack of rigor in environmental claims, Europe is redefining market rules, ensuring that sustainability is a technical guarantee rather than a mere marketing tool, thus protecting both honest consumers and companies that genuinely invest in real changes.
