‘Green Washing’

‘Green Washing’

This article illustrates how corporations engage in greenwashing by selectively highlighting minor environmental efforts while concealing substantial investments in fossil fuels. High-profile cases, such as those involving Lloyd’s Banking Group and Shell, demonstrate that misleading advertisements often omit critical context, creating a false impression of sustainability. Regulators in the UK and Singapore have increasingly intervened to ban these deceptive claims, highlighting the disconnect between corporate marketing and actual environmental impact. The prevalence of such practices undermines global climate goals, including the Paris Agreement’s targets, by allowing companies to manipulate stakeholder perceptions without delivering tangible results. Despite widespread commitments to net-zero emissions, many multinational firms continue financing carbon-intensive projects, thereby exacerbating climate change. This discrepancy not only deceives investors and consumers but also hinders collective progress toward reducing global warming, as evidenced by the significant economic damages linked to corporate emissions. Regulatory responses are evolving but remain inconsistent globally. While international frameworks like the EU CSRD and Indian SEBI’s BRSR mandate non-financial reporting, enforcement varies, often lacking robust penal provisions for non-compliance. The article argues for stronger, unified regulations and central ESG regulators to ensure accountability. Ultimately, it emphasizes the urgent need for transparent, verified sustainability reporting to prevent greenwashing and align corporate behavior with genuine environmental stewardship.
