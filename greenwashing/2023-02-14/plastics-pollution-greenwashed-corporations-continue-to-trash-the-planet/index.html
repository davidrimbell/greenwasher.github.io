Plastics pollution: greenwashed corporations continue to trash the planet

The collapse of soft-plastics recycling programs in Australian supermarkets, with millions of bags heading to landfills despite consumer efforts, reveals that these initiatives primarily serve as greenwashing tools rather than genuine environmental solutions. The article argues that recycling schemes allow corporations to generate profits while misleading the public into believing the crisis is being managed. This facade distracts from the reality that single-use plastic production continues to surge, leaving the systemic root causes of pollution unaddressed while individual consumers are left feeling that their minor contributions are sufficient. The core issue lies in shifting responsibility onto individuals to manage pollution through personal actions like recycling or buying "eco-friendly" alternatives, such as paid multi-use bags. This narrative creates a sense of guilt and demobilization, effectively sidetracking citizens from demanding substantial structural change. By framing environmental degradation as a matter of individual choice and "carbon footprints," corporations deflect attention from the economic system designed to accumulate wealth through resource extraction and waste generation, ensuring the real polluters remain accountable to neither the public nor the planet. Ultimately, this approach mirrors historical religious indulgences, where small payments were used to offset perceived sins, rather than confronting the actual source of wrongdoing. The article asserts that a tiny number of fossil fuel and cement companies are responsible for the vast majority of global emissions and pollution. Therefore, focusing on consumer guilt is not just ineffective but a deliberate strategy to protect these industrial giants, preventing the urgent, large-scale regulatory action required to mitigate the environmental crisis.
