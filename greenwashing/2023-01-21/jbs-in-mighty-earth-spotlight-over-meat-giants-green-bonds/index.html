JBS in Mighty Earth spotlight over meat giant’s green bonds

Mighty Earth has accused JBS, the world’s largest meat company, of greenwashing by filing a complaint with the US Securities and Exchange Commission. The NGO argues that JBS misled investors by issuing sustainability-linked bonds based on emissions reduction targets that exclude Scope 3 emissions. Since Scope 3, which covers the supply chain, represents the vast majority of the company's climate impact, Mighty Earth contends that the financial instruments are disconnected from the actual source of JBS’s environmental damage, effectively allowing the firm to raise billions under false pretenses. The core of the controversy lies in the discrepancy between JBS’s net-zero pledges and the specific metrics tied to its debt. While the company aims for net-zero by 2040, the bonds were structured around reducing only direct operational emissions, ignoring the extensive footprint generated by cattle farming and deforestation in its supply chain. Critics assert that this selective accounting masks the true scale of JBS’s contribution to global warming and deforestation, denying investors critical information needed to assess the genuine sustainability of the company’s operations. This case is highly relevant to greenwashing discussions as it exposes significant loopholes in current ESG investing safeguards. It highlights how corporations can exploit ambiguous standards and the lack of universally accepted methods for measuring complex supply chain emissions to access capital markets. The incident underscores the urgent need for stricter regulatory clarity and independent verification to prevent companies from marketing vague sustainability goals as concrete environmental commitments, thereby protecting investors from deceptive financial practices.
