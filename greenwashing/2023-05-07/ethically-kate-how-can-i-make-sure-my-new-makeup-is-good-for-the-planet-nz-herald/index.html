Ethically Kate: How can I make sure my new makeup is good for the planet? - NZ Herald

Ethically Kate: How can I make sure my new makeup is good for the planet? - NZ Herald

The article argues that makeup is inherently detrimental to the environment due to resource depletion and waste, meaning consumers should view sustainable options as merely "less bad" rather than truly beneficial. It critiques the prevalence of greenwashing in the beauty industry, urging readers to look past marketing claims and recognize that no cosmetic product is genuinely good for the planet. To navigate this landscape, individuals are advised to scrutinize ingredients and packaging, avoiding harmful chemicals and excessive, non-recyclable plastic. By choosing multifunctional, concentrated products in reusable materials like glass or aluminum, consumers can minimize their ecological footprint while ensuring better health outcomes, as sustainable formulations often nourish the skin rather than causing breakouts. This perspective is crucial for understanding greenwashing because it exposes the fallacy of "sustainable" beauty marketing. It highlights how companies may use recycled materials or organic ingredients to appear eco-friendly while ignoring the fundamental environmental costs of production and consumption, revealing that the core issue is the product's existence rather than its minor improvements.
