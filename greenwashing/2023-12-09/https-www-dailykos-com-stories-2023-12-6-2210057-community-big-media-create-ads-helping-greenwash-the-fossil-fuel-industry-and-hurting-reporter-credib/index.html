Greenwashing
ES EN

An investigative report reveals that major, trusted news organizations are partnering with fossil fuel companies to produce native advertising that mimics authentic editorial content. By blending promotional material for oil and gas with legitimate journalism, these outlets lend unearned credibility to industry talking points, effectively greenwashing the fossil fuel sector’s preferred climate solutions while creating confusion for readers. Prominent media entities, including The Financial Times, Politico, and The New York Times, have generated substantial revenue by hosting events and publishing sponsored content for major energy corporations. This commercial relationship allows these companies to bypass standard journalistic scrutiny, directly inserting their narratives about the energy transition into mainstream discourse under the guise of impartial reporting. This practice is critically relevant to understanding greenwashing because it demonstrates how the industry co-opts the media’s authority to legitimize its environmental claims. When journalists label these partnerships as dangerous and undermining, it highlights a systemic failure where financial interests compromise editorial integrity, signaling to the public that the climate crisis is less urgent than it actually is.
