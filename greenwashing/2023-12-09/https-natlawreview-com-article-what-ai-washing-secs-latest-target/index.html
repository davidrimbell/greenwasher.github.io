Greenwashing
ES EN

SEC Chair Gary Gensler has warned that regulators are increasingly targeting "AI washing," a deceptive practice where companies exaggerate their artificial intelligence capabilities to mislead investors. This regulatory scrutiny mirrors the established enforcement actions against greenwashing, where firms inflate their environmental sustainability efforts. By drawing this parallel, the SEC signals that false claims about AI technology will face the same legal consequences as misleading environmental disclosures, establishing a new frontier for securities fraud enforcement. The ambiguity surrounding the definition of AI creates significant risks for corporate communications. Companies may exploit the broad nature of the term to market basic automation or statistical analysis as advanced intelligent systems. This gap between promised capabilities and actual implementation can deceive stakeholders, creating liability for businesses that fail to distinguish between genuine machine learning and simpler technological processes. Such exaggerations erode trust and expose firms to potential litigation when the reality of their technology fails to meet the hype. To mitigate these risks, organizations must implement robust compliance programs that ensure all public statements are accurate and truthful. Verifying that AI claims reflect genuine learning and adaptive capabilities, rather than mere statistics, is essential for legal safety. This article is highly relevant to greenwashing because it demonstrates how regulatory frameworks are expanding beyond environmental claims to cover other forms of sustainability-adjacent misrepresentation. It highlights a broader trend where regulators are tightening oversight on vague, buzzword-heavy corporate promises, urging firms to prioritize transparency and substantive proof over marketing spectacle.
