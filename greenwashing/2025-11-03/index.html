How Green Labels Hide Fossil Fuels and Fake Carbon Offsets

Stories selected and summarised by AI, not reviewed by a person. Every source is linked.

Green labels can be confusing. They make shoppers feel good about helping the planet. But these labels can also hide real problems. They might cost you money or support bad practices.

Big companies use vague words about being “sustainable.” This keeps customers loyal. It also slows down rules that might hurt their profits. For example, Gas Networks in Australia got in trouble. The Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC) sued them. The company said it sold renewable gas. But court papers showed it was just natural gas. Natural gas is a fossil fuel. This trick helped the company keep making money. It avoided the risk of losing money on old equipment. But it hurt the planet. Shoppers thought they were helping clean energy. Instead, they kept using fossil fuels. This slows down the switch to electric appliances and better power systems.

Governments and big companies also use public money to hide pollution. They buy carbon offsets. This lets companies claim they are “carbon-neutral.” The UK government spent £200 million on this. They bought offsets for big corporations. Inside the government, warnings said this was risky for their reputation. Critics called the LEAF coalition a “pay-to-pollute” scheme. This lets companies keep harming the environment. They do not need to change how they work. Shoppers who trust these labels think they are supporting good brands. In reality, they are paying for companies to do nothing.

Words like “organic,” “natural,” and “biodegradable” often hide inaction. Whether a gas company lies about a green future or a government backs fake claims, the result is the same. It looks like progress, but it is not real. Consumers must ask for clear facts. We need real cuts in pollution, not clever tricks. The world needs true healing, not just green labels.
