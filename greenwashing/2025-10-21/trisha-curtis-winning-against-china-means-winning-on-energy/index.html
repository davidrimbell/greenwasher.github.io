TRISHA CURTIS: Winning Against China Means Winning On Energy

TRISHA CURTIS: Winning Against China Means Winning On Energy

The article argues that Western nations have engaged in greenwashing by prioritizing cheap Chinese renewable technology while neglecting domestic energy reliability. This strategy has inadvertently subsidized China’s industrial dominance, as its wind and solar equipment are produced using abundant, subsidized coal power. Consequently, Western efforts to decarbonize have weakened their own economic and security foundations, leaving them vulnerable to a rival that leverages cheap, reliable energy for manufacturing and military production. This energy disparity explains China’s control over critical supply chains, including rare earth processing, which requires immense, dirty, and stable power sources that wind and solar cannot provide. Meanwhile, regions like Europe and California suffer from high electricity costs, grid instability, and reduced industrial capacity due to the forced removal of baseload coal power in favor of intermittent renewables. The author contends that this misalignment undermines national security, as modern defense and technology sectors depend on consistent, affordable energy rather than fluctuating green sources. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the exposure of the hypocrisy and practical failures in Western energy policies. While political leaders in the US and Europe championed sustainability to appear environmentally responsible, they failed to address the environmental costs of imported Chinese goods and the economic reality that coal remains essential for a robust industrial base. The text concludes that true energy security and national strength require embracing affordable, reliable fossil fuels rather than adhering to a green agenda that compromises economic competitiveness and geopolitical power.
