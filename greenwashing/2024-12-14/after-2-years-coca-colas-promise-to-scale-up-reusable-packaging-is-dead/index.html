After 2 years, Coca-Cola’s promise to scale up reusable packaging is dead

Coca-Cola has quietly abandoned its previous pledges to reduce virgin plastic use and increase reusable packaging, shifting its strategy toward weaker recycling targets. This retreat from ambitious sustainability goals aligns with a broader industry trend where major corporations are failing to meet voluntary commitments, suggesting that environmental promises are often peripheral to core business operations rather than genuine strategic priorities. This reversal is highly relevant to greenwashing because it highlights a disconnect between public relations narratives and actual corporate behavior. By dropping specific reduction targets while maintaining a narrative of commitment, the company illustrates how businesses can use vague sustainability language to maintain their "social license to operate" without addressing the root causes of plastic pollution, effectively greenwashing their continued reliance on single-use fossil fuel-based packaging. Ultimately, the move signals a regression toward a linear economic model focused on managing waste rather than preventing it. Experts argue that prioritizing recyclability over reuse and production caps is an ineffective solution that ignores scientific recommendations for halting virgin plastic production, thereby allowing the corporation to evade accountability for its status as a leading global plastic polluter while continuing to exacerbate the environmental crisis.
