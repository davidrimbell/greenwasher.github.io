The trees of discord: from Emilia Romagna to Madagascar for carbon credits

The trees of discord: from Emilia Romagna to Madagascar for carbon credits

The investigation exposes how Tozzi Green, an Italian multinational, acquired extensive land in Madagascar under the guise of sustainable development, leading to serious allegations of land grabbing and the displacement of local communities who rely on the area for traditional grazing. Although the company claims to operate legally with state-approved contracts, local activists and international NGOs argue that these agreements exploit ambiguous land tenure systems to extract resources from vulnerable populations, prioritizing corporate profit over human rights and local livelihoods. Central to this conflict is the shift toward reforestation projects designed to generate voluntary carbon credits. By planting fast-growing, often non-native trees, the company aims to sell emission offsets to global markets, positioning itself as an environmental solution. However, critics highlight the inherent risks in these initiatives, noting that such monocultures may not support biodiversity and that the long-term permanence of these carbon sinks is uncertain, raising doubts about their actual efficacy in mitigating climate change compared to preserving existing ecosystems. This case is critically relevant to the concept of greenwashing because it illustrates how companies can use environmentally friendly narratives, such as carbon offsetting, to mask harmful socio-economic practices. The disparity between the project’s stated ecological benefits and the reality of land dispossession and community distress suggests a strategic use of "green" branding to legitimize extractive behavior, thereby misleading consumers and investors about the true environmental and social impact of their operations.
