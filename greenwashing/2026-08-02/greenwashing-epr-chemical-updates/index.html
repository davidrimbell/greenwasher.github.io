Greenwashing, EPR & Chemical Updates

The legal battle over California’s Truth in Recycling Act highlights the tension between combating greenwashing and protecting commercial speech. Industry groups argue that the law’s strict requirements for proving actual recycling infrastructure, rather than just theoretical capability, unlawfully restrict truthful advertising. This challenge is critical because it seeks to redefine how recyclability claims are validated, potentially shifting the burden from consumer information to systemic infrastructure realities. Similar constitutional disputes are emerging around Extended Producer Responsibility laws in California, Oregon, and Colorado. Plaintiffs contend these programs overreach state authority and burden interstate commerce, while environmental groups challenge specific implementation details, such as the inclusion of chemical recycling. These conflicts reveal deep ideological divides over what constitutes legitimate sustainability efforts versus deceptive marketing practices that exploit loopholes in current recycling systems. As states expand restrictions on hazardous substances and mandate producer compliance, the regulatory landscape becomes increasingly complex for manufacturers. Companies must navigate conflicting legal challenges and strict disclosure rules, such as those for PFAS labeling, which aim to increase transparency. These developments underscore the growing scrutiny on corporate environmental claims, forcing businesses to ensure their practices genuinely align with the "green" narratives presented to consumers.
