Greenwashing prompts calls for higher ESG data standards - IT-Online

Greenwashing prompts calls for higher ESG data standards - IT-Online

A recent report reveals widespread greenwashing within South Africa’s fossil fuel industry, where companies prioritize image rehabilitation over actual emission reductions. This deception is largely driven by incomplete or estimated data that undermines the credibility of sustainability claims. Such inaccuracies not only invite regulatory penalties and reputational damage but also mask inefficiencies that prevent genuine operational improvements. The core issue lies in the disconnect between sophisticated ESG platforms and the reality of poor data readiness, exposing the superficial nature of many current corporate sustainability strategies. Many businesses rely on ESG software that assumes flawless on-site metering and integrated systems, leading to misleading reports when these foundational elements are missing. Consequently, platforms often generate visually appealing but factually incorrect graphs, failing to address the complex need for local, site-level data collection. This technological gap highlights why many corporate sustainability efforts fall short; without accurate measurement of resources like electricity and waste, automated reporting tools cannot provide the actionable insights necessary for meaningful change. The reliance on static or estimated data renders these platforms ineffective for driving real environmental progress. To combat greenwashing, companies must shift focus from cloud-based reporting to establishing robust, ground-level data infrastructure. This involves auditing existing capabilities, installing precise metering equipment, and ensuring real-time data flow into integrated systems. By prioritizing verified, granular data, organizations can uncover inefficiencies, reduce costs, and meet evolving regulatory standards with integrity. Ultimately, authentic sustainability requires measurable operational improvements rather than superficial disclosures, ensuring that corporate actions align with public expectations and genuine environmental responsibility.
