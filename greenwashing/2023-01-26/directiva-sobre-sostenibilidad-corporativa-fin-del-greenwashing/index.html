Directiva sobre sostenibilidad corporativa. ¿Fin del 'greenwashing'?

The approval of the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) marks the end of voluntary environmental and social management within the European Union, establishing legally binding requirements for thousands of large companies. This regulatory shift equates sustainability data disclosure with financial reporting, obliging organizations to report not only their environmental impact but also their effects on human rights and society, as well as the associated risks and opportunities. The relevance of this legislation to the phenomenon of greenwashing lies in its ability to impose transparency and standardization—essential tools for combating greenwashing. By requiring comparable and reliable data, the directive makes it more difficult for companies to hide negative impacts behind vague or exaggerated claims. The mandatory nature of this information enables investors and stakeholders to genuinely assess sustainable performance, reducing the information asymmetry that has traditionally allowed misleading practices. Ultimately, this legal framework is expected to transform corporate perception and behavior, turning regulatory compliance into a competitive advantage in the eyes of increasingly demanding consumers and investors. Consistent application of the criteria will ensure proper implementation, guaranteeing that sustainability ceases to be a marketing strategy and becomes integrated into corporate management structures. This regulatory effort represents a crucial step toward a more ethical and sustainable market economy in the long term.
