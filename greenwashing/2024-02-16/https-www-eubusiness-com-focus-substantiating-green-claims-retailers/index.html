Greenwashing
ES EN

The European Parliament has advanced its draft report on the Substantiating Green Claims Directive, aiming to regulate environmental marketing claims. This legislative move addresses the critical need for transparency in greenwashing, ensuring that consumer trust is not eroded by misleading sustainability assertions. By establishing stricter standards, the directive seeks to distinguish genuine eco-friendly practices from deceptive marketing, thereby protecting shoppers from unsubstantiated environmental promises. Retail and wholesale representatives emphasize the sector’s vital role in facilitating sustainable consumption. They argue that reliable green labels are essential for informed decision-making but caution against overly burdensome pre-approval procedures. The industry advocates for simplified verification processes, particularly for small businesses, to balance rigorous sustainability goals with practical operational realities without stifling innovation or market agility. Furthermore, the proposal highlights the necessity for policy coherence across various EU regulations. Stakeholders urge legislators to ensure legal clarity and consistency with other Green Deal initiatives, such as corporate reporting and food system standards. Establishing supportive platforms for ongoing dialogue is crucial to developing effective delegated acts. This legislative effort is significant as it directly confronts the challenge of greenwashing by creating a unified framework that holds companies accountable for their environmental communications.
