Greenwashing
ES EN

The European Parliament is finalizing strict regulations to prohibit misleading environmental claims, requiring substantiated proof for terms like “eco-friendly” and banning unverified sustainability labels. This legislative shift aims to eliminate greenwashing by ensuring marketing messages are accurate and transparent, thereby protecting consumers from deceptive practices and promoting genuinely durable products. However, these increased regulatory pressures have triggered a counter-trend known as “green hushing.” Companies are increasingly choosing to undersell their environmental progress or limit disclosures to avoid scrutiny and potential greenwashing allegations. This strategic retreat suggests firms may now stick to minimum requirements rather than actively communicating their sustainability achievements. This dynamic is highly relevant to greenwashing because it highlights how over-regulation can inadvertently stifle honest corporate communication. While the intent is to curb deception, the fear of penalties may lead organizations to hide legitimate efforts, creating a new form of opacity. Understanding this tension is crucial for distinguishing between malicious fraud and cautious compliance in the evolving landscape of sustainable business practices.
