UK government suffer angry backlash from industry over plan to delay climate pledges

Prime Minister Rishi Sunak’s recent policy shifts, which delay bans on petrol cars and gas boilers while approving new fossil fuel licenses, are widely condemned as a retreat from the UK’s net-zero commitments. By prioritizing short-term economic relief over rigorous climate action, the government has alienated environmental experts and industry leaders who argue these moves undermine global climate leadership and jeopardize long-term prosperity. Critics view this not as pragmatic adjustment, but as a politically motivated rollback that jeopardizes the country’s future safety and economic stability. The reversal is particularly alarming given the UK’s history of pioneering ambitious climate legislation, including being the first major economy to legislate net-zero targets. Regulatory bodies like the Climate Change Committee have warned that these delays likely prevent the nation from meeting its legal obligations, effectively erasing years of progress. The approval of new coal and oil projects further signals a concerning slowdown in emissions reductions, suggesting the government is abandoning the urgent, cumulative responsibility it holds as a top historical emitter. This situation exemplifies greenwashing by framing regressive policies as a "more honest" and consensual path to sustainability. While the government claims to maintain its net-zero goals, the simultaneous approval of fossil fuel expansion and removal of efficiency mandates contradicts the stated ambition. By using the guise of public consent and economic relief to justify scaling back environmental protections, the administration engages in deceptive communication, obscuring the reality that these decisions actively hinder the transition to a low-carbon economy rather than facilitating it.
