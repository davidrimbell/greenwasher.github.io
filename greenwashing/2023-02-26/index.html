Why Green Labels Can Mislead Shoppers and Cost More

Shoppers in the early stages of buying often see mixed messages about eco-friendly products. This creates both good and bad risks for their choices.

Greenwashing can seem helpful. It makes hard choices easier. Many shoppers trust brands with green labels. This saves them mental energy. They do not have to check every claim. A study from the University of Queensland shows this clearly. People often buy products with green certifications. They do this even if the product is not truly better for the earth. This helps brands sell items faster.

But there is a big downside. Relying on false info can cost money and hurt health. Research in the Journal of Consumer Affairs shows a problem. Shoppers who believe greenwashing often pay more. They get products that do not keep their promises. This leads to regret. It also hurts trust in the market.

Also, vague claims can distract buyers. They might ignore important details like safety or durability. A report by the European Commission says this causes people to miss better options. For example, the UK’s Competition and Markets Authority fined several retailers. They used vague “green” claims. This shows how such practices confuse buyers and hurt fair competition.

Users must be careful. Green labels might feel safe, but they can hide problems. These problems affect long-term value and safety. Do not just look at big environmental promises. Look for specific proof. This helps you make smart choices. It ensures your purchases match your real needs and values.
