Net zero is the reason we have empty supermarket shelves

The article argues that recent food rationing exposes a critical failure in the UK’s agricultural strategy, driven by a government department overly focused on abstract environmental sustainability rather than practical food production. This ideological shift prioritizes land sparing and net-zero targets, such as converting fertile fields into solar farms, over essential agricultural output. The implication is that current policies are inherently flawed because they assume environmental management and high-volume food production are mutually exclusive, ignoring the potential benefits of regenerative farming that supports both biodiversity and yield. Consequently, the nation has become dangerously dependent on fragile international supply chains for fresh produce, revealing a severe lack of domestic resilience. The author contends that this vulnerability stems from a rigid adherence to inefficient land-use models inherited from the EU, which discourage the production of high-nutrient vegetables and fruits. Instead of building local infrastructure like glasshouses or diversifying feed sources, policymakers have created bureaucratic and financial barriers that prevent farmers from adapting to new realities, leaving the country exposed to global shocks and seasonal shortages. This situation is highly relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how environmental rhetoric can be used to justify policies that undermine food security under the guise of sustainability. By framing the reduction of agricultural output as an ecological virtue, the government masks the negative consequences of its "green" agenda, such as higher consumer costs and reduced access to essential goods. The article suggests that true sustainability requires a balanced approach that recognizes food production as a fundamental necessity, rather than allowing ideological commitments to create artificial scarcity and weaken national resilience.
