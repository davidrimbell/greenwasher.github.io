Greenwashing
ES EN

Semana del 28/4/13

The article defines “greenwashing” as the strategy by which corporations and products falsely project an image of environmental responsibility to conceal unsustainable or destructive practices. This manipulation allows companies to preserve their market share and power by presenting themselves as allies of the environment, while in reality their core activities generate pollution or resource depletion, using ecology as a smokescreen to divert attention from their actual negative impacts. A direct consequence of these practices is “boastful advertising,” in which minor ecological achievements are exaggerated or legally mandated initiatives are highlighted as if they were voluntary and proactive. Companies often invest more in public relations campaigns to appear “green” than in genuine environmental actions, and they frequently exert quiet pressure against environmental regulations. This creates an environment in which consumers find it increasingly difficult to distinguish between authentic environmental commitment and deceptive marketing designed solely to boost profits. This issue is relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how ecological misinformation exploits the public’s good faith to legitimize environmental harm. The text warns that the mere presence of green labels or claims does not guarantee sustainability, urging critical scrutiny beyond advertising. By identifying these tactics, consumers can avoid being misled and demand real transparency, understanding that third-party certification and independent research are essential tools for exposing environmental “image washing.”
