Decrecimiento y sostenibilidad

Decrecimiento y sostenibilidad

The article argues that the current green energy transition risks becoming a major example of greenwashing, where governments and corporations use renewable technologies as a facade for environmental respect while ignoring deeper systemic flaws. Antonio Turiel warns that the rapid expansion of inefficient and unrecyclable renewable infrastructure could lead to massive waste and public disillusionment, revealing the concept of "sustainable development" as an impostor strategy rather than a genuine ecological solution. Central to this critique is the incompatibility between endless economic growth and true environmental sustainability. The text highlights that reliance on new technologies ignores the fundamental issue of excessive consumption. Instead of pursuing unbounded growth, which depletes resources and generates non-recyclable debris, the author advocates for degrowth. This approach posits that living better with less energy is the only viable path to avoid future environmental disasters, contrasting sharply with the industrial push for more renewable capacity. This perspective is crucial for understanding greenwashing because it exposes how the narrative of "green growth" serves corporate and political interests rather than planetary health. By framing renewable expansion as the sole solution, the dominant economic model obscures the need for structural changes in consumption habits. Consequently, what is presented as an ecological emergency response often functions as a marketing tool that maintains the status quo, thereby perpetuating the very environmental exploitation it claims to solve.
