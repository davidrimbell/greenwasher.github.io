Column: Governor’s LNG deal raises bevy of concerns | Honolulu Star-Advertiser

Column: Governor’s LNG deal raises bevy of concerns | Honolulu Star-Advertiser

The article argues that Hawaii’s endorsement of liquefied natural gas as a transitional energy source represents a significant greenwashing risk by partnering with JERA, a supplier with a documented history of deceptive environmental practices and regulatory violations. This alliance prioritizes corporate interests over genuine climate action, potentially locking the state into long-term fossil fuel dependency under the guise of a temporary "bridge" solution. This misalignment between stated renewable goals and actual infrastructure investments undermines public trust and distracts from necessary, sustainable energy transitions. Critics highlight the severe financial and safety implications of this decision, noting that the proposed infrastructure could impose massive costs on ratepayers while offering unreliable service compared to alternatives. The technical vulnerabilities of offshore LNG facilities, which have failed catastrophically in other regions, pose existential threats to Hawaii due to its unique geological and environmental conditions. By ignoring these proven risks, the state exposes its citizens to preventable economic losses and public health hazards associated with toxic emissions and potential disasters. This case is crucial to understanding greenwashing because it illustrates how fossil fuel projects are rebranded as climate solutions to secure political and financial support. The article serves as a warning that labeling methane a "bridge" fuel can obscure its high global warming potential and toxic byproducts, thereby delaying decisive climate action. It emphasizes the need for transparency and rigorous scrutiny of energy partnerships to prevent corporate narratives from disguising harmful practices as environmental progress.
