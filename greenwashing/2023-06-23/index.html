Confirmed: new evidence shows tens of millions of carbon credits worthless
A new study by Professor Andrew Macintosh confirms that millions of Australian Carbon Credit Units issued for rural tree-regeneration projects are worthless, effectively disproving government assurances that the system was fundamentally sound. The research, utilizing detailed satellite data, reveals that the vast majority of these credits lack any basis in actual carbon sequestration, challenging the integrity of the nation’s primary market-based climate mechanism. The core issue lies with Human-Induced Regeneration projects, which claim to generate credits by allowing vegetation to recover naturally rather than through new planting. The data shows that most credited areas had never been significantly cleared, meaning no additional carbon absorption occurred beyond what would have happened anyway. In many cases, vegetation cover actually declined or remained unchanged, yet credits were issued as if substantial environmental benefits were achieved. This failure is a stark example of greenwashing, where the carbon credit system serves as a facade for climate inaction. By allowing heavy polluters to offset emissions using credits with no real-world impact, the scheme funnels money to intermediaries while masking the lack of genuine abatement. This undermines global climate goals by creating an illusion of progress, allowing industries to continue polluting under the false pretense of having compensated for their environmental footprint.

European consumer organizations have filed a coordinated complaint against KLM and sixteen other major airlines, accusing them of misleading passengers through climate-related compensation schemes. The core allegation is that these programs, which allow travelers to pay extra for carbon offsetting or sustainable aviation fuels, create a false impression of sustainability. By suggesting that flights can be made "neutral" or "green" through voluntary contributions, airlines are allegedly breaching EU rules against unfair commercial practices and deceiving consumers who wish to travel responsibly. The complaint highlights that the technological solutions promoted, such as sustainable aviation fuels, are currently not market-ready and constitute a tiny fraction of actual fuel usage. Consumer groups argue that depicting air travel as a sustainable mode of transport is pure greenwashing because fossil-based emissions persist regardless of these offset payments. The urgency stems from the fact that decarbonization technologies will not reach massive scale soon, meaning these schemes merely offer fake peace of mind rather than genuine environmental benefit. This action is critically relevant to understanding greenwashing as it illustrates how corporations exploit consumer goodwill to appear environmentally responsible without making substantial operational changes. By framing fossil-fuel-dependent activities as eco-friendly through minor financial contributions, airlines shift the burden of sustainability onto individuals while obscuring the true climate impact of their business models. This case underscores the need for stricter enforcement against misleading environmental claims and the distinction between genuine systemic change and superficial marketing tactics.
