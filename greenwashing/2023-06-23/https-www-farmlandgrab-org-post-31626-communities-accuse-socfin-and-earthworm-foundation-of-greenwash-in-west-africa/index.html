Greenwashing
ES EN

Communities in West Africa and Asia are rejecting a grievance assessment mission led by the Earthworm Foundation, commissioned by palm and rubber giant Socfin, alleging it constitutes greenwashing. Local advocates argue the process lacks independence and credibility because the Foundation is a paying member funded by corporations like Socfin. By prioritizing the company’s interests over genuine conflict resolution, this arrangement undermines the trust required for meaningful dialogue and fails to guarantee impartial justice for affected populations. The investigation is viewed as a superficial attempt to manage reputation rather than address systemic abuses, including land grabs, pollution, and lack of free, prior, and informed consent. Critics highlight that previous consultations by the Earthworm Foundation resulted in no tangible improvements, reinforcing the perception that such initiatives serve primarily to create an illusion of accountability. This dynamic allows multinational corporations to maintain controversial practices while projecting a sanitized image of sustainability to international stakeholders and consumers. This case is highly relevant to greenwashing as it exposes the structural flaws in corporate-led sustainability mechanisms. It demonstrates how paid third-party certifications can be manipulated to legitimize harmful operations without delivering redress, thereby misleading the public. The rejection of this mission by grassroots organizations underscores the urgent need for independent, community-driven oversight to ensure that sustainability claims reflect actual on-the-ground realities rather than performative corporate diplomacy.
