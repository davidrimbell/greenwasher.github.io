The EPA’s hydrogen push is a federal endorsement of greenwashing

The article critiques the EPA’s draft carbon rules for allowing gas and coal plants to transition to hydrogen, arguing this approach overlooks critical health and climate realities. Primarily, burning hydrogen generates significant nitrogen oxide pollution, which exacerbates respiratory and cardiovascular issues in environmental justice communities. By permitting these retrofits, the regulations effectively extend the lifespan of polluting infrastructure, disproportionately harming vulnerable populations despite claims of a cleaner energy transition. Furthermore, the piece challenges the efficacy of hydrogen blending in reducing carbon dioxide emissions, noting that mixing hydrogen with natural gas yields minimal decarbonization benefits. More critically, it highlights the severe indirect global warming potential caused by hydrogen leakage. Due to its small molecular size, hydrogen escapes easily and interferes with atmospheric methane removal, potentially amplifying near-term warming effects dramatically. This creates a scenario where the intended climate benefits are negated or worsened by the unique chemical properties of the fuel itself. This narrative is highly relevant to greenwashing as it exposes how labeling hydrogen as a clean transition fuel can mask its actual environmental and public health costs. By promoting hydrogen as a "silver bullet," industry groups and regulators may be obscuring the true impact of increased pollution and climate disruption. Recognizing these facts is essential to prevent the adoption of false solutions that prioritize industrial interests over genuine ecological and social well-being.
