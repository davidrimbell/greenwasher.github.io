Greenwashing
ES EN

The proliferation of environmental labels has created a confusing market where the lack of uniform criteria facilitates greenwashing, allowing companies to present an appearance of sustainability without basing it on real facts. This ambiguity erodes consumer trust and makes it difficult to distinguish truly responsible products from those that simply engage in misleading marketing. To combat this situation, the European Commission proposes a directive that establishes strict rules for environmental claims, requiring them to be based on verifiable scientific evidence and robust methodologies. By mandating validation by independent third parties and harmonizing requirements at the European level, the initiative aims to eliminate market fragmentation and stop the creation of new labeling schemes that do not meet high standards. The relevance of this article lies in marking a turning point in regulation against greenwashing, transforming voluntary ecological claims into strict obligations. For companies, non-compliance carries legal penalties and serious reputational risks, forcing them to review their marketing strategies internally and ensure that any green claim reflects real and verifiable environmental performance.
