You can now go to CASE for greenwashing related claims, according to minister Alvin Tan

Singapore has officially empowered consumers to report greenwashing directly to the Consumer Association of Singapore (CASE), signaling a significant shift in how misleading environmental marketing is handled. While no specific complaints have been lodged yet, this development establishes a formal channel for addressing false claims about product sustainability, reinforcing the government’s commitment to monitoring deceptive advertising practices. This initiative complements existing advertising guidelines that require advertisers to substantiate and clearly explain environmental claims. By mandating transparency and proof behind eco-friendly assertions, these regulations aim to curb the growing trend of brands exaggerating their green credentials. The absence of recent complaints suggests either effective self-regulation or a lack of consumer awareness, yet the new policy provides a safety net for those encountering deceptive marketing. The relevance to greenwashing lies in providing legal and organizational recourse for consumers who previously lacked direct support. High-profile cases, such as Innisfree’s misleading packaging and Mercedes-Benz’s nature-centric electric car ads, highlight the prevalence of such deceptive practices. By enabling formal complaints, Singapore strengthens accountability, ensuring that companies cannot easily exploit green sentiment without facing potential consequences, thereby protecting both consumer trust and genuine sustainability efforts.
