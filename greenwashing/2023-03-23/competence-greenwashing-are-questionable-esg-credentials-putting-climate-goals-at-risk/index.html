Competence greenwashing: Are questionable ESG credentials putting climate goals at risk?

Competence greenwashing: Are questionable ESG credentials putting climate goals at risk?

The rapid proliferation of ESG roles has outpaced the available talent pool, creating a surge in "competence greenwashing." As companies scramble to meet regulatory pressures and market expectations, they frequently hire executives who lack the necessary technical expertise, relying instead on buzzwords and superficial training. This disconnect between job titles and actual capability undermines the integrity of corporate sustainability efforts, as leaders are often ill-equipped to handle complex strategic decisions. This skills deficit is particularly acute in sectors like finance, where new entrants from non-technical backgrounds assume critical sustainability responsibilities without adequate grounding. The result is a significant erosion of trust, as organizations claim expertise they do not possess to satisfy external demands for net-zero commitments. While the expansion of green hiring is positive, the absence of deep technical knowledge in emerging hubs threatens to dilute genuine progress, turning sustainability centers of excellence into mere branding exercises rather than functional drivers of change. Ultimately, the industry faces a critical challenge: while leadership qualities like influence and innovation are valued, they cannot substitute for fundamental technical competency in areas like carbon management and biodiversity. The article highlights that relying on generalists or conversion-course graduates without robust technical support structures leads to ineffective governance. This relevance to greenwashing lies in the revelation that many high-profile sustainability appointments are symbolic gestures rather than substantive capacity building, risking credible accountability for the sake of perceived compliance.
