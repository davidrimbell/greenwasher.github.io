Greenwashing
ES EN

The European Commission has introduced strict regulations to combat greenwashing by mandating that companies substantiate environmental claims with scientific evidence before marketing products. This initiative addresses the widespread prevalence of vague, misleading, or unfounded assertions in the market, aiming to restore consumer trust and ensure that products labeled as eco-friendly are genuinely sustainable. By requiring independent verification, the proposal prevents businesses from exaggerating their environmental benefits to gain unfair competitive advantages. These rules target both explicit product claims and the proliferation of voluntary environmental labels, which currently confuse consumers and erode confidence. By prohibiting vague aggregate scoring systems and regulating new labeling schemes, the legislation seeks to eliminate greenwashing tactics that obscure the true environmental impact of goods. This ensures that comparisons between products are based on equivalent, reliable data, thereby creating a transparent marketplace where honest sustainability efforts are clearly distinguishable from marketing hype. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the proposal’s effort to level the playing field for genuinely sustainable companies who previously struggled against competitors making unsubstantiated claims. By establishing common norms for substantiation and communication, the EU aims to prevent environmental deception at the source. This regulatory framework ensures that only credible, verified information reaches consumers, protecting the integrity of the green market and encouraging authentic corporate responsibility.
