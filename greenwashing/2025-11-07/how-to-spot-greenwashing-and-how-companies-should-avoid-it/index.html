How to Spot Greenwashing and How Companies Should Avoid It

This article highlights the evolving landscape of greenwashing, defining it as environmental claims that lack factual support and mislead reasonable consumers. It emphasizes that these deceptive practices can occur anywhere in the supply chain, from sourcing to final product composition, creating significant legal and reputational risks for companies. The core message is that organizations must remain vigilant against both traditional and emerging forms of misleading marketing to avoid liability and maintain trust. A critical shift identified is the weaponization of greenwashing allegations for competitive advantage and political leverage. Beyond standard consumer protection lawsuits, competitors are now filing suits to undermine rivals’ market share, while state attorneys general are challenging renewable energy claims to protect fossil fuel industries. This demonstrates that greenwashing accusations are increasingly utilized as strategic tools to achieve business or political objectives, rather than solely addressing consumer deception or environmental harm. To mitigate these risks, companies should align their marketing with the FTC’s Green Guides and the strictest applicable state laws, as these standards heavily influence legal outcomes. Integrating legal review into the marketing process ensures that environmental claims accurately reflect actual practices, preventing the mismatch that triggers allegations. Ultimately, proactive compliance and careful substantiation of claims are essential defenses against the expanding and multifaceted threats of greenwashing litigation and regulatory scrutiny.
