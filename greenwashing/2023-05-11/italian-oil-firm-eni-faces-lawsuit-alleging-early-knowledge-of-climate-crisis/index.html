Italian Oil Firm Eni Faces Lawsuit Alleging Early Knowledge of Climate Crisis

Italian Oil Firm Eni Faces Lawsuit Alleging Early Knowledge of Climate Crisis

This article highlights a pivotal climate lawsuit in Italy where environmental groups aim to force Eni to drastically reduce carbon emissions, leveraging internal documents from the 1960s and 70s. These records prove that the company possessed early scientific evidence of the catastrophic climate impacts of fossil fuels, yet continued to expand production while publicly downplaying the risks. This pattern of concealing known dangers while ramping up output is a hallmark of corporate greenwashing, illustrating a deliberate disconnect between internal knowledge and public communication. The case draws direct parallels to successful legal precedents against Shell in the Netherlands, suggesting a growing global trend of holding energy giants accountable for historical deception. By suing both the corporation and government entities that hold significant stakes, the plaintiffs argue that Eni’s failure to warn the public or adjust its strategy constitutes harm driven by a long-standing pattern of obstruction. This legal strategy challenges the narrative that fossil fuel companies were merely caught off guard by climate science, instead framing their inaction as a calculated effort to protect profits despite clear warnings. Relevance to greenwashing is underscored by Eni’s history of marketing natural gas as a "clean" fuel during the 1980s and 1990s, a practice previously penalized by Italian authorities for misleading consumers. The lawsuit exposes the contradiction between Eni’s net-zero commitments and its continued expansion of oil and gas production, highlighting how corporate sustainability claims often mask business-as-usual practices. Ultimately, the case serves as a critical example of using legal mechanisms to pierce through green marketing narratives and enforce accountability for environmental misinformation.
