As regulators clamp down on forest destruction, conservationists flag greenwashing concerns - ICIJ

As regulators clamp down on forest destruction, conservationists flag greenwashing concerns - ICIJ

The article highlights a critical global shift from voluntary environmental certifications to stringent government regulations aimed at eliminating deforestation from supply chains. While billions of dollars were previously spent on green labels to assure consumers of sustainability, investigations reveal that these systems are frequently compromised. Auditors often approve products linked to illegal logging and authoritarian regimes, allowing companies to bypass genuine ecological responsibility. This regulatory tightening is a direct response to widespread greenwashing, where firms mislead the public by labeling destructive products as eco-friendly or using certifications in confusing ways. With major jurisdictions like the EU, UK, and Australia implementing strict rules against misleading environmental claims, the era of self-regulation is ending. These measures expose how voluntary schemes have historically undermined conservation efforts by legitimizing "dirty" products, necessitating legal accountability to ensure claims match reality. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the exposure of structural flaws in traditional certification models. The narrative demonstrates that without rigorous, government-backed verification, environmental marketing often serves as a veil for continued ecological harm. As lawmakers ban contractors involved in deforestation, the focus shifts from trusting private labels to demanding transparent, legally enforceable proof of sustainable sourcing, effectively closing the loopholes that allowed corporate greenwashing to thrive for decades.
