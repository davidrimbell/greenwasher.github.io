Canada's competition watchdog publishes final greenwashing guidelines

Canada's competition watchdog publishes final greenwashing guidelines

Canada’s Competition Bureau has released new guidelines to combat greenwashing, mandating that environmental claims must be accurate, substantiated, and assessed based on the overall impression they create. This shift emphasizes that vague statements about future intentions, such as aspirational net-zero goals lacking concrete plans, constitute misleading advertising. By requiring clear evidence for sustainability assertions, the rules aim to align marketing practices with the spirit of recently enacted anti-greenwashing laws. The relevance to greenwashing is highlighted by the recent handling of the Pathways Alliance investigation. After facing complaints that their net-zero claims were unsubstantiated, the group removed its controversial advertisements. The Bureau subsequently dropped the inquiry, a move interpreted as a strategic pause to issue these clarifying guidelines rather than pursue immediate penalties. This approach underscores the critical need for businesses to distinguish between genuine, evidence-backed actions and deceptive "pathway" promises that mislead consumers. Ultimately, these guidelines serve as a vital tool to restore consumer trust and ensure a transparent green transition. While corporate entities have previously lobbied against such regulations fearing overregulation, the new framework clarifies that truthful advertising is mandatory, not optional. This development signals a serious enforcement stance, encouraging companies to avoid "green hushing" or vague claims while empowering consumers to report deceptive practices that undermine genuine environmental efforts.
