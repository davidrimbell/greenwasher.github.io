Greenwashing
EN ES

The article argues that inviting COP30 to showcase indigenous territories risks trivializing their role, turning their culture into mere "wallpaper" for superficial emissions deals. This approach constitutes greenwashing, where the complex ecological stewardship of indigenous peoples is reduced to a cosmetic feature of corporate-style climate agreements. The author warns that failing to genuinely integrate indigenous leadership allows for "green colonialism," a form of fraud that masks ongoing ecological destruction behind performative environmental commitments. Central to this critique is the concept of "ecogenocide," where the deliberate annihilation of indigenous communities and their land care practices is treated as a crime against both humanity and the environment. The author highlights how regimes, such as Israel in Palestine, may reforest destroyed lands to present a facade of ecological responsibility while continuing ethnic cleansing. This illustrates how environmental policies can be weaponized to obscure atrocities, creating a narrative of sustainability that actively perpetuates the dispossession and destruction of indigenous social and ecological fabrics. True environmental justice requires placing indigenous peoples at the center of global decisions, backed by reparatory justice and the exclusion of perpetrators of ecocide from negotiations. The author urges COP30 to uphold UNESCO principles by recognizing indigenous custodianship rather than treating it as a technical metric. By refusing to normalize states engaged in genocide, the summit can avoid polluting genuine environmental measures with the fraud of greenwashing, ensuring that climate action does not come at the cost of cultural and ecological survival.
