Greenwashing
EN ES

The article argues that major fossil fuel and agricultural corporations have long concealed their knowledge of climate damage by initially denying science, then later adopting superficial green strategies to delay meaningful regulation. This two-phase approach allows these industries to maintain profitability while projecting an image of environmental responsibility. Consequently, their commitment to sustainability is revealed as a calculated tactic rather than a genuine ethical shift, designed to distract the public and influence policy without threatening their core business models. Recently, however, the return of pro-fossil fuel political leadership has triggered a retreat from this subtle "greenwashing" toward overt climate denial and expanded extraction. Companies are slashing renewable investments and increasing oil production, abandoning the pretense of transition in favor of unabashed profit maximization. This shift signifies that previous environmental pledges were largely performative, used to manage public relations until political conditions permitted a more aggressive defense of traditional energy sources without the burden of appearing environmentally conscious. This evolution is critical to understanding greenwashing because it exposes the inherent contradiction between corporate profit motives and ecological survival. The recent move away from green branding toward aggressive denial demonstrates that the primary goal of these tactics was never to mitigate climate change, but to buy time and maintain market dominance. Ultimately, the article highlights that greenwashing serves as a sophisticated mechanism to protect capitalist interests, revealing that without strict regulatory enforcement, corporate environmental claims are merely strategic obfuscations rather than commitments to planetary health.
