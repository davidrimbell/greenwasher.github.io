Scrumbles abandona la certificación B Corp y dona su cuota a Save the Children

Scrumbles, a British ethical pet food brand, has voluntarily withdrawn from B Corp certification, accusing the label of failing to prevent greenwashing. The company argues that the certification has lost its transformative power, becoming merely an aesthetic symbol rather than a guarantee of genuine ethical practice. By redirecting its recertification fees to Save the Children, Scrumbles demonstrates that corporate responsibility does not require third-party validation, emphasizing direct social impact over bureaucratic endorsement. This move reflects a broader crisis of credibility affecting the B Corp movement, highlighted by similar decisions from brands like Dr. Bronner’s. Critics contend that the standard has been exploited by large multinationals with significant marketing budgets, allowing them to appropriate sustainability discourse without fundamentally altering their business models. Consequently, the distinction has shifted from a rigorous verification tool to a potential instrument for sophisticated greenwashing, where companies project an image of integrity while avoiding substantial operational changes. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the debate over whether certifications ensure real impact or merely offer reputational shielding for inadequate practices. As scrutiny intensifies, organizations like B Lab are planning stricter standards to restore trust. However, Scrumbles’ departure underscores a growing skepticism among conscious consumers and businesses who fear that logos no longer correlate with tangible environmental or social benefits, signaling a pivotal moment for how ethical credibility is defined and verified.
