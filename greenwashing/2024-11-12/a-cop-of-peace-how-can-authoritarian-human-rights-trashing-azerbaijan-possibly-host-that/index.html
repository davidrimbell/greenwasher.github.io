A ‘Cop of peace’? How can authoritarian, human rights-trashing Azerbaijan possibly host that?

The article argues that hosting COP29 in Azerbaijan serves primarily as a mechanism for greenwashing, allowing an authoritarian petrostate to legitimize its actions while the global climate crisis worsens. By selecting a regime with an economy entirely dependent on fossil fuels and a record of severe human rights abuses, the summit prioritizes diplomatic optics over genuine environmental or ethical accountability. This approach enables the host nation to deflect criticism and present a sanitized image, masking the reality that its continued expansion of oil and gas production directly contradicts necessary climate goals. Furthermore, the piece highlights how the summit provides a platform for ethical laundering, where a regime accused of war crimes and ethnic cleansing can claim to foster global peace. The author contends that this is not merely a failure of climate policy but a deliberate strategy to whitewash atrocities through green initiatives and superficial truces. This disconnect undermines the integrity of international climate negotiations, as it allows nations to engage in high-level discussions on sustainability while actively violating the fundamental rights and safety of marginalized populations. This narrative is critical to understanding greenwashing because it expands the definition beyond mere corporate or environmental deception to include geopolitical and humanitarian masking. It demonstrates how climate summits can be exploited by repressive regimes to gain international legitimacy and obscure their complicity in broader crises, including the funding of military conflicts. Recognizing this dynamic is essential for distinguishing between sincere climate action and performative diplomacy that ignores the interconnected nature of ecological and human rights emergencies.
