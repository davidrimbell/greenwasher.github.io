Fossil fuel exporter Azerbaijan to host COP29 this week amid greenwashing claims

Azerbaijan’s hosting of COP29 in Baku highlights a stark contradiction between its desire to project climate leadership and its aggressive expansion of fossil fuel infrastructure. Despite leveraging its history as an ancient oil producer, the nation is actively ramping up natural gas exports to Europe, framing renewable energy development not as a transition away from hydrocarbons, but as a strategy to preserve gas reserves for future lucrative sales. This dual approach raises serious concerns about the sincerity of its environmental commitments. Critics argue that this narrative constitutes classic greenwashing, where superficial green initiatives mask a continued reliance on polluting industries. Activists highlight that while the government promotes a clean image, it simultaneously suppresses environmental monitoring and fails to address severe pollution from oil extraction near residential areas. Consequently, climate scientists rate the country’s emission reduction plans as critically insufficient, noting that recent increases in gas flaring undermine any claimed progress toward sustainability. This situation underscores the growing scrutiny of fossil fuel-exporting nations leading climate talks. By positioning itself as a renewable energy leader while simultaneously securing long-term fossil fuel contracts, Azerbaijan joins a pattern seen in previous host nations that face accusations of using high-profile diplomatic events to legitimize their carbon-intensive economies. The conference thus becomes a focal point for debates on whether such diplomatic platforms can drive genuine change or merely serve as vehicles for reputational laundering.
