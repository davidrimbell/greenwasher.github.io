Greta Thunberg protests against Azerbaijan hosting global climate summit

Greta Thunberg and other activists argue that Azerbaijan is exploiting the UN climate summit to mask its authoritarian record and human rights abuses. By hosting the conference in an oil-rich nation with a repressive regime, the event risks legitimizing a government accused of ethnic cleansing and suppressing civil society, turning a global environmental gathering into a platform for political whitewashing. This situation highlights the dangerous intersection of climate diplomacy and geopolitical power, where major fossil fuel producers can use high-profile international events to project an image of environmental responsibility. Critics contend that Azerbaijan’s commitment to clean energy is merely a strategic move to justify continued fossil fuel exports, demonstrating how nations may leverage sustainability narratives to deflect scrutiny from their actual environmental impact and democratic deficits. The controversy is critically relevant to greenwashing as it exemplifies the practice of using superficial environmental credentials to obscure significant ethical and ecological failures. It underscores the urgency of holding host nations accountable for their human rights records and fossil fuel dependencies, ensuring that climate forums do not become tools for regimes to sanitize their reputations while perpetuating the very systems driving the climate crisis.
