Earth systems students oppose proposed name change to major

Earth systems students oppose proposed name change to major

Students at Stanford’s Doerr School of Sustainability strongly oppose renaming their major to include “sustainability sciences,” citing the term’s association with corporate greenwashing. Critics argue that the word lacks precise definition and allows entities to mask inaction as environmental stewardship. Consequently, some students have altered their academic paths, fearing the university is prioritizing corporate climate narratives over genuine ecological responsibility and transformative planetary health strategies. The controversy highlights a critical tension in environmental education: the risk of diluting rigorous scientific discourse with vague, industry-friendly jargon. While administrators claim the name change better reflects the major’s scope and aligns with institutional goals, students view this as a shift toward conforming to industry standards. They insist on honest, specific language that addresses the true causes of the climate crisis rather than adopting terminology that facilitates superficial compliance and avoids accountability. This debate is highly relevant to understanding greenwashing because it illustrates how branding and terminology can be used to obscure a lack of substantive action. The students’ resistance underscores the danger of allowing commercial interests to redefine academic concepts, potentially turning educational programs into vehicles for legitimizing greenwashing practices. Their stance serves as a warning against adopting language that prioritizes appearance over genuine environmental impact and ethical clarity.
