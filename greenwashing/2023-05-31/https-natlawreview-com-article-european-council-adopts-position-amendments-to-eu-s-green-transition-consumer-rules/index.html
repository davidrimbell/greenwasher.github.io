Greenwashing
ES EN

The European Council has strengthened its stance against greenwashing by proposing stricter regulations on environmental claims in consumer products. Central to this effort is a push to ban vague, unsubstantiated terms like “eco-friendly” unless backed by recognized certification schemes. This shift aims to eliminate deceptive marketing that exploits consumer goodwill, ensuring that sustainability labels are trustworthy and comparable. By demanding rigorous verification through independent third parties, the Council seeks to create a transparent marketplace where claims reflect genuine environmental progress rather than mere advertising flair. A critical component of these new rules is the crackdown on future-oriented promises, such as vague net-zero targets, unless supported by clear, verifiable implementation plans. The proposal mandates that such claims be independently verified and that findings remain publicly accessible, preventing companies from creating false impressions that their products immediately contribute to a low-carbon economy. This focus on accountability ensures that businesses cannot rely on speculative projections to gain a competitive advantage, thereby raising the bar for credibility in corporate sustainability communications. This development is highly relevant to greenwashing because it signals a definitive regulatory trend toward holding corporations accountable for misleading environmental narratives. As enforcement mechanisms and collective redress procedures expand, companies face increased legal risks for unsubstantiated claims. Consequently, this legislative move underscores the global shift from voluntary sustainability reporting to mandatory, verified transparency, forcing businesses to align their marketing closely with actual environmental performance to avoid significant reputational and legal consequences.
