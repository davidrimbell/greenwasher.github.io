Cop28 president’s team accused of Wikipedia ‘greenwashing’

The article exposes how the COP28 president and his team engaged in coordinated efforts to manipulate public perception through Wikipedia edits, a tactic widely recognized as a form of greenwashing. By attempting to remove references to controversial fossil fuel investments and replacing them with positive framing about renewable energy, these actors sought to obscure the conflict between Al Jaber’s role as an oil executive and his leadership of global climate negotiations. This deliberate alteration of digital information aims to present a sanitized image that downplays environmental harm while highlighting green credentials, thereby misleading stakeholders about the true nature of the summit’s leadership. This manipulation serves to legitimize fossil fuel interests within international climate policy, raising serious concerns about the integrity of the COP process. Critics argue that allowing a CEO of a major oil company to preside over climate talks, while simultaneously working to airbrush criticism of that same company’s activities, represents a profound hypocrisy. The effort to control the narrative suggests that the primary goal was not to foster transparent dialogue on climate action, but rather to protect the reputational interests of the fossil fuel industry by neutralizing scrutiny before the summit begins. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the use of strategic communication to create a false impression of environmental responsibility. By paying individuals to edit Wikipedia entries and making undisclosed changes to highlight clean energy initiatives while suppressing details of expanded oil production, the actors are engaging in deceptive marketing practices. This behavior exemplifies how greenwashing can extend beyond corporate advertising to include the manipulation of informational sources, aiming to shift public discourse away from accountability and toward a fabricated narrative of sustainability and alliance with the climate movement.
