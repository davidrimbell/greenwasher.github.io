Stand.earth accuses Lululemon of greenwashing

Stand.earth has formally accused Lululemon of greenwashing, arguing that its “Be Planet” marketing campaign misleads consumers by hiding the fact that its climate pollution has doubled since the slogan’s launch. The complaint highlights a stark contradiction between the brand’s environmental ethos and its reliance on fossil fuel-derived materials, which contribute significantly to both greenhouse gas emissions and microplastic pollution in oceans. This discrepancy suggests that the company’s rapid growth is built on practices that undermine the very sustainability image it projects to the public. The core of the accusation lies in the disparity between Lululemon’s reported progress in its own facilities and its extensive supply chain impacts. While the brand claims success in reducing emissions within its owned operations, critics point out that the majority of its footprint comes from manufacturing in countries relying heavily on coal and fracked gas. This implies that Lululemon is selectively reporting data to appear sustainable while ignoring the substantial environmental harm caused by its suppliers and the materials used in its products. This case is a textbook example of greenwashing because it demonstrates how companies can use vague, positive communication to create an overly favorable perception of their environmental performance without addressing systemic issues. It underscores the danger of relying on partial transparency, where brands highlight minor internal improvements to distract from larger, unmitigated ecological damages in their broader supply chains. Such tactics allow corporations to profit from eco-conscious branding while continuing business models that exacerbate climate change.
