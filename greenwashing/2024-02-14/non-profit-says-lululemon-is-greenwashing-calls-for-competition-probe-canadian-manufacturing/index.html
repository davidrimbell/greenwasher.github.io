Non-profit says Lululemon is 'greenwashing', calls for competition probe - Canadian Manufacturing

A non-profit organization has requested an investigation into Lululemon, alleging that the athletic-wear giant is misleading consumers regarding its environmental footprint. Despite marketing campaigns that position the brand as a climate leader, internal reports indicate that the company’s total greenhouse-gas emissions have significantly increased since 2020. This discrepancy highlights a dangerous pattern where corporate sustainability rhetoric outpaces actual operational performance, suggesting that the brand’s public image is crafted to obscure its growing contribution to global warming rather than reflect genuine ecological stewardship. The core of this greenwashing lies in the disparity between Lululemon’s claims and its supply chain reality. The vast majority of its carbon footprint stems from manufacturing and raw materials, much of which relies heavily on fossil fuels and high-emission shipping methods like aviation. While the company boasts renewable energy use in its own offices and stores, these efforts are negligible compared to the industrial emissions generated by its global production network. This selective reporting allows the brand to present a sanitized view of its impact, ignoring the most polluting aspects of its business model that contribute disproportionately to climate change. This case is critically relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how corporations exploit vague sustainability goals to maintain market share while continuing harmful practices. By setting distant net-zero targets without immediate, substantial reductions in Scope 3 emissions, Lululemon exemplifies the industry-wide tactic of prioritizing messaging over measurable action. The accusation underscores that without transparency and immediate decarbonization of the entire supply chain, ambitious future pledges can serve primarily as marketing tools to deflect scrutiny, thereby delaying meaningful environmental progress and deceiving environmentally conscious consumers.
