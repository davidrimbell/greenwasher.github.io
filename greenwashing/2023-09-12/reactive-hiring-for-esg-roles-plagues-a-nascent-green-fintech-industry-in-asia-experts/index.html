‘Reactive’ hiring for ESG roles plagues a nascent green fintech industry in Asia: experts

The rapid expansion of sustainable finance in Asia is hindered by a severe talent shortage, forcing companies to repurpose generalist finance professionals into ESG roles. This "competence greenwash" occurs when underqualified staff, lacking specialized knowledge in both finance and sustainability, are promoted to meet market demand. This practice risks superficial integration of ESG principles rather than genuine expertise, potentially leading to ineffective strategies and compliance failures as regulations tighten. The industry is currently characterized by a lack of strict standards, allowing almost any product to be marketed as sustainable with minimal oversight. However, as central banks in regions like the EU and Singapore begin cracking down on regulatory loopholes, the consequences for non-compliance will become more severe. This shift exposes the danger of hiring inexperienced personnel for compliance roles, creating a critical challenge as the market moves from a forgiving environment to one demanding rigorous adherence to defined green standards. Consequently, there is an urgent need for professionals who possess a unique intersection of financial acumen, technological skills, and deep ESG domain knowledge. The current disconnect between these skill sets means the industry lacks a cohesive playbook to manage large data gaps and make informed investment decisions. Without addressing this expertise deficit, the sector risks undermining its own credibility and failing to deliver meaningful environmental progress despite growing consumer demand for responsible investing.
