The hidden cost of greenwashing the Indian Railways

The article critiques the Indian Railways’ aggressive electrification policy, which prematurely rendered hundreds of diesel locomotives redundant despite having significant remaining service lives. This rapid transition created a massive inventory of functional assets that were no longer needed domestically, prompting plans to export them after costly gauge conversions. Reusing these vehicles for African markets is framed not as sustainable engineering, but as a method to liquidate stranded assets. The focus shifts from genuine environmental benefit to economic disposal, suggesting the primary driver is managing excess inventory rather than reducing global carbon footprints. This scenario exemplifies greenwashing by disguising asset write-offs and operational inefficiencies as eco-friendly initiatives. It highlights how rapid infrastructure transitions can create waste, which is then repackaged as sustainable reuse, masking the underlying profligacy and strategic mismanagement of resources under the guise of environmental progress.
