While official narratives may emphasize conservation efforts, the reality on the ground reveals a significant gap between political rhetoric and actual environmental protection, illustrating the core deception of greenwashing. This disconnect allows harmful activities to continue under the guise of sustainability, undermining genuine preservation strategies. Greenwashing is further evident in how corporations and political entities manipulate environmental narratives to obscure their negative impacts. Major industries, such as meat production and mining, use misleading eco-friendly branding and fraudulent carbon credits to legitimize destructive practices. By presenting a false image of responsibility, these actors deflect attention from illegal deforestation and the criminalization of indigenous territories, effectively using environmental terminology to mask ongoing ecological harm and impunity. This issue is critical to understanding greenwashing because it demonstrates how systemic failures and corporate deception intersect with indigenous rights violations. The article reveals that environmental claims are often used to legitimize illegal land acquisition and political influence, rather than to protect ecosystems. Consequently, the phenomenon extends beyond mere marketing; it represents a structural tool that enables continued degradation by creating a facade of compliance, thereby obscuring the true extent of the crisis and hindering effective accountability.
