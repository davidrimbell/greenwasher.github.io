Hannah Marshall on gaslighting, greenwashing and greenlighting

Regulatory enforcement against greenwashing is intensifying, leading many corporations to retreat into "greenhushing" out of fear of legal repercussions or accusations of anti-competitive behavior. This silence undermines genuine sustainability efforts, as companies become hesitant to share their progress or collaborate with rivals to improve industry standards, viewing compliance merely as a barrier rather than an opportunity. The article introduces "greenlighting" as a counter-strategy, advocating for legal frameworks that empower rather than hinder authentic corporate communication. By grounding marketing claims in verified data and internal practices before crafting narratives, businesses can mitigate legal risks while building consumer trust. This approach encourages transparency about a company’s journey, including its shortcomings, which resonates more deeply than aspirational but unsubstantiated claims. This perspective is relevant to greenwashing because it addresses the root causes of deceptive practices: the disconnect between marketing and reality, and the legal anxiety that stifles honest dialogue. By reframing legal compliance as an enabler for responsible storytelling, companies can avoid misleading consumers and contribute meaningfully to climate solutions without falling into the traps of exaggeration or fear-induced silence.
