EPA Policy Rejects Plastic Industry’s Misleading Math on Recycled Products

EPA Policy Rejects Plastic Industry’s Misleading Math on Recycled Products

The Environmental Protection Agency has taken a historic stand against greenwashing by banning the "mass balance" accounting method for its Safer Choice label. This controversial practice allowed companies to market products with high recycled content percentages through mathematical adjustments, even when the physical products contained negligible actual recycled material. By requiring that recycled content be determined strictly by physical weight, the EPA has effectively closed a loophole that facilitated misleading advertising, ensuring that labels accurately reflect the tangible environmental benefits of the products. This policy shift directly targets the marketing strategies of chemical recycling technologies, such as pyrolysis, which often rely on mass balance to claim success rates they cannot physically achieve. The new standard mandates a minimum of 15% postconsumer recycled content, a threshold that many current industry practices fail to meet. Consequently, products previously marketed as sustainable through convoluted accounting are now excluded from federal recognition, signaling a broader government commitment to truth in labeling rather than accepting industrial definitions of sustainability that prioritize optics over reality. The ruling is highly relevant to the fight against greenwashing because it establishes a clear precedent that physical presence matters more than accounting tricks. It validates long-standing criticisms from environmentalists and researchers who argued that mass balance deceives consumers and undermines genuine waste reduction efforts. By aligning federal standards with consumer expectations of honesty, this decision pressures the broader plastics industry and other regulatory bodies, such as the FTC, to adopt similar transparency measures, marking a significant turning point in holding corporations accountable for their environmental claims.
