WA government accused of 'doctoring' secret gas report for net zero narrative

WA government accused of 'doctoring' secret gas report for net zero narrative

The Western Australian government faces allegations of greenwashing after significantly altering a Deloitte report commissioned to support claims that domestic gas exports aid Asia’s decarbonization. The initial draft warned that natural gas could crowd out cleaner alternatives and carry substantial climate risks, but the final version removed these critical warnings. By downplaying the dangers of infrastructure lock-in and technology inertia, the government appears to have manipulated scientific evidence to legitimize an expansion of fossil fuel exports under the guise of environmental stewardship. Furthermore, the final report dramatically inflated projections of future gas demand in Asia, claiming consumption could double compared to earlier estimates, while omitting the executive summary’s focus on risks entirely. This revisionism serves to construct a misleading narrative that Western Australian gas is a "secure" and "low-emissions" bridge to net zero. Critics argue this constitutes climate denial, as it ignores the consensus that reducing fossil fuel use, not increasing supply, is necessary to meet global climate goals. The manipulation suggests the report was tailored to provide political cover for controversial approvals of long-lived gas projects. This incident is highly relevant to greenwashing as it demonstrates how governments can exploit scientific commissions to rebrand harmful activities as environmentally beneficial. By editing out unfavorable data and emphasizing selective positives, the state creates a false impression of sustainability, misleading the public and international partners. Such practices undermine trust in climate policy and highlight the pervasive danger of corporate and state-sponsored greenwashing, where the appearance of eco-friendly action masks the reality of continued fossil fuel reliance and increased emissions.
