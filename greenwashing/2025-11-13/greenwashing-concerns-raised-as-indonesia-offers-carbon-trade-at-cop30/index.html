‘Greenwashing’ concerns raised as Indonesia offers carbon trade at COP30

‘Greenwashing’ concerns raised as Indonesia offers carbon trade at COP30

Indonesia’s announcement at COP30 to prioritize carbon trading as a central climate strategy has sparked intense criticism for potentially representing a shift toward commercializing environmental protection rather than achieving genuine ecological preservation. By aiming to generate significant revenue through the sale of carbon credits, the Indonesian delegation has been accused of placing economic gains above substantive mitigation, thereby risking the further commodification of land and forests under the guise of sustainability. This approach is widely interpreted as greenwashing because it allows major polluters to continue emitting greenhouse gases by purchasing offsets, rather than forcing necessary reductions in their own operations. Critics argue that this market-based mechanism serves as a financial tool for the state and big business, enabling them to maintain high emission levels without undergoing the structural changes required to truly decarbonize the economy or phase out fossil fuels. Furthermore, the emphasis on carbon trading raises serious concerns about social justice and land rights, particularly for indigenous and local communities whose territories may overlap with designated carbon project areas. Without prior acknowledgment and protection of these rights, the expansion of the carbon market risks exacerbating agrarian conflicts and marginalizing vulnerable populations, highlighting how environmental policies can perpetuate social inequities while failing to address the root causes of the climate crisis.
