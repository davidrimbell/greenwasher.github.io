Taiwan to ease sustainability reporting burden, draft anti-greenwashing rules

Taiwan to ease sustainability reporting burden, draft anti-greenwashing rules

Taiwan is streamlining corporate sustainability reporting to balance net-zero goals with reduced compliance burdens, responding to business concerns and international trends toward simplification. Authorities are harmonizing carbon accounting rules and delaying mandatory Scope 3 emissions reporting to lower initial costs, allowing firms to utilize existing data to avoid duplication. This pragmatic approach aims to maintain progress toward emissions targets without overwhelming companies with excessive regulatory requirements. Simultaneously, the government is introducing anti-greenwashing guidelines to address exaggerations in environmental claims and the misuse of sustainability awards. Drawing on regulatory frameworks from Europe and the United States, these rules will enforce truthfulness, proportionality, and evidence-backed statements in marketing and advertising. The guidelines extend previous financial sector measures to the broader corporate landscape, ensuring that environmental labels and rankings are transparent, verifiable, and free from misleading language. This development is critical to understanding greenwashing, as it highlights the dual challenge of reducing reporting fatigue while preventing deceptive marketing. By regulating how companies claim environmental achievements, Taiwan acknowledges that ease of compliance must not come at the cost of credibility. The shift underscores a global trend where regulators are increasingly focused on the integrity of sustainability disclosures, ensuring that "green" labels reflect genuine impact rather than strategic positioning.
