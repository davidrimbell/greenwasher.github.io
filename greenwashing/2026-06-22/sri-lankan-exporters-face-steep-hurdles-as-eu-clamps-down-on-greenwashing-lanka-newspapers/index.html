Sri Lankan Exporters Face Steep Hurdles as EU Clamps Down on Greenwashing - Lanka Newspapers

Sri Lankan Exporters Face Steep Hurdles as EU Clamps Down on Greenwashing - Lanka Newspapers

The European Union’s new anti-greenwashing regulations fundamentally shift the burden of proof for environmental claims, demanding rigorous, science-based evidence rather than accepting vague marketing terms like "eco-friendly." This strict regulatory framework aims to eliminate misleading sustainability assertions by requiring independent verification and transparent documentation for all products entering the market. Sri Lankan exporters face severe consequences as their primary industries, including apparel and tea, historically relied on broad sustainability narratives without formal certification. The inability of many small and medium-sized enterprises to afford compliance costs or restructure supply chains threatens to exclude them from European markets, regardless of their actual environmental practices. This creates a barrier where lack of infrastructure, rather than unsustainable behavior, determines market access. This situation is highly relevant to greenwashing as it highlights the gap between marketing claims and verifiable action. The regulations force businesses to move beyond superficial branding toward genuine transparency, exposing how unsubstantiated environmental claims can lead to trade barriers. Consequently, companies must prioritize authentic sustainability strategies to avoid penalties and maintain global competitiveness.
