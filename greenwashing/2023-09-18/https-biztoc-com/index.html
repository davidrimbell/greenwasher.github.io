Greenwashing
ES EN

Global markets experienced significant volatility as treasury yields surged to near two-decade highs, driven by robust economic data and renewed expectations of Federal Reserve interest rate hikes. This shift triggered a risk-averse environment, causing major stock indices to retreat and increasing borrowing costs for consumers, particularly in the housing market. Simultaneously, geopolitical tensions and trade dynamics added layers of uncertainty. While a temporary US-China trade truce provided brief respite, unresolved issues persist, and escalating rhetoric regarding Iran heightened fears of regional conflict. These macroeconomic and geopolitical pressures contribute to a volatile landscape where investors must navigate conflicting signals about economic stability and future growth. This article illustrates greenwashing relevance by highlighting how complex market narratives can obscure environmental and social realities. Investors may prioritize short-term financial metrics over long-term sustainability efforts, allowing companies to mask poor environmental practices behind strong financial performance or vague "green" marketing. The focus on yield and stock prices often diverts attention from actual ecological impacts, enabling businesses to exploit information asymmetry and mislead stakeholders about their true commitment to sustainability.
