Greenwashing
ES EN

The European Consumer Organisation argues that labeling food products as CO2 neutral constitutes greenwashing. This practice relies on carbon offsetting, where companies pay for future environmental projects to balance current emissions. Such claims are scientifically inaccurate and misleading, as consumers cannot verify the actual environmental impact or validity of these offsets. Consumers often trust these labels, assuming they indicate genuine ecological benefits. However, the underlying logic is flawed because offsetting allows immediate pollution in exchange for uncertain future compensation. This marketing strategy exploits consumer concern for the environment without delivering substantive climate action, creating a false sense of responsibility. Consequently, BEUC calls for a complete ban on these misleading labels. The relevance to greenwashing lies in exposing how corporations use vague environmental claims to mask unsustainable practices. By highlighting the gap between marketing promises and actual carbon reduction, the report warns against deceptive sustainability tactics that hinder genuine ecological progress.
