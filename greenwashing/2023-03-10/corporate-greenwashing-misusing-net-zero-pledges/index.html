Corporate greenwashing—misusing ‘net zero’ pledges

Corporate greenwashing—misusing ‘net zero’ pledges

A recent analysis reveals that major global corporations are largely engaging in greenwashing through misleading “net zero” and carbon-neutral claims. Rather than committing to the deep decarbonization required to meet Paris Agreement goals, these companies rely heavily on questionable offsetting and insetting mechanisms. These practices allow firms to claim neutrality while reducing their actual emissions by only a fraction, effectively continuing their significant climate impact under the guise of environmental responsibility. The core of this deception lies in the reliance on carbon credits, which often lack permanence and suffer from flawed science and imprecise accounting. For instance, offsets based on forest conservation are vulnerable to wildfires, rendering the claimed neutrality invalid when carbon is re-released. Furthermore, many “carbon-neutral” labels apply to a negligible percentage of a company’s total footprint, creating a false sense of completeness. This misleading marketing confuses consumers, who may feel justified in maintaining harmful consumption habits because they believe they are supporting sustainable practices. This article is critically relevant to greenwashing as it exposes how ambiguous terminology obscures the truth and shifts blame onto consumers. The report argues for strict regulatory interventions, particularly by the European Union, to ban vague neutrality claims and prohibit the use of offsets in future performance marketing. By enforcing transparent communication that distinguishes between genuine emission cuts and financial donations to projects, policymakers can prevent companies from using greenwashing to avoid substantive climate action and protect the integrity of the green transition.
