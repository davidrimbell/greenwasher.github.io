Bluewashing, Seafood Health, and Romantic Lobsters

The article critiques how seafood marketing obscures environmental harm, illustrating the concept of "bluewashing" through the controversy over farmed salmon. Despite scientific consensus on the significant ecological damage caused by aquaculture, industry lobbyists aggressively challenged health experts who recommended wild over farmed options. This aggressive push for a revised, more favorable narrative demonstrates how corporations manipulate public perception to align health recommendations with their profit motives, rather than genuine consumer well-being. Relevant to greenwashing, this case highlights the strategic pivot from environmental transparency to nutritional reassurance. The industry succeeded in forcing a reversal in media coverage, effectively silencing concerns about sustainability by emphasizing omega-3 benefits. By dominating the discourse through loud lobbying and funded messaging, they shifted the focus away from the substantial environmental costs, showing how corporations use selective health data to mask their ecological footprint and maintain market share. The article’s relevance lies in exposing the conflict of interest inherent in industry-funded health narratives. It reveals how corporations exploit scientific ambiguity to downplay environmental damage, a tactic analogous to historical greenwashing scandals. Ultimately, it warns consumers that health advice often serves commercial interests, urging skepticism toward mainstream media that fails to investigate corporate funding or the broader sustainability impacts of recommended products.
