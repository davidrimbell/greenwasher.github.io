Fashion’s Greenwashing Problem: How Canadian shoppers might be misled by eco claims - National | Globalnews.ca
The fashion industry is increasingly leveraging vague terms like "eco-conscious" to market products, creating an environment where consumers struggle to distinguish genuine sustainability from deceptive greenwashing. Experts warn that this lack of transparency allows companies to mislead buyers who assume their purchases are environmentally friendly, exploiting the time constraints and complex supply chains that make verification difficult for the average shopper. This practice contributes to significant waste, as synthetic textiles persist in landfills, highlighting the urgent need for clarity in marketing claims. To combat this ambiguity, authentic sustainable brands are prioritizing third-party certifications and radical transparency to build trust and differentiate themselves from opportunistic competitors. Companies investing in rigorous audits demonstrate that true sustainability involves comprehensive social and environmental accountability, rather than just surface-level labeling. However, this commitment often results in higher production costs, emphasizing that genuine ethical fashion requires a shift in consumer behavior toward buying less and valuing quality and longevity over cheap, disposable trends. Regulatory frameworks remain insufficient in Canada, allowing greenwashing to persist despite existing consumer protection laws, while other regions move toward stricter enforcement. The industry faces a growing challenge as some major players adopt "green hushing" to avoid liability, leaving the burden of education on nonprofits and conscious consumers. Ultimately, preventing misleading marketing requires stronger government intervention and public awareness, reinforcing the idea that the most sustainable garment is the one already owned.
