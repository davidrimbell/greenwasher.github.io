Government urged to introduce EPR for renewable energy sector - letsrecycle.com

The renewable energy sector faces a significant risk of greenwashing if it fails to address the environmental consequences of its own infrastructure. As the industry shifts from fuel-intensive to material-intensive systems, it is generating a massive wave of waste from solar panels, wind turbines, and batteries. Without proactive measures, this surge in end-of-life equipment will exacerbate environmental problems, undermining the sector’s core mission to combat climate change and potentially rendering its "green" claims hypocritical. To mitigate this, the report advocates for a transition to circular economy models driven by industry players, investors, and policymakers. This involves designing assets for recyclability, building circular value chains, and establishing appropriate economic conditions. By extending producer responsibility and funding research into recycling technologies, the sector can reduce its reliance on critical raw materials and decrease supply risks. These strategies are essential for decoupling renewable energy growth from increased material consumption. This approach is relevant to greenwashing because it exposes the gap between the clean energy narrative and the physical reality of resource extraction and waste. Ignoring the lifecycle impacts of renewable technology allows companies to claim environmental benefits while shifting negative burdens elsewhere. Implementing circularity ensures that the green transition is genuinely sustainable, preserving the integrity of the sector and preventing it from inadvertently driving new forms of environmental degradation.
