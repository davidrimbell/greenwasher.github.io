Proposed biogas plant branded a ‘greenwashed’ business idea with ‘no benefit’

Proposed biogas plant branded a ‘greenwashed’ business idea with ‘no benefit’

Local officials argue that a proposed anaerobic digestion plant near Haverhill exemplifies greenwashing by masking a profit-driven business scheme with environmental rhetoric. Critics assert the project offers no genuine economic or social advantage to the community, noting that the produced gas will likely be exported rather than used locally. This disconnect between stated ecological goals and actual local impact highlights how renewable energy projects can be misrepresented to secure approval while failing to deliver tangible benefits to residents. The proposal has sparked significant concern regarding increased heavy lorry traffic and severe road safety risks. Opponents fear that sourcing waste from a wide radius will inundate narrow village roads with large vehicles, creating long-term logistical nightmares and endangering motorists. These practical objections underscore the tension between industrial-scale green infrastructure and the immediate quality of life in rural communities, suggesting that the environmental label often obscures disruptive industrial realities. This case is relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how claims of sustainability are used to justify developments that lack genuine local merit. By framing an industrial logistics operation as an environmental solution, developers may exploit public support for green initiatives to bypass scrutiny of their actual economic and social impacts. The unanimous objection by councillors demonstrates the growing skepticism toward projects that prioritize broad renewable energy targets over specific, verifiable community benefits.
