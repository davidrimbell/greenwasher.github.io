Greenwashing
ES EN

This article highlights a formal complaint against Lululemon by Stand.earth, alleging that the brand’s “Be Planet” marketing campaign constitutes greenwashing. Despite claims of avoiding environmental harm, the company’s emissions have nearly doubled since the slogan’s inception, and the majority of its products are derived from non-recyclable fossil fuel materials. The complaint argues that Lululemon’s messaging creates a misleading illusion of sustainability that contradicts its actual environmental footprint. The situation is particularly significant for the greenwashing discourse because it tests emerging legal frameworks against misleading eco-claims. This case is the first under new French regulations targeting greenwashing and serves as a critical test for recent European directives designed to protect consumers from disingenuous corporate claims. Unlike simple lies, which are illegal, greenwashing often relies on vague, positive impressions that fall into a legal grey area, making such regulatory enforcement a pivotal moment for accountability in the fashion industry. Relevant to greenwashing, this case illustrates how companies exploit consumer desires for ethical purchasing through selective disclosure and ambiguous slogans. Experts note that this strategy confuses consumers rather than outright lying, a tactic that is increasingly being addressed by tightening laws in Canada and Europe. The complaint aims to force Lululemon to align its marketing with reality, highlighting the growing tension between corporate sustainability narratives and verifiable environmental actions in a sector responsible for substantial global emissions.
