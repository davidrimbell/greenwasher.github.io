How Fake Eco Labels Trick Shoppers and Hurt Trust

Stories selected and summarised by AI, not reviewed by a person. Every source is linked.

Greenwashing is when companies lie about being green. They want to look more eco-friendly than they really are. This trick affects buyers in two main ways. It can help them, but it can also hurt them.

Companies like greenwashing because it sells. It attracts people who care about the planet. A study from the University of Queensland found that shoppers like green labels. They buy these products even if the labels are not true. This lets companies charge more money. They make more sales without actually changing how they work. For these buyers, it feels like a good choice. They save money and effort while thinking they are helping the earth.

But there is a big downside. Shoppers might waste money on fake green products. These items do not help the environment. Research from the University of Michigan shows a problem. When people find out the truth, they feel betrayed. They stop trusting that brand. They also start doubting all green ads. This makes it hard for honest shoppers to find real sustainability.

Some examples show these risks. The Federal Trade Commission has rules called the “Green Guides”. These rules punish companies that use vague words. For example, a company might call a product “eco-friendly” without explaining why. This shows that not all green ads are true. Shoppers must check for independent certifications. Do not just trust what the company says.

This happens in energy too. A webinar by the Environmental Defense Fund talked about utilities. These power companies sometimes exaggerate their use of renewable energy. This confuses people who want to support clean power. They cannot easily check where their electricity comes from. The result is bad. People might pay for dirty energy. They think they are helping, but they are not.

To stay safe, look for third-party data. Ignore marketing slogans. Look for reports from groups like the Carbon Disclosure Project. These sources are more reliable than self-reported claims. By asking for proof, you avoid being tricked. You also support companies that truly care about the planet. This way, your money helps real change, not just a good image.
