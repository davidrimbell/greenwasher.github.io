MEPs back ruling to ban misleading green claims on products

MEPs back ruling to ban misleading green claims on products

European Parliament members have approved a draft proposal designed to curb the proliferation of greenwashing by banning unsubstantiated general environmental claims. This measure targets vague marketing terms like “eco” or “natural” without detailed evidence, aiming to eliminate misleading practices where companies exaggerate the sustainability of a product or its components. By restricting these generic assertions, the legislation seeks to restore consumer trust and ensure that environmental marketing reflects verifiable facts rather than empty slogans. Beyond advertising transparency, the directive addresses the issue of planned obsolescence by prohibiting design features that artificially limit product durability. Manufacturers will be forbidden from restricting functionality when using third-party spare parts or consumables, forcing them to provide clear repair information and extended guarantee labels. This shift encourages the market toward more durable, repairable goods, ensuring that producers cannot profit from creating items that malfunction prematurely or become unrepairable once initial warranties expire. This initiative is critically relevant to greenwashing as it establishes a legal framework for substantiating ecological claims and enforcing product longevity. It represents a significant step in the EU’s broader circular economy strategy, attempting to hold brands accountable for deceptive environmental messaging. Although some industry groups argue the proposals lack sufficient rigor, the directive aims to standardize sustainability labels and prevent the exploitation of consumer confusion regarding true environmental impact.
