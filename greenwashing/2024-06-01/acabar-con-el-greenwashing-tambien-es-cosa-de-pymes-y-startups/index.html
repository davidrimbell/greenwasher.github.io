Acabar con el ‘greenwashing’ también es cosa de pymes y startups

The new European directive against *greenwashing* aims to protect and empower consumers by promoting more transparent and truthful environmental communication. This response addresses the alarming reality that half of the ecological claims in the market were vague or misleading, creating widespread consumer vulnerability. The regulation establishes a strict framework to combat greenwashing, exempting only micro-enterprises from the legal obligation of verification, though not from reputational liability or potential complaints. Although small businesses avoid the immediate costs of adaptation, the regulation generates a significant ripple effect. Pressure throughout the value chain and the need to manage reputational risk will indirectly compel these businesses to align with sustainability standards. Conversely, small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) and large corporations must integrate these obligations to compete on equal footing, benefiting from a market that rewards genuine impact and penalizes ambiguous claims, thereby enhancing the integrity of the business sector. The relevance to *greenwashing* lies in the prohibition of using unverified labels and the requirement that any environmental claim be backed by independent third parties. The law promotes comprehensive transparency, preventing companies from hiding negative impacts behind compensation campaigns or misleading green imagery. The ultimate goal is to ensure that the information provided enables consumers to make informed decisions, transforming sustainability from a mere marketing tool into a verifiable and ethical commitment.
