Consumers struggle to spot ‘greenwashing’ in ads, says ESRI

A recent study reveals that while educating consumers to spot greenwashing increases their confidence, it paradoxically heightens skepticism toward all environmental claims, including genuine ones. This universal distrust hampers the ability of truly sustainable businesses to compete against those that mislead the public, creating an uneven market where deception often outweighs authentic eco-friendly practices. The findings suggest that consumer education alone is insufficient for combating deceptive marketing because it breeds broad suspicion rather than discernment. Instead of relying on buyer vigilance, the research supports the implementation of strict regulatory frameworks. By shifting the burden of proof to corporations, policy interventions can effectively level the playing field without alienating environmentally conscious shoppers. This article is crucial to the greenwashing discourse as it highlights the failure of self-regulation through education and advocates for legal bans on vague claims. It underscores the urgent need for Ireland to transpose EU directives into law, ensuring that sustainability labels are third-party verified. Ultimately, it demonstrates that combating greenwashing requires structural change to protect both the market integrity and consumer trust in legitimate environmental efforts.
