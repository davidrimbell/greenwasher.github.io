Greenwashing
ES EN

The fossil fuel sector has systematically implemented image-washing strategies, promoting itself as an ally in the fight against climate change through vague language such as “sustainability” and by participating in environmental summits, while its core operations continue to accelerate global warming. This fundamental contradiction reveals that most of their advertising messages aim to conceal the true environmental impact of their business, leveraging concepts like green innovation to divert attention from their reliance on oil and gas. The United Kingdom has set a precedent by banning advertisements from major oil companies, arguing that they mislead the public by downplaying their contribution to the climate crisis. This measure is pushing the European Union to regulate such practices through stricter regulations, following international recommendations that call for legislation against misleading advertising. The decision demonstrates that non-binding statements and superficial commitments are no longer sufficient to legitimize the activities of these companies. The relevance of this article lies in the fact that greenwashing is not merely a marketing tactic, but a critical obstacle to effective climate action, extending also to financial sectors such as banking and insurance. By normalizing and obscuring the reality of the crisis, these companies hinder the transition to clean energy. Therefore, regulating advertising and demanding transparency are indispensable steps to dismantle the disinformation mechanisms that protect polluting interests.
