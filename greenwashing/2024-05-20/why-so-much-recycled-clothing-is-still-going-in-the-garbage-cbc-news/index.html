Why so much recycled clothing is still going in the garbage | CBC News

Major retail brands like H&M, Nike, and Zara are frequently misleading consumers through vague sustainability claims, particularly regarding recycled materials. Investigations reveal that a significant portion of these "green" labels are unsubstantiated, with products often being predominantly made from virgin plastics or fossil fuels. This disconnect between marketing narratives and actual product composition suggests that companies are prioritizing image over genuine environmental impact. The reliance on recycled polyester is highlighted as a deceptive strategy that encourages continued overconsumption. Experts warn that using plastic bottles for clothing diverts them from closed-loop recycling systems, resulting in items that ultimately end up in landfills. This approach creates a false sense of circularity, leading consumers to believe they are making eco-friendly choices while supporting a linear economy of waste that lacks scalable, true textile-to-textile recycling technologies. This phenomenon is relevant to greenwashing because it demonstrates how companies exploit consumer goodwill with ambiguous terms like "conscious" or "natural-looking" to distract from substantial environmental harm. By focusing attention on minor sustainable attributes while ignoring the overall toxicity and waste generated by their products, retailers manipulate buyers into paying premiums for items that are not truly sustainable. This practice not only deceives shoppers but also undermines brands that are making authentic, transparent efforts toward sustainability.
