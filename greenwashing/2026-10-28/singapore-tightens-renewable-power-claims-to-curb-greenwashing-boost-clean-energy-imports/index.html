Singapore tightens renewable power claims to curb greenwashing, boost clean energy imports

Singapore has revised its renewable energy certificate standards to align corporate sustainability claims with electricity physically supplied to the national grid. This update ensures that businesses can only assert their consumption is renewable if backed by certificates linked to actual power imports or domestic generation. By distinguishing between local supply and general support for overseas projects, the new framework aims to eliminate ambiguity in sustainability reporting. The primary implication of this policy shift is a significant reduction in greenwashing risks. By enforcing stricter verification for claims regarding electricity consumption, the government strengthens trust in corporate environmental disclosures. This clarity allows businesses to confidently support cross-border clean energy projects, potentially improving the commercial viability of import initiatives while preventing companies from overstating their environmental impact through vague or unverified renewable energy assertions. This regulatory tightening supports Singapore’s broader strategy of increasing regional electricity imports and expanding domestic solar deployment. While local solar capacity is growing, it remains insufficient for long-term energy needs, making reliable, verifiable clean imports essential. The updated standards facilitate this transition by ensuring that the renewable energy landscape remains transparent and credible, thereby encouraging greater investment in genuine clean infrastructure.
