Greenwashing
ES EN

The European Commission has introduced strict regulations to combat the pervasive issue of greenwashing, where vague environmental claims mislead consumers into believing products are more sustainable than they truly are. By requiring all green claims to be backed by scientific evidence and verified by independent bodies, the new rules aim to eliminate unsubstantiated marketing tactics. This shift ensures that consumers can confidently distinguish between genuine environmental efforts and deceptive practices, thereby fostering a more transparent market. A critical aspect of this crackdown is the regulation of carbon offsetting and the concept of "carbon neutral" labels, which remain contentious despite new disclosure requirements. Critics argue that allowing companies to purchase carbon credits rather than reducing emissions perpetuates confusion and serves as a greenwashing tool, as many consumers struggle to understand the difference between offsetting and actual reduction. Consequently, while the new framework mandates clearer explanations of these mechanisms, it highlights the ongoing tension between corporate compensation strategies and the need for authentic, measurable climate action. Beyond labeling, the proposal strengthens consumer rights through "right to repair" mandates, compelling manufacturers to offer repairs for durable goods like electronics and appliances for up to a decade. This initiative challenges the throwaway culture by prioritizing product longevity over replacement, directly addressing the environmental impact of waste. Ultimately, these measures represent a significant step toward holding corporations accountable, ensuring that sustainability claims are not just marketing buzzwords but are grounded in verifiable, impactful ecological responsibility.
