Greenwashing
ES EN

Sustainability-marketed consumer goods are experiencing significantly higher growth rates than conventional products, yet this demand is often exploited by brands employing misleading marketing tactics. A substantial portion of these claims are deceptive, creating a false perception of environmental benefit that exceeds reality. This practice not only erodes consumer trust but also exposes companies to severe legal and reputational risks as scrutiny from regulators and consumers intensifies. Greenwashing frequently manifests through hidden trade-offs and vague aspirational goals rather than transparent actions. For instance, packaging labeled as biocompostable often lacks the necessary industrial infrastructure to break down, misleading consumers into believing they are making eco-friendly choices. Similarly, companies may publicize long-term net-zero targets without detailing immediate, credible plans to achieve them, thereby capitalizing on consumer goodwill without delivering tangible environmental improvements. To combat this, brands must shift from superficial labeling to rigorous, data-backed ingredient assessment. Genuine sustainability requires understanding the full lifecycle impact of sourcing decisions, such as comparing the carbon and water footprints of different raw materials. By substantiating claims with verifiable data and focusing on ingredient-level changes rather than packaging aesthetics, companies can avoid greenwashing pitfalls and communicate authentic value to informed stakeholders.
