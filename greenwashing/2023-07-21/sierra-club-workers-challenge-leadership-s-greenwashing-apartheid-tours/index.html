Sierra Club Workers Challenge Leadership's Greenwashing ‘Apartheid Tours’

Sierra Club workers have passed a resolution demanding the cancellation of educational tours to Israel, arguing that these trips constitute greenwashing by whitewashing apartheid and environmental destruction in Palestine. The union asserts that continuing to partner with Israeli entities, including those involved in settler expansion and ecological harm, contradicts the organization’s stated values of racial and Indigenous justice, regardless of superficial attempts to include Palestinian voices in the itinerary. The article highlights the deep, structural links between colonialism in Palestine and settler colonialism in the United States, connecting the struggles of Palestinian and Native American peoples. It critiques the Sierra Club leadership for ignoring these intersections, noting that environmental degradation caused by Israeli policies mirrors the exploitation of Indigenous lands in the U.S., such as water contamination and land dispossession. This situation is relevant to greenwashing because it exposes how environmental organizations can legitimize oppressive regimes by framing political and military actions as neutral or beneficial ecological initiatives. By offering curated tours that ignore systemic human rights violations, the Sierra Club risks using an eco-friendly veneer to obscure its complicity in colonial structures, thereby misleading the public about the true nature of its operations and ethical commitments.
