How to identify a sustainable fashion brand and avoid “greenwashing”?

The article warns against the manipulation of sustainable language in the fashion industry, pointing out that terms like “eco-friendly” hold no value if they are not backed by verifiable data. The author emphasizes that consumers must scrutinize garment labels and demand concrete evidence from brands regarding their progress. True sustainability is grounded in transparency and the ability to demonstrate measurable results, rejecting empty promises that seek to mask a lack of genuine commitment to the environment. The article criticizes the partial assessment of corporate impact, where a brand may have good labor practices or use recycled materials while maintaining an overproduction model and generating excessive waste. Coherence between rhetoric and business model is crucial; promoting responsible consumption while simultaneously encouraging constant collection turnover represents a fundamental contradiction. Ignoring these broader aspects allows companies to hide structural inefficiencies behind marginal improvements in efficiency that do not reduce the overall environmental footprint. This analysis is vital for understanding greenwashing, as it exposes how marketing uses eco-friendly appearances to divert attention from unsustainable practices embedded in the business. Its relevance lies in empowering consumers to distinguish between communication strategies and genuine corporate conviction, demanding that sustainability be demonstrated through real decisions and operational changes. By prioritizing transparency over perfection, we can combat misinformation and foster a truly responsible industry, where critical evaluation helps avoid being misled by deceptive labels.
