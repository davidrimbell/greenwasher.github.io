European carbon market: EU agrees to expand reserve to curb sharp price spikes

The European Union has agreed to ease its carbon market to protect industrial competitiveness amid price volatility. This measure aims to balance economic stability with climate objectives by allowing companies to accumulate larger reserves of emission allowances. The adjustment acts as an immediate containment mechanism, preventing sharp price spikes that could harm local industry while a more comprehensive structural reform of the ETS system is negotiated. The relevance of this agreement in the context of greenwashing lies in the risk that corporations may exploit these regulatory exceptions to project an image of environmental responsibility without implementing real changes. By facilitating access to pollution permits, a misleading narrative could emerge in which commercial success is falsely associated with sustainability, masking inertia toward effective decarbonization under the cover of seemingly flexible regulations. Finally, although emissions have decreased significantly since 2005, the tension between competitiveness and ecology exposes the vulnerability of market-based mechanisms. This debate is crucial for understanding how political concessions can serve as tools for greenwashing, where climate rhetoric persists while economic structures allow for continued carbon generation, distorting public perception of genuine environmental commitment.
