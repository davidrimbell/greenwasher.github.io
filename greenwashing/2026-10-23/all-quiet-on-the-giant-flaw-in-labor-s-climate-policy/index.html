All quiet on the giant flaw in Labor's climate policy

Independent experts widely agree that Australia’s carbon credit system lacks credibility, with short-term units often failing to ensure permanent carbon removal. The government’s primary abatement policy, the safeguard mechanism, allows heavy polluters to offset emissions using these questionable credits rather than reducing them directly. This reliance enables corporations to maintain high emission levels by purchasing insufficient units, some of which may represent fictional or easily reversible sequestration, thereby undermining genuine climate action. Multiple stakeholders, including climate groups and policy institutes, argue that this over-reliance on offsets disincentivizes actual operational changes and sustains emissions-intensive activities. They propose caps or discounts on credit usage, emphasizing that nature-based solutions carry significant risks regarding permanence and measurement. Critics contend that offsets should serve only as a last resort for residual emissions, not as a primary, cost-effective escape route for industries to avoid investing in necessary technological upgrades or structural decarbonization. The Productivity Commission’s recent recommendation against restricting credit use ignores these integrity concerns, prioritizing low costs for businesses over scientific rigor. This approach exacerbates greenwashing by allowing polluters to appear compliant while continuing harmful practices under the guise of purchasing temporary environmental benefits. The article highlights how policy frameworks can be manipulated to create an illusion of sustainability, masking the reality that no actual reduction in greenhouse gas emissions has occurred.
