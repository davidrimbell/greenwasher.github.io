Consumidores y productores aplauden la ley de la UE contra el 'ecopostureo' si ayuda a una compra más responsable

The European Union’s new legislation against greenwashing represents a pivotal step in protecting consumers from misleading environmental claims by banning vague labels such as “eco” or “natural” unless they are backed by proof. This regulatory shift is crucial because it directly targets the deceptive marketing tactics that have long enabled companies to falsely present their products as sustainable, thereby ensuring that environmental assertions are substantiated by recognized certifications and transparent evidence rather than mere buzzwords. Beyond labeling, the article highlights a critical nuance in the sustainability discourse: the potential contradiction of consuming certified organic products that have been transported over long distances, which creates a significant carbon footprint. This insight reveals a deeper layer of greenwashing, where the “green” label of the product itself overshadows the environmental damage caused by its logistics. It calls for a more holistic view of sustainability that accounts for origin and transportation impacts, not just production methods. The relevance to greenwashing is further underscored by the crackdown on unregulated trends such as “regenerative agriculture,” which companies exploit due to the absence of standardized definitions. By prohibiting unsubstantiated durability claims and practices that lead to early obsolescence, the law not only combats fraud but also challenges the systemic manipulation of consumer perception. This legislative framework compels corporations to align their marketing with genuine environmental performance, dismantling the opacity that facilitates eco-posturing and enabling truly informed consumer choices.
