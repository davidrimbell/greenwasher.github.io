Asheville-based artist highlights pesticide companies’ greenwashing in new Charlotte installation

Asheville-based artist highlights pesticide companies’ greenwashing in new Charlotte installation

Kirsten Stolle’s exhibition “Under the Influence” exposes the deceptive corporate practices that define greenwashing, framing it as an environmental façade masking harmful realities. By recontextualizing historical propaganda from agrochemical giants like Monsanto and Bayer, the artwork reveals how these entities sanitize their public image through meticulous branding and rhetorical manipulation. This artistic intervention serves as a critical lens for understanding how corporations use aesthetic appeal to obscure their lack of genuine environmental responsibility, allowing them to evade public scrutiny and maintain consumer trust despite their damaging practices. The installation connects historical corporate strategies to contemporary health crises, illustrating the tangible consequences of such deception. Through visual collages linking pesticide advertisements to modern litigation regarding toxic chemicals, Stolle highlights the enduring legacy of harmful products. This narrative demonstrates that greenwashing is not merely a marketing tactic but a mechanism that facilitates the continued sale of dangerous substances, directly impacting public health through issues like Parkinson’s disease. The art forces viewers to confront the direct line between corporate branding decisions and real-world human suffering, emphasizing the severity of ignoring environmental truths for profit. Finally, the work critiques the legal and political systems that enable this impunity, showing how greenwashing extends into legislative lobbying. By displaying documents that reveal corporate efforts to secure immunity from lawsuits, the exhibition underscores how companies manipulate policy to avoid accountability for environmental harm. This relevance to greenwashing lies in exposing the structural support that allows deceptive practices to persist, moving beyond individual corporate lies to systemic failure. The artwork challenges the audience to recognize that true sustainability requires dismantling these protective legal frameworks rather than accepting superficial ecological branding.
