Greenwashing
EN ES

The article examines the phenomenon of “greenhushing,” in which companies conceal their sustainability achievements out of fear of being accused of greenwashing or facing excessive regulatory scrutiny. This fear of public backlash leads many brands to silence their genuine efforts, creating a significant risk to their reputation and market value by failing to capitalize on the growing consumer demand for sustainable practices. The relevance of this issue lies in the disconnect between actual performance and public perception, which allows brands that actively communicate their initiatives to gain substantial competitive advantages. Brands that choose silence are missing out on enormous potential for value enhancement, while those that successfully balance communication with action demonstrate that transparency in sustainability can drive sales growth and strengthen customer loyalty, particularly in sectors where brand image plays a differentiating role. Finally, the text emphasizes that the impact of sustainability varies by sector, being more critical in luxury fashion and technology than in basic goods. The key lesson for companies is that, although the political and regulatory climate may encourage discretion, the cost of not communicating ESG (environmental, social, and governance) progress outweighs the risk of criticism, as consumers economically reward and enhance the perceived brand value of organizations that demonstrate genuine and visible commitment.
