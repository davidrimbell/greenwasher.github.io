Greenwashing
EN ES

The article highlights a critical intersection between aggressive technological expansion and unverified safety assurances, a core dynamic in modern greenwashing. Major corporations and AI firms are securing massive funding and launching ambitious products while relying on voluntary, self-regulated safety audits. This lack of enforceable accountability creates a facade of responsibility, allowing companies to market innovation as inherently safe without genuine oversight, thereby misleading stakeholders about the actual risks involved. Furthermore, significant financial losses and shelved product releases reveal the gap between corporate ambition and operational reality. By highlighting failed ventures and sudden delays alongside boasts of technological leadership, firms construct a narrative of progress that obscures underlying instability. This selective reporting serves as a form of "progress washing," where the potential for future success is used to justify current investments and mask present failures or ethical shortcuts, distorting the true state of the industry. Ultimately, this environment fosters consumer and investor complacency through the illusion of control. When safety measures are non-binding and setbacks are framed as strategic pauses rather than systemic failures, organizations evade scrutiny. This is relevant to greenwashing as it demonstrates how entities can claim environmental or safety responsibility through superficial commitments, while the substantive actions—such as leveraging risky technologies or ignoring proven hazards—remain unchecked and unverified.
