Acabar con el ‘greenwashing’ también es cosa de pymes y startups

Acabar con el ‘greenwashing’ también es cosa de pymes y startups

The approval of the new European Directive marks a turning point in the fight against greenwashing, establishing clear rules to protect consumers from misleading environmental claims. Its primary objective is to ensure that communications regarding ecological performance are truthful and clear, responding to the reality that more than half of the studied claims lacked a factual basis. This empowers consumers to make informed decisions, transforming their role into a key driver of the ecological transition. Although micro-enterprises are exempt from the formal obligation of verification, they are not free from reputational risks or the ripple effects extending to their supply chains. For other small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) and large companies, this regulation represents an opportunity to compete on a level playing field. Those that genuinely integrate sustainability into their business models will be rewarded, while those relying on vague or false messages will face sanctions and a loss of market trust. The article’s significance lies in its emphasis on radical transparency and independent verification as anti-greenwashing tools. It warns against practices such as the use of uncertified labels, an exclusive focus on offsets without actual emission reductions, or the omission of negative impacts throughout the value chain. Merely complying with the regulation is not enough; companies must avoid “image washing” through vague language or misleading aesthetics, prioritizing honesty and data accuracy to build genuine credibility.
