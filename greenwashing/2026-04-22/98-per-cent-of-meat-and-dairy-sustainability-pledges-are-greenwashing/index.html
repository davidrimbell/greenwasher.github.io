98 per cent of meat and dairy sustainability pledges are greenwashing

The dairy and meat industries are heavily accused of greenwashing, with nearly all environmental claims analyzed deemed deceptive. This scrutiny is vital because animal agriculture drives significant global emissions, yet major corporations respond with vague promises rather than concrete action. The analysis reveals that most pledges lack supporting evidence or rely on distant net-zero targets and carbon offsets, effectively masking the true scale of their environmental impact while maintaining business-as-usual practices. Most statements offered no proof, with only a handful backed by scientific literature. While many firms have set net-zero goals, these often prioritize superficial marketing over substantial emission reductions. Consequently, the industry’s sustainability efforts are exposed as largely performative, designed to create an illusion of progress without addressing the root causes of their climate contribution, thereby misleading consumers and stakeholders about the reality of their ecological footprint. This article highlights how powerful actors use misinformation to protect their status quo, similar to tactics seen in tobacco and fossil fuel sectors. It underscores the urgent need for transparency and accountability in corporate environmental reporting. Understanding this pattern is crucial for consumers and regulators to distinguish genuine sustainability initiatives from strategic PR, ensuring that climate actions result in measurable reductions rather than mere window dressing.
