98 per cent of meat and dairy sustainability pledges are greenwashing
The dairy and meat industries are heavily accused of greenwashing, with nearly all environmental claims analyzed deemed deceptive. This scrutiny is vital because animal agriculture drives significant global emissions, yet major corporations respond with vague promises rather than concrete action. The analysis reveals that most pledges lack supporting evidence or rely on distant net-zero targets and carbon offsets, effectively masking the true scale of their environmental impact while maintaining business-as-usual practices. Most statements offered no proof, with only a handful backed by scientific literature. While many firms have set net-zero goals, these often prioritize superficial marketing over substantial emission reductions. Consequently, the industry’s sustainability efforts are exposed as largely performative, designed to create an illusion of progress without addressing the root causes of their climate contribution, thereby misleading consumers and stakeholders about the reality of their ecological footprint. This article highlights how powerful actors use misinformation to protect their status quo, similar to tactics seen in tobacco and fossil fuel sectors. It underscores the urgent need for transparency and accountability in corporate environmental reporting. Understanding this pattern is crucial for consumers and regulators to distinguish genuine sustainability initiatives from strategic PR, ensuring that climate actions result in measurable reductions rather than mere window dressing.

Consumers urged to scrutinise greenwashing skincare product claims
Consumers are urged to scrutinize skincare claims as greenwashing intensifies alongside growing demand for non-toxic goods. The core deception involves brands marketing products as environmentally friendly or natural while containing minimal sustainable ingredients and significant harmful chemicals. This discrepancy misleads conscious buyers who expect ethical composition, thereby undermining genuine sustainability efforts and perpetuating ecological damage through toxic runoff that harms local waterways and ecosystems. The relevance of this issue to greenwashing lies in how it exploits consumer trust for profit without delivering environmental benefits. By highlighting only select plant-based components, companies create a false narrative of ecological responsibility. This tactic obscures the reality that the majority of the product remains potentially hazardous, forcing consumers to do extensive research to avoid supporting businesses that prioritize marketing over actual planetary health. Experts emphasize that individual choices must align with broader systemic shifts toward renewable energy and waste reduction. The warning serves as a critical reminder that superficial branding cannot replace substantive action in protecting natural resources. Ultimately, combating greenwashing requires holding corporations accountable for transparent ingredient listing and supporting policies that prioritize genuine sustainability over performative environmentalism, ensuring that consumer power drives real ecological preservation rather than deceptive marketing.
