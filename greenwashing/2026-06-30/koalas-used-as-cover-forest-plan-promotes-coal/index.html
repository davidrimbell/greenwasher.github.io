Koalas Used as Cover: Forest Plan Promotes Coal

The Federal Government’s proposed carbon credits method for native forests enables fossil fuel companies to greenwash their climate pollution. By allowing emissions from new coal and gas projects to be offset by forest protection, the scheme creates a false equivalence, suggesting that saving trees justifies continued expansion of high-emission industries. This approach fundamentally contradicts scientific consensus, which demands the immediate cessation of both fossil fuel approval and forest destruction. Research indicates strong public skepticism, with a majority of Australians viewing such carbon offsets as greenwash. Citizens recognize that these mechanisms help polluters appear environmentally responsible without actually reducing emissions. There is significant support for directly stopping new fossil fuel projects, reflecting a broader rejection of strategies that treat climate action as a trade-off rather than an urgent necessity. This article is relevant to greenwashing because it exposes how complex carbon accounting can be manipulated to preserve fossil fuel infrastructure under the guise of environmental stewardship. It highlights the risk of using nature-based solutions as loopholes for continuing business-as-usual emissions, thereby undermining genuine climate mitigation efforts while misleading the public and stakeholders.
