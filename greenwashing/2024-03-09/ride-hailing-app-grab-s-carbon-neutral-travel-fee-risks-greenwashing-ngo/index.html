Ride-hailing app Grab's 'carbon neutral' travel fee risks greenwashing: NGO

The article examines Grab’s customer-facing carbon offsetting initiative, which allows users to neutralize travel emissions for a small fee by purchasing credits from the Katingan Mentaya Project. While Grab defends the integrity of these credits through third-party ratings and exchanges, the specific project faces severe criticism regarding its "additionality." Investigations reveal that the forest protection was already legally mandated, meaning the credited emissions reductions did not actually occur due to the project, raising serious doubts about the validity of the offsets sold to consumers. This case highlights a critical gap in greenwashing, where companies may market environmental neutrality based on flawed or ineffective carbon credits. The discrepancy between Grab’s claim of verified impact and the independent findings that no real emissions were prevented illustrates the risk of misleading consumers. By offering a seemingly simple, low-cost method to appear carbon-neutral, the platform potentially obscures the lack of tangible climate action, relying on credits that experts argue lack credibility and do not represent genuine reductions in greenhouse gases. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the broader industry trend of using questionable offsets to claim net-zero status. Critics warn that such practices distract from the urgent need for actual decarbonization, such as electrifying fleets, and violate emerging global standards that prohibit using low-integrity credits for neutrality claims. As regulatory bodies and consumer advocates increasingly scrutinize these mechanisms, companies relying on them risk reputational damage and legal challenges for misrepresenting their environmental footprint to the public.
