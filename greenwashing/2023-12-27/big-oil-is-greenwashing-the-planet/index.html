Big Oil Is Greenwashing the Planet

The fossil fuel industry, exemplified by the UAE’s leadership of COP28, employs strategic greenwashing to maintain public support while aggressively expanding oil and gas production. By appointing a CEO of a major national petroleum company to head climate negotiations, the summit highlighted how oil-producing nations manipulate global forums to appear committed to sustainability. This allows them to dilute urgent calls for a complete fossil fuel phase-out, instead promoting vague "transitions" that enable continued extraction and profit maximization despite scientific warnings about catastrophic climate impacts. Major corporations like ExxonMobil and Shell engage in systematic deception by promoting unviable technologies, such as algae biofuels and carbon capture, through misleading advertising campaigns. These initiatives are largely symbolic, designed to create an illusion of environmental responsibility while the companies invest minimally in actual clean energy alternatives. Legal actions, such as lawsuits brought by Massachusetts, argue that these practices constitute deceptive marketing, as firms highlight negligible green investments while simultaneously ramping up fossil fuel development and obscuring their lack of immediate plans to decarbonize their portfolios. The persistence of these greenwashing tactics delays the necessary global shift toward renewable energy, exacerbating the climate crisis. By legitimizing harmful technologies and obscuring their role in pollution, oil companies hinder effective policy-making and public understanding. Ultimately, the article warns that allowing these bad actors to control the narrative not only profits the industry but threatens global stability, as the refusal to rapidly abandon fossil fuels leads to increasingly unlivable temperatures and environmental chaos worldwide.
