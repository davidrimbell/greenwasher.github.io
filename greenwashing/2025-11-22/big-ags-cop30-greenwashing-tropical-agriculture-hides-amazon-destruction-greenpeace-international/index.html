Big Ag’s COP30 greenwashing: ‘Tropical Agriculture’ hides Amazon destruction - Greenpeace International

Big Ag’s COP30 greenwashing: ‘Tropical Agriculture’ hides Amazon destruction - Greenpeace International

Industrial agriculture is aggressively lobbying at COP30 to reframe food systems as part of the climate solution, despite being responsible for roughly one-third of global emissions. Major corporations are utilizing sophisticated greenwashing tactics, such as promoting “climate-smart” narratives and unverified carbon-negative claims, to divert attention from their central role in deforestation and pollution. This strategy aims to convince policymakers that business-as-usual practices, like livestock expansion, can mitigate global heating without requiring systemic change. The article highlights how companies like JBS exploit the concept of regenerative agriculture to mask their environmental footprint, suggesting that soil carbon sequestration can offset potent methane emissions. However, scientific evidence indicates that soil has a limited capacity for carbon storage and cannot counterbalance ongoing livestock emissions. Furthermore, the lack of transparent, peer-reviewed data from corporate-sponsored studies raises serious doubts about these “carbon-negative” assertions, revealing them as marketing tools designed to sell carbon credits rather than achieve genuine climate mitigation. This disconnect between corporate marketing and scientific reality underscores the urgency of recognizing greenwashing as a barrier to effective climate policy. True progress requires rejecting these misleading narratives in favor of binding targets to halt deforestation and transition toward agroecology. By exposing these tactics, the article urges governments to prioritize Indigenous leadership and enforce strict regulations, ensuring that climate decisions are driven by ecological integrity rather than the profit motives of big agribusiness.
