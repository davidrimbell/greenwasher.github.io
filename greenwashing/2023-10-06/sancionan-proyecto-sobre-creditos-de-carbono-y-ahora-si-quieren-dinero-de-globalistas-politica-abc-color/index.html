Sancionan proyecto sobre “créditos de carbono” y ahora sí quieren dinero de “globalistas” - Política - ABC Color

Sancionan proyecto sobre “créditos de carbono” y ahora sí quieren dinero de “globalistas” - Política - ABC Color

Paraguay has approved a controversial law regulating the carbon credit market, allowing multinational polluters to offset their emissions by funding forest preservation projects, particularly in countries like Paraguay. This initiative establishes a state-managed registry and sets minimum operational conditions, including registration fees for private actors while exempting indigenous communities. The legislation aims to formalize voluntary environmental compensation mechanisms, creating a structured framework for international financial flows tied to ecological sustainability. The approval process sparked significant debate regarding transparency, indigenous rights, and the influence of foreign entities. Opponents criticized the lack of rigorous consultation with local populations and questioned the role of international certification bodies, which verify that projects genuinely counteract pollution. Concerns were raised about potential human rights violations and the dominance of non-governmental organizations and global corporations in administering these funds, suggesting that the benefits might not fully reach local communities or guarantee true environmental integrity. This situation is relevant to greenwashing because it highlights the risks of commodifying nature without adequate oversight. When complex ecological impacts are reduced to financial transactions verified by external actors, there is a danger that polluters can buy their way out of responsibility rather than reducing actual emissions. The law’s passage, despite warnings about opacity and external control, illustrates how regulatory frameworks can potentially facilitate superficial environmental accounting, allowing corporations to maintain their carbon-intensive operations under the guise of supporting conservation efforts abroad.
