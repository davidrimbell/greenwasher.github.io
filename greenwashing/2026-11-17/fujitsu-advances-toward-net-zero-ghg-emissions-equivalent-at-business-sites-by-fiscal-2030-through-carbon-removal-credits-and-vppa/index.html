Fujitsu advances toward net-zero GHG emissions equivalent at business sites by fiscal 2030 through carbon removal credits and VPPA

Fujitsu has announced a strategic pivot to achieve net-zero greenhouse gas emissions at its business sites by 2030, primarily by offsetting hard-to-abate Scope 1 emissions with nature-based carbon removal credits. This move highlights a growing industry trend where companies rely on offsets to bridge the gap between current operational realities and ambitious climate targets, rather than eliminating emissions entirely through immediate infrastructure changes. For Scope 2 emissions, the company will utilize Virtual Power Purchase Agreements to procure renewable energy with "additionality," ensuring its purchases directly support new solar capacity. While this promotes renewable development, it also shifts focus away from direct physical energy transition, potentially allowing corporations to claim clean energy status without necessarily changing their immediate consumption patterns or grid dependencies. This case is relevant to greenwashing concerns because it demonstrates how firms may use financial instruments like carbon credits and long-term contracts to achieve "net-zero equivalent" status on paper. Critics argue that such strategies can mask continued fossil fuel use and delay genuine decarbonization, raising questions about whether the environmental benefit is substantive or merely a reputational accounting exercise.
