Brazilian Tech’s Green Claims Are Misleading

The Brazilian tech industry has a big problem. It is not being honest about its impact on the environment. New data shows that 85% of green claims by these companies are misleading. This is just a form of fake marketing.

Company leaders care more about quick money and new tech like Artificial Intelligence and the metaverse. They do not care about real sustainability. These groups use eco-marketing to look clean and green. But their actual harm to the planet is growing.

This confuses shoppers. It is hard to tell the difference between a cool brand name and real action. You see words about being natural and renewable. But there are no real promises to protect resources. This lack of proof hurts public trust. Companies do not use strict, transparent certifications to back up their claims.

Look at the cosmetics industry. Those companies are more open and accountable. The tech sector looks bad by comparison. This shows how dangerous the current behavior is.

Shoppers lose time checking products based on lies. They might support industries that hurt the climate for short-term profit. This hurts the whole tech sector’s reputation.

There is a better way. Informed users can demand real proof. When shoppers ask for evidence instead of clever ads, they force change. This pressure makes companies focus on real results, not just show. It creates a better market for biodegradable and carbon-neutral solutions. We must stay alert. Progress should not cost the planet.


The article reveals that the Brazilian technology sector has experienced a significant regression in its environmental commitment, with 85% of its products’ green claims constituting cases of greenwashing. Far from advancing sustainability, the industry has increased deceptive practices, using eco-marketing as a mere sales tool while its verified certifications have plummeted. This trend demonstrates a profound disconnect between corporate promises and operational reality, prioritizing economic growth over climate responsibility. This situation is explained by a “conservative shift” within the sector, driven by global political changes and internal corporate priorities that view environmental agendas as obstacles to innovation. Business leaders have relegated social and ecological impact in favor of technologies such as the metaverse and artificial intelligence, sending a clear message that planetary stewardship can be sacrificed for performance. This stands in stark contrast to other sectors, such as cosmetics and toys, which have strengthened their transparency and environmental certifications due to consumer demands. The text’s relevance to greenwashing lies in showing how the lack of oversight and pressure for immediate growth facilitate corporate “makeup” in key industries. The analysis concludes that this behavior is not merely a marketing strategy but an ethical failure that erodes public trust. By building a digital future on unstable foundations and false promises, the technology sector risks its legitimacy and misses the opportunity to be a crucial transformative agent in addressing the climate crisis, opting instead for the comfort of deception rather than genuine action.
