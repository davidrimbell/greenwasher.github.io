Anuario Greenwashing 2025 revela cómo muta el ecopostureo global

The 2025 Greenwashing Annual Report highlights a disturbing shift in corporate environmental deception, driven by the erosion of global climate governance and the retreat of European regulations. This regulatory vacuum, combined with rising climate denialism, has allowed companies to mutate their practices from overt product-level falsehoods to more subtle narratives around ESG investments and institutional marketing. Consequently, consumers are increasingly misled by strategic communications that lack the transparency and accountability previously sought through stricter environmental reporting standards. Despite the decline in regulatory enforcement and public debate, greenwashing persists by adopting new terminology and distancing itself from controversial concepts like ESG. Corporations are reframing their sustainability efforts around risk and resilience, creating an opaque environment where green claims become harder to verify. This evolution exploits the absence of consensus certifications and robust legal frameworks, such as the delayed implementation of Spain’s Sustainable Consumption Law, leaving consumers vulnerable to sophisticated forms of "eco-posturing" that mimic genuine commitment without delivering substantive action. This analysis is crucial for understanding greenwashing because it reveals how the practice adapts to political and regulatory setbacks, becoming more entrenched and deceptive. By identifying these new tactics, such as the manipulation of language and the reliance on unverified corporate narratives, stakeholders can better recognize the gap between marketing and reality. Understanding this mutation is essential for advocating for stronger transparency laws and protecting consumers from narratives that prioritize brand reputation over actual environmental stewardship.
