Companies with more women in the boardroom are less prone to greenwashing: study

Research highlights a critical link between boardroom gender diversity and corporate transparency, revealing that companies with more women on their boards are significantly less likely to engage in greenwashing. Female directors tend to promote honest disclosures and ethical decision-making, effectively reducing the gap between a firm’s stated environmental commitments and its actual sustainability performance. This dynamic is particularly pronounced in controversial industries, suggesting that diverse leadership acts as a crucial check against managerial opportunism and deceptive practices. The implications of these findings underscore the urgent need for regulatory intervention to combat unpoliced greenwashing globally. Experts argue that mandatory quotas for women on corporate boards are essential to disrupt groupthink, enhance long-term strategic thinking, and ensure genuine contributions to climate goals. By mandating gender diversity, policymakers can leverage the proven tendency of women to prioritize sustainability and ethical governance, thereby forcing corporations to align their public narratives with their operational realities rather than relying on superficial marketing. However, cultural factors such as national religiosity can mitigate the positive impact of female directors, as traditional gender roles in more religious societies may limit women’s influence within corporate structures. This complexity emphasizes that legal mandates must be part of a broader effort to shift cultural norms and enforce accountability. Ultimately, addressing greenwashing requires not only stricter penalties for misleading claims but also proactive measures to ensure diverse leadership is empowered to drive authentic environmental stewardship across all sectors and regions.
