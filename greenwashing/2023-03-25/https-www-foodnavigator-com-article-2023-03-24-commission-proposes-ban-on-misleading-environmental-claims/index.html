Greenwashing
ES EN

The European Commission has proposed strict criteria to combat the pervasive issue of greenwashing, where companies make vague or unfounded environmental claims that mislead consumers. This regulatory move aims to restore trust in "green" marketing by legally binding firms to substantiate their assertions with robust scientific evidence before they are communicated to the public. By mandating independent verification and full transparency regarding environmental impacts, the proposal seeks to eliminate the ambiguity that currently allows misleading labels to flourish. This legislation is directly relevant to greenwashing because it addresses the systemic lack of evidence behind popular eco-claims, which disproportionately harms companies committed to genuine sustainability. The proposed rules force businesses to provide a complete picture of their products' lifecycle, banning the practice of highlighting minor sustainable features while ignoring broader environmental trade-offs. This ensures that "green" labels reflect actual performance rather than marketing tactics, creating a level playing field where honest sustainability is rewarded and deception is penalized. However, critics argue the measures do not go far enough, particularly regarding carbon offsetting and climate-neutral claims. While the proposal requires distinction between emission reductions and offsets, consumer advocates call for stricter bans on these practices to prevent "climate-washing." The effectiveness of this anti-greenwashing framework ultimately depends on rigorous enforcement and uniform interpretation across member states, ensuring that the new standards genuinely protect consumers and the environment rather than just shifting the burden of proof.
