Explainer: Lululemon probe thrusts green claims into spotlight

Stand.earth has accused Lululemon of greenwashing, alleging that its "Be Planet" marketing campaign misleadingly portrays the brand as environmentally positive while its operations simultaneously double carbon emissions. The advocacy group argues that the company’s reliance on fossil-fuel-derived materials and coal-powered manufacturing in its supply chain directly contradicts its public promises to restore a healthy planet, prioritizing profit over ecological responsibility. This discrepancy has triggered a formal investigation by the Canadian Competition Bureau to determine if Lululemon has made false or unsubstantiated environmental representations. If found guilty, the company could face significant financial penalties and be forced to modify its marketing strategies. This case highlights the growing regulatory scrutiny facing fashion brands that make broad sustainability claims without transparent, verifiable evidence to support them. The article underscores the widespread nature of greenwashing in the apparel industry, where tools like sustainability indices often fail to capture the full environmental impact of finished garments. It emphasizes that mere partnerships with certification bodies are insufficient; brands must ensure complete supply chain visibility. This incident serves as a critical warning that consumers and regulators are increasingly demanding accuracy in green claims, challenging the industry’s tendency to mask harmful practices behind vague wellness and sustainability slogans.
