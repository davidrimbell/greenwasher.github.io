Greenwashing
ES EN

Objetivo: Frenar a los ecoimpostores

The proliferation of environmental labels on consumer products is often an empty marketing ploy designed to capitalize on ecological concerns without offering real guarantees. This expansion of *greenwashing* has generated consumer distrust, as a significant majority of environmental claims in the European Union turn out to be vague, misleading, or lacking any scientific evidence to support them. To combat this lack of rigor, the European Commission has pushed for a directive requiring companies to substantiate, verify, and communicate their environmental claims using evidence-based methodologies. The main objective is to empower consumers with transparent and truthful information, protecting them from misleading advertising and ensuring that declared sustainability aligns with the actual environmental impact of products. This regulatory framework is crucial because it distinguishes between mere *eco-posturing* and genuine commitment to the circular economy. By demanding rigorous certifications, it rewards companies that truly integrate sustainability into their value chains, as demonstrated by regional certification initiatives, while exposing the hypocrisy of those who espouse green rhetoric but maintain destructive practices—a phenomenon also denounced by key figures in climate activism.
