El greenwashing de los verdes en las alcaldías - Razón Pública

The text defines greenwashing as the practice by which entities, both private and public, disguise their lack of genuine commitment to sustainability through empty rhetoric and green appearances. This phenomenon is exploited to improve corporate or political image, boost stock value, and access financial benefits, while in reality concealing the destructive impacts their activities have on the environment and public health. The article’s relevance lies in its exposure of how local governments use grandiose environmental promises to gain electoral advantages without implementing tangible changes. This behavior is illustrated by analyzing the mandates of two mayoralties, where development plans promised critical improvements in water management, wetland conservation, and sustainable mobility. However, the outcome was the abandonment of these goals and the worsening of ecological crises, demonstrating that political declarations served merely as “makeup” to deceive the public. The most serious consequence of these greenwashing strategies is the erosion of citizen trust and disinterest in genuinely environmental agendas. Upon perceiving the deception, society rejects not only the implicated political leaders but also the environmental causes they instrumentalized, relegating sustainability to the background. This underscores the urgency of distinguishing genuine actions from empty rhetoric, especially in the face of the imminent climate crisis and the shrinking window of time available to correct environmental trajectories.
