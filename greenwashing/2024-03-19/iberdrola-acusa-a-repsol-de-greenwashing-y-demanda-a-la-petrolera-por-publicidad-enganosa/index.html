Iberdrola acusa a Repsol de "greenwashing" y demanda a la petrolera por publicidad engañosa

Iberdrola has formally accused Repsol of greenwashing, filing a lawsuit for misleading advertising that violates unfair competition laws. The core conflict stems from Repsol’s self-portrayal as a sustainability leader despite being Spain’s highest greenhouse gas emitter and focusing primarily on fossil fuels. Iberdrola argues that this discrepancy constitutes deceptive omission, aiming to establish a legal precedent against corporate hypocrisy in the energy transition narrative. The dispute highlights the intensifying tension between traditional oil giants and renewable pioneers as fossil fuel companies attempt to enter the green market. While Repsol defends its rapid transformation by citing significant investments in renewables and electric vehicle infrastructure, the legal action suggests that marketing claims must align with actual operational priorities. This case underscores the difficulty of proving deceptive intent, yet it marks a significant shift from rhetorical criticism to judicial accountability in corporate communications. This article is relevant to greenwashing as it illustrates the emerging trend of competitors using legal frameworks to police environmental claims. It demonstrates how the debate is moving beyond public relations into courtroom battles, forcing energy companies to ensure their sustainability messaging is substantiated by tangible business shifts. Consequently, it sets a critical benchmark for transparency, indicating that vague "green" branding will face stricter scrutiny from rivals and regulators alike.
