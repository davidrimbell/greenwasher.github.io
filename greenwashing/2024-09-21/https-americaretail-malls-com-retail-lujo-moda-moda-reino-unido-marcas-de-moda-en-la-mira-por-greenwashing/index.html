Greenwashing
EN ES

The UK’s Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) has issued key warnings to prominent fashion brands, marking a significant shift in the regulation of environmental claims. This move underscores corporate responsibility to ensure that sustainability assertions are accurate and verifiable, aiming to restore consumer trust in an industry long criticized for its ecological impact and misleading practices. The article highlights how current marketing strategies often rely on vague labels or deceptive claims, such as labeling products as “recycled” when they contain only minimal amounts of reused materials. These practices not only manipulate public perception but also lead consumers to make decisions based on false information, perpetuating environmental irresponsibility and obscuring the lack of authenticity behind so-called sustainable collections. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the new regulatory framework that enables the imposition of severe fines, driving a cultural shift toward radical transparency. This legal pressure, combined with growing public awareness, seeks to align commercial discourse with genuine action, demonstrating that authenticity and accountability are essential to avoid deception and achieve true sustainability in the fashion industry.
