Anglo American and the greenwashed mineral rush - Red Pepper

Anglo American and the greenwashed mineral rush - Red Pepper

The article reveals that major mining corporations like Anglo American are employing greenwashing tactics by rebranding their expansion into critical minerals as essential for the green transition, while obscuring their long history of colonial exploitation and environmental violence. By shifting from coal to copper and iron ore under the guise of sustainability, these companies mask the fact that their operations continue to rely on extractive models that cause severe ecological damage and health crises in vulnerable communities, such as the lead poisoning in Zambia and water scarcity in Chile. This narrative is relevant to greenwashing because it exposes the deceptive marketing strategy used to justify massive mining projects as "climate solutions" despite their identical destructive impacts to fossil fuel extraction. The article demonstrates that labeling mineral extraction as "green" is a public relations tool that deflects attention from the corporate refusal to pay for cleanup or provide reparations, effectively sanitizing a business model built on racialized violence and environmental sacrifice zones. Ultimately, the piece argues that this industrial approach threatens to perpetuate imperialist inequalities rather than achieve genuine decarbonization. It calls for solidarity movements to recognize that the push for critical minerals often serves the high-consumption habits of wealthy nations and military interests, not equitable climate action. Therefore, resisting these mining giants is crucial to dismantling the myth of sustainable extraction and addressing the root causes of environmental injustice.
