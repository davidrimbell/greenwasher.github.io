Brussels has a one-legged plan to fight greenwashing

The EU’s ambitious anti-greenwashing strategy suffered a significant setback, with only half its components surviving legislative challenges. This partial failure undermines the bloc’s ability to comprehensively curb deceptive environmental marketing across all sectors. The remaining directive specifically targets vague terminology like "green" or "eco-friendly," forcing companies to substantiate any positive ecological claims with rigorous evidence. This measure aims to eliminate ambiguous language that currently allows brands to mislead consumers about their products’ actual environmental impact. Relevance lies in demonstrating the fragility of regulatory frameworks against corporate lobbying. While the surviving rules offer some protection, the dismantled half leaves loopholes, highlighting how easily greenwashing defenses can be weakened, thereby perpetuating consumer deception and environmental harm.
