‘Starwashing’: How space companies use the greenwashing playbook while engaging in environmentally damaging practices

The article introduces "starwashing," a corporate strategy where space firms promote the expansion of human presence beyond Earth while obscuring the severe environmental damage caused by their activities. By leveraging the emotional appeal of cosmic wonder and the noble goal of benefiting "all humankind," these companies distract from the negative realities of their operations, effectively extending the deceptive practices of traditional greenwashing into the celestial sphere. Key examples reveal how companies justify harmful practices through misleading narratives. Firms claim that moving industry to space or launching reusable rockets will mitigate Earth’s climate crisis, yet they ignore significant pollution from rocket emissions and the depletion of resources. Furthermore, the push for orbital data centers and satellite constellations to support AI growth exacerbates space debris and threatens astronomical observation, all while marketing these developments as essential progress for humanity rather than ecological burdens. This phenomenon is highly relevant to greenwashing because it exploits public concern for the environment to legitimize industries with substantial ecological footprints. Just as greenwashing masks terrestrial harm with false eco-friendly claims, starwashing uses the allure of space exploration to bypass scrutiny of both off-Earth resource exploitation and near-term environmental risks on our own planet. The article highlights the urgent need for accountability as commercial space activities increasingly impact both Earth and the broader cosmic environment.
