Ignore buzzwords and watch out for wool: how to choose more eco-friendly fabrics – and avoid greenwashing

Ignore buzzwords and watch out for wool: how to choose more eco-friendly fabrics – and avoid greenwashing

The article argues that consumers must look beyond marketing buzzwords to identify true sustainability in fashion, as many brands engage in greenwashing by substituting expensive natural fibers with cheap, petroleum-based synthetics. A primary tactic involves blending plastic fibers like polyester with natural materials while marketing the product as primarily natural, or labeling synthetic polyurethane as "vegan leather." These practices prioritize profit over environmental integrity, creating products that are difficult to recycle and often of lower quality, thereby misleading shoppers who believe they are making eco-conscious choices. Experts recommend avoiding virgin polyester and acrylic due to their massive carbon footprints, reliance on fossil fuels, and tendency to release microplastics and toxins during their lifecycle. Instead, consumers should prioritize certified natural fibers such as organic cotton, linen, and wool, which are biodegradable and have lower environmental impacts. The text also highlights specific semi-synthetic options like Tencel and EcoVero as preferable alternatives to conventional viscose, emphasizing that genuinely sustainable choices require transparent sourcing and high-quality materials that do not compromise long-term ecological health. This narrative is crucial to understanding greenwashing because it exposes how vague terminology like "eco-polyester" or "vegan leather" serves as a distraction tactic. By revealing the industry’s reliance on blended fibers to cut costs while maintaining premium pricing, the article illustrates how confusion is deliberately manufactured to obscure the true environmental cost of clothing. Recognizing these red flags empowers consumers to reject misleading claims and support brands that offer genuine transparency and durability, rather than falling for superficial sustainability labels.
