Comunicar la sostenibilidad, y que te crean | El Diario Vasco

Comunicar la sostenibilidad, y que te crean | El Diario Vasco

The article argues that while sustainability is crucial for corporate reputation, its communicative use often tempts companies to exaggerate their environmental performance, creating fertile ground for greenwashing. This tendency risks eroding trust if companies prioritize a polished image over verifiable reality. The core conclusion is that credibility, rather than mere positive exposure, must be the foundation of any sustainability strategy to avoid deceptive practices. To combat this, organizations must embrace radical transparency by acknowledging both successes and ongoing challenges, including past errors. This approach fosters empathy and authenticity, which are essential for connecting with increasingly skeptical audiences. By admitting the inherent environmental impacts of industrial activities and outlining concrete steps for improvement, companies move beyond performative gestures. This honesty serves as an antidote to the "retouched photography" of corporate responsibility, demonstrating that sustainability is a complex journey rather than a static achievement. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the demand for objective evidence, such as specific indicators and verified data, to support sustainability claims. Modern consumers, particularly younger generations, require tangible proof of commitment rather than vague statements of intent. The article warns that without this factual backing and consistent, coherent messaging, any claim of eco-friendly behavior is likely to be perceived as insincere. Ultimately, preventing greenwashing requires aligning communication strictly with actual operational practices, ensuring that the narrative reflects the authentic, albeit imperfect, reality of the company’s environmental efforts.
