Greenwashing
ES EN

This article highlights how corporations frequently use unsubstantiated claims, such as promoting paperless billing to "save trees," to mask cost-cutting motives rather than genuine environmental benefits. These deceptive marketing tactics constitute greenwashing, misleading consumers who increasingly suspect that such sustainability arguments are primarily financial strategies disguised as ecological stewardship. The narrative emphasizes that these false narratives ignore consumer preference for choice, as many people want the option to receive physical communications rather than being forced into digital alternatives. By misleading the public, companies not only breach environmental marketing regulations but also undermine a paper industry with a strong track record of sustainable forest management and positive ecological impact. The relevance to greenwashing lies in its broader economic and perceptual damage. Such deceptive practices harm an industry supporting hundreds of thousands of jobs and distort public perception of paper’s true environmental footprint. Consequently, challenging these claims is vital to protect livelihoods, ensure accurate environmental reporting, and prevent significant financial losses driven by opportunistic marketing rather than actual sustainability improvements.
