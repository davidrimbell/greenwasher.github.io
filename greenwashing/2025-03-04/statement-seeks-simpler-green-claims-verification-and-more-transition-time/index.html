Statement seeks simpler Green Claims verification and more transition time

Major European industry associations are urging policymakers to revise the Green Claims Directive to ensure it is proportionate and practical. They argue that overly strict substantiation and verification requirements risk stifling innovation and competitiveness rather than preventing greenwashing. The groups warn that ambiguous rules may lead to "green hushing," where companies withhold sustainability information to avoid legal risks, ultimately depriving consumers of transparency and hindering the transition to a greener economy. The statement highlights that a one-size-fits-all approach creates unnecessary administrative burdens and legal uncertainty across member states. Industry leaders contend that claiming recyclable content or renewable energy usage is as complex as carbon footprinting, contradicting the Commission’s view that these are straightforward. Furthermore, they argue that banning claims on products with hazardous substances is misplaced, as it duplicates existing regulations like REACH without correlating with misleading claims. This overlap could disproportionately penalize compliant businesses and create inconsistent market conditions. This conflict is central to understanding greenwashing because it reveals the tension between rigorous consumer protection and realistic business implementation. If regulations are too burdensome, companies may abandon environmental communication entirely, making it harder for consumers to distinguish genuinely sustainable products from those that are not. The industry’s push for clarity and simplified procedures aims to create a functional framework where accurate green claims are encouraged rather than discouraged, ensuring that anti-greenwashing laws actually deliver meaningful information to the market.
