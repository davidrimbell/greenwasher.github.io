Fashion greenwashing glossary: what do ‘circular’, ‘sustainable’ and ‘zero waste’ really mean?
This article highlights how the fashion industry systematically misleads consumers by co-opting vague sustainability terminology without adhering to substantive environmental practices. Regulatory bodies are increasingly targeting this greenwashing, yet brands continue to exploit technical-sounding phrases to mask pollution and waste, creating a landscape where marketing claims often diverge sharply from operational reality. Key terms like "circular," "organic," and "carbon neutral" are frequently distorted to imply broader environmental benefits than they actually deliver. For instance, using recycled components does not constitute a truly circular system if there is no viable end-of-life solution, just as offsetting emissions does not equate to reducing one's own supply chain impact. Similarly, the label "organic" is often misapplied to conventionally farmed materials, while "regenerative" claims lack widespread verification, allowing companies to present superficial changes as radical transformation. Ultimately, the pervasive misuse of words like "sustainable" and "zero waste" reveals a systemic failure to prioritize holistic, long-term ecological health over short-term branding. This relevance to greenwashing lies in its ability to confuse consumers, enabling brands to profit from the perception of ethical responsibility while continuing harmful production methods. The article serves as a critical guide for distinguishing authentic environmental stewardship from deceptive marketing, urging a rigorous re-evaluation of what these terms truly require in practice.
