Greenwashing
ES EN

The European Union is tightening regulations to combat misleading environmental advertising by banning unsubstantiated generic claims like "carbon neutral" or "eco-friendly" without proof. Additionally, the legislation restricts reliance on carbon offsetting schemes for such claims and prohibits unapproved sustainability labels, aiming to ensure that environmental assertions are backed by recognized certifications and verifiable performance data. This regulatory shift addresses the credibility issues surrounding carbon offsetting, which has often been criticized for funding ineffective or redundant projects. By forcing companies to substantiate their green narratives, the EU seeks to create a level playing field and protect consumers who are increasingly vulnerable to deceptive marketing. This move represents a significant step in the bloc’s broader strategy to establish a comprehensive anti-greenwashing regime aligned with its net-zero goals. The relevance to greenwashing lies in how this directive specifically targets the consumer-facing aspect of the problem, which is often the most visible form of deceptive green marketing. While greenwashing also affects institutional investors and sophisticated stakeholders, this regulation focuses on empowering everyday consumers to make genuine sustainable choices. It signals a global trend, as other jurisdictions including the UK, US, and Australia are simultaneously refining their own guidelines to curb similar deceptive practices in environmental marketing.
