'A classic example of greenwashing': UN climate summit website criticised over 'low carbon' toggle

The COP28 summit’s website faced significant criticism for greenwashing after developers revealed its "low carbon" toggle was largely ineffective. Initially, the feature merely hid images visually while still downloading them in the background, resulting in negligible energy savings. This discrepancy between the perceived environmental benefit and actual digital impact led experts to label the initiative as a misleading claim designed to create an illusion of sustainability rather than delivering substantive change. Following public scrutiny, organizers updated the site to actually reduce data transfer by preventing the background loading of large images. While this adjustment was welcomed as a positive step toward transparency and reduced energy usage, experts argue it remains insufficient. The core issue persists because the low-carbon mode is not the default setting, forcing users to opt into a sustainable experience rather than making it the standard. This approach suggests that genuine environmental responsibility was treated as an afterthought rather than a foundational design principle. This case is highly relevant to the broader discourse on greenwashing because it highlights how superficial technical adjustments can mask a lack of genuine commitment to sustainability. It demonstrates that merely offering optional eco-friendly features, especially when presented as inferior alternatives to the "full experience," fails to address the root causes of digital carbon footprints. True leadership in climate action requires integrating low-impact design by default, ensuring that digital platforms actively contribute to emission reductions rather than simply appearing to do so.
