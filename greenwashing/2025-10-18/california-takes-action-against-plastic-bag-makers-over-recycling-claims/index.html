California takes action against plastic bag makers over recycling claims

California takes action against plastic bag makers over recycling claims

California is suing three manufacturers and settling with four others for falsely claiming their plastic bags are recyclable. This action targets deceptive marketing that misleads consumers into believing these items belong in recycling streams, when they often end up in landfills or incinerators. The investigation was triggered by regulatory concerns that such labeling undermines broader plastic pollution reduction efforts. Critics argue that loopholes in bans on single-use bags allowed companies to market thicker plastic alternatives under the guise of being environmentally responsible and recyclable. This case is relevant to greenwashing because it exposes how corporations exploit ambiguous recycling labels to create a false impression of sustainability. By penalizing these claims, regulators highlight the danger of marketing practices that shift responsibility to consumers while failing to provide viable disposal infrastructure, thereby distorting the environmental reality of plastic waste.
