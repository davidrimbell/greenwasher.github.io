Greenwashing
ES EN

The European Union is implementing strict regulations that will take effect in 2026 to combat greenwashing, banning vague claims such as “eco” or “green” that lack substantiation. The central objective is to require that any environmental claim be specific, verifiable, and backed by tangible evidence before it can be used in commercial contexts. This represents a radical shift: consumers will no longer have to contend with ambiguous marketing and will instead be able to trust statements that have been systematically justified. To ensure transparency, the new framework mandates oversight by independent verifiers who validate the evidence provided by companies. This model draws inspiration from the EU Ecolabel, which lends credibility through rigorous audits. The significance of this article lies in its establishment of a crucial precedent for defining the legal boundaries of sustainability, compelling brands to demonstrate not just an isolated feature but the actual environmental impact of their products, thereby substantially reducing ambiguity in labeling. The application of these rules is already yielding practical results across various sectors, from household cleaning to hospitality, where leading companies are adopting third-party certifications to avoid penalties and protect their reputation. National authorities, such as the Dutch Authority for Consumers and Markets (ACM), are penalizing the misleading use of terms like “carbon neutral” and promoting the adoption of rigorous standards. This global trend underscores that combating greenwashing requires not only regulation but also the active involvement of regulatory bodies and companies that prioritize truthfulness over image, ultimately empowering consumers with reliable information.
