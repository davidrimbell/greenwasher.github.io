Parliament wants to improve consumer protection against misleading claims | News | European Parliament

The European Parliament has adopted the Green Claims Directive to combat greenwashing by requiring companies to substantiate environmental marketing claims with rigorous evidence before advertising products as sustainable. This legislation mandates pre-approval by designated verifiers for terms like "biodegradable" or "eco-friendly," aiming to protect consumers from vague and misleading advertising while ensuring a level playing field for honest businesses. The directive introduces strict enforcement mechanisms, including significant financial penalties and exclusion from public procurement for violators, while offering simplified procedures and extended deadlines for smaller enterprises. It also bans reliance on carbon offsetting for primary green claims, allowing them only for residual emissions after substantial reduction efforts, and requires high-integrity certification for any remaining offsetting schemes used. This development is critically relevant to greenwashing because it directly addresses the widespread issue of unfounded eco-labels that deceive buyers and distort market competition. By shifting the burden of proof onto companies and establishing clear legal standards, the directive seeks to restore trust in sustainable products and ensure that environmental marketing reflects genuine ecological benefits rather than mere corporate deception.
