Greenwashing
EN ES

The article exposes how large agribusiness manipulates public opinion by pitting urban environmentalists against rural farmers, a tactic used to deflect blame from the industry itself. This narrative distortion serves as a form of greenwashing by suggesting that strict ecological regulations are the primary cause of agricultural distress, rather than addressing the systemic issues of corporate power and unfair trade practices that genuinely undermine small producers and local livelihoods. Critically, the piece highlights the European Union’s Carbon Removal Certification Framework as a dangerous tool of greenwashing. By promoting carbon farming and credits, this policy offers a scientifically invalid mechanism that benefits major polluters through "false confidence" while delaying real emission reductions. It encourages land grabbing by external agents and prioritizes market-based solutions over immediate, tangible actions, thereby legitimizing continued pollution under the guise of climate neutrality. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the contrast between these superficial compliance strategies and the demand for genuine systemic change. The author argues that true sustainability requires abandoning trade agreements that favor cheap, carbon-intensive imports and instead implementing fair pricing and protecting small-scale agriculture. This shift would prioritize local food security and biodiversity, exposing how current environmental policies often serve corporate interests rather than delivering the ecological and social justice promised by sustainability agendas.
