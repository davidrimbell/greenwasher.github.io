ScotGov warned to ditch 'greenwash fest' amidst minister code breaches row

This article highlights how major fossil fuel companies are using carbon capture technology to justify continued gas expansion, a strategy critics label as a sophisticated form of greenwashing. By promoting carbon capture as a viable climate solution, entities like SSE and Equinor aim to extend the lifespan of polluting infrastructure while masking their lack of genuine commitment to renewable energy transitions. This narrative shift allows these corporations to secure government support and public funding under the guise of environmental responsibility, despite their simultaneous scaling back of actual green investment targets. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the alleged compromise of governmental integrity, where ministers appear to endorse these dubious projects while supposedly acting as impartial regulators. Environmental groups accuse the Scottish Government of breaching ministerial codes by attending sponsored events and expressing support for developments they are tasked with evaluating. This dynamic illustrates a critical greenwashing mechanism: leveraging political access and high-profile industry conferences to legitimize controversial fossil fuel projects, thereby creating an illusion of regulatory approval and societal benefit that masks the underlying environmental harm and financial risks to consumers. Furthermore, the article underscores how carbon capture is employed as a deceptive tool to delay immediate emissions reductions and protect profits from oil and gas extraction. With major energy firms weakening their own climate promises and investing less in renewables, the push for costly carbon capture facilities serves primarily to delay accountability. This practice misleads the public and policymakers into believing that existing fossil fuel operations can be made "clean" without substantive change, ultimately obstructing a faster, more reliable transition to genuinely sustainable energy sources.
