Santos figured out net zero roadmap ‘literally on the fly’, court hears in world-first greenwashing case

The landmark greenwashing case against Santos reveals how major energy firms can systematically mislead investors by framing fossil fuels as sustainable solutions. By labeling natural gas a “clean fuel” and hydrogen production as “zero emission,” the company created a false impression of environmental responsibility. This narrative obscured the reality that burning gas generates significant carbon dioxide, thereby deceiving stakeholders about the actual climate impact of their core business operations. Furthermore, the case exposes how corporate net-zero plans can be constructed on unstable and undisclosed assumptions rather than rigorous scientific evidence. Santos allegedly manipulated baseline emissions and omitted critical data regarding the substantial carbon output of its hydrogen projects. This practice demonstrates a dangerous precedent where companies present vague, shifting roadmaps as credible strategies, effectively hiding the true scale of their future emissions while claiming to be committed to decarbonization. This legal battle is pivotal for holding companies accountable for the veracity of their climate claims. It challenges the industry’s ability to use vague terminology to bypass scrutiny and establishes that misleading statements about net-zero goals constitute deceptive conduct. Ultimately, the outcome will determine whether environmental reporting requires strict factual grounding, preventing corporations from exploiting greenwashing to secure investor trust without delivering genuine ecological benefits.
