James Cameron afirmó sentirse usado en su visita a Jujuy: "Me tendieron una emboscada"

James Cameron has accused the authorities in Jujuy of attempting to use his image to legitimize lithium extraction, promoting a sustainability narrative that concealed the conflict with Indigenous communities. The director feels he was a victim of a public relations campaign designed to equate the importance of mineral resources with the climate crisis—a stance he firmly rejects by prioritizing local rights over industrial production. The key revelation of the incident is that the information provided by the government was selective and misleading, ignoring the severe impacts on water, health, and the self-determination of Indigenous peoples. Cameron states that although he initially believed the evaporation method was harmless, he has now discovered the environmental degradation and the lack of prior consultation, which compels him to intervene morally in support of those affected. This case is relevant to the discourse on greenwashing because it illustrates how political and business actors can employ superficial environmental communication strategies to divert public attention from human rights violations and ecological damage. The deliberate manipulation of the narrative to project a “green” image while silencing the voices of affected communities underscores the need for critical scrutiny of sustainability claims that do not encompass comprehensive social justice.
