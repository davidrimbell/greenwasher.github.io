Maquillaje verde: activistas se manifestaron en la Expo Uruguay Sostenible 2023 contra el “enverdecimiento de proyectos contaminantes”

The article highlights a significant controversy surrounding Uruguay’s “Expo Sostenible 2023,” organized by the Ministry of Environment, which faced intense criticism for hosting sponsors such as UPM, Nestlé, and McDonald’s. Environmental organizations argued that featuring these corporations—many of which face backlash for damaging the environment, particularly regarding water usage during a severe national crisis—constitutes a blatant contradiction. Activists staged protests accusing the government of complicity, asserting that the event serves to legitimize extractive industries rather than genuinely promote sustainability. This situation is directly relevant to greenwashing, defined here as a deceptive practice where organizations present a false or exaggerated eco-friendly image while concealing harmful environmental impacts. Experts note that the government’s endorsement of these sponsors acts as a state-level form of “greenwashing,” masking the reality of resource depletion and pollution with superficial branding. The event is criticized as a publicity stunt that ignores urgent ecological issues, such as the water crisis, in favor of projecting an unsustainable, artificially “natural” national identity. Ultimately, the article illustrates how limited state oversight and a reliance on corporate investment can facilitate the spread of greenwashing. The paradox of an environment ministry celebrating polluting industries amid a declared ecological emergency underscores the danger of prioritizing economic incentives over transparency. This case serves as a stark example of how institutional approval can validate misleading environmental claims, thereby confusing the public and undermining genuine efforts to protect natural resources.
