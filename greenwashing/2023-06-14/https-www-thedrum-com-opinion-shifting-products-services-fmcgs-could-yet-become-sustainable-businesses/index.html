Greenwashing
ES EN

The article argues that genuine sustainability requires a fundamental shift from product-led to service-led business models. Rather than relying on incremental packaging innovations or telling consumers what to do, brands must design holistic services that prioritize the entire user experience. This approach reduces the carbon footprint associated with manufacturing and distributing individual consumer units, moving away from the traditional volume-based logic that often fuels waste. To make this transition effective, companies must anchor their strategies in universal human values such as caring, belonging, and empowerment. The text highlights that people do not need to be inherently focused on environmentalism to act sustainably; instead, they respond to solutions that align with their personal motivations and socioeconomic realities. By connecting with these psychological entry points, brands can foster empathy and drive meaningful behavioral change that resonates with the broader population. This perspective is critical to understanding greenwashing because it exposes the limitations of superficial eco-marketing. True sustainability is not merely a product attribute or a marketing message but a complex systemic overhaul involving collaboration, service design, and psychological insight. When brands fail to adopt these deeper structural and human-centric changes, their sustainability claims often ring hollow, serving more as cosmetic fixes for a broken business model than as genuine commitments to a sustainable future.
