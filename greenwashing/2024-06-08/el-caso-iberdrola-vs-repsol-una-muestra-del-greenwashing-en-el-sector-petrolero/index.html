El caso Iberdrola vs Repsol, una muestra del greenwashing en el sector petrolero

Greenwashing functions as a corporate strategy that enables transnational companies to expand their market share by adopting a facade of environmental responsibility, without their actual practices reflecting such commitment. This phenomenon is exemplified by the recent advertising dispute between major energy giants in Spain, where mutual accusations reveal how the electricity and oil sectors invest heavily in image-building campaigns to cleanse their reputations, distorting the reality of their business models. The significance of this case lies in the obvious contradiction between marketing rhetoric and industrial operations. Oil companies spend hundreds of millions on climate communications while allocating only a tiny fraction to low-emission initiatives, and they continue to increase hydrocarbon production even after the Paris Agreement. This demonstrates that greenwashing does not aim to resolve the crisis but rather to obscure the industry’s historical responsibility—having anticipated climate impacts decades ago, only to subsequently hinder decarbonization policies and distract society. Ultimately, this manipulation of ecological language poses a significant democratic and ecological risk by presenting fossil fuels as transitional solutions when they are, in fact, the root of the problem. The incongruity between real investments and public statements has led several cities and countries to ban these advertisements, recognizing that greenwashing is not merely deceptive advertising but an active mechanism that perpetuates the climate crisis by obstructing the urgent transition to clean energy.
