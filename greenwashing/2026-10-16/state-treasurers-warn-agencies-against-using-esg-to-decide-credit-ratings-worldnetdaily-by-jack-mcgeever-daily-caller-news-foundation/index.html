State treasurers warn agencies against using ESG to decide credit ratings * WorldNetDaily * by Jack McGeever, Daily Caller News Foundation

State financial officers from seventeen states have accused major credit rating agencies of undermining economic stability by allowing political climate agendas to distort credit assessments. The officials argue that agencies like S&P, Moody’s, and Fitch rely on flawed or retracted environmental data to penalize fossil-fuel-dependent regions and American energy companies. This approach threatens public finances by potentially lowering credit ratings, which would directly increase borrowing costs for states and jeopardize the security of public pension funds. The core grievance centers on the subjective nature of "transition" and "physical" climate risks used in these ratings. Critics contend that agencies are treating speculative political predictions as objective financial facts, often applying inconsistent standards that disadvantage domestic industries while favoring foreign entities. By incorporating ideologically driven metrics rather than hard financial evidence, the agencies are accused of acting as activists rather than neutral analysts, thereby eroding their credibility as reliable sources of objective financial information. This conflict is highly relevant to the discourse on greenwashing as it highlights the tension between genuine environmental risk assessment and the superficial adoption of ESG criteria for political signaling. The article illustrates how "green" labeling in financial ratings can mask biased methodologies that prioritize ideological narratives over factual accuracy. It underscores the broader concern that financial institutions may be leveraging sustainability frameworks to advance specific political outcomes, effectively greenwashing their decisions by presenting politically motivated assumptions as prudent risk management.
