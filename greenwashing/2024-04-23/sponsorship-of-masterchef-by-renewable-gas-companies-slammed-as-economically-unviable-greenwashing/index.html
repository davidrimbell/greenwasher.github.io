Sponsorship of MasterChef by 'renewable gas' companies slammed as economically unviable 'greenwashing'

The sponsorship of *MasterChef Australia* by Australian Gas Infrastructure Group is being criticized as a cynical effort to maintain the relevance of fossil fuel infrastructure as households transition to electrification. By utilizing low-emission alternatives like hydrogen and biomethane on a high-profile cooking show, the gas network operator aims to demonstrate the viability of carbon-neutral gas. This strategy seeks to preserve consumer reliance on traditional flame cooking and existing gas networks, despite state governments actively banning gas connections in new homes to promote fully electric alternatives. Experts and environmental groups argue this marketing tactic constitutes greenwashing, designed to create a false impression that renewable gases are viable, scalable replacements for traditional methane. Critics highlight that hydrogen production remains economically unfeasible compared to direct electrification, which becomes increasingly cost-effective as electricity prices drop. Furthermore, the limited supply of biomass resources makes large-scale biomethane production unrealistic. Consequently, the industry’s push for these alternatives is viewed not as a genuine climate solution, but as a defensive maneuver to protect profitable assets from becoming stranded as cleaner technologies dominate the market. This situation is highly relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how industries may rebrand persistent fossil fuel dependency under the guise of sustainability. By associating "low-emission" labels with products that ultimately delay the necessary shift to electrification, companies can manipulate public perception and policy decisions. The controversy underscores the danger of greenwashing, where superficial environmental claims obscure the fundamental incompatibility of extending gas infrastructure with genuine net-zero goals, thereby misleading consumers about the true trajectory of energy efficiency and climate action.
