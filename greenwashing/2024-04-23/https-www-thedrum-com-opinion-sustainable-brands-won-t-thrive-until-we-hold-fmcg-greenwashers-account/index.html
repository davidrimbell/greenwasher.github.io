Greenwashing
ES EN

Dove and its parent company Unilever have been labeled “Greenwasher of the Year” by Greenpeace for making unsubstantiated environmental claims while continuing to flood low-income communities with billions of single-use plastic sachets annually. This discrepancy between marketing messaging and actual environmental impact has triggered regulatory scrutiny, with authorities investigating vague assertions about recyclability and reduced plastic usage. The case highlights how dominant market players can leverage scale to project an eco-friendly image despite minimal progress in waste reduction, specifically regarding reusable packaging options. This scenario underscores the critical relevance to greenwashing by exposing the disparity between corporate sustainability narratives and tangible ecological harm. Large incumbents often use relative claims, such as “less plastic,” to mask the fact that products remain entirely plastic-based waste. By highlighting these contradictions, the article illustrates how misleading marketing distracts from the urgent need to eliminate waste, allowing powerful brands to avoid accountability while smaller, genuinely sustainable alternatives struggle for visibility. The narrative further emphasizes the systemic challenges faced by disruptive brands attempting to compete against well-funded incumbents. These smaller entities often lack the legal resources to counter cease-and-desist orders or challenge false advertising, forcing them to rely on consumer-led reporting to regulatory bodies. Ultimately, the article argues that holding global leaders accountable requires collective consumer vigilance and credible, evidence-based comparisons to expose the environmental reality behind polished corporate campaigns.
