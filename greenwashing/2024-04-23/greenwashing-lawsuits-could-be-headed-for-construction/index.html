Greenwashing lawsuits could be headed for construction

The article argues that while the construction industry has thus far avoided the surge in greenwashing litigation that has impacted other sectors, it faces imminent legal risks. As stricter building emission regulations take effect in key states, companies are under pressure to market their products as sustainable. However, relying on vague terminology like "eco-friendly" without rigorous data is increasingly viewed as deceptive, making the sector a likely next target for consumer and regulatory lawsuits alleging false advertising. Legal experts emphasize that vague environmental claims are dangerous because they imply broader benefits than exist. To mitigate this risk, companies must ensure their sustainability statements are specific and supported by measurable, verifiable data. It is crucial to clarify whether claims cover the entire product lifecycle or only specific stages, as omitting upstream supplier emissions or downstream disposal impacts can lead to accusations of misleading consumers about a product’s true environmental footprint. Finally, the reliance on carbon offsets is identified as a significant vulnerability, with recent lawsuits challenging the accuracy and validity of many credits. The piece concludes that construction firms must align their public statements with verifiable operational reductions rather than speculative accounting practices. As regulatory scrutiny intensifies, the industry is urged to adopt transparent, substantiated claims to avoid the legal liabilities that have already ensnared major retailers and manufacturers.
