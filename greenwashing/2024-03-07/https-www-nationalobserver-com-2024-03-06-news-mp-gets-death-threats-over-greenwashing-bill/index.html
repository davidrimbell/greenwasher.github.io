Greenwashing
ES EN

Canadian MP Charlie Angus faces intense harassment and death threats after introducing legislation to prohibit fossil fuel companies from using misleading advertising that portrays their industry as a climate solution. This backlash is largely fueled by a conservative campaign that falsely claims the bill criminalizes the promotion of oil and gas and threatens jail time. In reality, the proposed law targets corporate deception regarding health and environmental impacts, drawing inspiration from existing tobacco regulations, and does not penalize individual citizens for their speech. The controversy highlights the urgent need for Canada to close regulatory gaps that allow greenwashing to persist, unlike the European Union and the United Kingdom, which have already restricted such practices. Critics argue that the right-wing opposition is deploying disinformation to obscure the severe health and environmental consequences of fossil fuels, including tens of thousands of annual premature deaths. By framing reasonable regulation as an attack on liberty, political actors effectively derail substantive discussions about the industry’s true contribution to the climate crisis. This dynamic exemplifies how disinformation campaigns weaponize cultural identity to resist climate action, a phenomenon researchers identify as "petro-masculinity." It demonstrates how the fossil fuel industry and its political allies leverage misinformation to generate political backlash and silence critical policy debates. The article underscores the danger of such strategies, showing how false narratives are used to protect profitable but harmful industries from accountability, thereby impeding necessary efforts to combat global warming and protect public health.
