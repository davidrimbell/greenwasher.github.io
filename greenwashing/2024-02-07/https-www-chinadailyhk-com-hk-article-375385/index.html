Greenwashing
ES EN

The European Union has finalized its first regulatory framework for ESG ratings, aiming to establish rigor and transparency in sustainability investing. By requiring separate evaluations for environmental, social, and governance factors, the rules seek to ensure that ratings reflect the true impact of corporate operations on society and the planet, rather than just financial performance. This shift emphasizes double materiality, where both the effect of ESG issues on a company and the company’s impact on the environment are assessed. This legislative move is directly relevant to greenwashing because regulators suspect companies are inflating their sustainability profiles to attract capital. Without standardized oversight, investors risk being misled by opaque or incomplete data. The new requirements mandate explicit disclosures and the inclusion of human rights in social assessments, forcing ratings providers to be more accountable and reducing the ambiguity that allows for misleading sustainability claims. Furthermore, the EU mandates that all raters, including those based outside the bloc, undergo authorization and supervision. This creates a barrier to entry for unregulated actors and ensures that ratings used within the EU meet strict standards. By fostering investor confidence through transparent and regulated assessments, the EU hopes to drive a genuine transition toward a socially responsible economy, moving beyond superficial marketing tactics toward substantive sustainable finance practices.
