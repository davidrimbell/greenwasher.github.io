Mentiras verdes para ganar dinero: así funciona la tela de araña del ecopostureo para atrapar a los consumidores

Mentiras verdes para ganar dinero: así funciona la tela de araña del ecopostureo para atrapar a los consumidores

The article exposes greenwashing as a massive corporate strategy that manipulates consumers through ambiguous labels such as “emissions-neutral” or “sustainable,” thereby concealing companies’ actual pollution levels. This disconnect between marketing narratives and verifiable data erodes public trust and distorts the market, allowing unsustainable businesses to exploit buyers’ good faith while obscuring their true environmental footprint. Institutional responses are growing through a complex network of European regulations designed to penalize these deceptive practices. New directives mandate transparency, independent verification, and severe sanctions for those making unfounded green claims, aiming to bring order to what was previously a chaotic landscape. However, the proliferation of rules also generates confusion, complicating the distinction between genuine commitments and mere environmental posturing. This context is crucial for understanding greenwashing because it reveals the systemic scale of the problem, which extends beyond product labeling to affect even financial investment and corporate reporting. The existence of inter-company lawsuits and the intervention of regulatory bodies demonstrate that greenwashing is no longer merely an ethical lapse but a concrete legal and financial risk. Combating eco-deception therefore requires scientific rigor and strict oversight to protect citizens and ensure that the ecological transition is grounded in tangible facts rather than advertising fiction.
