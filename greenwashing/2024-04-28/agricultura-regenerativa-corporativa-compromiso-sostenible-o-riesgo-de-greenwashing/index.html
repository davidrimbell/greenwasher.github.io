Agricultura regenerativa corporativa: ¿Compromiso sostenible o riesgo de greenwashing?

The article examines the global rise of regenerative agriculture, initiated by pioneering practices such as those of Gabe Brown, and its evolution toward carbon farming. This trend enables farmers to be economically compensated for sequestering carbon in the soil, creating an emerging market where large agro-industrial corporations purchase credits to offset their own emissions. While this represents a significant paradigm shift, the rapid growth of these initiatives raises questions about their actual environmental impact beyond the advertising narrative. The critical relevance of this phenomenon lies in the widespread adoption of corporate commitments. Today, it is nearly impossible to find large companies in the sector that do not announce ambitious goals linked to soil regeneration, transforming what was once a rarity into a standard communication practice. This saturation of declarations increases the risk that actual actions may not align with the projected image, leaving the door open to misleading practices where marketing precedes technical substance. The author concludes that, although corporate interest is positive, it is imperative to audit the scientific robustness behind each initiative to avoid greenwashing. Without rigorous verification, the ambiguous promotion of regenerative agriculture could lose credibility and undermine its real potential as a climate tool. Therefore, technical transparency and independent verification are essential to ensure that these commitments translate into tangible soil improvements and do not degenerate into mere instruments for corporate image washing.
