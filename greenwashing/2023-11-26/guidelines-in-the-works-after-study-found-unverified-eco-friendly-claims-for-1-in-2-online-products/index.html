Guidelines in the works after study found unverified eco-friendly claims for 1 in 2 online products

The Competition and Consumer Commission of Singapore is developing new guidelines to combat the widespread prevalence of greenwashing, following a study revealing that half of sampled online products featured overstated environmental claims. The investigation found that retailers frequently used vague, unsubstantiated assertions or confusing technical jargon to market items as eco-friendly, misleading consumers about the actual sustainability of goods ranging from electronics to fashion. This regulatory response is critically relevant to understanding greenwashing because it highlights how businesses exploit the growing consumer demand for sustainability to gain a competitive advantage without genuine environmental responsibility. By exposing these deceptive practices, the study underscores the urgency of distinguishing between credible sustainable actions and marketing tactics designed solely to capitalize on eco-conscious purchasing trends, thereby protecting market integrity. Ultimately, the initiative aims to level the playing field by holding suppliers accountable for truthful advertising under consumer protection laws. This shift encourages a transition toward responsible business practices, ensuring that companies which invest in genuine sustainability are not disadvantaged by those using greenwashing to deceive buyers. It empowers consumers to make informed decisions and drives the market toward authentic environmental benefits rather than superficial claims.
