EXCLUSIVE: Big Tech's 'Misleading' Green Energy Claims May Plunge Nation Into Blackouts, AGs Warn

Major tech companies are accused of exacerbating blackouts through misleading "100% renewable energy" claims. By utilizing unbundled renewable energy certificates, these firms market themselves as entirely green despite relying on a grid dominated by fossil fuels. This narrative falsely suggests that intermittent sources like wind and solar are sufficient for the massive power demands of data centers, thereby misleading the public and policymakers about energy reliability. The widespread acceptance of these claims pressures utilities to prematurely retire reliable baseload power sources, such as coal and natural gas, in favor of green energy goals required by tech giants. This transition threatens grid stability, as intermittent renewables cannot currently sustain the consistent, high-volume energy needs of modern infrastructure. Consequently, the aggressive push for net-zero commitments by big tech accelerates the shutdown of essential power plants, significantly increasing the risk of widespread blackouts and destabilizing the national energy supply. This situation is a critical example of greenwashing, where corporate sustainability marketing masks the reality of continued fossil fuel dependence. The letter highlights how regulatory loopholes allow companies to claim environmental leadership while shifting the burden of energy insecurity onto the broader grid and local communities. It underscores the deceptive nature of labeling reliance on renewable credits as actual renewable consumption, revealing a disconnect between corporate branding and the physical limitations of the power system.
