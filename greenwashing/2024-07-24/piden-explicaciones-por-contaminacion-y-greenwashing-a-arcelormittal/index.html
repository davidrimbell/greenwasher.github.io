Piden explicaciones por contaminación y greenwashing a ArcelorMittal

The article exposes ArcelorMittal’s greenwashing strategy, which leverages its sponsorship of the Paris 2024 Olympic Games to project an image of sustainability by manufacturing a torch purportedly featuring “low emissions.” Activists from the “Shiny Claims, Dirty Flames” campaign demand transparency regarding the use of public funds for decarbonization in Asturias, questioning why the company prioritizes reducing energy costs and share buybacks for its shareholders over genuine investments in clean and just technologies. The use of carbon capture is firmly rejected as a viable solution, with critics arguing that it is an immature technique employed to maintain polluting production processes, including coke batteries, under the pretext of future exports. Social pressure calls for the permanent closure of these facilities and the adoption of renewable energy-based alternatives, such as green hydrogen, rather than relying on “false” solutions that prolong dependence on fossil fuels and delay real climate action. This situation is relevant to the study of greenwashing because it illustrates how highly polluting industries exploit sports activism and corporate marketing to divert attention from their financial and environmental opacity. The text highlights the disparity between rhetoric on climate leadership and the reality of minimal decarbonization investments versus enormous profits for shareholders, underscoring the need to distinguish between promotional appearances and tangible commitments to global climate limits.
