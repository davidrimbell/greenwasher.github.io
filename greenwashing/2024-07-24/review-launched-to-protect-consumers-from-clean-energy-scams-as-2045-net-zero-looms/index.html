Review launched to protect consumers from clean energy scams as 2045 net zero looms

Consumer Scotland is launching a critical investigation into the emerging market for green home heating technologies, aiming to address significant gaps in consumer knowledge and protection. With Scotland’s new legislative targets requiring millions of homes to switch to cleaner heating systems by 2045, there is urgent concern that the rapid expansion of this sector is outpacing available advice. This lack of clarity creates an environment where buyers are vulnerable to scams, conflicting information, and rogue traders, potentially undermining the public’s trust in the transition to net zero. This situation highlights a major facet of greenwashing: the manipulation of consumer confusion and lack of regulatory oversight to sell expensive or unreliable "green" solutions. When policymakers and industry leaders fail to provide a transparent, unified framework for subsidies and standards, they allow misinformation to flourish. Consumers may be misled into making substantial financial investments in technologies that do not perform as advertised, turning well-intentioned environmental mandates into opportunities for market failure and exploitation rather than genuine sustainability. The investigation seeks to expose these pitfalls to drive stronger consumer protections and confidence, which are essential for the success of national climate ambitions. By highlighting the risks of mis-selling and the absence of approved advice, the report underscores that greenwashing thrives in the shadows of regulatory ambiguity. Ultimately, empowering homeowners with clear, accurate information is not just a consumer protection issue but a prerequisite for achieving effective decarbonization without compromising public trust or financial security.
