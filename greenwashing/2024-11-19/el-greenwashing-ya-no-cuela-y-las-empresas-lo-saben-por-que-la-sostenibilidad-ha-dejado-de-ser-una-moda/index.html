El “greenwashing” ya no cuela y las empresas lo saben: por qué la sostenibilidad ha dejado de ser una moda

El “greenwashing” ya no cuela y las empresas lo saben: por qué la sostenibilidad ha dejado de ser una moda

The article argues that the true challenge of the green transition lies in supporting small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), which lack the resources to decarbonize independently. While large corporations have evolved their sustainability strategies from superficial gestures to comprehensive, cross-company integration, SMEs face significant financial and technical barriers. Consequently, big companies are positioned as essential engines of change, leveraging their influence to transform supply chains and providing digital technologies that help smaller entities reduce their environmental impact effectively. Societal demand plays a crucial role, as the majority of consumers express willingness to support sustainable products, yet price remains the primary decision factor. This dynamic highlights a tension where genuine corporate action is required to justify potential premium pricing. The text emphasizes that large firms must move beyond mere communication to demonstrate tangible results, using their workforce and operational scale to drive substantial carbon reductions. This shift is necessary to meet the expectations of an increasingly conscious public that demands seriousness over marketing. This discussion is highly relevant to greenwashing because it underscores the critical distinction between superficial environmental claims and substantive operational changes. Greenwashing often thrives on vague commitments or minor symbolic actions, whereas the article insists on proof of impact and structural integration of sustainability. It warns that companies, particularly those targeting conscious consumers, must deliver measurable reductions and support ecosystem-wide transitions to avoid accusations of deception. The focus on demonstrating real value rather than just communicating intent serves as a safeguard against the hollow promises typical of greenwashing practices.
