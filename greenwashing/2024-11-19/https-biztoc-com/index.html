Greenwashing
ES EN

The article highlights a market environment driven by geopolitical tensions and inflationary pressures, which indirectly illuminates the mechanisms of greenwashing. As oil prices surge due to the U.S.-Iran impasse and conflicts in Ukraine, energy costs skyrocket, creating intense pressure on consumers and businesses. This volatility forces corporations to scramble for cost controls and narrative shields, often leading to superficial environmental claims rather than substantive operational changes. Concurrently, central banks signal potential rate hikes due to persistent inflation, further tightening financial conditions. In this high-cost atmosphere, firms may engage in strategic communication to appease stakeholders without incurring the capital expenditure required for genuine sustainability transitions. The focus shifts toward managing investor sentiment through visible but lightweight "green" initiatives, leveraging the chaos to deflect scrutiny from actual carbon footprints or supply chain ethics. This context is relevant to greenwashing because economic distress often incentivizes performative sustainability. Companies facing margin compression from rising energy and borrowing costs are more likely to prioritize marketing over material reduction efforts. The article underscores how macroeconomic instability creates a fertile ground for deceptive branding, where entities exploit public concern for the environment to maintain legitimacy and market share while avoiding the rigorous, costly structural reforms necessary for true ecological responsibility.
