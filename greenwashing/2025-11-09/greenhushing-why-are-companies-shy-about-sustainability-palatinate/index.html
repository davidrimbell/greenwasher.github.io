Greenhushing – why are companies shy about sustainability? - Palatinate

Greenhushing – why are companies shy about sustainability? - Palatinate

Recent data suggests that large corporations are not abandoning sustainability, contrary to prevalent media narratives. Contrary to the belief that big business has turned its back on ESG goals, surveys indicate a significant increase in corporate commitment to decarbonization. This reveals that the decline in public pledges is not a retreat from environmental responsibility, but rather a strategic shift in how these commitments are communicated. This trend, termed “greenhushing,” involves companies intentionally keeping their sustainability efforts quiet to avoid political scrutiny and legal backlash, particularly in the United States. While greenwashing involves exaggerating environmental benefits, greenhushing is the opposite: possessing strong ESG records but suppressing visibility to evade criticism from conservative politicians and investors who view such initiatives as financial distractions. This silence is a defensive mechanism against a hostile regulatory environment rather than a lack of ambition. This phenomenon is highly relevant to greenwashing discourse because it highlights the complexity of corporate environmental signaling. Greenwashing relies on visible deception, whereas greenhushing obscures genuine progress, potentially normalizing the absence of sustainability leadership. By hiding their achievements, companies may inadvertently signal that ESG is not a business norm, undermining the cultural shift toward transparency and potentially allowing profit-driven motives to ultimately override genuine environmental stewardship.
