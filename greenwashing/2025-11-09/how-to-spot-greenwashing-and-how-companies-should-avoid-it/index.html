How to Spot Greenwashing and How Companies Should Avoid It

How to Spot Greenwashing and How Companies Should Avoid It

Greenwashing, defined as making environmentally friendly claims lacking factual support, poses significant legal and reputational risks as allegations expand beyond traditional consumer protection into competitive and political arenas. While plaintiff classes and environmental groups traditionally target misleading marketing to correct deceptive practices, a new trend emerges where competitors use greenwashing suits to gain market share, and political actors weaponize these claims to protect fossil fuel industries against renewable energy transitions. This evolution highlights how environmental assertions are increasingly leveraged as strategic tools rather than genuine sustainability efforts. For instance, manufacturers may sue rivals over unverified eco-claims to secure corrective advertising, while state attorneys general might challenge renewable energy promises to undermine grid reliability and maintain demand for coal. These developments demonstrate that greenwashing allegations are no longer solely about consumer deception but are integral to broader business tactics and political leverage, significantly complicating the regulatory landscape for corporations. To mitigate these risks, companies must rigorously align their marketing with actual practices by adhering to the FTC’s Green Guides and understanding state-specific consumer protection laws. Implementing internal legal reviews of environmental claims ensures that messaging withstands the "reasonable consumer" standard, preventing mismatches between advertising and operational reality. By proactively addressing these standards, businesses can defend against both traditional litigation and the emerging wave of politically or competitively motivated greenwashing accusations.
