Greenwashing firms and public entities face growing threat of litigation

Greenwashing firms and public entities face growing threat of litigation

US legal expert Maren Salaheldin warns that Irish companies and public bodies face escalating litigation risks for greenwashing their carbon reduction commitments. As regulatory scrutiny intensifies, unsubstantiated environmental claims are increasingly vulnerable to legal challenges, signaling that ESG-related disputes will become a dominant legal frontier in the coming decade. The impending transposition of the EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive places significant pressure on Irish businesses. Many organizations lack the necessary data systems for compliance, creating a gap between stated sustainability goals and verifiable evidence. This disparity exposes them to heightened legal and reputational risks as they struggle to meet stricter disclosure requirements. This shift is relevant to greenwashing because it establishes a legal framework where misleading environmental marketing carries tangible consequences. The doubling of global climate lawsuits and high-profile domestic cases demonstrate that vague or aspirational claims no longer suffice. Consequently, entities must ensure their sustainability assertions are substantiated to avoid severe financial and reputational damage in the "court of public opinion" and actual courts.
