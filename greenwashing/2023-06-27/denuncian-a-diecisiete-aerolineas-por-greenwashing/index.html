Denuncian a diecisiete aerolíneas por ‘greenwashing’

A European consumer organization has accused numerous airlines of greenwashing, alleging that their environmental marketing is deceptive. These carriers encourage passengers to purchase carbon credits to offset flight emissions, despite the uncertain real-world benefits of such compensation. This practice misleads travelers into believing their travel is sustainable when it is not, thereby violating EU laws on unfair commercial practices. The complaint further highlights that airlines charge additional fees for Sustainable Aviation Fuels (SAFs), which remain largely unviable and are governed by low mandatory targets. By marketing these unreliable solutions, companies create an illusion of eco-responsibility while failing to address the fundamental unsustainability of air travel in the near future. This tactic exploits consumer goodwill for profit. This case is crucial for understanding greenwashing because it exposes how the aviation sector uses vague claims and unproven technologies to mask its environmental impact. It demonstrates the gap between marketing narratives and scientific reality, urging regulators to enforce transparency. Recognizing these patterns helps consumers identify misleading environmental claims across various industries.
