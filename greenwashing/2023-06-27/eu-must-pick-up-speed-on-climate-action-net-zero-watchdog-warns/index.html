EU must pick up speed on climate action, net-zero watchdog warns

The European Climate Neutrality Observatory warns that the EU is moving too slowly to meet its 2030 and 2050 climate goals, with progress falling short in critical sectors like transport, buildings, and industry. While electricity generation is nearly on track, the deployment of renewables and grid infrastructure is lagging, and the transport transition remains inadequate despite growing electric vehicle adoption. Crucially, natural carbon removals are decreasing, actively working against net-zero targets, while engineered solutions are advancing too slowly to make a significant impact. The financial sector presents the most alarming deficit, as the EU continues to subsidize fossil fuels and reduce environmental taxation. This ongoing financial support for carbon-intensive industries contradicts public commitments to climate neutrality and threatens to derail the entire green transition by diverting essential capital away from renewable technologies and infrastructure. The report highlights that significant gaps in data and reliable benchmarks further obscure the true state of decarbonization, complicating policy effectiveness. This article is vital to understanding greenwashing because it exposes the stark disconnect between high-level political rhetoric and actual implementation. It illustrates how governments can claim leadership in sustainability while simultaneously undermining their own goals through contradictory fiscal policies and insufficient action. By revealing these systemic failures and the reliance on fossil fuel subsidies, the report demonstrates that current climate strategies lack the rigor and consistency required, serving as a clear example of performative environmentalism rather than substantive change.
