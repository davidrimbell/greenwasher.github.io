'Ecopostureo': Reino Unido investiga si son engañosas las afirmaciones 'verdes' de Unilever sobre sus productos y el respeto al medio ambiente

The UK’s Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) has launched a formal investigation into Unilever to determine whether its environmental claims are exaggerated or misleading. The probe focuses on cleaning and personal care products, examining whether vague language and the use of natural symbols create an inaccurate impression of the company’s actual ecological impact. The regulator warns that certain statements about ingredients or recyclability lack clarity, potentially misleading consumers about the sustainability of packaging or products. By scrutinizing fast-moving consumer goods, the CMA aims to protect shoppers who seek to make ethical choices but may fall victim to deceptive marketing strategies. This case is significant for understanding greenwashing, as it illustrates the shift from general warnings to concrete regulatory action against multinational corporations. It demonstrates that vague environmental claims are not merely questionable practice but a direct target of competition authorities, setting a precedent for demanding genuine transparency and penalizing eco-posturing in the retail market.
