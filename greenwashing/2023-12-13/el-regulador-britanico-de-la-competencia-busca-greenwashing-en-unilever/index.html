El regulador británico de la competencia busca 'greenwashing' en Unilever

The UK’s Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) has launched an investigation into Unilever to determine whether the company has exaggerated the environmental credentials of its products. This scrutiny is heightened by Unilever’s longstanding public commitment to sustainability and social activism, making the allegations of deception particularly serious and sensational in the eyes of the public. The regulator is questioning vague statements, ambiguous logos, and unclear claims about recycling—common practices that undermine consumer trust. If non-compliance is confirmed, Unilever could face backlash against conscious capitalism, potentially making executives more reluctant to set ambitious sustainability targets if their financial performance is weak. This case is relevant to understanding greenwashing, as it illustrates how companies use green marketing to differentiate their brands, often in a misleading manner. The regulatory action aims to protect consumers who make purchasing decisions based on ethical values and to prevent companies that genuinely comply with environmental standards from suffering competitive disadvantages against rivals that merely simulate social responsibility.
