The article argues that heavy government investment in blue and grey hydrogen poses a significant greenwashing risk, potentially undermining the transition to truly renewable energy. By classifying fossil fuel-derived hydrogen as "low carbon" through carbon capture technologies, policymakers and industries risk creating a misleading narrative that justifies continued reliance on natural gas. This strategy threatens to overshadow green hydrogen, which is the only sustainable option, thereby delaying genuine decarbonization efforts under the guise of environmental progress. A primary concern is the reliance on carbon capture and storage (CCS) to validate blue hydrogen, a technology widely criticized for its inefficiency and environmental hazards. Critics highlight that methane leaks and imperfect carbon sequestration mean these projects do not offer the claimed emission reductions. Consequently, vast amounts of public funding are directed toward sustaining fossil fuel infrastructure rather than supporting hard-to-abate industries with truly clean solutions, raising serious questions about the authenticity of these "low-carbon" labels. Despite current cost advantages for fossil-based methods, the economic landscape is shifting rapidly as green hydrogen prices decline due to technological advancements. The article suggests that prioritizing blue hydrogen in the short term may prove economically detrimental as green alternatives become cheaper and more competitive. This divergence highlights the danger of locking in outdated infrastructure; by favoring cheaper, less sustainable options now, governments may hinder the long-term viability of the renewable hydrogen sector they ostensibly aim to protect.
