Dove and Marmite maker Unilever to be investigated in UK over ‘greenwashing’

The UK’s Competition and Markets Authority is investigating Unilever for potentially misleading consumers with vague environmental claims on essential household products. The regulator fears that the company’s use of green imagery, such as leaves, and ambiguous statements about recyclability create a false impression of eco-friendliness that does not align with the products' actual environmental impact. This scrutiny highlights the core mechanism of greenwashing, where marketers focus on minor sustainable features to mask broader ecological harms, such as the massive production of single-use plastics. Despite public commitments to sustainability, reports indicate Unilever continues to sell billions of non-reusable sachets, missing its own plastic reduction targets. This discrepancy between marketing narratives and operational reality suggests that consumers are being deceived into believing they are making greener choices than they actually are. The case is highly relevant to greenwashing discussions as it demonstrates how powerful brands can exploit consumer trust in sustainability trends. By targeting daily essentials, companies can drive sales under a veneer of environmental responsibility while failing to address significant waste issues. This investigation serves as a critical check on whether corporate sustainability promises are substantiated or merely cosmetic, signaling a stricter regulatory approach to enforcing transparency in environmental marketing.
