Diana Clement: Are your investments green or just greenwashed? - NZ Herald

Diana Clement: Are your investments green or just greenwashed? - NZ Herald

The article highlights the confusion surrounding sustainable KiwiSaver funds, where generic search results often mask providers making misleading claims. This ambiguity stems from a lack of industry-wide transparency, allowing managers to easily justify "green" labels without substantive action. Consequently, investors cannot rely on surface-level marketing or paid search rankings to identify genuinely ethical options. To navigate this greenwashing landscape, investors must perform rigorous due diligence rather than accepting superficial labels. By scrutinizing disclosure documents for specific, measurable targets and seeking third-party certifications, one can uncover whether funds truly align with sustainability goals. This process often reveals that some advertised "sustainable" funds still hold significant investments in controversial industries like fossil fuels and tobacco. This topic is critically relevant to greenwashing as it exposes the gap between marketing claims and actual investment practices. It demonstrates how easily misleading terminology can deceive consumers, undermining the integrity of sustainable finance. The piece underscores the urgent need for stricter standards and greater accountability to ensure that "green" investments deliver on their environmental and social promises.
