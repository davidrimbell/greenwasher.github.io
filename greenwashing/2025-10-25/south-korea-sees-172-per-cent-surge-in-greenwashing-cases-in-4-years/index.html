South Korea sees 172 per cent surge in greenwashing cases in 4 years

South Korea sees 172 per cent surge in greenwashing cases in 4 years

South Korea’s recent audit reveals a dramatic surge in greenwashing cases, highlighting a critical failure in regulatory oversight rather than just corporate malice. The overwhelming majority of violations involved expired environmental certifications, exposing significant blind spots in post-certification management. This trend suggests that current systems fail to effectively monitor products after initial approval, allowing misleading claims to persist unchecked and eroding public trust in eco-labeling schemes. This local crisis reflects a broader regional awakening to the dangers of deceptive sustainability marketing. As Asian nations like Singapore, Japan, and Australia introduce stricter guidelines and penalties, it becomes clear that voluntary measures are insufficient to curb exaggerated environmental claims. The sharp increase in consumer-flagged violations indicates growing public scrutiny, forcing governments to transition from passive oversight to active enforcement to maintain market integrity. The article is highly relevant to greenwashing discussions as it illustrates the gap between certification issuance and ongoing compliance. It demonstrates how greenwashing often relies on administrative neglect rather than outright fraud, emphasizing the need for robust, continuous monitoring systems. Furthermore, it underscores the global momentum toward regulating environmental claims, showing that without rigorous post-certification management, eco-labels risk becoming empty symbols rather than reliable indicators of sustainability.
