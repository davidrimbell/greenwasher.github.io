Greenwashing
ES EN

Peter Krull argues that the financial industry frequently misleads investors by repackaging traditional high-carbon indices with superficial ESG adjustments, effectively marketing "less bad" conventional portfolios as sustainable investments. This practice highlights a critical greenwashing dynamic where labels are applied to existing, often harmful, business models rather than driving genuine transition. By merely layering risk metrics onto standard indexes, firms create an illusion of sustainability that masks the continued reliance on fossil fuels and damaging industries, deceiving clients who believe their capital is supporting positive change. Furthermore, Krull challenges the common perception that ESG measures a company’s impact on the world, clarifying that it actually measures external risks to the company’s financial stability. This distinction reveals a fundamental gap in current sustainable investing frameworks, where the focus remains on protecting shareholder value from environmental or social liabilities rather than actively mitigating those very issues. When providers claim their funds are sustainable based solely on this risk-based approach, they obscure the reality that these investments do not contribute to solving the climate crisis, thereby perpetuating a system that prioritizes corporate resilience over planetary health. True sustainability requires a fundamental shift from tracking historical performance to identifying ground-up solutions that drive a cleaner, more equitable economy. Krull contends that relying on backward-looking indexes fails to capture the companies actively building the next economic model, such as insurers integrating climate risk into their policies. This perspective is vital to understanding greenwashing because it exposes how the industry’s obsession with quantifiable, backward-looking metrics allows it to avoid investing in the transformative innovation necessary for genuine sustainability, instead offering incremental adjustments that maintain the status quo.
