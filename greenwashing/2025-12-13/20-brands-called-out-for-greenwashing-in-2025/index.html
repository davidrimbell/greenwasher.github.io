20 brands called out for greenwashing in 2025

20 brands called out for greenwashing in 2025

The 2025 landscape reveals a significant escalation in corporate greenwashing, particularly through the tactic of "greenrinsing," where firms initially set ambitious net-zero goals to attract investment before quietly weakening or abandoning them. Major multinational corporations across energy, aviation, and retail sectors were flagged for diluting sustainability targets or relying on unproven technologies like carbon capture to justify continued fossil fuel expansion. This trend highlights a growing disconnect between corporate marketing narratives and actual environmental performance, as companies prioritize short-term investor appeal over genuine decarbonization. Simultaneously, regulatory bodies in regions such as Australia, Singapore, and the UK increased enforcement actions, issuing penalties and banning misleading advertisements for vague claims about "clean" gas, biofuels, and carbon neutrality. Despite these strides, inconsistencies remained globally; while national regulators in Europe and Asia tightened restrictions, broader political shifts led to the withdrawal of some proposed European regulations. The divergence in enforcement underscores the complexity of combating greenwashing, as legal outcomes often hinge on specific jurisdictional definitions of economic injury and substantiation requirements. This article is crucial for understanding greenwashing because it demonstrates how the practice has evolved from simple exaggeration to complex strategic obfuscation, such as rolling back deforestation cut-off dates or labeling toxic fuels as renewable. It illustrates that even when courts penalize specific instances, widespread issues like the misuse of carbon credits and the promotion of false solutions persist. The data serves as a vital warning that without rigorous, harmonized global standards, companies can continue to manipulate sustainability language to maintain consumer trust while delaying necessary climate action.
