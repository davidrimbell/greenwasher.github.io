Italia multa con un millón de euros a Shein por 'greenwashing': "Una fachada de ecologismo basada en falsedades"

Italia multa con un millón de euros a Shein por 'greenwashing': "Una fachada de ecologismo basada en falsedades"

Italy’s Competition Authority fined Shein one million euros for greenwashing, citing misleading environmental claims about its “eco-friendly” product lines. The regulator determined that vague assertions regarding recyclability and the use of sustainable fibers were unsubstantiated, thereby falsely leading consumers to believe that the entire product range was genuinely sustainable, when in fact only a small fraction of it incorporated such materials. Moreover, the company’s broad commitments to reduce emissions contradicted the actual rise in its carbon footprint. By marketing products from the highly polluting fast-fashion sector under a veneer of ecological responsibility, Shein engaged in deceptive practices that obscured the true environmental impact of its manufacturing processes and business model. This case underscores the critical importance of verifying sustainability claims against actual operational data. It illustrates how retailers often exploit ambiguous terminology to create an illusion of eco-consciousness, deceiving consumers who genuinely seek responsible purchasing options. Consequently, rigorous regulatory oversight is essential to hold companies accountable for the gap between their marketing narratives and their real-world environmental performance.
