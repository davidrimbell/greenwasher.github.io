Greenway sues SWBTS, board chair, claims ‘defamation’ has made him ‘unemployable’ • Biblical Recorder

This article details a legal dispute between a former seminary president and the institution’s board, centering on allegations of financial manipulation and retaliatory removal. The former president claims that necessary repairs to the presidential residence were exploited by trustees to fabricate financial justifications for his ousting, despite his knowledge of the property's condition prior to his tenure. He further alleges that leadership concealed significant funding shortfalls and blocked his attempts to address administrative issues, ultimately forcing his resignation through coercive negotiation tactics. The conflict escalated over political disagreements and accusations of mismanagement, with the former president asserting that the seminary’s leadership violated their separation agreement by refusing to issue a joint public statement. This refusal highlights a broader pattern of controlling information and shaping public perception to protect the institution’s reputation while shifting blame onto the departing leader. The board dismisses these claims as baseless, maintaining that their actions were transparent and properly audicated, thereby framing the lawsuit as an unfounded attempt at financial extortion. While this narrative does not involve environmental sustainability, it serves as a critical case study in the mechanics of greenwashing. It illustrates how organizations can weaponize transparency—or the lack thereof—to conceal internal failures, such as financial mismanagement or ethical breaches, behind a veneer of procedural correctness. The article underscores how powerful entities may manipulate public statements and audit processes to deflect accountability, demonstrating that "greenwashing" extends beyond ecological claims to include broader strategies of reputational defense and narrative control.
