Greens call for Audit Scotland to Probe Highland Council over net zero targets

Highland Council’s decision to delay its net-zero commitment by two decades raises serious concerns about the integrity of its climate pledges. By shifting the target from 2025 to 2045, the council acknowledges the impossibility of meeting its original, ambitious goals, mirroring the Scottish Government’s own failure to meet national 2030 emissions targets. This strategic postponement suggests that initial emergency declarations were not backed by feasible plans, risking the perception that such actions are merely symbolic rather than substantive commitments to environmental protection. The situation highlights a critical gap between stated ambitions and actual operational progress. The council’s previous strategy focused only on its own internal operations, excluding the broader emissions within its jurisdiction. Consequently, the delayed deadline underscores a lack of meaningful action over several years, leaving the public questioning whether local authorities are genuinely combating climate change or simply adjusting timelines to avoid accountability for unmet objectives. This case is highly relevant to greenwashing as it exemplifies how organizations may declare crises to appear proactive while simultaneously undermining those claims through unrealistic targets and subsequent delays. When public bodies abandon early pledges without transparent justification or robust alternative strategies, they risk eroding public trust. Scrutinizing such discrepancies is essential to distinguish genuine sustainability efforts from superficial branding that fails to deliver tangible environmental results.
