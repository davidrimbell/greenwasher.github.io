"La miel solar no existe": Justicia Alimentaria denuncia que el "ecopostureo" de Endesa viola la normativa europea

The article challenges the "solar honey" narrative promoted by major energy companies Endesa and Iberdrola, which present apiculture projects in their photovoltaic parks as a harmonious solution that integrates clean energy and biodiversity. Although the industry labels this practice as a mutual benefit for the local economy and the environment, the Food Justice report exposes that this apparent symbiosis conceals a real competition for fertile agricultural land, a scarce and vital resource. The central criticism points out that the compatibility between solar monocultures and agriculture is often a practical failure, with evident limitations in key crops such as wheat or strawberries, while the displacement of traditional farmers is ignored. The analysis highlights that corporations use this strategy to divert attention from the expropriation of productive lands, falsely arguing that there is no conflict between producing food and energy, when in reality, solar megaprojects prioritize private profit over food sovereignty. This dynamic is relevant to greenwashing because it constitutes a clear example of eco-posturing and deceptive business practices. By using terminology that evokes traditional quality certifications without meeting their standards, companies manipulate consumer perception to wash their environmental image. The report concludes that this is not genuine sustainable innovation, but a marketing technique that allows major energy oligopolies to appropriate the discourse of decarbonization while continuing an extractive model that degrades rural territory.
