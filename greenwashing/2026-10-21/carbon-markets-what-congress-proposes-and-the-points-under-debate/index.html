Carbon markets: what Congress proposes and the points under debate

Argentina is advancing in the development of a specific legal framework to regulate carbon markets, aiming to provide predictability and facilitate financing for climate mitigation projects. Although the national government already has a prior national strategy, current legislative initiatives in the Senate and the Chamber of Deputies seek to structure the rules of the game by distinguishing between voluntary and compliance markets, establishing rigorous certifications and controls that verify the actual reduction of emissions. The legislative debate reflects deep tensions over the distribution of competencies between the national government and the provinces, as well as over control and transparency mechanisms. While some bills prioritize integration into capital markets and the attraction of foreign investment, environmental organizations and provincial representatives demand strict safeguards to protect biodiversity and the rights of local communities. This divergence underscores the difficulty of balancing economic interests with genuine environmental integrity. This article is crucial for understanding greenwashing because it illustrates how the absence or weakness of robust regulatory frameworks can turn carbon credits into tools for image washing. Without effective guarantees of traceability, additionality, and respect for local rights, these markets risk allowing companies or states to finance superficial projects while continuing their high emissions, creating a false perception of sustainability. Therefore, the fight against greenwashing depends on the quality and rigor of these new laws.
