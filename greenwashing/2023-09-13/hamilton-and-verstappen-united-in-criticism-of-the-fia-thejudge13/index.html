Hamilton and Verstappen united in criticism of the FIA | thejudge13

Formula 1’s push toward its 2030 net-zero goal has triggered significant resistance from drivers like Max Verstappen and Lewis Hamilton, who argue that sustainability measures are degrading the racing product. The introduction of stricter tyre allocations and qualifying formats, designed to reduce the massive logistical carbon footprint of transporting thousands of tyres, has been criticized for limiting track time and fan engagement. Drivers feel these constraints prioritize environmental targets over sporting quality, creating tension between the sport’s ecological ambitions and its entertainment value. The controversy highlights a critical disconnect in greenwashing narratives within motorsport: while regulatory bodies claim to tackle waste, the solutions often fail to address the most egregious sources of environmental harm. Drivers have pointed out that the sport still discards vast quantities of fully usable wet tyres after every weekend, a practice far more wasteful than the limited tyre usage protocols being debated. This suggests that the chosen sustainability initiatives may be superficial fixes that inconvenience the sport’s stars while leaving deeper, systemic inefficiencies unchecked. This article is relevant to greenwashing because it exposes how environmental pledges can be used to justify operational changes that prioritize perceived progress over genuine effectiveness. By focusing on complex logistical reductions like tyre allocation rather than obvious waste streams like unused wet tyres, the FIA and Pirelli risk appearing to solve minor problems to mask major failures. This dynamic illustrates how corporations might adopt visible, albeit limited, eco-friendly measures to deflect criticism, ultimately serving their public image rather than delivering substantial ecological benefits.
