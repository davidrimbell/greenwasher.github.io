Even public transit isn’t immune to ‘total greenwashing’

Recent advertisements on Vancouver buses claim natural gas reduces emissions, a assertion widely criticized as deceptive greenwashing by environmental experts. These campaigns, funded by industry lobbyists, ignore scientific evidence that methane leakage from gas infrastructure makes it as climate-damaging as coal. By omitting this crucial context, the ads mislead the public about the true environmental impact of fossil fuels, contributing to harmful misinformation. The persistence of these advertisements highlights a regulatory gap in Canada, which lacks strict limits on fugitive methane emissions unlike its international counterparts. Critics argue that allowing such misleading pro-gas messaging on public transit normalizes climate denial and obscures the health and ecological harms associated with gas production. This disparity between reported data and actual leakage underscores the systemic nature of the deception, as the industry profits while the public remains uninformed about the severity of the climate threat. This controversy illustrates the urgent need for public institutions to scrutinize advertising content rather than relying solely on legal compliance. While some Canadian cities have begun banning fossil fuel ads to protect public discourse, Vancouver continues to host them, reflecting a broader failure to prioritize climate science over corporate influence. Recognizing these ads as greenwashing is essential for holding transport organizations accountable and ensuring public spaces do not become platforms for industry propaganda that undermines climate action.
