'Greenshouting’ risks amplifying spin, not transparency, in Asia: experts

The article argues that the Western trend of “greenshouting,” where companies actively highlight their sustainability achievements, is often ineffective or counterproductive in Asia. Instead of fostering transparency, this approach frequently exacerbates greenwashing by allowing corporations to treat Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) metrics as image management tools. Deep-seated cultural norms prioritizing reputation and face-saving lead firms to sanitize disclosures and manage narratives rather than provide honest, verifiable accounts of their environmental impact. This dynamic creates a significant gap between public commitments and actual performance, resulting in what experts term “theatrical” sustainability. Because reporting functions are often situated within public relations teams focused on storytelling rather than risk interrogation, companies produce polished but substantively hollow reports. Consequently, mandatory disclosure regulations are easily met on paper without ensuring robust accountability, enabling businesses to maintain control over their image while avoiding scrutiny of their true operational realities. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the shift toward “greenhushing,” where firms deliberately downplay their efforts to avoid backlash or because consumer demand prioritizes price over ethics. With many brands removing visible sustainability messaging to focus on operational integration, the risk of subtle greenwashing persists. This article highlights that without structural governance changes and verifiable data, increased vocalization or silence alike can obscure rather than clarify corporate environmental responsibilities, making it harder for stakeholders to distinguish genuine progress from strategic omission.
