How RF+ Certification Ends Greenwashing in Food

Stories selected and summarised by AI, not reviewed by a person. Every source is linked.

Shoppers face a tricky market. Many environmental claims hide the truth. This confusion helps and hurts buyers. It affects how people think about and choose products.

Good news: Careful shoppers push companies to change. Businesses want to be open. They use strict rules to prove their claims. For example, the launch of RF+ certification shows how new rules stop lies in the food industry. This certification checks if farms use regenerative agriculture. It uses independent auditors to verify this. It also follows European rules against misleading ads. These rules give brands legal safety. They give shoppers proof of healthy soil and wildlife. This shift makes it easier to check products. You can make smart choices without reading confusing marketing words.

Bad news: Fake claims cost buyers time and money. Shoppers often waste effort checking products that are not truly green. A study by the European Commission found that 42% of green claims were exaggerated, false, or deceptive. This shows the risk of buying products that do not help the earth. Also, fake marketing hurts trust. When shoppers see misleading labels, they doubt all green claims. This doubt slows down the use of real sustainable options. It makes it harder for people to buy things that match their values.

To fix this, shoppers should look for proof, not just words. Choose certifications that need independent checks. Look for standards that check carbon neutrality or biodegradability. These rules ensure your money helps the environment. It stops fake activism. By asking for transparency, you protect the market. You help create a healthier planet. This approach changes shopping. It becomes a confident step toward real change.
