¿Qué garantiza un certificado ecológico y cómo identificarlo?
The article highlights that while organic agriculture offers genuine environmental and health benefits, its economic premium has incentivized deceptive marketing practices. Many brands exploit the growing consumer demand for sustainable goods by employing greenwashing techniques, falsely labeling conventional products as organic. This manipulation undermines the integrity of the market, as consumers pay higher prices for benefits that do not exist, effectively deceiving those who wish to support genuine ecological practices. To combat this deception, the text emphasizes that accurate labeling is the primary guarantee of authenticity. Regulations require strict compliance with specific criteria for using terms like "organic" or "ecological," ensuring that consumers can distinguish legitimate products from misleading alternatives. The European Organic Logo serves as a key indicator, confirming that products contain at least 95% organic ingredients and adhere to rigorous standards regarding production and storage. Relevance to greenwashing lies in the necessity of visual verification. The article explains how specific seals, such as those from Galicia’s regulatory council, provide official guarantees of control and inspection. By understanding these distinct markers and origin codes, consumers can identify whether a product truly meets organic standards or is merely part of a marketing campaign designed to exploit environmental concerns. Recognizing these details is crucial for avoiding being misled by false claims.

Reading Buses accused of hypocrisy as most of fleet still uses diesel
The article highlights a sharp conflict between Reading Buses’ operational reality and its corporate climate messaging, illustrating the core tension in greenwashing. A campaigner accused the council-owned company of hypocrisy for promoting an open-top bus despite relying heavily on a diesel fleet. This juxtaposition raises critical questions about whether promoting public transport via high-emission vehicles undermines broader net-zero targets, suggesting a disconnect between political declarations and tangible environmental actions. Critics argue that displaying a diesel vehicle in a town with severe air pollution constitutes misleading marketing, implying environmental stewardship where none exists. Although the company defends its use of diesel by claiming it removes far more cars from the road than it emits, this argument often serves as a deflection. By focusing on relative benefits rather than absolute emissions reductions, the bus company risks appearing to prioritize image over the urgent need to eliminate fossil fuel dependence, a hallmark tactic used to mask continued harmful practices. This case is highly relevant to greenwashing because it demonstrates how organizations can use selective facts to create a false perception of sustainability. The company emphasizes its small number of biogas and electric buses while downplaying the majority diesel fleet. This strategy allows them to claim progress without making the costly, immediate transition required to truly align with climate goals, thereby misleading the public about the actual speed and sincerity of their decarbonization efforts.
