Green washing: The Hindu Editorial on amendments and the Forest (Conservation) Amendment Bill, 2023

The 2023 Forest Conservation Amendment Bill redefines forest land to exclude unclassified areas, significantly weakening protections established by a 1996 Supreme Court judgment. By limiting regulatory scope to land officially recorded as forest after 1980, the legislation allows states to repurpose vast tracts of ecologically significant woodlands for industrial and strategic military purposes without central approval. This shift prioritizes state-led industrial expansion over the preservation of natural ecosystems, raising concerns about the long-term integrity of India’s biodiversity. Critics argue that these amendments incentivize commercial tree plantations rather than natural forest regeneration. While this approach helps meet quantitative carbon sink targets, it risks substituting complex, resilient natural habitats with monoculture plantations vulnerable to market forces. The reliance on artificial carbon stocks is deemed unstable, as strong financial incentives may lead to their rapid exploitation or conversion, thereby failing to provide the permanent ecological security that genuine forest conservation offers. This debate is crucial for understanding greenwashing, as it highlights how policy changes can manipulate statistical definitions to claim environmental progress while eroding actual ecological protection. By redefining what constitutes a forest, the bill enables governments to appear compliant with climate goals through superficial afforestation, masking the continued degradation of natural forests. This disconnect between quantitative metrics and qualitative ecological health exemplifies how regulatory loopholes facilitate the greenwashing of industrial development under the guise of sustainable growth.
