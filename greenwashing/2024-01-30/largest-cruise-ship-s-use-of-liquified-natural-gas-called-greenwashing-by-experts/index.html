Largest Cruise Ship's Use Of Liquified Natural Gas Called Greenwashing By Experts

Largest Cruise Ship's Use Of Liquified Natural Gas Called Greenwashing By Experts

The launch of the world’s largest cruise ship highlights the cruise industry’s persistent reliance on liquefied natural gas, a practice experts widely condemn as greenwashing. By marketing LNG as an environmentally friendly alternative, companies create a misleading perception of sustainability while continuing to drive significant climate impact. This narrative obscures the reality that the industry’s carbon footprint remains disproportionately high compared to other forms of tourism. The core of this deception lies in the phenomenon of methane slip, where unburned methane escapes during combustion. Although LNG reduces carbon dioxide emissions, the potent nature of methane traps significantly more heat in the atmosphere over the short term. Consequently, the overall greenhouse gas impact of these vessels may exceed that of traditional fuels, rendering the touted environmental benefits negligible or even counterproductive when measured against global warming potential. This case is relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how the industry invests in false climate solutions rather than genuine innovations. Experts urge a shift toward renewable hydrogen or methanol, which offer true emission reductions without the hidden costs of methane leakage. By prioritizing LNG, the sector fails to address the fundamental intensity of its operations, continuing to exploit environmental concerns for marketing advantage while delaying necessary technological transitions.
