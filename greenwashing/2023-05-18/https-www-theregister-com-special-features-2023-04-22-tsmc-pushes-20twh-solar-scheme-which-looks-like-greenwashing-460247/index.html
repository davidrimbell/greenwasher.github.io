Greenwashing
ES EN

TSMC and ARK Power announced a partnership to source renewable energy, but the framing obscures the actual scale. By aggregating twenty years of energy totals, TSMC creates an illusion of massive impact, whereas the real annual commitment is significantly smaller. This discrepancy highlights how companies often use cumulative figures to exaggerate their environmental contributions, misleading stakeholders about the immediate tangible benefits of their sustainability initiatives. The initiative also relies on a joint procurement model to reduce suppliers’ carbon footprints, which directly impacts TSMC’s scope-3 emissions. While this strategy helps TSMC meet its net-zero goals by lowering indirect emissions across its supply chain, it functions less like a charitable effort and more like a strategic maneuver to manage its own reporting metrics. By shifting some financial and operational burdens to suppliers through bundled services, TSMC ensures broader compliance without solely bearing the cost, effectively externalizing parts of its decarbonization effort. This approach is relevant to greenwashing because it demonstrates how corporations can claim substantial progress on climate goals through complex supply chain adjustments rather than direct operational changes. The emphasis on aggregate numbers and indirect emission reductions allows TSMC to project a greener image while its total emissions remain considerable. Such tactics risk obscuring the true environmental impact, prioritizing narrative control over transparent, significant reduction in actual carbon output.
