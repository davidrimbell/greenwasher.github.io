Genç (BBVA) pide ser cuidadoso con las acusaciones de 'ecoblanqueo' para no desmotivar a los bancos

Onur Genç, CEO of BBVA, warns against the risks of indiscriminately accusing banks of greenwashing, noting that excessive pressure can discourage voluntary efforts toward sustainability. He argues that the focus should prioritize detecting bad intentions rather than conducting exhaustive reviews of every data point, thereby preventing institutions from abandoning their climate commitments out of fear of negative repercussions. The article’s relevance to the greenwashing debate lies in how regulatory rigidity can affect banking transparency. By calling for greater standardization of rules and a focus on the energy transition rather than current emissions, the aim is to balance accountability with the need to keep financial actors engaged. This stance questions whether excessive rigor in identifying greenwashing is actually hindering climate action, or whether it simply calls for better evaluation tools. Finally, the importance of coordinating regulations and not neglecting the social dimension of sustainability is emphasized. The alignment among banking sector leaders in calling for appropriate incentives and fair treatment during the transition reflects a fundamental tension: ensuring that scrutiny of greenwashing does not become an obstacle to green financial innovation and social inclusion.
