Greenwashing
ES EN

The Swiss Commission for Loyalty ruled that FIFA’s claims of a carbon-neutral 2022 World Cup were misleading, highlighting a significant discrepancy between marketing and environmental reality. This decision exposes the core mechanics of greenwashing, where organizations utilize vague, unproven, or non-compliant offsetting methods to create a false impression of sustainability without undertaking substantive reductions in their actual carbon footprint. Critics demonstrated that FIFA relied on low-quality carbon credits and drastically undercounted total emissions by excluding stadium construction impacts. By assigning only a negligible fraction of construction emissions to the tournament, the organizer manipulated data to appear compliant with standards that were never truly met. This strategic obfuscation allowed FIFA to project an eco-friendly image while avoiding accountability for the true scale of its environmental impact. This case is highly relevant to greenwashing as it illustrates how major events exploit regulatory loopholes and opaque accounting to disguise ecological harm. It serves as a cautionary example of how claims of neutrality, when unsupported by rigorous verification and realistic calculations, function as mere PR tools. The ruling underscores the urgent need for stricter oversight and transparent metrics to prevent organizations from capitalizing on environmental concerns without delivering genuine sustainability.
