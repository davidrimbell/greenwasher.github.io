Greenwashing
ES EN

The article argues that the Scottish government’s promotion of hydrogen as a key industrial opportunity constitutes greenwashing, primarily by incorrectly labeling fossil-fuel-derived "grey" and "blue" hydrogen as low-carbon solutions. By relying on methane and partial carbon capture, these methods perpetuate environmental harm, yet they are being championed to justify continued investment in the oil and gas sector. This classification allows major energy corporations to maintain their market dominance and delay a genuine transition to renewables, effectively using hydrogen rhetoric to mask the continued extraction of fossil fuels. Furthermore, the narrative highlights the inefficiency of hydrogen technology, particularly for heating, when compared to direct electrification via heat pumps. Critics contend that the government is being misled by lobbying efforts from oil giants like BP and Shell, which have engaged extensively with ministers to promote this costly and less effective alternative. The substantial public funding allocated to this industry is viewed not as a climate solution, but as a subsidy for polluters who are desperate to extend the lifespan of oil and gas infrastructure under the guise of net-zero compliance. This situation is relevant to greenwashing because it demonstrates how political entities can legitimize harmful industrial practices through selective definitions and marketing hype. The government’s refusal to rule out fossil-based hydrogen and its engagement with the very industries it should be regulating illustrates a systemic failure to prioritize verifiable environmental benefits over corporate interests. Ultimately, the case serves as a cautionary example of how "low carbon" labels can be exploited to distract from the urgent need for efficient, renewable energy alternatives.
