The Human Cost of Net Zero - American Renaissance

The Human Cost of Net Zero - American Renaissance

The article critiques the uncritical acceptance of Jeremiah Thoronka’s piezoelectric energy device, highlighting how Western green elites prioritized his narrative over verifiable facts. Journalists discovered that his celebrated pilot project had been dismantled with no evidence of its existence elsewhere, yet award organizers and media outlets continued to champion him as a clean-energy innovator. This suggests a willingness to overlook reality to support a desired outcome. Thoronka appears to succeed through "psycho-engineering" rather than genuine technological breakthroughs, feeding the Western establishment’s wishful belief that Africa’s energy needs can be met instantly without fossil fuels or nuclear power. The lack of documentation or functional devices indicates that the acclaim is based on appealing to environmentalist fantasies rather than scientific merit. Consequently, the accolades he received reflect a desire to believe in easy decarbonization solutions. This case is highly relevant to greenwashing because it demonstrates how unverified claims can gain legitimacy through prestigious awards and media coverage. It reveals a systemic vulnerability where greenwashing thrives not just through corporate deception, but through the complicity of institutions that prefer comforting narratives over rigorous scrutiny. The phenomenon illustrates how the green movement can inadvertently validate superficial or non-existent technologies, undermining genuine climate action by rewarding performative innovation over substantive progress.
