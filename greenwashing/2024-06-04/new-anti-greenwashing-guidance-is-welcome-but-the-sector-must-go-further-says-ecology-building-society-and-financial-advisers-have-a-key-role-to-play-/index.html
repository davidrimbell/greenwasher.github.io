New anti-greenwashing guidance is welcome, but the sector must go further says Ecology Building Society – and financial advisers have a key role to play - IFA Magazine

The article highlights the UK Financial Conduct Authority’s new anti-greenwashing rules, which mandate robust evidence for sustainability claims to protect investors and ensure a level playing field. While this regulatory step is welcomed as a necessary foundation for genuine green finance, it is viewed merely as a preliminary measure rather than a comprehensive solution. The focus on credible claims aims to clarify what constitutes truly green investments, helping to curb misleading marketing in an evolving market. However, the piece argues that transparency must go beyond regulatory compliance to become an industry norm. Many savers remain unaware of how their money is used, creating a trust gap that voluntary principles alone cannot fully bridge. The author emphasizes that full visibility into where funds are allocated is essential for building genuine confidence. Without explicit transparency, customers cannot make informed decisions that align with their ethical values, leaving the sector vulnerable to continued skepticism and potential greenwashing despite new guidelines. Ultimately, financial advisers are positioned as critical catalysts for this transformation, tasked with educating clients and directing them toward institutions that offer clear, verifiable sustainable options. By leveraging emerging standards and proactively seeking out transparent providers, advisers can empower clients to influence responsible business behavior. This shift is vital as public demand for ethical investing grows, requiring the financial sector to evolve from merely labeling products as green to demonstrably proving their positive environmental impact through open and accountable practices.
