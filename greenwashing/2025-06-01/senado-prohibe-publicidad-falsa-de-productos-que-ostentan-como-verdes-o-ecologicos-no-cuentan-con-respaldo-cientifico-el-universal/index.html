Senado prohíbe publicidad falsa de productos que ostentan como “verdes” o “ecológicos”; no cuentan con respaldo científico | El Universal

Senado prohíbe publicidad falsa de productos que ostentan como “verdes” o “ecológicos”; no cuentan con respaldo científico | El Universal

The Senate has approved a measure to ban environmental claims that lack verifiable support, directly targeting greenwashing as deceptive advertising. This reform aims to prohibit labels such as “eco-friendly” or “climate neutral” unless they are backed by solid evidence, preventing companies from misleading consumers who seek truly sustainable products. The initiative highlights how corporations often exploit consumer ignorance by attaching false sustainability credentials to harmful items, such as plastic bags labeled as biodegradable. By strictly regulating these assertions, the legislation seeks to dismantle the distortion of environmental concepts driven by corporate interests, ensuring that marketing does not perpetuate harmful production models under the guise of ecological responsibility. This development is highly relevant to greenwashing because it establishes a legal framework to combat misleading environmental marketing. It shifts the burden of proof onto companies, demanding transparency and scientific validation for their sustainability claims. Consequently, it protects consumers from manipulation while encouraging genuine corporate accountability in addressing environmental crises through honest communication rather than superficial branding strategies.
