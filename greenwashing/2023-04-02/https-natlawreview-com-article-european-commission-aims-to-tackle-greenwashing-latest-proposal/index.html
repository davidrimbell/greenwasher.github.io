Greenwashing
ES EN

The European Commission has proposed the Green Claims Directive to combat misleading environmental assertions by establishing rigorous standards for substantiating and verifying green claims. This legislative move seeks to ensure that information provided to consumers is reliable, comparable, and verifiable, thereby creating a fair market where companies making genuine sustainability efforts are properly recognized and rewarded by the public. The proposal mandates that all environmental claims, whether specific or generic, must be based on recognized scientific evidence and assess the entire lifecycle of a product. It requires third-party verification by accredited independent bodies before claims can be used in commercial communications. This ensures that assertions are accurate across all significant impacts and prevents vague or unsubstantiated statements from misleading consumers, while also applying to non-EU businesses targeting European markets. This initiative is highly relevant to greenwashing discussions as it represents a significant shift toward formalized global regulatory responses to deceptive marketing practices. By harmonizing standards across the EU and mandating external certification, the directive addresses the historical lack of accountability in sustainability reporting. It aligns with similar enforcement trends worldwide, signaling that voluntary sustainability labels without rigorous, third-party backing will no longer be sufficient to legitimize corporate environmental narratives.
