First transatlantic flight with 100% ‘sustainable’ fuel is greenwashing, critics say

The transatlantic flight using 100% sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) has sparked intense debate between the aviation industry and environmental campaigners. While airlines and politicians hail the trip as a milestone toward net-zero emissions, critics argue it is a misleading marketing exercise that obscures the reality of current aviation impacts. This conflict highlights the fundamental disconnect between industry optimism and scientific skepticism regarding the viability of biofuels for long-haul travel. Experts emphasize that SAFs are chemically similar to conventional jet fuel, meaning they emit the same amount of carbon dioxide at the point of combustion. The perceived environmental benefits rely entirely on reductions achieved during the production phase, which functions similarly to a carbon offset rather than a true solution. Consequently, claims of "guilt-free flying" are deemed scientifically inaccurate, as the technology does not address the immediate carbon output of the flight itself. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the potential for this narrative to distract from more effective climate actions, such as drastically reducing air travel. Critics warn that promoting SAF as a primary decarbonization strategy risks normalizing excessive flying and diverting attention from the unsustainable nature of scaling biofuel production. By portraying a limited technological workaround as a comprehensive solution, the industry may delay necessary systemic changes, thereby perpetuating environmental harm under the guise of innovation.
