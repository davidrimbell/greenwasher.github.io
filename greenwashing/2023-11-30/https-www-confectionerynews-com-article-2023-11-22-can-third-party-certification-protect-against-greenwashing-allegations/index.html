Greenwashing
ES EN

This article highlights the growing legal risks companies face when relying on sustainability certifications to back their environmental marketing claims. Despite the common assumption that third-party certifications provide a complete shield against greenwashing allegations, attorneys warn that such credentials do not guarantee protection from litigation if the claims are misleading or poorly substantiated. The core issue lies in how these certifications are presented and the rigor behind the certifying body itself. Brands often invest heavily in these labels expecting security, yet they remain vulnerable if the underlying vetting processes are insufficient or if the marketing amplifies the certification’s scope beyond what is actually proven. Consequently, relying solely on a label without deep due diligence creates a false sense of security. Relevance to greenwashing is critical here, as regulators and litigants are increasingly scrutinizing the gap between marketed environmental benefits and verifiable reality. This case illustrates that greenwashing defenses are no longer sufficient; companies must ensure their claims are strictly aligned with actual practices and robust evidence to avoid legal consequences, moving beyond simple labeling toward genuine transparency.
