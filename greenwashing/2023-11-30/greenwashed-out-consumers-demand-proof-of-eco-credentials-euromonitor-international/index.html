‘Greenwashed out’ consumers demand proof of eco credentials: Euromonitor International

Consumers are experiencing severe "green fatigue," realizing that individual sustainable actions are insufficient to combat escalating climate catastrophes. Consequently, they are shifting the responsibility squarely onto corporations and governments, rejecting empty promises in favor of genuine collective action. This skepticism arises because people feel their efforts are overshadowed by the scale of environmental destruction, leading to a demand that businesses take full ownership of their carbon footprints and prove their impact rather than relying on misleading narratives. To rebuild trust, companies must prioritize complete transparency and concrete evidence over vague marketing claims. Since offsetting is no longer seen as adequate, organizations need to accurately map and reduce both direct and indirect emissions across their supply chains. Sharing specific, tangible data on packaging and operations allows consumers to see the direct link between their purchases and positive environmental outcomes, thereby validating the brand’s commitment through facts rather than greenwashing tactics. This shift is critical because regulatory frameworks are tightening globally to penalize deceptive environmental advertising. With stricter laws emerging in regions like the EU and South Korea, businesses must align their practices with credible certifications and affordable production methods. Ultimately, the article highlights that surviving in this new landscape requires democratizing sustainable options and embedding integrity into core business strategies, as consumers will no longer tolerate greenwashing amidst economic strain and environmental urgency.
