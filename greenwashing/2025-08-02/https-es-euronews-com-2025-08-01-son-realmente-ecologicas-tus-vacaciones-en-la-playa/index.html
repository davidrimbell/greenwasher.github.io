Greenwashing
EN ES

Coastal tourism in Europe, particularly in the Mediterranean, faces a sustainability crisis in which the sector generates a disproportionate environmental footprint, significantly contributing to global emissions and local pollution. Despite the perception of clean waters, tourism infrastructure degrades marine ecosystems and puts pressure on local communities, while climate change threatens the very viability of these destinations. This tension underscores the urgency of aligning the tourism economy with genuine ecological preservation, moving beyond traditional growth models. The relevance of this text lies in the proliferation of greenwashing within the travel industry. With more than half of green claims in the EU being vague or misleading, there is a high risk that companies will use superficial eco-labels to attract environmentally conscious consumers without implementing real structural changes. This misalignment between marketing and operational practice erodes public trust and dilutes genuine efforts toward sustainability, turning certification into a potential tool for manipulation rather than an environmental guarantee. To combat this, there is an emphasis on the need for transparent and verifiable criteria, such as independent audits and clear international standards, which can distinguish authenticity from greenwashing. True responsibility entails tangible changes in operations, such as protecting wildlife through strict protocols and reducing underwater noise. Only through rigorous scrutiny of operational practices, beyond advertising claims, can travelers ensure that their choices genuinely support marine conservation and do not simply facilitate environmental exploitation under an ecological facade.
