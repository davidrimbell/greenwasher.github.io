Coca-Cola HBC, Nestlé, Danone under fire over “greenwashing” claims

Major multinational corporations, including Coca-Cola Hellenic, Nestlé, and Danone, face legal action from prominent consumer and environmental groups for alleged greenwashing regarding their water bottle packaging. The complaint argues that marketing terms like “100% recyclable” and the use of eco-friendly imagery deceive consumers by creating a false sense of environmental neutrality. Despite these claims, the actual recycling rates for PET bottles in the EU are significantly lower, with only a small fraction of bottles being recycled back into new bottles, contradicting the sustainability narrative presented to the public. The core issue highlights a critical disconnect between corporate sustainability rhetoric and on-the-ground reality. Environmental advocates emphasize that single-use plastic is neither circular nor sustainable, as recycling infrastructure cannot keep pace with production volumes. By implying their products are environmentally neutral, these companies hinder consumers’ ability to make informed, responsible choices. This deceptive practice exploits the growing consumer desire for sustainable options, effectively manipulating public perception while the industry continues to rely heavily on non-circular, virgin plastic packaging. This case is highly relevant to greenwashing as it illustrates how vague or unsubstantiated environmental claims can mislead the public and delay meaningful regulatory action. It underscores the urgent need for standardized, rigorous verification of sustainability labels to prevent companies from exploiting green consumerism. The allegations challenge the industry’s self-regulatory approach, calling for stricter enforcement and clearer definitions of recyclability to ensure that marketing claims accurately reflect the environmental impact of products.
