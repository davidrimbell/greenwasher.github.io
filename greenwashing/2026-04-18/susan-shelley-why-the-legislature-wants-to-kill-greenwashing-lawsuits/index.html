Susan Shelley: Why the Legislature wants to kill ‘greenwashing’ lawsuits

This article argues that California’s proposed Assembly Bill 1911 threatens to institutionalize greenwashing by legally shielding companies that make misleading carbon neutrality claims. By creating a presumption of validity for environmental marketing based solely on purchasing credits from approved programs, the bill undermines consumer protection laws. This legislative move prioritizes industry stability over transparency, effectively allowing corporations to continue advertising eco-friendly practices without rigorous proof of actual emissions reductions or genuine ecological benefit. The core issue lies in the inherent flaws of the voluntary carbon market, which suffers from inaccurate accounting, double-counting, and opaque verification processes. The article illustrates how credits are often generated through speculative projects that may not even exist yet, relying on questionable methodologies rather than verified, immediate impact. Consequently, the "carbon neutral" label frequently serves as a sophisticated marketing tool that obscures the reality that companies are not altering their harmful operations, but merely buying permission to continue polluting. Relevance to greenwashing is critical because this legislation threatens to normalize deceptive environmental advertising. If passed, the bill would insulate companies from lawsuits alleging false advertising, thereby removing a key mechanism for exposing fraud in the carbon credit industry. The author warns that this is not merely about corporate accountability but represents a systemic effort to maintain public illusion, ensuring that the lucrative market for carbon offsets remains protected from scrutiny while consumers are misled into believing they are supporting a genuine green transition.
