OVO to end use of renewable energy certificates over greenwashing concerns - edie

OVO Energy has discontinued its investment in Renewable Energy Guarantees of Origin (REGOs), arguing that these instruments no longer drive additional renewable generation but instead inflate consumer costs. This strategic shift highlights a critical disconnect between public perception and reality; while many customers believe REGO-backed tariffs directly increase green supply, research indicates they merely offset high-carbon usage without expanding actual renewable capacity. By recognizing this limitation, OVO aims to redirect funds toward tangible energy efficiency upgrades and smart metering, thereby reducing bills and grid pressure while eliminating the illusion of green impact that characterizes common greenwashing practices. The article underscores the severe reputational and financial risks companies face when relying on vague green claims. OVO’s CEO explicitly labels greenwashing as an unaffordable luxury, emphasizing that the current marketing of REGO schemes misleads consumers into believing their homes are powered entirely by renewables when they are not. This discrepancy fuels widespread consumer skepticism, with a majority doubting the genuineness of marketed green tariffs. Consequently, the move away from REGOs serves as a corrective measure to restore trust, demonstrating that sustainability efforts must be grounded in verifiable results rather than financial instruments that offer no genuine environmental additionality. Finally, the piece advocates for industry-wide structural changes, including the introduction of a standardized kitemark for truly additive green energy deals. This proposed certification aims to combat misinformation and hold providers accountable, addressing the broader crisis of credibility in corporate sustainability claims. The relevance to greenwashing is profound, as it illustrates the urgent need for transparent, government-backed standards to distinguish authentic decarbonization strategies from deceptive marketing. Without such rigorous oversight and a shift toward actual efficiency improvements, the energy sector risks perpetuating hollow environmental promises that fail to meet legal climate commitments.
