Greenwashing companies should be 'named and shamed' to help consumers choose - The Big Issue

The report concludes that the UK’s reputation as a global climate leader is at risk due to weak net-zero transition targets and insufficient corporate accountability. To address this, it advocates for a "naming and shaming" policy that publicly identifies companies failing to meet their environmental pledges. This approach aims to protect public trust by exposing those who make unfounded claims, thereby leveraging economic incentives to force genuine compliance with credible regulatory frameworks. A critical finding is the significant gap between corporate aspirations and actual progress, with only a small fraction of major firms adhering to scientifically defined standards. The analysis highlights that consistency and achievability are currently lacking in medium and long-term planning. Without robust, science-based targets that are uniformly applied across sectors, the economic benefits of the green transition will remain out of reach for the nation. This issue is central to the fight against greenwashing because it exposes how vague or unverified corporate claims undermine the integrity of climate action. By calling for a strong government regulator to monitor progress and enforce transparency, the article underscores the urgent need to distinguish genuine sustainability efforts from misleading marketing. Ultimately, establishing trust through rigorous oversight is essential to prevent businesses from exploiting vague environmental promises without delivering real-world results.
