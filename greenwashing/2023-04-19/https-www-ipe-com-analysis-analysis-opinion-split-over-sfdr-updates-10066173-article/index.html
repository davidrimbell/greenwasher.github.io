Greenwashing
ES EN

The European Commission’s latest guidance on the Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation clarifies that financial institutions must develop and disclose their own methodologies for defining sustainable investments, rather than adhering to uniform regulatory standards. This approach emphasizes transparency in how firms assess contributions to environmental goals and adherence to governance practices, effectively shifting the burden of definition onto market participants. While this offers immediate interpretive clarity, it confirms the regulation’s role as a disclosure regime rather than a standard-setting one. This decentralization raises significant concerns regarding the efficacy of current sustainability claims. Critics argue that allowing institutions to calculate sustainable investments using arbitrary methods renders comparative analysis impossible and undermines consumer trust. The lack of standardized criteria means that similar financial products may be labeled sustainable based on vastly different internal metrics, making it difficult for clients and regulators to verify the actual environmental or social impact of these investments. The article is highly relevant to greenwashing because it highlights a regulatory framework that prioritizes procedural transparency over substantive impact. By permitting firms to self-define their sustainability metrics without objective benchmarks, the system potentially enables companies to present ambiguous or misleading environmental claims as credible. This structural loophole allows for the illusion of sustainability, where the focus remains on explaining internal methodologies rather than demonstrating genuine adherence to rigorous, comparable environmental standards, thereby facilitating the very greenwashing the regulation aims to prevent.
