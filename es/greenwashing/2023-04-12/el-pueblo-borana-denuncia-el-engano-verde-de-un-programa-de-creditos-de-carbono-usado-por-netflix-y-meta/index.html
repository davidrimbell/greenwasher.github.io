El pueblo borana denuncia el "engaño verde" de un programa de créditos de carbono usado por Netflix y Meta

The article exposes a stark example of greenwashing, revealing how Northern Rangelands Trust’s carbon offset project in Kenya falsely claims environmental benefit while actively dismantling indigenous Borana communities. By replacing traditional nomadic pastoralism with centralized control without proper consent, the initiative prioritizes corporate interests over human rights, constituting a severe ecological and social deception. The certification body’s suspension of credits signals recognition of these fundamental flaws, validating the indigenous group’s assertion that the project is an environmental hoax. This case highlights the critical danger of carbon markets that ignore land rights, demonstrating how corporate compensation schemes can serve as vehicles for resource extraction rather than genuine sustainability. This narrative is crucial for understanding greenwashing because it illustrates the disconnect between certified carbon credits and their actual on-the-ground impact. It proves that without free, prior, and informed consent, such projects may exacerbate inequality and displace vulnerable populations under the guise of fighting climate change, thereby undermining both ecological integrity and social justice.
