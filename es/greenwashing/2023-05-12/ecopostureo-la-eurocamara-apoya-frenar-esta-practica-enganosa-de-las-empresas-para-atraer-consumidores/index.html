"Ecopostureo": la Eurocámara apoya frenar esta práctica engañosa de las empresas para atraer consumidores

The European Parliament has approved legislation aimed at combating greenwashing by banning misleading environmental claims lacking scientific proof. This move restricts vague slogans like "natural" or "carbon-neutral," ensuring consumers are not deceived by ecoposturing. It also prohibits relying solely on carbon offsets or making false durability promises, thereby increasing transparency and accountability in marketing practices. Furthermore, the directive targets planned obsolescence and restrictive repair practices to promote product longevity. Manufacturers cannot limit functionality due to third-party parts or intentionally shorten product lifespans. By demanding clearer information on repairability and warranty extensions, the law incentivizes companies to design durable goods, shifting market focus from disposable items to sustainable, long-lasting alternatives. This regulation is crucial for combating greenwashing because it establishes a legal framework that punishes deceptive environmental marketing. It empowers consumers with accurate data, forcing businesses to substantiate their sustainability efforts with evidence rather than empty rhetoric. Consequently, it fosters a genuine transition toward ecological responsibility, reducing the gap between advertised eco-friendliness and actual environmental impact.
