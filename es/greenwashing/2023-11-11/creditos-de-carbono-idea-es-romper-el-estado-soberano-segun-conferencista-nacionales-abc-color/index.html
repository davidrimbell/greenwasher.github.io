Créditos de carbono: “idea es romper el Estado soberano”, según conferencista - Nacionales - ABC Color

The article warns that Paraguay’s carbon credit system may facilitate a neocolonial agenda, where international NGOs impose supranational structures on national sovereignty. It argues this green movement is an ideologically planned mechanism rather than an organic sociological shift. These organizations effectively act as neogovernmental entities, selling detrimental solutions to local producers while preserving colonial power dynamics. The author urges producers to resist passive acceptance by developing autonomous national projects instead. This is relevant to greenwashing because it reframes environmental policies not as ecological progress, but as strategic tools for geopolitical control. It highlights how corporate or foreign interests mask political domination under the guise of environmental protection, challenging the genuine intent behind such initiatives.
