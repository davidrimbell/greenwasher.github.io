Cuando no crear también contamina: el arte contra el greenwashing

Josep Piñol’s conceptual art project exposes the absurdity of voluntary carbon markets by converting a non-existent construction into certified emission reductions. By simply signing a commitment not to build a monumental installation, the artist generated thousands of carbon credits without physical intervention. This performance highlights how the system allows entities to profit from inaction, treating the mere promise of reduced pollution as a tangible, tradable asset rather than a substantive environmental effort. The work critiques the opacity and lack of regulation in these markets, where certifications often lack real-world value. Drawing parallels to historical indulgences, Piñol illustrates how corporations use these credits to cleanse their images while continuing harmful practices. The artist’s decision to release most credits prevents their use for corporate greenwashing, underscoring the risk that these mechanisms serve more as financial speculation tools than genuine climate solutions, thereby perpetuating the very emissions they claim to mitigate. This article is highly relevant to understanding greenwashing because it demonstrates how abstract financial instruments can legitimize continued pollution. It reveals that the credibility of sustainability claims often rests on faith rather than verification, allowing companies to buy the "right to sin." By showing that even an artist’s theoretical non-action can be monetized as eco-friendly, the piece underscores the urgent need to scrutinize the actual impact of corporate carbon offsets versus their marketing value.
