Bruselas propone acabar con el 'ecopostureo"' al utilizarse un etiquetado confuso o falso con supuestas certificaciones medioambientales - Agroinformacion

The European Commission has introduced legislation to combat greenwashing by targeting the widespread use of vague, confusing, or false environmental labels. With hundreds of distinct eco-labels currently in circulation, a significant majority make unverified or ambiguous claims, misleading consumers who seek to make sustainable choices. This initiative aims to restore trust in the market by ensuring that environmental statements are grounded in scientific evidence and transparency rather than marketing opportunism. Central to this regulatory framework is the enforcement of strict standards for voluntary claims, prohibiting ambiguous metrics like aggregate environmental scores and requiring comparable data when products are assessed against one another. The proposal seeks to prevent the proliferation of new certification systems unless they demonstrably add value beyond existing European standards. By mandating independent verification and periodic reviews, the legislation ensures that labels are reliable and transparent, thereby protecting honest businesses from unfair competition by those making dubious environmental promises. This regulatory shift is highly relevant to greenwashing discussions as it directly addresses the core mechanism of deceptive marketing: the exploitation of consumer goodwill through unsubstantiated ecological assertions. By empowering consumer organizations to take legal action against non-compliant manufacturers and harmonizing rules across the bloc, the EU aims to eliminate the ambiguity that allows greenwashing to thrive. Ultimately, this move prioritizes consumer protection and market fairness, ensuring that environmental commitments are verifiable truths rather than empty slogans.
