El Gobierno lanza una guía para "acabar" con el 'ecopostureo' de "algunas empresas" que hacen "publicidad engañosa"

This article highlights the European Commission’s new guidelines aimed at combating greenwashing by demanding transparency and verifiable proof for environmental claims. It emphasizes that vague or unfounded sustainability advertising constitutes unfair commercial practice, effectively penalizing companies that rely on vague ecological positioning rather than concrete, substantiated facts. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the strict regulatory shift requiring firms to demonstrate and verify all environmental assertions before publishing them. By distinguishing between generic, misleading statements and specific, evidence-based communications, the regulations target the core of deceptive marketing, ensuring that "green" labels reflect actual impact rather than mere impression management. Ultimately, the introduction of severe financial penalties underscores the government’s commitment to protecting consumer trust. This regulatory framework forces businesses to move beyond superficial eco-posturing, ensuring that environmental marketing is grounded in reality and fully transparent, thereby eradicating the ambiguity that typically facilitates greenwashing strategies.
