ArcelorMittal: emisiones tóxicas y acusaciones de ‘greenwashing’ en su informe

ArcelorMittal is at the center of a controversy regarding alleged greenwashing, where its public sustainability discourse clashes with its tangible environmental impact. Critics argue that the company’s sustainability reports are vague and overly dependent on external variables, such as energy prices, to justify delayed climate actions. This discrepancy raises serious questions about corporate credibility, suggesting that the steelmaker is prioritizing strategic posturing over the immediate, structural changes necessary for genuine decarbonization. The situation is exacerbated by the company’s reliance on public subsidies and free emission permits, which financially relieve pressure and reduce the urgency for industrial transformation. Despite these benefits and increasing profits, the firm continues to delay critical investments in cleaning its production processes. Consequently, ArcelorMittal remains one of Spain’s largest polluters, with installations in Asturias emitting toxic substances that pose significant health risks to local populations, including chronic respiratory issues, while the promised environmental improvements remain insufficient. This case highlights the broader challenge of aligning corporate rhetoric with operational reality during the energy transition. The accusation of greenwashing stems from marketing steel as a key material for green technologies while maintaining a high-carbon production model. Ultimately, the debate underscores the need for a just transition that not only achieves real ecological reductions but also addresses social concerns, ensuring that industrial transformation does not come at the cost of public health or jobs.
