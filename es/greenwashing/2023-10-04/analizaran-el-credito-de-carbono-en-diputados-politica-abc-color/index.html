Analizarán el crédito de carbono en Diputados - Política - ABC Color

This article highlights a controversial legislative framework in Paraguay that treats carbon reduction as a commercial commodity. By establishing a system where entities define carbon standards without mandated criteria, the law risks enabling arbitrary certification. This opacity creates fertile ground for greenwashing, as projects could claim environmental benefits without rigorous, transparent verification of actual emissions reductions. The lack of clear methods for constituting carbon standards allows for potential manipulation of claims. Without strict oversight, entities may market vague "avoided" or "captured" emissions as genuine climate action. This undermines the integrity of environmental markets, allowing corporations to appear sustainable while potentially masking continued pollution or insufficient ecological stewardship. This case is relevant to greenwashing because it demonstrates how weak regulatory frameworks can legitimize ambiguous environmental claims. When laws fail to enforce precise definitions and verification methods, they facilitate the creation of artificial scarcity and false narratives. Consequently, businesses can exploit these loopholes to sell carbon credits that lack substantive environmental value, misleading consumers and stakeholders about their true ecological impact.
