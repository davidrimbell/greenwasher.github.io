Un informe pone en evidencia lo que esconde el 'greenwashing' de los supermercados

Major Spanish supermarket chains prioritize advertising meat and animal fats, dedicating the vast majority of their marketing efforts to products with high environmental impacts. This strategy contradicts official nutritional guidelines that recommend reducing meat consumption and increasing plant-based proteins. By heavily promoting these items, supermarkets fail to encourage healthier, more sustainable dietary habits among consumers, despite positioning themselves as leaders in sustainability through eco-labels and reduced plastic initiatives. The analysis reveals significant discrepancies between marketing claims and actual product offerings. Most chains promote exotic fruits with high carbon footprints due to transportation, rather than local produce. Similarly, few supermarkets offer genuinely local organic products, creating a disconnect between their sustainability narratives and the reality of their supply chains. This lack of alignment suggests that their green initiatives are superficial, focusing more on image management than on substantive changes to their most polluting activities. This case is relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how companies use selective sustainability messaging to mask environmentally harmful practices. Supermarkets claim responsibility while indirectly driving emissions through the promotion of high-impact animal products and long-distance transport foods. The report highlights a critical transparency gap, arguing that these companies rely on misleading advertising rather than genuine operational changes to appear eco-friendly, thereby deceiving consumers who seek authentic sustainable options.
