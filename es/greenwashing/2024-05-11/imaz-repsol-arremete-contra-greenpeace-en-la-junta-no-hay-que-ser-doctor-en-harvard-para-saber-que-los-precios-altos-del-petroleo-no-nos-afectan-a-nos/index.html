Imaz (Repsol) arremete contra Greenpeace en la Junta: "No hay que ser doctor en Harvard para saber que los precios altos del petróleo no nos afectan a nosotros, sino a las familias"

Repsol’s recent shareholder meeting highlighted a fierce public clash between the oil major and environmental activists regarding its greenwashing accusations. CEO Josu Jon Imaz aggressively rejected criticisms from green groups, arguing that their demands for rapid production cuts ignore economic realities. He contended that abruptly stopping fossil fuel extraction in Europe would drastically increase energy prices, disproportionately hurting ordinary families and industries. This defensive stance underscores how companies often reframe sustainability critiques as anti-business or unrealistic to shield their core revenue streams. The incident illustrates the broader strategic divergence within the energy sector, specifically between traditional oil companies and renewable-focused utilities. Repsol’s leadership argued that Europe’s strict regulatory approach undermines its competitiveness against global rivals like the US and China. By accusing activist groups of colluding with electric utility lobbies, the executives attempted to shift blame away from their own environmental practices. This narrative serves to discredit external scrutiny, positioning the company as a pragmatic actor navigating complex global markets rather than an entity misleading the public about its ecological impact. Ultimately, Repsol confirmed its commitment to maintaining fossil fuel exploration while expanding renewable fuels, a hybrid strategy that critics often label as greenwashing. The company plans to maximize cash flow from traditional oil and gas while marketing its renewable initiatives, arguing that the world still needs fossil fuels. This duality is central to the relevance of the article, as it demonstrates how energy giants may continue heavy carbon-intensive operations while simultaneously promoting themselves as sustainable leaders. Such practices risk deceiving consumers and investors who perceive these companies as transitioning away from fossil fuels, thereby perpetuating the core deception characteristic of greenwashing.
