The article argues that Morocco is engaging in systematic greenwashing by leveraging the COP28 climate summit to legitimize its illegal occupation of Western Sahara. Activists plan to expose how the regime uses high-profile renewable energy projects, such as green hydrogen and solar farms located in occupied territory, to create a facade of environmental stewardship. This strategy aims to obscure the systematic violation of human rights and the illegal exploitation of natural resources, effectively using climate commitments as a cover for territorial annexation. Central to this practice is the complicity of major multinational corporations, including Siemens Gamesa and Enel, which operate within Western Sahara despite international legal rulings declaring such activities illegal. The text highlights that the vast majority of these green initiatives are implemented without the consent of the Sahrawi population. Consequently, these projects serve not only to optimize the theft of resources like phosphates and fisheries but also to garner private international support for Morocco’s false claim of sovereignty over the region. This narrative is crucial to understanding greenwashing because it illustrates how climate action can be weaponized to mask geopolitical crimes and resource extraction. The Sahrawi activists’ presence in both public and diplomatic zones of the summit underscores the urgency of distinguishing genuine environmental efforts from those designed to sanitize political oppression. Their campaign, supported by international media and solidarity movements, seeks to reveal the disconnect between the global fight against climate change and the continued illegal domination of Western Sahara, demonstrating that ecological projects cannot be separated from their unlawful context.
