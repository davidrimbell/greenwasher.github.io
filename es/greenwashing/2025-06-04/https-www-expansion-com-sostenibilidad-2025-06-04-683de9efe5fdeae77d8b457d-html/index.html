The article highlights Kymco’s achievement in obtaining ISO 14064-1 certification for its carbon footprint, presenting this voluntary disclosure as a testament to its environmental responsibility. By quantifying emissions and undergoing independent verification, the company attempts to establish credibility and transparency regarding its operational impact. However, the data reveals that nearly seventy percent of emissions stem from transoceanic maritime transport from Asia. This significant carbon burden, inherent to the globalized supply chain model, contrasts with the narrative of local environmental stewardship often projected by such certifications. This case is relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how companies use third-party verification of specific metrics to construct an eco-friendly image, potentially distracting from the larger, more damaging aspects of their business model. The focus on accurate measurement can mask the substantial environmental costs associated with long-distance logistics, inviting scrutiny over whether such reporting constitutes genuine sustainability or strategic reputation management.
