Santiago Vilanova: “En Barcelona manda el ecoblanqueo”

Santiago Vilanova argues that the concept of sustainability has been hollowed out by greenwashing, where institutions like the Barcelona City Council use superficial environmental rhetoric to mask outdated governance and contradictory policies. This "eco-whitewashing" creates a false sense of progress, allowing administrations to prioritize short-term economic interests, such as tourism, over genuine ecological protection. By treating environmental issues as marketing tools rather than urgent crises, these institutions avoid implementing the radical changes necessary to address climate change, leaving the problem largely unmanaged despite public awareness. The article highlights how this disconnect is evident in Barcelona’s failing air quality policies, where fragmented departmental goals prevent a cohesive solution to pollution. Vilanova critiques the limitations of current measures like low-emission zones, asserting that they are insufficient without an integrated mobility plan. The core implication is that without systemic shifts, such as drastically reducing car usage and closing polluting facilities like the Sant Adrià incinerator, cities will fail legal and environmental standards. This underscores the danger of relying on symbolic actions instead of substantive, holistic strategies that truly mitigate environmental harm. Ultimately, the piece serves as a critical commentary on greenwashing by illustrating how political structures can co-opt ecological language to maintain the status quo. It is relevant because it exposes the gap between environmental branding and actual policy execution, warning that without transparent participation and a commitment to degrowth, sustainability becomes merely a facade. Vilanova’s call for a unified, metropolitan ecological pact emphasizes that authentic sustainability requires abandoning contradictory narratives in favor of concrete, scientifically grounded actions.
