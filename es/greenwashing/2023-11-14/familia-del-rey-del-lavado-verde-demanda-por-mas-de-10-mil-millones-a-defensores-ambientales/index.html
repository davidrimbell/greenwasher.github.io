Familia del “Rey del lavado verde” demanda por más de $10 mil millones a defensores ambientales

The article exposes how the lack of effective state mechanisms to protect environmental defenders, despite international treaties like Escazú, allows corporate intimidation to flourish. It highlights that critical alert systems remain undeveloped, leaving activists vulnerable to legal and economic retaliation. This institutional failure enables companies to silence opposition, undermining the democratic rights to information and participation that the agreement seeks to guarantee. Central to the narrative is the case of the Oasis La Campana development, where residents face multimillion-dollar lawsuits for denouncing illegal expansion near a Biosphere Reserve. The developers, who hold seats in environmental advisory councils while their project violates ecological norms, use the judicial system as a weapon of pressure. This demonstrates how regulatory gaps allow businesses to bypass environmental impact assessments while targeting those who expose these infractions, creating a climate of fear rather than fostering genuine sustainability. This situation is highly relevant to greenwashing as it illustrates its most aggressive dimension: the use of environmental credentials to shield illegal activities from scrutiny. The developers’ involvement in conservation foundations while simultaneously fragmenting ecosystems represents a classic facade of responsibility masking destructive practices. The article argues that such "lavado verde" is not merely misleading marketing but a structural problem where corporate power infiltrates governance to legitimize harm, necessitating urgent legal frameworks to sanction and prevent this distortion of environmental stewardship.
