Ribera irrumpe en la guerra entre Iberdrola y Repsol y respalda el ataque de Galán a Imaz: "Llega a los tribunales el hartazgo por el greenwashing"

The conflict between Iberdrola and Repsol, culminating in a lawsuit for alleged greenwashing, highlights a deeper ideological struggle within Europe’s energy transition. The Spanish government, represented by Minister Teresa Ribera, explicitly supports Iberdrola’s stance, criticizing Repsol’s leadership for climate denialism. This alignment underscores a critical distinction in environmental policy: the push for full economic electrification through dedicated renewable sources versus the "technological neutrality" model favored by fossil fuel giants. This dispute is not merely legal but reflects competing visions for how the European Union should define and subsidize sustainable projects, shaping which industries qualify for green financing and political favor. Beyond ideology, the litigation serves as a fierce commercial battle for market dominance in the electricity sector. Iberdrola’s legal action is a strategic response to Repsol’s significant gains in consumer supply, which challenged the traditional market equilibrium and industry alliances. By accusing Repsol of promoting sustainable initiatives while still encouraging fossil fuel use, Iberdrola aims to undermine the competitor’s credibility and protect its leadership position. This highlights how greenwashing accusations can be weaponized in competitive markets to disrupt rivals who attempt to pivot their branding without altering core business models. This case is highly relevant to understanding greenwashing because it illustrates how environmental credibility is used as a competitive asset. It demonstrates that claims of sustainability are no longer just marketing tools but central elements in legal and political battles for market share. The involvement of state officials further shows how government policy can validate or delegitimize corporate environmental narratives, making the distinction between genuine transition strategies and superficial "green" packaging a matter of both regulatory consequence and commercial survival.
