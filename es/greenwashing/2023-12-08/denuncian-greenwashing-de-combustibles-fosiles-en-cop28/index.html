Denuncian greenwashing de combustibles fósiles en COP28

Paul Polman denounces COP28 as fossil fuel greenwashing, arguing that the summit fails to deliver concrete actions against CO2 emissions. Instead of achieving a total phase-out, negotiations are stalled by industry narratives like carbon capture. This delay is identified as a new form of denial, prioritizing gradual reductions over the urgent elimination required to ensure planetary survival and climate justice. The controversy highlights the critical role of leadership conflicts of interest in environmental governance. The COP president’s ties to the oil and gas sector raise concerns that economic interests are overshadowing scientific necessity. By claiming there is no conclusive evidence for a total phase-out, authorities may be misleading the public and delaying necessary transitions away from carbon-dependent economies toward genuinely sustainable models. This situation is relevant to greenwashing because it demonstrates how industries co-opt international climate forums to project sustainability without substantive change. It reveals a strategy where vague promises and new technologies serve as distractions from the core issue of extraction. Ultimately, it warns against accepting corporate-influenced solutions that maintain the status quo under the guise of climate action, exposing the gap between rhetorical commitments and actual environmental impact.
