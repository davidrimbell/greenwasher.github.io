India is experiencing a revival of natural construction, driven by environmental concerns and a desire to reclaim traditional lifestyles. Architects and communities are increasingly using earth, bamboo, and cal, moving away from industrial methods toward sustainable, contextually relevant structures. This shift challenges the stigma often surrounding natural building techniques. By embracing vernacular practices that align with local climates and ecosystems, the industry is rectifying past extremes where Western-style industrialization threatened these traditions to near extinction. This article is relevant to greenwashing because it highlights the genuine, historical roots of sustainable materials. It distinguishes authentic ecological alignment from superficial environmental claims, proving that true sustainability in India comes from returning to indigenous wisdom rather than adopting empty modern trends.
