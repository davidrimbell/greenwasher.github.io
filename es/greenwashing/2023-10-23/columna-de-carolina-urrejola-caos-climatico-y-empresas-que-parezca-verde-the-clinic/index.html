Columna de Carolina Urrejola | Caos climático y empresas: que parezca verde | The Clinic

The article exposes how Chilean companies, particularly in pollution-intensive sectors like salmon farming and real estate, engage in greenwashing by adopting ESG criteria primarily for marketing rather than substantive operational change. Rather than addressing the climate crisis through genuine sustainability, these firms use environmental language and aesthetics to appease stakeholders and regulators. This strategic "greening" allows them to maintain harmful practices and avoid stricter restrictions while projecting an image of responsibility, effectively prioritizing economic pragmatism over ecological integrity. Specific examples illustrate this disconnect, such as salmon producers advertising pristine Patagonian waters while operating in protected parks, or real estate firms buying land to claim conservation status while continuing to subdivide and develop it. These actions exploit legal loopholes and vague regulations, leveraging the high value placed on biodiversity in public discourse. The involvement of polluting industries in conservation funding further raises concerns about the capture of conservation efforts by corporate interests, undermining the credibility and trust in environmental initiatives. This dynamic is crucial to understanding greenwashing as it reveals a systemic trend where corporate survival depends on the appearance of sustainability rather than actual reduction of environmental impact. The article highlights the urgent need for robust, legally binding frameworks and consensus on common criteria to prevent corporate capture of conservation efforts. Without enforceable standards and true accountability, the gap between marketing claims and ecological reality will continue to widen, exacerbating climate injustice and hindering meaningful progress.
