«La palabra sostenibilidad no es una moda, pero hay mucho lavado verde»

The article highlights that while sustainability is becoming a crucial long-term investment for the international tourism sector, particularly in the Middle East, it is simultaneously plagued by superficial commitments. The interviewee acknowledges that the term "sustainability" is frequently misused as a marketing trend, giving rise to greenwashing where companies issue false carbon footprint reports to appear eco-friendly without making substantial operational changes. To combat this deception, the text emphasizes that genuine efficiency requires concrete technological implementation and verifiable actions, such as diverting waste from landfills and closing the circular economy loop, rather than relying solely on certification labels. The narrative suggests that true sustainability involves deep cultural and infrastructural shifts, like using advanced waste processing machinery, which serves as a tangible proof of commitment distinct from mere public relations statements. This discussion is highly relevant to greenwashing because it exposes the gap between corporate messaging and actual environmental impact. By identifying the prevalence of fraudulent emissions reports and contrasting them with rigorous, on-site inspections and real resource management, the article underscores the necessity of transparency. It serves as a warning that without independent verification and substantial operational reform, sustainability claims in the travel industry remain hollow exercises in reputation management.
