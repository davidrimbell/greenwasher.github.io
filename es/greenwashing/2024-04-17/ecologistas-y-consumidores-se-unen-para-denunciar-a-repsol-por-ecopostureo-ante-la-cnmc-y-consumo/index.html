Ecologistas y consumidores se unen para denunciar a Repsol por ecopostureo ante la CNMC y Consumo

Repsol, España's largest oil company, faces mounting legal challenges from environmental groups and consumer associations for alleged greenwashing. The complaints center on its biobrandy marketing, which claims sustainability while allegedly masking the deforestation in Indonesia caused by palm oil production. Critics argue that these advertising practices mislead the public by hiding significant environmental impacts and presenting fossil-fuel-adjacent products as eco-friendly alternatives. This case highlights a growing trend where regulators and courts are increasingly scrutinizing vague environmental claims by energy firms. Previous denunciations, including one by Iberdrola, underscore how competitors also view these tactics as unfair competition. International precedent shows that regulatory bodies are starting to penalize misleading "green" advertising, reinforcing the idea that sustainability claims must be substantiated by verifiable, transparent data rather than marketing rhetoric. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the systemic risk these practices pose to genuine climate action. By creating false impressions of environmental responsibility, such campaigns confuse consumers and delay the transition away from fossil fuels. This lawsuit exemplifies the critical need for stricter enforcement against ecoposturing, ensuring that corporate sustainability narratives reflect actual ecological impact rather than strategic obfuscation designed to protect profit margins.
