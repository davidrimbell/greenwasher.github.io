El regulador de la publicidad da la razón a Repsol ante la denuncia de Iberdrola por 'greenwashing'

Autocontrol rejected Iberdrola’s claim that Repsol’s advertising misleads consumers about its renewable fuel offerings. The watchdog concluded that the average, informed consumer would clearly understand the "100% renewable" label refers specifically to the new fuel line, not the entire company or its fossil fuel products. Consequently, Iberdrola was also ordered to pay administrative fees, affirming the legitimacy of Repsol’s marketing strategy in the eyes of the advertising self-regulatory body. This ruling is significant for greenwashing debates because it establishes a benchmark for distinguishing between permissible product-specific claims and deceptive generalizations. Autocontrol’s analysis suggests that consumers are capable of distinguishing between specific innovations and a company’s overall portfolio. Therefore, advertising a specific green initiative without implying the entire corporation has transformed does not constitute misleading behavior, protecting companies that clearly delineate their sustainable products from broader corporate practices. However, the relevance of this case extends beyond the administrative decision, as Iberdrola has escalated the matter to commercial courts alleging unfair competition and deceptive advertising. This ongoing judicial challenge highlights the tension between industry self-regulation and legal accountability in environmental marketing. It underscores how claims of greenwashing are contested not just by regulators, but by competitors, creating a complex landscape where the definition of truth in advertising remains a pivotal legal and ethical battleground for energy companies.
