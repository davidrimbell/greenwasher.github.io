The primary conclusion of the article is that combating greenwashing requires clear, harmonized legislation and accessible data to ensure transparency for investors. Experts emphasize that without uniform standards, misleading marketing practices will persist, damaging institutional reputations and confusing retail clients. To achieve this, financial institutions demand "intelligent regulation" that offers legal certainty and conceptual clarity. A homogeneous taxonomy is essential to differentiate genuinely sustainable products from mere marketing, preventing legal risks for companies and ensuring that commitments to sustainability are credible and measurable rather than superficial. Furthermore, the sector highlights the critical role of accompanying companies, particularly SMEs, through their green transition via financial education. By fostering close relationships, financial entities can better filter genuine sustainability efforts, ensuring that investments are truly aligned with environmental goals rather than exploiting regulatory gaps for greenwashing purposes.
