Ecologistas denuncian el 'lavado verde' de la macroexplotación de 3.000 vacas lecheras en Bétera (Valencia)

The article highlights a stark contradiction between corporate environmental branding and local reality, using the More Holstein facility as a prime example. Although the company holds a B Corp certification, implying high social and environmental standards, local residents face severe pollution, including noise, flies, and waste odors. This discrepancy suggests that such eco-labels may serve more as marketing tools than genuine guarantees of sustainability, allowing industrial operations to maintain a positive public image while inflicting significant harm on nearby communities. The relevance to greenwashing lies in the perception of legitimacy this certification provides to an operation accused of long-term illegal activities and regulatory violations. Activists argue that the B Corp status acts as a "green" shield, obscuring the true socio-environmental costs and potentially enabling further expansion. This case illustrates how corporations can leverage prestigious certifications to distract from persistent ecological damage, such as illegal dumping and water contamination, thereby misleading stakeholders about their actual operational impact. Ultimately, this incident reflects a broader systemic issue where economic interests are prioritized over citizens' rights and environmental protection. The convergence of multiple international activist groups underscores that this is not an isolated event but part of a widespread model of industrial intensification. By exposing the gap between certified values and lived experiences, the article warns that without rigorous scrutiny, greenwashing labels risk becoming mere cover stories for unsustainable agricultural practices that degrade natural resources and marginalize local populations.
