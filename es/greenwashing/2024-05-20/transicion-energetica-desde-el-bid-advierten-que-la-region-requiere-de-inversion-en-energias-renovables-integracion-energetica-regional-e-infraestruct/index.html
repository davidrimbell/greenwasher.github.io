Transición energética | Desde el BID advierten que la región requiere de inversión en energías renovables, integración energética regional e infraestructura resiliente

The article highlights that while Latin America has significantly expanded its renewable energy capacity, the current pace remains insufficient to meet 2050 net-zero targets. To bridge this gap, regional integration is positioned as a critical strategy, allowing countries to share clean energy surplus and balance demand through interconnected grids. This approach not only enhances energy security but also reduces reliance on fossil fuels, offering substantial economic and environmental benefits by displacing inefficient, polluting sources. However, the transition faces significant hurdles, particularly regarding regulatory barriers that hinder the deployment of essential energy storage technologies. While storage is vital for managing the intermittency of renewables, inconsistent national frameworks complicate investment and implementation. The Inter-American Development Bank emphasizes the need for harmonized regulations and technical support to create stable environments that foster innovation and ensure resilient infrastructure capable of withstanding climate-induced extremes. This narrative is relevant to greenwashing because it underscores the complexity behind true decarbonization. It reveals that merely reporting increased renewable capacity or launching high-profile integration projects can mask underlying failures in regulatory reform and technological readiness. True sustainability requires more than superficial infrastructure investments; it demands systemic changes in policy and storage solutions to ensure that energy transitions are robust, equitable, and genuinely effective rather than just visually appealing.
