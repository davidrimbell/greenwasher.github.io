Denuncian a diecisiete aerolíneas por ‘greenwashing’

A European consumer organization has accused numerous airlines of greenwashing, claiming their environmental marketing is deceptive. These carriers encourage passengers to buy carbon credits to offset flight emissions, despite the uncertain real-world benefits of such compensation. This practice misleads travelers into believing their travel is sustainable when it is not, violating EU unfair commercial practices laws. The complaint further highlights that airlines charge extra fees for Sustainable Aviation Fuels that remain largely unviable and regulated by low mandatory targets. By marketing these unreliably effective solutions, companies create an illusion of eco-responsibility while failing to address the fundamental unsustainability of air travel in the near future. This tactic exploits consumer goodwill for profit. This case is crucial for understanding greenwashing because it exposes how the aviation sector uses vague claims and unproven technologies to mask its environmental impact. It demonstrates the gap between marketing narratives and scientific reality, urging regulators to enforce transparency. Recognizing these patterns helps consumers identify misleading environmental claims across various industries.
