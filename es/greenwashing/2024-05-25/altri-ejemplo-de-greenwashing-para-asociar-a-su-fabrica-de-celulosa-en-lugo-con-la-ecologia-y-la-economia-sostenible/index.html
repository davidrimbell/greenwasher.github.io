Altri, ejemplo de 'greenwashing' para asociar a su fábrica de celulosa en Lugo con la ecología y la economía sostenible

The article exposes Altri’s strategic campaign to rebrand its controversial cellulose plant in Palas de Rei as an environmentally friendly initiative, effectively using greenwashing to counteract social and ecological opposition. By leveraging visual cues like green tones and nature imagery, alongside a high-profile lobbying firm with deep political connections, the company aims to dissociate its industrial activities from their actual environmental impact, presenting the project instead as a model of sustainable innovation and economic progress supported by regional and national authorities. Experts and local communities highlight that the paper industry is inherently one of the most damaging sectors due to massive water extraction, deforestation via eucalyptus monocultures, and the release of toxic emissions and heated wastewater. The proposed facility plans to draw millions of liters of water daily from the Ulla River, a critical source for drinking water and local ecosystems, and return highly treated, warm effluents that risk altering river temperatures and harming marine life. These realities contradict the company’s claims of minimal ecological footprint and strict adherence to sustainability principles. This case is relevant to greenwashing because it illustrates how corporations utilize sophisticated public relations and political alliances to mask hazardous industrial practices behind a facade of eco-responsibility. By renaming entities with nature-inspired labels and securing significant public funding under the guise of green investment, Altri attempts to legitimize a project that environmentalists argue exacerbates desertification, pollution, and resource depletion. The discrepancy between the marketed narrative of sustainability and the documented ecological degradation underscores the deceptive nature of corporate environmental branding when divorced from genuine operational accountability.
